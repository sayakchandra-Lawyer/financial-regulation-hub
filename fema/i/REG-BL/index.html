Applies to
Persons resident in India borrowing or lending in foreign exchange, or borrowing in rupees from, or lending in rupees to, persons resident outside India; Authorised dealer banks, NBFCs and housing finance institutions lending to non-residents

Key concepts

  • General prohibition with listed exceptions (regulation 3)
  • Restricted end-uses for all borrowed funds (regulation 3A)
  • Foreign currency borrowing, including ECB and trade credit (regulation 4)
  • Foreign currency lending, including lending abroad (regulation 5)
  • Rupee borrowing from non-residents (regulation 6)
  • Rupee lending to non-residents (regulation 7)
  • Repo by non-residents (regulation 7A)
  • Change of residential status (regulation 8)
  • Schedule I: ECB framework; Schedule II: trade credit; Schedule III: lending in foreign currency

Key obligations

  • regulation 3: no resident may borrow or lend in foreign exchange, or borrow rupees from or lend rupees to a non-resident, except as the regulations allow or with RBI permission
  • regulation 3A: borrowed funds may not be used for chit funds, Nidhi companies, real estate business or farmhouses, agriculture and plantations (with listed exceptions), trading in transferable development rights, transacting in securities (except strategic corporate actions under company law, the SEBI takeover code, SARFAESI or the IBC), repaying a domestic rupee loan taken for a restricted end-use or classified as NPA, or on-lending for any of these
  • regulation 3A: borrowing for a construction-development project allows plots to be sold only after trunk infrastructure is developed; an industrial park needs at least 10 units, no unit above 50% and at least 66% of the allocable area for industry
  • regulation 4B(v) and (vi): a resident individual may borrow up to USD 250,000 (or another amount RBI decides) from relatives abroad, and a resident individual studying abroad may borrow up to USD 250,000 outside India for education fees and maintenance; both are subject to the terms RBI sets in consultation with the Government
  • regulation 6B(vi): a resident individual may borrow rupees from an NRI or OCI relative on a non-repatriation basis, received by inward remittance or from the lender's NRE, NRO, FCNR(B) or SNRR account; repayment goes only to the lender's NRO account
  • regulation 7B(i): a registered NBFC or housing finance institution may give a housing loan or vehicle loan to an NRI or OCI cardholder on RBI's terms, and the borrower must not use the money for a restricted end-use
  • regulation 7B(ii): a resident entity may lend rupees to its NRI or OCI employees under its staff welfare scheme
  • regulation 7B(iii): a resident individual may lend rupees to an NRI or OCI relative within the Liberalised Remittance Scheme limit
  • regulation 7A(iv): an AD bank may lend rupees to residents of Bhutan, Nepal and Sri Lanka, and to banks there, for cross-border trade

Deadlines and cycles

  • Schedule I para 16: Form ECB 1 to get a Loan Registration Number before drawdown; Revised Form ECB 1 and Form ECB 2 within 7 calendar days of the end of the month concerned

If you breach it

  • Schedule I para 16(2): a borrower who misses a reporting timeline may pay a late submission fee after completing the reporting

General consequences under the statute. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).
