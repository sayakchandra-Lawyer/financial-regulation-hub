Applies to
Every person resident in India, and every person resident outside India dealing in foreign exchange, foreign securities or Indian assets covered by the Act; Authorised persons, including authorised dealer banks

Key concepts

  • Person resident in India and person resident outside India (s. 2(v) and (w))
  • Dealing in foreign exchange only through an authorised person (s. 3)
  • Capital account transactions (s. 6): RBI specifies permissible debt instruments transactions (s. 6(2)); the Central Government covers non-debt instruments (s. 6(2A)) and decides what is a debt instrument (s. 6(7))
  • Duties of authorised persons (s. 10)
  • Directions to authorised persons (s. 11)
  • Penalties (s. 13) and enforcement of orders (ss. 14 and 14A)
  • Compounding (s. 15)
  • Adjudication and appeals (ss. 16 to 19 and 35)
  • Contravention by companies (s. 42)

Key obligations

  • s. 3: no person may deal in or transfer foreign exchange or foreign security, or make any payment to or receive any payment from a person resident outside India, except as the Act, rules or regulations allow or with general or special permission of RBI
  • s. 6(2): RBI, in consultation with the Central Government, specifies the permissible capital account transactions involving debt instruments (which covers borrowing, lending and guarantees), the limits and the conditions; s. 6(2A) gives the Central Government the same power for non-debt instruments (the former s. 6(3) was omitted from 15-10-2019)
  • s. 10(5): an authorised person must, before carrying out a transaction, require the customer to make such declaration and give such information as reasonably satisfies it that the transaction will not contravene the Act
  • s. 10(4) and 11: authorised persons must comply with RBI's directions and furnish the information RBI calls for

Deadlines and cycles

  • s. 14: civil imprisonment may follow if a penalty is not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay
  • s. 15(1): compounding within 180 days of the application
  • s. 16(6): the adjudicating authority tries to dispose of a complaint within one year
  • s. 17 and 19: appeal within 45 days of receiving the order (the Tribunal may require the penalty to be deposited first)
  • s. 35: appeal to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days, extendable by another 60 days

If you breach it

  • s. 11(3): an authorised person that breaches RBI directions or fails to file a return may be fined up to Rs 10,000, and up to Rs 2,000 a day for a continuing breach
  • s. 13(1): up to three times the sum involved, or up to Rs 2 lakh where the amount cannot be quantified, plus up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues
  • s. 13(1A): a person found to have acquired foreign exchange, foreign security or immovable property outside India in contravention of the Act, of aggregate value above the threshold notified under s. 37A, faces a penalty of up to three times the sum involved and confiscation of an equivalent value in India
  • s. 13(1B) to (1D): on the adjudicating authority's recommendation, the Directorate of Enforcement may file a criminal complaint; on conviction the person may be imprisoned for up to five years and fined
  • s. 42(1): every person in charge of and responsible to the company for the conduct of its business is deemed guilty with the company, unless that person proves the contravention took place without their knowledge or that they exercised due diligence to prevent it
  • s. 42(2): separately, a director, manager, secretary or other officer is deemed guilty where the contravention is proved to have taken place with their consent or connivance or to be attributable to their neglect

General consequences under the statute. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).
