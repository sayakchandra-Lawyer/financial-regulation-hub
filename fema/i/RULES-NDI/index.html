Applies to
Indian companies receiving foreign investment, including by conversion of ECB; Promoters pledging shares to secure borrowing; NBFCs taking pledges of shares held by non-residents

Key concepts

  • Equity instruments: equity shares, fully and mandatorily convertible debentures and preference shares, share warrants
  • Pledge of shares (rule 9(8))
  • Convertible notes for start-ups (rule 18)
  • Other financial services: 100% under the automatic route (Schedule I, F.10)

Key obligations

  • rule 2(k): equity instruments are equity shares (including partly paid shares), fully and mandatorily convertible debentures and preference shares that are fully paid, and share warrants; debentures and preference shares that are not fully and mandatorily convertible are not equity instruments and are ECB under Schedule I para 4 of the Borrowing and Lending Regulations
  • rule 9(8): a promoter of an Indian company that has raised ECB may pledge its shares in the company, or in its group companies, to secure the ECB, on terms that are co-terminus with the ECB and subject to an end-use certificate and the AD bank's no objection
  • rule 9(8): a non-resident may pledge shares of an Indian company to a bank in India, an overseas bank, or an RBI-registered NBFC, for credit facilities to the Indian company or its group
  • rule 18: a start-up may issue convertible notes of at least Rs 25 lakh in one tranche to a non-resident

General consequences under the statute. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).
