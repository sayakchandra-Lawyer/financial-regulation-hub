Indian entities lending to, or guaranteeing the obligations of, their foreign entities; AD banks that report and monitor these transactions

Key concepts

  • Loans to a foreign entity (regulation 3 and 4)
  • Guarantees for a foreign entity (regulation 5)
  • Pledge and charge (regulation 6)
  • Reporting (regulation 10)
  • Late submission fee (regulation 11)
  • Bar on further commitment until delays are regularised (regulation 12)

Key obligations

  • regulation 3: an Indian entity may lend to, or give a non-fund commitment for, a foreign entity only if it is eligible to make overseas direct investment, has made such investment and has control of the foreign entity
  • regulation 4: every loan must be backed by a loan agreement and carry interest on an arm's length basis
  • regulation 5: guarantees may not be open-ended; 50% of a performance guarantee counts towards the financial commitment limit; an invoked guarantee is treated as lending
  • regulation 6: the Indian entity may pledge its shares in the foreign entity, or create a charge on its assets, to secure funding for itself or the foreign entity

Deadlines and cycles

  • regulation 10(2): a financial commitment is reported at the time of the outward remittance or of making the commitment, whichever is earlier; a disinvestment within 30 days of receiving the proceeds; a restructuring within 30 days of the restructuring
  • regulation 10(4): Annual Performance Report for each foreign entity by 31 December each year (by 31 December of the next year where the entity's accounting year ends on 31 December)

If you breach it

  • regulation 11: late reporting may be regularised by a late submission fee
  • regulation 12: no further financial commitment or transfer is allowed until delayed reporting is regularised

General consequences under the statute. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).
