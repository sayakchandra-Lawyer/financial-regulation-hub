Key concepts

  • One ECB framework replacing the old tracks, eligible-borrower lists and recognised-lender rules
  • Borrowing limit: higher of USD 1 billion outstanding or 300% of net worth
  • Minimum average maturity of 3 years (1-3 years for manufacturers up to USD 150 million)
  • Market-based cost, with the trade credit ceiling for maturities under 3 years
  • New end-use list in regulation 3A
  • New Forms ECB 1 and ECB 2

Key obligations

  • Schedule I para 1: entities under restructuring or insolvency resolution may borrow only if the approved plan allows it; an entity facing a FEMA investigation, adjudication or appeal may borrow but must disclose it in Form ECB 1
  • Schedule I para 2: an ECB may be raised from any person resident outside India, a foreign branch of an RBI-regulated lender, or a financial institution in IFSC
  • Schedule I para 3: the ECB may be in foreign currency or rupees, and the currency may be changed in any direction at an exchange rate that does not raise the liability
  • Schedule I para 4: any commercial borrowing with interest and principal repayment counts, including FCCBs and FCEBs; preference shares or debentures that are not fully and mandatorily convertible, issued on or after 30-04-2007, are ECB; trade credit up to 3 years, export advances, Debt Instruments Regulations investments, convertible notes and FVCI debt are not
  • Schedule I para 5: a borrower may raise ECB up to the higher of (a) outstanding ECB of USD 1 billion, or (b) total outstanding borrowing, external and domestic, of 300% of net worth on the last audited standalone balance sheet; outstanding borrowing excludes non-fund based credit and mandatorily convertible securities, and the proposed ECB (other than a refinancing ECB) is counted when testing the limit; the limit does not apply to entities regulated by a financial sector regulator
  • Schedule I para 6: minimum average maturity of 3 years; manufacturers may borrow at 1-3 years up to USD 150 million outstanding; no call or put option before the minimum maturity, with exceptions for conversion, repayment from fresh equity, refinance, waiver and corporate actions
  • Schedule I para 7 to 9: cost in line with market conditions (below 3 years, within the trade credit ceiling); prepayment and penal charges at market terms; related-party ECB at arm's length
  • Schedule I para 10: drawdown only after the Loan Registration Number; rupee proceeds credited to the designated AD bank by the end of the following month and, pending use, kept in an unencumbered fixed deposit of up to one year
  • Schedule I para 11: security by charge on assets or by guarantee under the Guarantees Regulations 2026, with a security clause in the loan agreement and a no-objection certificate from existing lenders; entities regulated by RBI shall not issue any guarantee
  • Schedule I para 13: conversion of ECB, including matured unpaid ECB, into equity under the NDI Rules with no extra cost, with lender consent and subject to RBI's restructuring norms where the borrower has RBI-regulated lenders
  • regulation 1(3): ECBs with a Loan Registration Number obtained before 16-02-2026 continue to comply with the regulations that applied then (see the earlier ECB framework entry), except that reporting follows the amended regulations; a later refinancing must keep the minimum average maturity of the original borrowing (Schedule I para 12)

Deadlines and cycles

  • Revised Form ECB 1 within 7 calendar days of the end of the month in which a change takes effect
  • Form ECB 2 within 7 calendar days of the end of the month of drawdown or debt servicing

General consequences under the statute. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).
