Key concepts

  • Compounding authority in RBI set by the sum involved
  • Directorate of Enforcement compounds s. 3(a) contraventions
  • Application fee and the compounding order
  • Contraventions that cannot be compounded (rule 9)

Key obligations

  • rule 4(1): RBI officers compound by the sum involved: up to Rs 60 lakh by an Assistant General Manager, up to Rs 2.5 crore by a Deputy General Manager, up to Rs 5 crore by a General Manager, and above Rs 5 crore by a Chief General Manager
  • rule 4(4): application to RBI's Foreign Exchange Department in the prescribed form with a fee of Rs 10,000 plus applicable GST; contraventions of s. 3(a) go to the Directorate of Enforcement (rule 5)
  • rule 9: no compounding where the amount involved cannot be quantified, where s. 37A applies, where the Directorate of Enforcement considers it a serious contravention suspected of money-laundering, terror financing or affecting sovereignty, where a penalty has already been imposed under s. 13, or where it needs further investigation
  • rule 4(2): a contravention cannot be compounded within three years of an earlier similar contravention being compounded

Deadlines and cycles

  • rule 8: the compounding order is passed within 180 days of the application
  • rules 10 and 11: the compounding amount is paid within 15 days of the order, failing which the application is treated as never made and the contravention is dealt with under the Act

If you breach it

  • If the sum is not paid on time, the compounding fails and adjudication under s. 16 can follow

General consequences under the statute. Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).
