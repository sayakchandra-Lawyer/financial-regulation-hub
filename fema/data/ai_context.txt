SHARED STATUTORY PENALTY TEXTS:
P1: FEM Act, 1999: a person who contravenes the Act, or any rule, regulation, notification, direction or order under it, is liable to a penalty of up to three times the sum involved where it can be quantified, or up to Rs 2 lakh where it cannot, and a further penalty of up to Rs 5,000 a day while the contravention continues (s. 13(1)). The adjudicating authority may also order confiscation of the currency, security or property involved (s. 13(2)). A penalty not paid within 90 days of the notice to pay can lead to civil imprisonment (s. 14). A contravention may be compounded by RBI or the Directorate of Enforcement within 180 days of the application (s. 15), subject to the exclusions in the Compounding Rules. Every officer in charge of a company's business when it contravened is also liable unless they show lack of knowledge or due diligence (s. 42). Appeals lie to the Special Director (Appeals) or the Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (ss. 17 and 19), and to the High Court on a question of law within 60 days (s. 35).

GLOSSARY (plain-language meanings, not RBI text):
FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999): The law that governs dealings in foreign exchange and transactions between residents and non-residents. RBI makes regulations and directions under it; the Central Government makes rules.
Person resident in India: Broadly, an individual who stayed in India for more than 182 days in the previous financial year (with exceptions for those who left or came for specified purposes), and any company or body registered in India. Everyone else is a person resident outside India.
Capital account transaction: A transaction that changes the assets or liabilities, including contingent liabilities, outside India of a resident, or in India of a non-resident. Borrowing, lending and guarantees across the border are capital account transactions.
Authorised dealer (AD bank): A bank authorised by RBI under s. 10 of FEMA to deal in foreign exchange. Category-I AD banks handle ECB, trade credit and guarantee reporting for their customers.
ECB (External commercial borrowing): A commercial loan raised by an Indian entity from outside India, in foreign currency or rupees, including bonds, FCCBs and FCEBs, on the terms of Schedule I to the Borrowing and Lending Regulations.
LRN (Loan Registration Number): The number RBI allots to an ECB on a Form ECB 1 filed through the designated AD bank. The money can be drawn only after the LRN is issued.
MAMP (Minimum average maturity period): The shortest average life an ECB may have: three years, or one to three years for a manufacturing borrower up to USD 150 million outstanding.
All-in-cost: The total cost of a borrowing: interest, fees, expenses, guarantee fees and similar charges, but not commitment fees or withholding tax paid in rupees. It is capped for trade credit and for ECBs shorter than three years.
End-use restrictions: Purposes for which money borrowed under the Borrowing and Lending Regulations may not be used in India, such as real estate business, chit funds, transacting in securities (other than strategic corporate actions) and repaying NPA rupee loans.
Trade credit (TC): Credit for imports into India given by the overseas supplier (suppliers' credit) or by a bank or financial institution abroad (buyers' credit). Trade credit up to three years is not ECB.
Form TC: The monthly statement in which AD banks report trade credits approved by their branches, due by the 10th of the following month.
FCCB (Foreign currency convertible bond): A foreign currency bond issued by an Indian company that can be converted into its shares. It is a form of ECB.
FCEB (Foreign currency exchangeable bond): A foreign currency bond that can be exchanged into the shares of another company (the offered company). It is a form of ECB.
Form ECB 1: The application, filed through the designated AD bank, that gives the ECB's terms and obtains the LRN. Changes go in a Revised Form ECB 1 within seven calendar days of the end of the month.
Form ECB 2: The monthly return of ECB drawdowns and debt servicing, due within seven calendar days of the end of the month.
Late submission fee (LSF): A fee that regularises late FEMA reporting without compounding. Typically Rs 7,500 for returns that do not capture flows, and Rs 7,500 plus 0.025% of the amount for each year of delay for returns that do.
Compounding: Settling an admitted FEMA contravention by paying an amount fixed by RBI (or the Enforcement Directorate for s. 3(a)), instead of adjudication. Governed by the Compounding Rules 2024 and RBI's Master Direction.
Adjudication: Proceedings under s. 16 of FEMA in which an adjudicating authority decides a contravention and may impose a penalty under s. 13.
Guarantee: A contract to perform the promise, or discharge the liability, of another person if that person defaults. Under FEMA a resident may be a party to a cross-border guarantee only as the Guarantees Regulations 2026 allow.
GRN forms: The guarantee reporting forms (GRN Issue, GRN Modification and GRN Invocation) filed with the AD bank, which reports to RBI on CIMS and allots a Guarantee Transaction Number.
Financial commitment: The total of an Indian entity's overseas direct investment, debt to and guarantees for its foreign entities. It may not exceed 400% of net worth, and above USD 1 billion a year needs RBI approval.
Form FC: The form in which an Indian entity reports a financial commitment, including a loan or guarantee to a foreign entity, through its AD bank.
APR (Annual Performance Report): The yearly report on each foreign entity in which an Indian entity has made overseas direct investment, due by 31 December.
NRI (Non-resident Indian): A person resident outside India who is a citizen of India.
OCI cardholder (Overseas Citizen of India): A person registered as an Overseas Citizen of India cardholder under the Citizenship Act, 1955. The B&L Regulations treat OCIs like NRIs for rupee loans.
NRO account (Non-Resident Ordinary account): A rupee account for a non-resident's income and money in India. Loans by residents to NRI relatives and staff loans are credited here.
NRE account (Non-Resident External account): A repatriable rupee account of an NRI or OCI, funded from abroad.
FCNR(B) (Foreign Currency Non-Resident (Bank) account): A term deposit in foreign currency held by an NRI or OCI with an AD bank in India.
SNRR account (Special Non-Resident Rupee account): A rupee account that any non-resident may open with an AD bank for business in India, including at an IFSC branch since June 2026.
LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme): The scheme that lets a resident individual remit up to USD 2,50,000 a financial year for permitted purposes. Rupee loans to NRI relatives count within it.
FPI (Foreign portfolio investor): A non-resident investor registered with SEBI that may buy listed and some unlisted Indian debt within RBI's limits, such as 15% of outstanding corporate bonds.
General Route: The standard route for FPI debt investment, with the conditions in para 4 of the Non-resident Investment in Debt Instruments Directions. The Voluntary Retention Route was merged into it on 01-04-2026.
Equity instruments: Equity shares, fully and mandatorily convertible debentures and preference shares, and share warrants. Instruments that are not fully and mandatorily convertible are debt and fall under the ECB rules.
Convertible note: An instrument issued by a start-up as debt that is repayable or convertible into shares within ten years. A non-resident may buy it for Rs 25 lakh or more in one tranche, and it is not ECB.
FERR (Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve): A cash reserve of 20% of the rupee notional amount that AD banks keep with RBI on certain rupee derivatives where foreign currency is bought, from October 2026.
Contracted exposure: An existing foreign currency exposure, such as an ECB or an import payable, that a user may hedge with an AD bank under the Risk Management Direction.
NDDC (Non-deliverable derivative contract): A rupee derivative settled in foreign currency without delivery of rupees. From October 2026 AD banks may offer it to non-residents only for hedging.
IFSC (International Financial Services Centre): A centre set up under the IFSCA Act, 2019, such as GIFT City. Financial institutions in an IFSC are recognised ECB lenders.
PRAVAAH: RBI's online portal for applications and references, including compounding applications and overseas investment references.
CIMS (Centralised Information Management System): RBI's online return filing system (Sankalan portal), used by AD banks for guarantee reporting and other FEMA returns.

FEMA CHANGES (2026-01-01 to 2026-11-26):
- 2026-10-23 Circular: Compounding of certain FEMA contraventions delegated to RBI Regional Offices (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 28/2026-27). Applies: Applicants compounding contraventions of the Borrowing and Lending Regulations, the Guarantees Regulations or the overseas investment framework.. Changes: Borrowing and lending, and guarantee, contraventions are compounded by the Regional Office with jurisdiction over the applicant's registered office. Overseas investment cases go to one of seven Regional Offices by the prefix of the unique identification number; mixed cases stay with the Central Office. Actions: Check which RBI office should receive any pending or planned compounding application.
- 2026-10-12 Circular: Non-deliverable rupee derivatives for non-residents only for hedging; reserve on AD banks' own hedges (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 27/2026-27). Applies: AD banks with IFSC banking units, standalone primary dealers, and non-resident users.. Changes: AD banks may offer non-deliverable rupee derivatives to non-residents only for hedging. A 20% Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve applies to AD banks' own rupee hedges where they buy foreign currency. Actions: Update hedging documentation for non-resident users.
- 2026-10-10 Circular: Foreign Exchange Risk Reserve of 20% on rupee derivatives hedging current account payments (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 26/2026-27). Applies: AD banks and users buying foreign currency forward against rupees for current account transactions.. Changes: AD banks keep 20% of the rupee notional of each such contract above USD 2 million as cash with RBI until the contract ends. Splitting deals to stay below the threshold is a violation. Actions: Expect revised pricing on forward cover above USD 2 million.
- 2026-10-10 Circular: No rebooking of cancelled rupee derivatives; threshold without proof of exposure cut to USD 5 million (A.P. (DIR Series) Circular No. 25/2026-27). Applies: Users hedging with AD banks, including ECB and trade credit borrowers, and AD banks.. Changes: Cancelled rupee derivatives may not be rebooked; rollovers on maturity are still allowed. Positions without establishing the underlying exposure fall from USD 100 million to USD 5 million. Users give an undertaking that the same exposure is not hedged with another AD bank. Actions: Review hedging policy for ECB and trade credit exposures. Keep evidence of the underlying exposure ready.
