# National Housing Bank (NHB) register summary for review

Public material: NHB Act, NHB circulars/advisories/policies, RBI HFC Directions 2025. Please check each point for errors of law or fact (amounts, days, thresholds, effective dates, section numbers), important omissions for HFCs, refinance borrowers, home loan customers and HFC auditors, and misleading wording. Number your findings and cite the instrument.

## The National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (as amended, including by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019 and the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023) (Act 53 of 1987; issued 1987-12-23; In force (amended))
- Housing finance institutions that are companies (housing finance companies, HFCs): Chapter V
- Scheduled banks, HFCs and other institutions that borrow from NHB (s. 14)
- Auditors of HFCs (s. 33) and depositors of HFCs (ss. 36A and 36B)
- NHB is the principal agency to promote housing finance institutions and give them financial and other support (preamble, s. 14)
- s. 14(b): NHB may lend and give other financial assistance for housing to HFCs, scheduled banks, state co-operative agricultural and rural development banks and other notified institutions (the basis of refinance)
- Since 09-08-2019 the Reserve Bank registers and regulates HFCs (ss. 29A, 29B, 30, 30A, 33(1A), 33A, 35B); NHB keeps supervision, inspection and returns (ss. 31, 32, 33(3), 34)
- s. 29A(1): no company may carry on housing finance as its principal business without a certificate of registration and net owned fund of Rs 10 crore or the higher amount RBI notifies (now Rs 20 crore)
- s. 29B: liquid assets on deposits (approved securities and bank or NHB deposits), with penal interest to NHB for any shortfall
- s. 29C: reserve fund of at least 20% of net profit before any dividend
- s. 36A: an NHB officer authorised by the Central Government may order an HFC to repay a matured deposit
- s. 29A(1)-(2): apply to RBI for a certificate of registration and keep the minimum net owned fund; certificates NHB granted before 09-08-2019 are deemed granted under the Act
- s. 29B(1)-(2): keep at least 5% of deposits in unencumbered approved securities and, with bank or NHB deposits or NHB bonds, at least 10% (or the higher rate RBI notifies; RBI HFC Directions 2025 para 76 now sets 15% from 01-07-2025)
- s. 29C(1)-(2): transfer at least 20% of net profit to a reserve fund every year; any appropriation from it must be for a purpose RBI specifies and be reported to NHB and RBI within 21 days
- s. 31: a deposit-taking HFC must give NHB and RBI the statements on deposits NHB specifies, and send its annual accounts to large depositors if NHB requires
- s. 32: every HFC must furnish the statements, information or particulars NHB or RBI calls for and comply with directions under Chapter V
- s. 33(1)-(2): the auditor must enquire whether the HFC has furnished the deposit statements and, if not, report to NHB and RBI the aggregate deposits, and include that report in the audit report under s. 143(2) of the Companies Act, 2013
- s. 34(2): directors, officers and employees must produce books and documents and give information to NHB's inspecting authority within the time it fixes
- s. 35: no one may solicit deposits for an HFC without its written authority and an advertisement complying with RBI orders
- s. 29C(2): report an appropriation from the reserve fund to NHB and RBI within 21 days
- s. 29B(5): penal interest on a liquid asset shortfall is payable within 14 days of NHB's demand
- s. 29A(7): appeal against rejection or cancellation of registration to the Central Government within 30 days
- s. 52A(3): a penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand notice
- s. 29A(6): RBI may cancel the certificate of registration, among other grounds, for failing to comply with a direction of RBI or NHB, to maintain proper accounts or to produce books for inspection by RBI or NHB
- s. 29B(4): penal interest to NHB of 3% a year above the bank rate on any liquid asset shortfall, rising to 5% if it continues into later quarters
- s. 33(3): NHB may, and on RBI's direction must, order a special audit at the HFC's cost
- s. 33B: NHB may apply to wind up an HFC that cannot pay its debts, is disqualified under s. 29A or has been barred from taking deposits for 3 months or more
- s. 33C: RBI may debar an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 for up to 3 years
- ss. 49 and 52A: see the general consequences

## The Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019, Chapter VI Part VII: amendments to the National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (Act 23 of 2019, ss. 154 to 172; issued 2019-08-01; In force)
- Housing finance companies and their auditors
- Moved registration and regulation of HFCs from NHB to the Reserve Bank with effect from 09-08-2019
- Limited Chapter V to housing finance institutions that are companies
- Gave RBI the power to direct auditors (s. 33(1A)) and NHB a duty to inspect when RBI directs (s. 34(1))
- s. 29A(2) as amended: applications pending with NHB on 09-08-2019 stood transferred to RBI, and existing certificates continue as if granted under the amended Act
- ss. 30 and 30A: RBI, not NHB, regulates deposit advertisements and gives prudential directions to HFCs
- s. 34(4): NHB must send RBI a copy of every inspection report

## RBI Press Release 2019-2020/419: Transfer of Regulation of Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) to Reserve Bank of India (Press Release 2019-2020/419; issued 2019-08-13; In force)
- Housing finance companies
- HFCs are treated as a category of NBFC for regulatory purposes from 09-08-2019
- NHB continues to supervise HFCs, HFCs continue to file returns with NHB, and grievance redressal for HFC customers stays with NHB
- HFCs were to follow NHB's directions until RBI issued its own framework (it did so on 17-02-2021, now replaced by the 2025 Directions)
- Applications for registration under s. 29A(2) go to RBI

## Reserve Bank of India (Housing Finance Companies) Directions, 2025 (read in full in the RBI section) (RBI/DoR/2025-26/365; DoR.FIN.REC.284/03-10-119/2025-26; issued 2025-11-28; In force (amended))
- HFCs registered under s. 29A of the NHB Act (Middle and Upper Layers)
- Auditors of HFCs (Chapter IX)
- The RBI rulebook for HFCs; this page lists only the parts that send an HFC to NHB
- Reporting and instructions that the RBI directions address to the supervisor are to be made to NHB as the supervisor of HFCs (paras 5 and 8)
- Principal business test: at least 60% of total assets in housing finance and 50% in individual housing loans (para 10(9)); minimum net owned fund Rs 20 crore (para 16)
- para 144: tell customers, at branches and on the website, that if the HFC does not reply within one month or the reply is unsatisfactory, they may complain to NHB online or by post
- para 234: supervision of HFCs rests with NHB; para 236: NHB inspects HFCs under s. 34 of the NHB Act
- para 237: send NHB the audited financial statements, auditor's report and Board's report within 15 days of the AGM; para 238: deposit-taking HFCs add an auditor's certificate on depositor liabilities
- para 239: comply with any reporting requirement NHB prescribes
- para 221: quarterly report to NHB on counterfeit notes detected, within 7 days of the quarter end (nil report if none)
- para 54: take NHB's prior approval before approaching the Registrar of Companies to change the accounting year
- para 127: the auditor must report unfavourable or qualified statements and non-compliance to NHB and RBI
- 15 days after the AGM: annual accounts to NHB (para 237)
- 7 days after each quarter: counterfeit note report to NHB (para 221)
- One month: wait before a customer may escalate to NHB (para 144)
- para 235: RBI or NHB may impose a penalty under s. 52A of the NHB Act for any contravention of the Act or directions
- para 233: an unreported appropriation from the reserve fund is a violation attracting penalty

## Early Warning Signals Framework in HFCs (NHB(ND)/DOS/Sup. Circular No. 9/2022-23) (NHB(ND)/DOS/Sup. Circular No. 9/2022-23; issued 2023-04-26; In force)
- HFCs with assets of Rs 1,000 crore or more as on 31-03-2023
- Frauds in HFCs often arise from seller impersonation, fake income or title documents and builder-borrower collusion, and are spotted only after EMIs stop
- Early warning signals are to be tracked as part of credit monitoring so an alert fires before the account becomes an NPA or a fraud
- Adopt an Early Warning Signals framework integrated with the credit monitoring system, using NHB's suggestive list of indicators and adding others
- Report implementation and progress to the Audit Committee of the Board and build an escalation matrix for breaches
- Place the circular before the next Board meeting to settle an implementation plan
- Mechanism to be in place by 01-04-2024

## Advisory regarding Data Submitted to the Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India (CERSAI) (HO/DoS/DoC/2026/05473; issued 2026-07-17; In force)
- All registered HFCs
- Every creation of a security interest must be filed with CERSAI under s. 23 of the SARFAESI Act (also para 213 of the RBI HFC Directions 2025 and Chapter VII of the RBI NBFC Credit Risk Management Directions, 2025)
- CERSAI flagged wrong borrower, property and address details, including records naming people who never borrowed, which can mislead later lenders and cause disputes over priority and enforcement
- Ensure accurate CERSAI filings, set internal controls and reconcile periodically to find and correct errors in data reported to CERSAI

## Appointment of Arbitrators for Resolution of Disputes (RBI letter to banks and NBFCs, circulated by NHB to HFCs) (RBI DoR.CO.BCG(MCS).No.S6464/01.01.008/2026-27; issued 2026-11-12; In force)
- Banks and NBFCs, including HFCs
- A Delhi High Court order of 30-09-2026 raised concern about lenders unilaterally appointing the same arbitrators across thousands of disputes, and asked RBI to caution lenders
- Appoint arbitrators only in line with the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 and court rulings on independence, impartiality and neutrality
- Report the action taken to the supervisor (NHB for HFCs)

## Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies - Fraud Risk Management) Directions, 2026 – Advisory on submission of Fraud Monitoring Returns (HO/DOS/DAK/2026/05615; issued 2026-09-07; In force)
- All registered HFCs (the RBI fraud directions tell HFCs to report frauds and robberies to NHB, not RBI)
- FMR-1: report of each fraud; FMR-2: quarterly frauds outstanding; FMR-3: case-wise quarterly progress; FMR-4: theft, burglary, dacoity and robbery
- Returns go through NHB's CRaMIS portal; FMR-1 for frauds in unregulated group entities and FMR-4 go by e-mail to the nodal team or senior supervisory manager
- File FMR-1 for every fraud, whatever the amount, immediately on classification and in any case within 14 days of classifying it as fraud
- File FMR-2 and FMR-3 on CRaMIS within 15 days of each quarter end
- Report frauds in group entities not regulated by any financial regulator, and in overseas financial group entities, to NHB
- Report theft, burglary, dacoity and robbery (including attempts) to NHB's Department of Supervision within 7 days in the FMR-4 format, and also through FMR-1 to FMR-3
- Withdrawal of an FMR, removal of a perpetrator's name or closure of a case needs an application to NHB with justification, approved by an official of at least director rank
- 14 days from classification: FMR-1
- 15 days after quarter end: FMR-2 and FMR-3
- 7 days from occurrence: FMR-4 robbery and theft report

## Master Circular – Returns to be submitted by Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) (NHB(ND)/DOS/Sup. Circular No. 10/2023-24, updated as on 21-07-2023) (NHB(ND)/DOS/Sup. Circular No. 10/2023-24; HO/DOS/DAK/2023/01603; issued 2021-12-31; In force (updated as on 21-07-2023))
- All registered HFCs; some returns only for deposit-taking HFCs or by asset size
- All returns are filed online on NHB's XBRL-based CRaMIS portal by an official the Board authorises
- Returns are compiled from the books; Ind AS companies file on Ind AS figures; audit is no reason to delay
- Schedule I (annual), II (half-yearly, prudential norms), III (quarterly, liquid assets; deposit-taking HFCs), IV (quarterly financials), V (monthly trends), LCR (weekly), ALM-I and ALM-II, 20 major exposures, Broad Liquidity, SARFAESI return, Statutory Auditor's Certificate, FDI and FATF certificates, overseas investment and interest rate futures statements
- Keep a reporting system that files every return on time; delay is reckoned from the due date and repeated failure is treated as non-submission
- Keep CRaMIS details current whenever the MD and CEO, directors, statutory auditors, principal, nodal, compliance or grievance redressal officers, branches or corporate office change
- Schedule V monthly by the 10th of the next month; Schedule IV quarterly (15th of the month after the quarter, 15 May for March); Schedule II half-yearly by 12 May and 12 November; Schedule I annually by 30 June
- LCR return weekly (as at Friday, by the next Wednesday) for deposit-taking HFCs and non-deposit HFCs with assets of Rs 5,000 crore or more
- ALM returns for deposit-taking HFCs and non-deposit HFCs with assets of Rs 100 crore or more; Schedule III and the Broad Liquidity return for deposit-taking HFCs
- Statutory Auditor's Certificate every year confirming the HFC is in housing finance business needing a certificate of registration, advised within one month of finalising the balance sheet
- Statutory auditors certify the March figures in Schedules I, III and IV and the September and March Schedule II
- Late or missing returns make the HFC liable to penal action under the NHB Act (see s. 52A(1A) and (1D))
- NHB will take a serious view of incorrect information

## Purpose of Loan – Advisory (MRO/DOS/DAK/2023/01688; issued 2023-07-27; In force)
- All registered HFCs, for loans against property and other non-housing loans
- Sanction letters and loan agreements often described the purpose only as 'Non-Housing' or 'Loan Against Property'
- State the purpose clearly (business, or non-business such as medical expenses) in the sanction letter, loan agreement and most important terms and conditions
- Encouraged: take a declaration at application that the loan will be used only for the sanctioned purpose and not for illegal, anti-social or speculative purposes

## Practices adopted by HFCs for EMI collections – Advisory (NHB (MRO)/DOS/002/2024-25; issued 2025-02-20; In force)
- All registered HFCs
- Inspections found: pre-EMI or advance EMIs kept from the first disbursement; EMIs collected mid-month while interest is capitalised at month end; 2-3 advance EMIs taken and set off against overdues; statements that do not show the balance after each instalment
- Stop these practices and give customers the benefit of interest adjustment and transparent statements

## Disbursement of housing loan to individuals linked to the stages of construction (MRO/DOS/DOC/2025/04924; issued 2025-11-20; In force)
- All registered HFCs
- Issued at the Department of Financial Services' request after a Karnataka High Court order of 05-08-2025 concerning subvention schemes for housing loans
- Disburse housing loans strictly in line with the stage of construction; no upfront disbursal for incomplete, under-construction or greenfield projects or houses
- Follow RBI's instructions on construction-linked disbursal (paras 87 to 89 of the 2021 RBI HFC Directions, now carried into the 2025 Directions)
- Check that every new loan product's terms are aligned with the RBI HFC Directions and RBI circulars

## Advisory on levy of Penal Charges by Housing Finance Companies (HFCs) (HO/DOS/DAK/2026/05245; issued 2026-04-23; In force)
- All registered HFCs
- Para 30 of the RBI NBFC Responsible Business Conduct Directions, 2025: penal charges are to instil credit discipline, not to raise revenue
- Inspections found bounce charges levied on top of penal charges for the same EMI default, bounce charges recovering collection costs already priced into the prime lending rate, and separate visit and similar charges
- Review the Board-approved policy on penal charges and similar charges, by whatever name, so that they are not used to raise revenue

## Advisory with respect to compliance with RBI circular on Fair Practices Code for Lenders – Charging of Interest dated April 29, 2024 (HO/DOS/DAK/2026/05489; issued 2026-07-24; In force)
- All registered HFCs
- RBI's circular of 29-04-2024: interest must run from actual disbursement, not from sanction, agreement or the cheque date
- Inspections found no audit trail of cheque or draft handover, and cancelled payment instruments still reported as loans
- Keep proof of cheque or demand draft handover: the borrower's acknowledgement with instrument details on the remittance letter or disbursement advice
- Do not report as loans and advances, in NHB returns or in quarterly and annual financial statements, any instrument cancelled up to the date the financial statements are finalised

## Compliance with Directions regarding Usage of Designated Numbering Series (1600xx and 140xx) for Service, Transactional, and Promotional Calls (HO/DOS/DAK/2026/05419; issued 2026-07-07; In force)

## National Housing Bank Grievance Redressal Policy, version 1.7 (2025-26) (Grievance Redressal Department, NHB; version 1.7; issued 2025-12-01; In force)
- Customers (applicants, depositors, borrowers, co-borrowers and their legal heirs) of HFCs
- Applicants and beneficiaries of PMAY-CLSS and RHISS loans for which NHB is the central nodal agency
- HFCs, which must answer complaints NHB takes up
- HFCs are not covered by the RBI Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026; complaints against HFCs go to NHB
- Complaints are lodged on NHB's GRIDS portal (grids.nhbonline.org.in), on CPGRAMS, or by post to NHB, New Delhi
- NHB plays a facilitative role: it takes the complaint up with the HFC; it does not replace courts or consumer forums, which the customer may approach at any time
- The customer must first complain to the HFC; NHB takes the complaint if the HFC does not reply within one month or the reply is unsatisfactory (para 5.3)
- The complaint must give the customer's details, loan or deposit account, the date of the complaint to the HFC and supporting documents (para 7.1)
- HFCs must respond to NHB on complaints and give an interim reply with reasons if resolution is delayed
- NHB aims to resolve a complaint within 21 days of receipt (para 6)
- Appeal on GRIDS to NHB's Chief Grievance Redressal Officer within 30 days of closure; NHB aims to decide appeals within 15 days (paras 8.1 and 8.3)
- A complaint is out of time if received more than one year after the HFC's reply, or one year and one month after the complaint to the HFC if it never replied (para 7.2)

## Public Grievances with respect to Housing Finance Companies – Advisory (HO/DOS/DAK/2023/01787; issued 2023-08-16; In force)

- Give specific written reasons when rejecting a loan application
- No harassment of borrowers, such as calls at odd hours or use of force in recovery

- Disclose complaints in the notes to accounts as RBI requires

## Refinance Booklet for Primary Lending Institutions (PLIs), with effect from 15-12-2025 (NHB Refinance Booklet (w.e.f. 15-12-2025); issued 2025-12-15; In force)
- HFCs, scheduled commercial banks, small finance banks, regional rural banks, scheduled co-operative and urban co-operative banks, apex co-operative housing finance societies and agriculture and rural development banks
- Schemes: Regular Refinance (Liberalized Refinance Scheme, LRS), Affordable Housing Fund, LRS fixed rate refinance, special refinance for small ticket loans (with or without rooftop solar), construction finance for affordable housing, and slum redevelopment and low-cost housing
- Refinance is against individual housing loans for purchase, construction, repair or upgradation; loans against property are not eligible; for HFCs, 75% of the loan pool flagged to NHB must meet the priority sector definition
- HFCs must meet the RBI principal business criteria (60% of assets in housing finance, 50% in individual housing loans)
- Refinance up to 100% of eligible loans, for 1 to 15 years, repaid in equal quarterly instalments on 1 January, April, July and October after one clear quarter
- Interest is compounded monthly and paid quarterly; rates are fixed (annual reset) or floating (quarterly reset) and linked to NHB's prime lending rate
- HFCs secure refinance by a charge on book debts (more security at NHB's discretion); banks give a letter of authority to debit their RBI current account
- The HFC bears the credit risk and must repay NHB on due dates whether or not its borrowers pay
- CEO and CFO certify every quarter, within 15 days, that the flagged loan pool conforms to the sanction terms; any adverse balance must be remitted or replenished
- Refinance returns: NHB-HFC-04 (quarterly), 05 (half-yearly adverse balance), 06, 07, 08 and 09, within the periods the booklet sets
- Prepayment rules depend on the scheme: free for fund-based schemes with 2 weeks' notice; 2 months' notice and charges for others; LRS fixed rate has a 2-year lock-in and then 2% (up to 5 years) or 1%
- Limits are sanctioned for NHB's year (July to June) and are valid for one year from the sanction letter
- Payment more than 3 working days late: penal charge of 2% on the amount in default for the whole delay
- Late payment beyond 3 working days attracts a 2% penal charge (para 13.6)
- NHB may call for additional security if the security becomes inadequate (para 7.2)

## Pool of flagged IHL for availing refinance (Refinance Circular No. 4/2024-25 and addendum) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 4/2024-25 (09-10-2024) and addendum (16-10-2024); issued 2024-10-09; In force)
- HFCs availing refinance
- Individual housing loans meeting the priority sector definition (as quoted then: up to Rs 35 lakh in metropolitan centres and Rs 25 lakh elsewhere, for dwellings costing up to Rs 45 lakh and Rs 30 lakh)
- At least 75% of the loan pool assigned to NHB against the refinance amount must be priority sector compliant individual housing loans
- The pool beyond 75% of the refinance amount and margin may be non-priority sector individual housing loans (addendum of 16-10-2024)

## Regarding submission of Book Debt to NHB for availing refinance (Refinance Circular No. 3/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 3/2024-25; issued 2024-10-08; In force)
- HFCs availing refinance
- Refinance is admissible only against individual housing loans for buying, building, repairing or upgrading a house; loans against property and plot loans with no construction are not eligible
- Check the pool flagged to NHB, carve out any loan against property or pure plot loan, and remit the equivalent amount with the adverse balance

## Penal charges for submission of ineligible Loan Pool against Refinance Facility (Refinance Circular No. 13/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 13/2024-25; HO/ROD/DAK/2025/04421; issued 2025-01-31; In force)
- HFCs with refinance outstanding on 01-01-2025 or availing it later
- A refinance covenant in the nature of a lender-to-borrower obligation
- Flag only loans that meet the scheme's eligibility criteria, at disbursement and on replenishment, under every scheme
- NHB levies a penal charge of 5% on the aggregate outstanding of loans later found ineligible, from the date of disbursement

## Replenishment of Flagged Loans for Adverse Balance Adjustment (Refinance Circular No. 10/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 10/2024-25; HO/ROD/DAK/2025/04283; issued 2025-01-08; In force)
- Primary lending institutions with refinance outstanding
- An adverse balance arises when the flagged pool shrinks because loans close or turn NPA, which otherwise must be repaid on top of quarterly instalments
- At NHB's discretion, substitute closed or impaired loans with standard loans of equal value and no overdues, on the annexed terms, instead of remitting the adverse balance

## Appointment of Nominee Director on the Board of Housing Finance Companies under Refinance Arrangements (Refinance Circular No. 9/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 9/2024-25; HO/ROD/DAK/2025/04272; issued 2025-01-07; In force)
- HFCs with existing or future refinance from NHB
- A refinance covenant in the nature of a lender-to-borrower obligation
- NHB may appoint a nominee director where (a) NHB refinance is 30% or more of the HFC's total borrowings at the latest quarter end, (b) it is Rs 10,000 crore or more and at least 15% of total borrowings, or (c) NHB considers it necessary to protect its interest as lender

## Requirement of Prior Consent for Appointment or Continuation of MD/CEO/Head of the Institution in cases of Promoter Shareholding Dilution by HFCs under Refinance Arrangements (Refinance Circulars No. 11/2024-25 and 15/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 11/2024-25 (08-01-2025) and No. 15/2024-25 (02-05-2025); issued 2025-01-08; In force)
- HFCs with existing or future refinance from NHB whose single largest promoter holds, or will hold, less than 26%
- A refinance covenant in the nature of a lender-to-borrower obligation
- Get NHB's prior written consent to appoint or continue the MD, CEO or head of the institution
- The request must be backed by a Board resolution passed on or after 08-01-2025; for existing appointments a fresh resolution may be passed (circular of 02-05-2025)

## Compliance with Principal Business Criteria (PBC) for Refinance Eligibility (Refinance Circular No. 12/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 12/2024-25; HO/ROD/DAK/2025/04287; issued 2025-01-09; In force)
- HFCs seeking refinance
- The relaxation is only for refinance eligibility; it does not relax the HFC's duty to meet RBI's criteria
- Meet the principal business criteria for both the preceding quarter and the preceding month when sanction and disbursement are considered
- An HFC that missed the criteria in its 31 March accounts but meets them in later quarters stays eligible only if RBI has condoned the breach in writing

## Introduction of Fixed Rate Refinance under Liberalized Refinance Scheme (LRS) (Refinance Circular No. 07/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 07/2024-25; HO/ROD/DAK/2025/04189; issued 2025-01-02; In force)
- HFCs, scheduled banks and co-operative sector institutions that meet NHB's eligibility norms
- Refinance at a fixed, lower rate against individual housing loans meeting the priority sector criteria
- No concessions apply on top of the fixed rate (circular of 07-01-2025)
- Apply under the annexed guidelines; prepayment is locked in for 2 years and then carries a charge (see the Refinance Booklet)

## Introduction / Enhancements in Concessions under the Liberalized Refinance Scheme (Refinance Circular No. 8/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 8/2024-25; HO/ROD/DAK/2025/04271; issued 2025-01-07; In force)
- Primary lending institutions, for future disbursements
- 25 bps concession for pools of individual housing loans that fully meet the priority sector criteria (para 12.1(i) of RBI's PSL Directions)
- Concession for loans to women borrowers raised from 10 bps to 25 bps
- Concessions cannot be combined; none applies to LRS fixed rate refinance
- Claim only one concession per refinance and only on pools that meet its conditions

## Refinance for 'Plot + Construction' Loans and 'Construction Linked' Loans (Refinance Circular No. 14/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 14/2024-25; issued 2025-04-02; In force)
- HFCs availing refinance
- For loans on ongoing construction (plot plus construction or construction on own land), refinance is available only if not more than half the construction was complete when the first tranche was disbursed
- The stage of construction must be shown by the technical evaluation report at first disbursement
- Flag only eligible loans

## Change in Refinance Sanction terms and conditions (Refinance Circular No. 16/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 16/2024-25; issued 2025-05-06; In force)
- All primary lending institutions
- Every sanction carries a processing fee of 0.20% of the sanctioned amount or Rs 20 lakh, whichever is lower, plus taxes, payable before the first disbursement
- Refinance for repair, renovation and upgradation loans is priced at the LRS rate plus 50 bps

## Requirement of external rating of refinance limit sanction by NHB (Refinance Circulars No. 5/2024 and No. 1/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 5/2024 (21-03-2024) and No. 1/2024-25 (05-09-2024); issued 2024-03-21; In force)
- All eligible primary lending institutions
- Linked to NHB's own Basel capital requirements
- Submit an external rating of the NHB refinance exposure (availed or not) when accepting a sanction; existing exposures were to be rated by 08-04-2024
- The rating must be public on the rating agency's website, or NHB will not consider it (circular of 05-09-2024)

## NHB's right to carry out valuation of underlying residential units flagged by HFCs for availing refinance (Refinance Circular No. 2/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 2/2024-25; issued 2024-09-05; In force)
- HFCs availing refinance
- NHB may, through its own valuers, value up to 10% of the properties in the flagged pool; book values in the pool must be consistent with those valuations

## Earmarking of Loan Pool Flagged to NHB for Refinance in the Internal System of Primary Lending Institutions (Refinance Circular No. 06/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 06/2024-25; issued 2024-12-23; In force)
- All primary lending institutions availing refinance
- Earmark loans flagged to NHB distinctly in internal systems and give NHB the system data when it asks

## Release of Excess Loan Pool (Refinance Circular No. 5/2024-25) (NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 5/2024-25; issued 2024-12-06; In force)
- HFCs with refinance outstanding
- An HFC may ask NHB to release excess flagged loans after a lock-in of two years from disbursement, only once in two years and subject to the sanction terms
- NHB's decision is final

## Timely Submission of Certificates on Adverse Balance (Refinance Circular No. 01/2023-24) and revised return formats (Refinance Circular No. 4/2024) (NHB(ND)/ROD/HFC/Refinance Circular 01/2023-24 (27-07-2023); NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 4/2024 (13-03-2024); issued 2023-07-27; In force)
- HFCs with refinance outstanding
- Quarterly certificate of adverse balance (NHB-HFC-10) within 15 calendar days of the quarter; remit any adverse balance by the end of the next month
- Half-yearly certificate (NHB-HFC-05) within 60 calendar days of the half year; remit any adverse balance within the next two months
- Use the revised Annexure I formats of HFC-05 and HFC-10 and file on NHB's ARRS portal (circular of 13-03-2024)

## Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana – Urban 2.0: Scheme Guidelines (Interest Subsidy Scheme vertical) (Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, PMAY-U 2.0 Operational Guidelines; issued 2024-09-01; In force)
- Primary lending institutions that sign an MoU with a central nodal agency such as NHB
- EWS, LIG and MIG households in urban areas
- EWS: household income up to Rs 3 lakh; LIG: Rs 3 to 6 lakh; MIG: Rs 6 to 9 lakh (para 3.3)
- Interest subsidy of 4% a year on the first Rs 8 lakh of a loan of up to Rs 25 lakh, for a house worth up to Rs 35 lakh and up to 120 sq m carpet area, for tenure up to 12 years; maximum Rs 1.80 lakh, paid in 5 yearly instalments (para 5.4.3)
- Applies to home loans sanctioned and disbursed on or after 01-09-2024 (para 5.4.1)
- Check on the unified web portal that the property has not already received the subsidy; subsidy is available once per property and is lost on a balance transfer (para 5.4.5)
- Treat family members borrowing on the same property as one family for income and benefit (para 5.4.5)

## Timely and hassle-free loans to beneficiaries of PMAY-U/PMAY-U 2.0 (MRO/DOS/DOC/2025/04785; issued 2025-09-23; In force)
- All registered HFCs
- Lists of approved beneficiaries under the Beneficiary Led Construction and Affordable Housing in Partnership verticals are on the PMAY 2.0 unified web portal
- Credit risk on such loans can be covered by the Credit Risk Guarantee Fund Scheme for Low Income Housing, run through NCGTC
- Sanction and disburse loans to eligible PMAY-U and PMAY-U 2.0 beneficiaries on time and without hassle, using the portal and the guarantee scheme

## Credit Risk Guarantee Fund Trust for Low Income Housing (CRGFTLIH): operational guidelines to lending institutions (CRGFTLIH operational guidelines (NHB page dated 21-11-2023); issued 2023-11-21; In force)
- Banks and HFCs registered as member lending institutions that lend to EWS and LIG households in urban areas
- A Government of India scheme guaranteeing housing loans to EWS and LIG households; the eligible loan ceiling was raised from Rs 5 lakh to Rs 8 lakh from 01-10-2014
- NHB's advisory of 23-09-2025 says the scheme now runs through the National Credit Guarantee Trustee Company (NCGTC)
- Execute the undertaking with the Trust, appoint nodal officers, and send guarantee application forms with the guarantee fee as the guidelines set out

## What changed (updates)
- 2026-11-12 Arbitrators must be independent; report action to NHB: After a Delhi High Court order of 30-09-2026, RBI cautioned lenders against appointing the same arbitrators unilaterally across many disputes. Arbitrators are to be appointed only in line with the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 and court rulings on independence and neutrality. HFCs report the action taken to NHB.
- 2026-09-24 Branch notice: insurance is voluntary and not linked to a loan: Every branch must display, in English and Hindi or the State's language, a notice that buying insurance is voluntary and not linked to any loan. The notice board should be at least 2 feet by 2 feet; NHB gives suggested wording.
- 2026-09-07 Fraud monitoring returns to be filed with NHB: FMR-1 for every fraud within 14 days of classification; FMR-2 and FMR-3 on CRaMIS within 15 days of each quarter end. Theft, burglary, dacoity and robbery reported within 7 days in FMR-4. Frauds in unregulated group entities are also reported to NHB; withdrawal or closure of an FMR needs NHB's approval.
- 2026-07-24 Interest only from actual disbursement; cancelled cheques not loans: Keep the borrower's acknowledgement of cheque or draft handover, with instrument details. Cancelled instruments must not be shown as loans in NHB returns or financial statements.
- 2026-07-17 Accurate CERSAI filings and periodic reconciliation: CERSAI found wrong borrower, property and address data, including records naming people who never borrowed. HFCs must set internal controls and reconcile CERSAI data periodically.
- 2026-07-07 1600xx only for service calls; 140xx for promotional calls: 1600xx numbers may be used only for service and transactional calls, never for marketing. Promotional calls only from 140xx numbers.
- 2026-06-29 Complaints flowchart on the website: A flowchart under the website's 'Complaint' section must show how to complain, the escalation route and the officers who handle grievances.
- 2026-04-23 Penal and bounce charges not to raise revenue: Inspections found bounce charges on top of penal charges for the same default and visit charges recovering costs already in the lending rate. The Board policy on penal and similar charges must be reviewed.
- 2026-04-07 Grievance officer details on website and in branches: Full contact details of grievance redressal officers on the website. The complaint procedure displayed in every branch and office.
- 2026-03-24 Monthly report of prime lending rate components: Component-wise prime lending rate or benchmark rate data to NHB every month, by the 10th, from 01-04-2026.
- 2026-03-05 KYC lapses found in inspections: Inspections found late CKYCR uploads, late periodic updation and identification lapses. HFCs must comply strictly with the RBI NBFC KYC Directions, 2025.
- 2026-01-30 Accessibility for persons with disabilities: HFCs must follow RBI's accessibility instructions in the NBFC conduct and KYC Directions, 2025, which implement the Supreme Court's directives of 30-04-2025 on accessible digital KYC.
- 2025-12-15 New refinance booklet for lenders: One booklet now sets the terms of all refinance schemes, including eligibility, security, repayment, prepayment and returns. CEO and CFO certify the flagged loan pool every quarter within 15 days; late payment over 3 working days carries a 2% penal charge.
- 2025-12-12 Digital services to meet IS 17802 accessibility standards: Websites, apps and devices to meet IS 17802 Parts I and II before the standard is made mandatory.
- 2025-12-11 Service calls only from 1600-series numbers: Transactional and service calls only from 1600-series numbers: by 01-02-2026 for HFCs with assets above Rs 5,000 crore, by 01-03-2026 for others.
- 2025-12-01 NHB Grievance Redressal Policy (GRIDS): Complaints against HFCs go to NHB on GRIDS after one month with the HFC; NHB aims to resolve them within 21 days. Appeals within 30 days of closure, decided within 15 days.
