### F1

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “keep at least 5% of deposits in unencumbered approved securities”
- Problem: The summary updates the aggregate liquid-assets requirement to 15% but omits the corresponding increase in approved securities to 10% from 1 July 2025. Readers could wrongly treat 5% securities plus other eligible assets as sufficient. It also omits the reference date for measuring public deposits.
- Suggested fix: Distinguish statutory minima from operational requirements: from 1 July 2025, 10% in approved securities and 15% aggregate eligible liquid assets, calculated against public deposits outstanding on the last working day of the second preceding quarter.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [Reserve Bank of India (Housing Finance Companies) Directions, 2025, paragraphs 76(1)–(3)](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12939).

### F2

- Severity: major
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “at least 60% of total assets in housing finance and 50% in individual housing loans”
- Problem: Both denominators exclude intangible assets. The wording also leaves room to interpret the 50% as a proportion of housing-finance assets. These are separate tests against the same adjusted total-assets denominator.
- Suggested fix: State that housing-finance assets must constitute at least 60%, and qualifying individual housing-finance assets at least 50%, of total assets net of intangible assets. Correct every repeated description.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [RBI circular RBI/2020-21/60, dated 22 October 2020, Annex, paragraph 1](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11988&Mode=0); carried into paragraph 10(9) of the 2025 HFC Directions.

### F3

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “maximum Rs 1.80 lakh, paid in 5 yearly instalments”
- Problem: Material eligibility and payment conditions are missing: loan tenure must exceed five years; instalment release requires an active loan with more than 50% principal outstanding. The summary also omits the prohibition on the beneficiary family owning a pucca house anywhere in India and the exclusion for government-scheme house allotments during the preceding 20 years. Income and property-value limits alone do not establish eligibility.
- Suggested fix: Add these conditions and distinguish the ₹1.80 lakh maximum actual release from its ₹1.50 lakh maximum net present value. Clarify that 12 years concerns the subsidy calculation, rather than presenting it as an unequivocal maximum contractual loan tenure.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [MoHUA, PMAY-U 2.0 Scheme Guidelines, September 2024, paragraphs 1.3, 3.2, 5.4.3–5.4.4 and 5.4.10](https://pmaymis.gov.in/PMAYMIS2_2024/Operational-Guidelines-of-PMAY-U-2.pdf).

### F4

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “ss. 49 and 52A: see the general consequences”
- Problem: No “general consequences” section is supplied. The principal post–Jan Vishwas monetary sanctions are therefore missing, including the auditor-specific penalty.
- Suggested fix: Add the section 52A maxima: (1A) ₹1.50 lakh plus ₹7,500 per continuing day after the first; (1B) ₹10 lakh for auditors; (1C) ₹10 lakh or twice the quantifiable amount, whichever is higher, plus ₹1 lakh per continuing day; (1D) ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 per continuing day. Explain that these are distinct contraventions and maximum penalties, not automatic charges.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [NHB Act, 1987, section 52A(1A)–(1D), inserted by the Jan Vishwas amendments effective 22 January 2024](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_2_11_00053_198753_1517807327356&filename=a1987-53.pdf&type=actfile).

### F5

- Severity: major
- Category: logic
- Location: “75% of the loan pool flagged to NHB must meet the priority sector definition”
- Problem: This conflicts with the later description measuring 75% against the refinance amount and allowing non-priority-sector loans in the additional pool and margin. A percentage of total collateral is not equivalent to a percentage of refinance outstanding. The underlying addendum could not be retrieved, so the correct denominator remains unverified here.
- Suggested fix: Reconcile both entries against the signed circular and addendum. State the denominator explicitly, explain treatment of margin, and include one numerical example.
- Confidence: medium
- Evidence: Internal inconsistency between the supplied booklet summary and addendum summary; NHB(ND)/ROD/Refinance Circular No. 4/2024-25, dated 9 October 2024, and addendum dated 16 October 2024, listed in [NHB’s circular register](https://www.nhb.org.in/financing_post/circulars-2024-25/?prophazecheck=1).

### F6

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “as quoted then: up to Rs 35 lakh in metropolitan centres and Rs 25 lakh elsewhere”
- Problem: The historical qualification is useful, but the register never supplies the revised PSL housing limits or explains which version governs current refinance eligibility. From 1 April 2025, RBI introduced three population bands with loan/house-cost limits of ₹50/63 lakh, ₹45/57 lakh and ₹35/44 lakh. The related reference to “para 12.1(i)” is also historical; housing appears in paragraph 13 of the 2025 PSL Directions.
- Suggested fix: Preserve the historical figures but add the current PSL framework and verify whether each NHB scheme incorporates changing RBI criteria or retains specified historical limits. Do not assume RBI’s revision automatically changes every refinance covenant.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [RBI Priority Sector Lending—Targets and Classification Directions, 2025, paragraphs 1.2, 4.3 and 13.1](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12799).

### F7

- Severity: major
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “One month: wait before a customer may escalate to NHB”
- Problem: One month is not an unconditional waiting period. A customer dissatisfied with an HFC’s response may approach NHB without waiting for that period to expire. The shorthand contradicts the more accurate wording elsewhere.
- Suggested fix: Replace it with: “Escalate after an unsatisfactory response, or if no response is received within one month.” Correct the updates section too.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [NHB Grievance Redressal Policy, version 1.7, paragraph 5.3](https://www.nhb.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Grievance-Redressal-Policy.pdf).

### F8

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “the customer may approach at any time”
- Problem: This correctly preserves access to other forums but omits the consequences for NHB’s process. Paragraph 7.2 excludes, among other matters, sub judice cases, matters already decided by judicial or quasi-judicial authorities, and specified matters under investigation. Readers could expect parallel NHB consideration to remain available.
- Suggested fix: Retain access to other remedies, but explain that NHB’s complaint mechanism has separate admissibility exclusions and may not consider the same dispute.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [NHB Grievance Redressal Policy, version 1.7, paragraphs 5.2 and 7.2(iii)–(iv)](https://www.nhb.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Grievance-Redressal-Policy.pdf).

### F9

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “Scheduled banks, HFCs and other institutions that borrow from NHB (s. 14)”
- Problem: The Act summary omits the statutory trust affecting refinance collections and securities—an important obligation for refinance borrowers.
- Suggested fix: Add section 16B: specified collections are held in trust for NHB to the extent of outstanding accommodation, and relevant securities are also held in trust. Cross-reference NHB’s conditional early-repayment power under section 20.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [NHB Act, 1987, sections 16B and 20](https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_2_11_00053_198753_1517807327356&filename=a1987-53.pdf&type=actfile).

### F10

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “ALM-I and ALM-II, 20 major exposures, Broad Liquidity”
- Problem: These returns are named without their deadlines, although the section otherwise functions as a filing calendar. Asset-size applicability also lacks the prescribed measurement date.
- Suggested fix: Add the applicable schedules: ALM-I, 20-major-exposure and Broad Liquidity returns within 10 days after quarter-end; ALM-II within 20 days after the March and September half-years; Schedule III within 15 days after quarter-end. State that the LCR and ALM asset thresholds use published accounts at 31 March of the previous year.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [NHB Master Circular No. 10/2023-24, updated 21 July 2023, Chapter II and Annex I](https://www.nhb.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Master-Circular-Return-updated-21-07-2023_ENG.pdf).

### F11

- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “para 127: the auditor must report unfavourable or qualified statements and non-compliance to NHB and RBI”
- Problem: The auditor summary omits the mandatory separate report to the Board. Reporting exceptions to regulators does not replace that report.
- Suggested fix: Add paragraphs 123–126: a separate Board report covering registration, principal-business criteria, NOF, reserves, prudential compliance and applicable deposit matters; inability to express an opinion must be explained.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [RBI Housing Finance Companies Directions, 2025, paragraphs 123–128](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=12939).

### F12

- Severity: minor
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “until RBI issued its own framework (it did so on 17-02-2021”
- Problem: This conflates the consolidated 2021 Master Direction with RBI’s earlier substantive regulatory framework. RBI issued revised HFC regulations on 22 October 2020, superseding relevant NHB instructions while retaining others.
- Suggested fix: Identify 22 October 2020 as the revised-framework date and 17 February 2021 as the subsequent consolidated Master Direction date.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [RBI/2020-21/60, DOR.NBFC(HFC).CC.No.118/03.10.136/2020-21, dated 22 October 2020, paragraphs 2 and 4](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11988&Mode=0).

### Overall

The register is not ready to serve as a reliable compliance checklist: several summaries omit conditions that change calculations, eligibility or filing obligations. The most important change is to distinguish statutory minima and historical circular terms from currently applicable operational requirements. Several newer NHB PDFs could not be retrieved, so this review does not certify every supplied date, threshold or refinance term.
