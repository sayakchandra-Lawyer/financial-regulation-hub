Key concepts

  • Two-stage process on NSE's NEAPS portal: in-principle approval before the issue, then a final listing application after allotment
  • In-principle documents: draft General Information Document (GID); latest rating press release (not older than one year) with rating letters; debenture trustee consent and due diligence certificate (needed even for unsecured issues); issuer certificate (Annexure I); PAN, Aadhaar, driving licence, bank account and passport numbers of promoters and PAN of directors; SCORES ID
  • First-time issuers also file incorporation papers, MoA and AoA, three years' annual reports and, for an NBFC, RBI's certificate showing whether it is deposit-taking or not
  • The GID must carry audited or limited-review financials not older than six months, audited by a peer-reviewed auditor
  • Final listing documents: depository credit confirmation; board or committee resolutions authorising the borrowing and the allotment with the list of allottees; debenture trust deed; GID and KID; rating; DT due diligence certificate; LPCC confirmation of the settlement guarantee fund contribution; material contracts; REF (where NSE is the designated exchange); listing fees; SEBI fee of Rs 5,000 per KID

Key obligations

  • Obtain NSE's in-principle approval before giving the placement memorandum and term sheet (GID and KID) to the EBP, or before the issue opens where EBP does not apply
  • Annexure I certificate: confirm eligibility under the NCS Regulations; that the issuer, promoters, promoter group and directors are not debarred by SEBI; whether any of them is a wilful defaulter (with GID disclosure); that no promoter or director is a fugitive economic offender; whether any SEBI or exchange fine is pending; RBI or other approvals; any past delay in listing and the penal interest paid; REF deposit; and that the issuer will activate the ISIN in both depositories
  • Confirm that the Articles allow the debenture trustee to appoint a nominee director; a first-time issuer may instead undertake, at in-principle approval, to amend them within six months of listing and report compliance (Chapter XXIII of the SEBI NCS Master Circular)
  • File applications complete in all respects on NEAPS: in-principle by 3 p.m. and final listing by 1.30 p.m. for same-day approval
  • Pay fees into the issuer's PAN-based virtual account at IDBI Bank

Deadlines and cycles

  • In-principle approval: before the GID/KID goes to the EBP (EBP issues) or before issue opening (non-EBP issues)
  • Listing application and listing: by T+3 working days, T being the issue closing date (Chapter VII of the SEBI NCS Master Circular)

If you breach it

  • Delay in listing beyond T+3: penal interest of 1% a year over the coupon to investors from allotment to listing (Chapter VII, para 6 of the SEBI NCS Master Circular); past delays must be disclosed in later in-principle applications
  • Incomplete applications are not processed the same day

General consequences under the statute. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956: a listed entity must comply with the conditions of its listing agreement (s. 21); a recognised stock exchange may delist securities on the prescribed grounds after recording reasons (s. 21A); failure to comply with listing or delisting conditions attracts a penalty of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 crore (s. 23E), and breach of the Act, rules, the exchange's bye-laws or regulations or SEBI directions with no separate penalty attracts a penalty under s. 23H. SEBI Act, 1992: failure to furnish documents, returns or reports, or furnishing false or incomplete ones, attracts Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15A(a)). Exchanges also act under SEBI's standard operating procedures: fines per day or per instance, freezing of promoter holdings, moving the scrip to the Z category, suspension of trading and compulsory delisting; and, for debt and CP issuers, a bar on new issues through any EBP and on further listing until fines are paid.
