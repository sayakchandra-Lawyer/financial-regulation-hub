SOP fines for debt and CP issuers

Non-compliance with continuous disclosures by issuers of listed non-convertible securities and commercial paper (SEBI master circular for listing obligations of debt issuers, Annex IX-A and IX-B, with BSE guidance note)

Key concepts

  • Fines (Annex IX-A Part A): no compliance officer, no RTA, investor complaints Rs 1,000 a day each; reg 50 intimations Rs 5,000 per instance per item; financial results under reg 52 Rs 5,000 a day; reg 52(4)/(6) line items Rs 1,000 a day; use of proceeds Rs 1,000 a day; annual report Rs 2,000 a day; security cover disclosure (reg 54) Rs 1,000 a day
  • Payment obligations: reg 57(1) status Rs 2,000 a day per ISIN; reg 57(4) and (5) quarterly details and certificates Rs 1,000 per ISIN (rows as the SOP table still lists them; reg 57 itself now centres on the payment certificate); no prior exchange approval for a structural change in the securities (reg 59) Rs 50,000 per instance; late record date notice (reg 60(2)) Rs 10,000 per ISIN
  • Listed CP (Part B): financial results Rs 5,000 a day; line items Rs 1,000 a day; payment certificate Rs 1,000 a day per ISIN
  • No double fine where the same common obligation has been fined under the equity SOP

Key obligations

  • Fines start from the working day after the due date; the exchange reviews within 30 days and the issuer must comply and pay within 15 days of the notice, then within 10 days of a reminder
  • Place the non-compliance before the board at its next meeting and send the board's comments to the exchange

If you breach it

  • If the issuer still does not comply and pay, the exchange tells the other exchanges and all EBPs, which then refuse any new issue by it on the EBP platform and any further listing of its NCS or CP until it complies and pays
  • An issuer with unpaid SOP fines must disclose them when applying for in-principle approval, and loses the merchant-banker exemption for Rs 10,000 NCDs

General consequences under the statute. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956: a listed entity must comply with the conditions of its listing agreement (s. 21); a recognised stock exchange may delist securities on the prescribed grounds after recording reasons (s. 21A); failure to comply with listing or delisting conditions attracts a penalty of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 crore (s. 23E), and breach of the Act, rules, the exchange's bye-laws or regulations or SEBI directions with no separate penalty attracts a penalty under s. 23H. SEBI Act, 1992: failure to furnish documents, returns or reports, or furnishing false or incomplete ones, attracts Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15A(a)). Exchanges also act under SEBI's standard operating procedures: fines per day or per instance, freezing of promoter holdings, moving the scrip to the Z category, suspension of trading and compulsory delisting; and, for debt and CP issuers, a bar on new issues through any EBP and on further listing until fines are paid.
