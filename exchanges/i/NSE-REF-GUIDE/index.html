Recovery Expense Fund (REF)

Guidance on Recovery Expense Fund (NSE), read with SEBI circular of 22-10-2020

SEBI NCS Regulations, 2021; Chapter IV of the SEBI Master Circular for Debenture Trustees
Applies to
Every issuer listing debt securities, secured or unsecured, by public issue or private placement

Key concepts

  • A fund held by the designated stock exchange that the debenture trustee can draw on to enforce rights after a default
  • Amount: 0.01% of the issue size (number of bonds x face value), capped at Rs 25 lakh per issuer across all issues
  • Paid in cash (into the issuer's PAN-based virtual account) or by bank guarantee in NSE's format

Key obligations

  • Deposit REF with the designated stock exchange named in the offer document before final listing
  • A transferee company under a scheme of arrangement must deposit REF before or at final listing
  • Bank guarantee: at least 12 months with a 3-month claim period (or 15 months if no claim period), kept valid until 6 months after the maturity of the listed security; NSE does not accept a guarantee issued by the issuer itself

Deadlines and cycles

  • REF and interest are released only after the last listed security has been repaid and the debenture trustee gives a no-objection certificate

General consequences under the statute. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956: a listed entity must comply with the conditions of its listing agreement (s. 21); a recognised stock exchange may delist securities on the prescribed grounds after recording reasons (s. 21A); failure to comply with listing or delisting conditions attracts a penalty of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 crore (s. 23E), and breach of the Act, rules, the exchange's bye-laws or regulations or SEBI directions with no separate penalty attracts a penalty under s. 23H. SEBI Act, 1992: failure to furnish documents, returns or reports, or furnishing false or incomplete ones, attracts Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15A(a)). Exchanges also act under SEBI's standard operating procedures: fines per day or per instance, freezing of promoter holdings, moving the scrip to the Z category, suspension of trading and compulsory delisting; and, for debt and CP issuers, a bar on new issues through any EBP and on further listing until fines are paid.
