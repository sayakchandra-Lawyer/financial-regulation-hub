REF: when trustees can use it

Modifications to Chapter IV (Recovery Expense Fund) of the SEBI Master Circular for Debenture Trustees

Key concepts

  • REF is for prompt enforcement or legal proceedings by the debenture trustee after a default
  • Lead debenture trustee: one chosen by the other trustees, or one representing more than 50% of the outstanding value

Key obligations

  • para 2.1: after a default the trustee may be reimbursed from REF for consents from debenture holders, voting, meetings, court applications, legal fees, asset-recovery services and legal consultants
  • para 2.2: no prior approval of holders is needed for those purposes; the trustee informs holders by email and on its website; for any other purpose it needs holders' prior consent and informs the designated exchange
  • para 2.3: the trustee submits an independent auditor's certificate of expenses, which the exchange verifies before release
  • para 2.6 and 2.7: the trustee keeps accounts of REF spending and updates holders annually

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 2.4: the designated exchange releases REF to the trustee within five working days of the intimation

General consequences under the statute. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956: a listed entity must comply with the conditions of its listing agreement (s. 21); a recognised stock exchange may delist securities on the prescribed grounds after recording reasons (s. 21A); failure to comply with listing or delisting conditions attracts a penalty of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 crore (s. 23E), and breach of the Act, rules, the exchange's bye-laws or regulations or SEBI directions with no separate penalty attracts a penalty under s. 23H. SEBI Act, 1992: failure to furnish documents, returns or reports, or furnishing false or incomplete ones, attracts Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15A(a)). Exchanges also act under SEBI's standard operating procedures: fines per day or per instance, freezing of promoter holdings, moving the scrip to the Z category, suspension of trading and compulsory delisting; and, for debt and CP issuers, a bar on new issues through any EBP and on further listing until fines are paid.
