Liquidity Window Facility for bond investors

Revised operating guidelines for the Liquidity Window Facility for investors in debt securities, with issuer compliance requirements

Applies to
Issuers that offer investors a put option through the exchange's liquidity window; Investors in those debt securities, through brokers or OBPPs

Key concepts

  • The facility is optional, chosen per ISIN at issue, and only for new issues (Chapter XXV of the SEBI NCS Master Circular); it starts only one year after issue; no re-issues in that ISIN, which is outside the ISIN cap; the aggregate limit is at least 10% of the final issue size; each window stays open three working days, monthly or quarterly; payout may not be below the valuation by more than 100 basis points, plus accrued interest
  • Investors exercise the put through the exchange during the window; securities are blocked in their demat accounts by early pay-in
  • Eligible investors are fixed on T-2 (T is the first day of the window); allocation is in full or proportionate, all categories at par; a retail-only window is capped at Rs 2 lakh per investor

Key obligations

  • Disclose the exchanges, eligible investor categories and method in the offer document
  • Give notice of each window on NEAPS at least 3 working days before it opens, and apply with the list of eligible investors, a confirmation and a board resolution at least 2 working days before
  • Upload the put price from a SEBI-registered rating agency's valuation by 6 p.m. on T-1
  • Appoint an RTA for allocation and a clearing member for settlement; name a designated exchange if more than one is used

Deadlines and cycles

  • Window open 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.; bids to the RTA on T+2; allocation file to the clearing corporations by 12.30 p.m. on T+3; settlement on T+4 on a gross basis without settlement guarantee
  • Post-window disclosures within three working days

If you breach it

  • If the issuer's clearing member does not fund in full, investors are not paid and their securities are returned

General consequences under the statute. Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956: a listed entity must comply with the conditions of its listing agreement (s. 21); a recognised stock exchange may delist securities on the prescribed grounds after recording reasons (s. 21A); failure to comply with listing or delisting conditions attracts a penalty of Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 crore (s. 23E), and breach of the Act, rules, the exchange's bye-laws or regulations or SEBI directions with no separate penalty attracts a penalty under s. 23H. SEBI Act, 1992: failure to furnish documents, returns or reports, or furnishing false or incomplete ones, attracts Rs 1 lakh to Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15A(a)). Exchanges also act under SEBI's standard operating procedures: fines per day or per instance, freezing of promoter holdings, moving the scrip to the Z category, suspension of trading and compulsory delisting; and, for debt and CP issuers, a bar on new issues through any EBP and on further listing until fines are paid.
