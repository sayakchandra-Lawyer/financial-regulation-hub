Key concepts

  • Three-stage registration: NOC, R1 and R2 approvals, R3 certificate
  • Minimum paid-up equity: Rs 100 crore for life, general or standalone health; Rs 200 crore for reinsurance
  • Promoters hold over 50% of paid-up equity, dilutable to 26% on conditions
  • Investor limits: one investor below 25%, all investors below 50%
  • Other forms of capital (preference shares, subordinated debt)
  • Amalgamation and transfer of business

Key obligations

  • regulation 21: prior IRDAI approval for a share transfer or issue that takes a holder above 5%, or above 10%, 25%, 50% or 75%, or makes them the single largest shareholder
  • regulation 8: promoter shares are locked in; IRDAI may relax lock-in for listing, distress or reorganisation
  • regulation 27: report changes in shareholding as specified
  • regulation 44: prior approval to accrue or pay coupon on other forms of capital in specified cases

Deadlines and cycles

  • regulation 45: report issue of other forms of capital within 15 days

If you breach it

  • regulation 28: action for violation, including under the Insurance Act

General consequences under the statute. Insurance Act, 1938: IRDAI may issue directions, including disgorgement of wrongful gains (s. 34). An insurer or intermediary that fails to file a return, comply with IRDAI directions or maintain solvency is liable to up to Rs 1 lakh a day, capped at Rs 10 crore (s. 102). Registration can be suspended or cancelled (s. 3 for insurers, s. 42D for intermediaries). Penalty cases follow the IRDAI Penalties Regulations 2026 and orders are published within 30 days (s. 105E).
