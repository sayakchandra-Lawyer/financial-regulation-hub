Applies to
All insurers, intermediaries, insurance intermediaries and distribution channels

Key concepts

  • Zero tolerance for fraud
  • Board-approved anti-fraud policy
  • Fraud Monitoring Committee headed by a KMP
  • Red flag indicators
  • Industry fraud database at the IIB

Key obligations

  • para 5.3.1: Board-approved anti-fraud policy, reviewed at least once a year
  • para 5.3.2: Fraud Monitoring Committee reports quarterly to the Risk Management Committee
  • para 7.3: share blacklisted distributors, hospitals, vendors and fraudsters with the IIB
  • para 10.1: intermediaries keep their own anti-fraud policies and tell the insurer when they suspect fraud
  • para 10.2: other distribution channels follow the insurer's anti-fraud policy and report suspicions to it

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 5.3.2.4: quarterly FMC report to the RMC
  • para 12.2: annual return FMR-1 within 30 days of the financial year end

General consequences under the statute. Insurance Act, 1938: IRDAI may issue directions, including disgorgement of wrongful gains (s. 34). An insurer or intermediary that fails to file a return, comply with IRDAI directions or maintain solvency is liable to up to Rs 1 lakh a day, capped at Rs 10 crore (s. 102). Registration can be suspended or cancelled (s. 3 for insurers, s. 42D for intermediaries). Penalty cases follow the IRDAI Penalties Regulations 2026 and orders are published within 30 days (s. 105E).
