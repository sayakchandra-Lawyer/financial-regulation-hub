Applies to
Insurers (life, general, health, reinsurance); Insurance agents and insurance intermediaries, including corporate agents

Key concepts

  • Certificate of registration for insurance business (s. 3)
  • Foreign investment in an Indian insurance company up to 100% of paid-up equity (s. 3AA, from 05-02-2026)
  • Prior approval of IRDAI for share transfers above set limits (s. 6A)
  • IRDAI directions, including disgorgement (s. 34)
  • Commission and remuneration to agents and intermediaries within limits set by IRDAI (s. 40)
  • Prohibition of rebates (s. 41)
  • Appointment of insurance agents (s. 42) and registration of intermediaries (s. 42D)
  • No premium, no risk (s. 64VB)
  • Policy cannot be called in question after three years (s. 45)

Key obligations

  • s. 3: no person may carry on insurance business without a certificate of registration from IRDAI
  • s. 40: commission or remuneration may be paid only to a registered agent or intermediary and within the limits and disclosure norms IRDAI sets
  • s. 41: no one may offer a rebate of commission or premium as an inducement; an agent's commission on a policy covering their own life, health or property is not treated as a rebate
  • s. 42: an agent may act for at most one life, one general, one health insurer and one insurer of each other specified class
  • s. 42A: insurers may not appoint principal, chief or special agents or use multilevel marketing
  • s. 42D: no person may act as an insurance intermediary without registration; registration continues while the annual fee is paid
  • s. 43: insurers must keep a register of agents and keep records for five years after an agent ceases to act
  • s. 64VB: risk may be assumed only after premium is received in advance; refunds go directly to the insured, never through the agent

Deadlines and cycles

  • s. 45: a life policy cannot be called in question on any ground after three years from the latest of issue, commencement of risk, revival or rider date
  • s. 43: agent records kept for five years after the agent ceases to act
  • s. 105E: an order imposing a penalty is published as a press release within 30 days

If you breach it

  • s. 102: failure to file returns, comply with directions, maintain solvency or follow treaty directions: up to Rs 1 lakh a day, maximum Rs 10 crore
  • s. 103: carrying on insurance business without registration: penalty up to Rs 25 crore and imprisonment up to 10 years
  • s. 40(3): breach of commission rules: penalty up to Rs 1 lakh
  • s. 41(2): offering or accepting a rebate: penalty up to Rs 10 lakh
  • s. 42(4): agent acting in breach of s. 42: up to Rs 10,000; appointing insurer up to Rs 1 crore
  • s. 42(5): insurer is responsible for the acts and omissions of its agents: penalty up to Rs 1 crore
  • s. 105B: failure to meet rural, social sector and motor third party obligations: up to Rs 25 crore
  • s. 105BA: acting as an unregistered intermediary: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 10 lakh; appointing or dealing through one: Rs 10 lakh to Rs 1 crore

General consequences under the statute. Insurance Act, 1938: IRDAI may issue directions, including disgorgement of wrongful gains (s. 34). An insurer or intermediary that fails to file a return, comply with IRDAI directions or maintain solvency is liable to up to Rs 1 lakh a day, capped at Rs 10 crore (s. 102). Registration can be suspended or cancelled (s. 3 for insurers, s. 42D for intermediaries). Penalty cases follow the IRDAI Penalties Regulations 2026 and orders are published within 30 days (s. 105E).
