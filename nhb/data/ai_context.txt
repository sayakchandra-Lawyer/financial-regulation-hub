NHB REGISTER: National Housing Bank Act, 1987 and what NHB still does after RBI took over HFC regulation on 09-08-2019: supervision and inspection, returns, lending conduct advisories, complaints (GRIDS), refinance to lenders and government housing schemes (as of 2026-11-26). RBI regulates HFCs through its HFC Directions 2025 (see the RBI register for the full rulebook). One block per instrument. Fields: applies, excluded, concepts, duties, deadlines, breach (stated consequences), penalties (code of the shared statutory penalty text below), refs, repeals.

SHARED STATUTORY PENALTY TEXTS:
P1: NHB Act, 1987 (Chapter V and s. 52A): the NHB or the Reserve Bank may impose a penalty on a HFC of up to Rs 25,000 for a false statement in a return or document (s. 52A(1)(a)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for carrying on housing finance without a certificate of registration or accepting deposits against a direction (s. 52A(1)(b)); up to Rs 1.5 lakh per default plus Rs 7,500 a day for not producing books or not furnishing a statement or information (s. 52A(1A)); up to Rs 10 lakh on an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 (s. 52A(1B)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V (s. 52A(1C)); and up to Rs 1 lakh plus Rs 10,000 a day for any other contravention (s. 52A(1D)). A show-cause notice and hearing come first (s. 52A(2)) and the penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand (s. 52A(3)). As offences, a wilfully false statement carries up to 3 years and fine (s. 49(1)), housing finance without a certificate 1 to 5 years and Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (s. 49(2A)), and taking deposits against a direction up to 3 years and a fine up to twice the deposit (s. 49(3)). The Reserve Bank may also cancel the certificate of registration (s. 29A(6)), prohibit deposits and bar dealing with assets (s. 33A) and debar an auditor for up to 3 years (s. 33C); the NHB may order a special audit (s. 33(3)) and apply for winding up (s. 33B). HFCs must display on their website any penalty imposed by RBI or NHB (RBI HFC Directions 2025, para 52).

GLOSSARY (plain-language meanings, not statutory text):
NHB (National Housing Bank): A statutory body, wholly owned by the Government of India, set up under the NHB Act, 1987 to promote housing finance. Since 09-08-2019 it supervises and inspects HFCs, collects their returns, handles complaints against them and gives refinance to housing lenders; RBI registers and regulates HFCs.
HFC (Housing finance company): A company registered with RBI under s. 29A of the NHB Act whose principal business is housing finance: at least 60% of total assets in housing finance and at least 50% in individual housing loans, with net owned fund of at least Rs 20 crore.
Certificate of registration (CoR): The licence RBI grants under s. 29A of the NHB Act to carry on housing finance. Certificates NHB granted before 09-08-2019 continue as if granted by RBI.
Primary lending institution (PLI): A lender that gives housing loans to individuals and can borrow refinance from NHB against them: HFCs, scheduled commercial banks, small finance banks, regional rural banks, co-operative banks and similar institutions.
Refinance: A loan from NHB to a lender against a pool of individual housing loans the lender has already made. The lender keeps the credit risk and repays NHB on fixed quarterly dates whatever its borrowers pay.
LRS (Liberalized Refinance Scheme): NHB's regular refinance scheme for individual housing loans. Rates are linked to NHB's prime lending rate, with concessions for smaller loans, lower-income borrowers and some regions; a fixed-rate option is available.
Flagged loan pool: The individual housing loans a lender earmarks to NHB as backing for refinance. The pool must meet the scheme's eligibility terms at all times; ineligible loans must be replaced or the shortfall repaid.
Adverse balance: The shortfall when the outstanding flagged loan pool falls below the refinance outstanding (for example after prepayments or NPAs). It must be certified and made good by remitting money or replenishing the pool.
Book debts: Amounts owed to the lender by its borrowers. HFCs secure NHB refinance by a charge over the book debts covering the flagged loan pool.
CRaMIS (Comprehensive Risk-based Analysis and Monitoring Information System): NHB's online portal through which HFCs file their supervisory returns.
FMR (Fraud monitoring returns): The fraud reports HFCs file with NHB: FMR-1 for each fraud (within 14 days of classification), FMR-2 and FMR-3 quarterly (within 15 days of quarter end) and FMR-4 for theft, burglary, dacoity and robbery (within 7 days).
PBC (Principal business criteria): The asset tests an HFC must meet: housing finance at least 60% of total assets and individual housing loans at least 50%. HFCs report their position to NHB every month, and refinance depends on meeting them.
PLR (Prime lending rate): The benchmark rate an HFC uses to price floating rate loans. HFCs report its components (cost of funds, margin, risk premium) to NHB every month.
GRIDS (Grievance Registration and Information Database System): NHB's online portal for complaints against HFCs. A customer may use it if the HFC has not replied within one month or the reply is unsatisfactory; NHB aims to resolve complaints within 21 days and hears appeals filed within 30 days.
CERSAI (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India): The central registry where lenders must file every security interest they create, such as a mortgage of a house. Wrong filings can mislead later lenders and cause disputes over priority.
Early warning signals (EWS): Indicators, tracked as part of credit monitoring, that a loan may turn bad or fraudulent, so that the lender acts before it becomes an NPA.
Penal charges: Charges for breach of loan terms, such as late payment. They must not be used to raise revenue and should not be stacked with bounce charges for the same default.
PMAY-U 2.0 ISS (Pradhan Mantri Awas Yojana (Urban) 2.0, Interest Subsidy Scheme): The Government scheme that gives an interest subsidy on home loans to eligible urban families, paid through lenders. NHB is one of the central nodal agencies.
CRGFTLIH (Credit Risk Guarantee Fund Trust for Low Income Housing): A Government-backed trust, managed by NHB, that guarantees housing loans to low-income borrowers given without collateral or third-party guarantee.
Liquid assets (Section 29B): Approved securities and bank or NHB deposits that a deposit-taking HFC must hold against its public deposits (now 15%); a shortfall attracts penal interest payable to NHB.
Reserve fund (Section 29C): At least 20% of net profit that an HFC must transfer every year before declaring a dividend. Any use of it must be reported to NHB and RBI within 21 days.
Section 52A: The penalty power under the NHB Act. RBI or NHB may impose money penalties on an HFC or its auditor after a show-cause notice and hearing.

- 2026-11-12 Advisory: Arbitrators must be independent; report action to NHB (RBI DoR.CO.BCG(MCS).No.S6464/01.01.008/2026-27). Applies: Banks and NBFCs, including HFCs..
