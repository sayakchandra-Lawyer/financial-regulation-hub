Key concepts

  • The RBI rulebook for HFCs; this page lists only the parts that send an HFC to NHB
  • Reporting and instructions that the RBI directions address to the supervisor are to be made to NHB as the supervisor of HFCs (paras 5 and 8)
  • Principal business test (para 10(9)): housing finance at least 60%, and housing finance for individuals at least 50%, of total assets net of intangible assets; minimum net owned fund Rs 20 crore (para 16)

Key obligations

  • para 144: tell customers, at branches and on the website, that if the HFC does not reply within one month or the reply is unsatisfactory, they may complain to NHB online or by post
  • para 234: supervision of HFCs rests with NHB; para 236: NHB inspects HFCs under s. 34 of the NHB Act
  • para 237: send NHB the audited financial statements, auditor's report and Board's report within 15 days of the AGM; para 238: deposit-taking HFCs add an auditor's certificate on depositor liabilities
  • para 239: comply with any reporting requirement NHB prescribes
  • para 221: quarterly report to NHB on counterfeit notes detected, within 7 days of the quarter end (nil report if none)
  • para 54: take NHB's prior approval before approaching the Registrar of Companies to change the accounting year
  • paras 123 to 126: besides the Companies Act audit report, the auditor makes a separate report to the Board covering registration and the principal business criteria, net owned fund, the s. 29C reserve, the borrowing limits and, for deposit-taking HFCs, deposit matters, giving reasons for any unfavourable or qualified statement or for being unable to give an opinion
  • para 127: the auditor must report unfavourable or qualified statements and non-compliance to NHB and RBI

Deadlines and cycles

  • 15 days after the AGM: annual accounts to NHB (para 237)
  • 7 days after each quarter: counterfeit note report to NHB (para 221)
  • Escalation to NHB: if the HFC does not reply within one month, or at once if its reply is unsatisfactory (para 144)

If you breach it

  • para 235: RBI or NHB may impose a penalty under s. 52A of the NHB Act for any contravention of the Act or directions
  • para 233: an unreported appropriation from the reserve fund is a violation attracting penalty

General consequences under the statute. National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (Chapter V and s. 52A): the National Housing Bank or the Reserve Bank may impose a penalty on a housing finance company of up to Rs 25,000 for a false statement in a return or document (s. 52A(1)(a)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for carrying on housing finance without a certificate of registration or accepting deposits against a direction (s. 52A(1)(b)); up to Rs 1.5 lakh per default plus Rs 7,500 a day for not producing books or not furnishing a statement or information (s. 52A(1A)); up to Rs 10 lakh on an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 (s. 52A(1B)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V (s. 52A(1C)); and up to Rs 1 lakh plus Rs 10,000 a day for any other contravention (s. 52A(1D)). A show-cause notice and hearing come first (s. 52A(2)) and the penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand (s. 52A(3)). As offences, a wilfully false statement carries up to 3 years and fine (s. 49(1)), housing finance without a certificate 1 to 5 years and Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (s. 49(2A)), and taking deposits against a direction up to 3 years and a fine up to twice the deposit (s. 49(3)). The Reserve Bank may also cancel the certificate of registration (s. 29A(6)), prohibit deposits and bar dealing with assets (s. 33A) and debar an auditor for up to 3 years (s. 33C); the National Housing Bank may order a special audit (s. 33(3)) and apply for winding up (s. 33B). HFCs must display on their website any penalty imposed by RBI or NHB (RBI HFC Directions 2025, para 52).
