NHB Act, 1987

The National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (as amended, including by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2019 and the Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act, 2023)

Applies to
Housing finance institutions that are companies (housing finance companies, HFCs): Chapter V; Scheduled banks, HFCs and other institutions that borrow from NHB (s. 14); Auditors of HFCs (s. 33) and depositors of HFCs (ss. 36A and 36B)
Does not apply to
Housing finance institutions that are firms or unincorporated associations: their deposits stay under Chapter IIIC of the RBI Act (s. 29)

Key concepts

  • NHB is the principal agency to promote housing finance institutions and give them financial and other support (preamble, s. 14)
  • s. 14(b): NHB may lend and give other financial assistance for housing to HFCs, scheduled banks, state co-operative agricultural and rural development banks and other notified institutions (the basis of refinance)
  • Since 09-08-2019 the Reserve Bank registers and regulates HFCs (ss. 29A, 29B, 30, 30A, 33(1A), 33A, 35B); NHB keeps supervision, inspection and returns (ss. 31, 32, 33(3), 34)
  • s. 29A(1): no company may carry on housing finance as its principal business without a certificate of registration and net owned fund of Rs 10 crore or the higher amount RBI notifies (now Rs 20 crore)
  • s. 29B: liquid assets on deposits (approved securities and bank or NHB deposits), with penal interest to NHB for any shortfall
  • s. 29C: reserve fund of at least 20% of net profit before any dividend
  • s. 36A: an NHB officer authorised by the Central Government may order an HFC to repay a matured deposit

Key obligations

  • s. 29A(1)-(2): apply to RBI for a certificate of registration and keep the minimum net owned fund; certificates NHB granted before 09-08-2019 are deemed granted under the Act
  • s. 29B(1)-(2): keep liquid assets against public deposits: the Act sets at least 5% in unencumbered approved securities and, with bank or NHB deposits or NHB bonds, at least 10%, or the higher rates RBI notifies. RBI HFC Directions 2025 para 76 now require 10% in approved securities and 15% in all, from 01-07-2025, measured on public deposits outstanding on the last working day of the second preceding quarter
  • s. 29C(1)-(2): transfer at least 20% of net profit to a reserve fund every year; any appropriation from it must be for a purpose RBI specifies and be reported to NHB and RBI within 21 days
  • s. 31: a deposit-taking HFC must give NHB and RBI the statements on deposits NHB specifies, and send its annual accounts to large depositors if NHB requires
  • s. 32: every HFC must furnish the statements, information or particulars NHB or RBI calls for and comply with directions under Chapter V
  • s. 33(1)-(2): the auditor must enquire whether the HFC has furnished the deposit statements and, if not, report to NHB and RBI the aggregate deposits, and include that report in the audit report under s. 143(2) of the Companies Act, 2013
  • s. 34(2): directors, officers and employees must produce books and documents and give information to NHB's inspecting authority within the time it fixes
  • s. 16B: a borrowing institution holds repayments and recoveries on loans refinanced by NHB, to the extent of NHB's outstanding accommodation, and the related securities, in trust for NHB
  • s. 20: NHB may call for immediate repayment of its loan if the application had false or misleading information, the terms are breached, the borrower may be unable to pay its debts, or to protect NHB's interests
  • s. 35: no one may solicit deposits for an HFC without its written authority and an advertisement complying with RBI orders

Deadlines and cycles

  • s. 29C(2): report an appropriation from the reserve fund to NHB and RBI within 21 days
  • s. 29B(5): penal interest on a liquid asset shortfall is payable within 14 days of NHB's demand
  • s. 29A(7): appeal against rejection or cancellation of registration to the Central Government within 30 days
  • s. 52A(3): a penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand notice

If you breach it

  • s. 29A(6): RBI may cancel the certificate of registration, among other grounds, for failing to comply with a direction of RBI or NHB, to maintain proper accounts or to produce books for inspection by RBI or NHB
  • s. 29B(4): penal interest to NHB of 3% a year above the bank rate on any liquid asset shortfall, rising to 5% if it continues into later quarters
  • s. 33(3): NHB may, and on RBI's direction must, order a special audit at the HFC's cost
  • s. 33B: NHB may apply to wind up an HFC that cannot pay its debts, is disqualified under s. 29A or has been barred from taking deposits for 3 months or more
  • s. 33C: RBI may debar an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 for up to 3 years
  • ss. 49 and 52A: see the general consequences

General consequences under the statute. National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (Chapter V and s. 52A): the National Housing Bank or the Reserve Bank may impose a penalty on a housing finance company of up to Rs 25,000 for a false statement in a return or document (s. 52A(1)(a)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for carrying on housing finance without a certificate of registration or accepting deposits against a direction (s. 52A(1)(b)); up to Rs 1.5 lakh per default plus Rs 7,500 a day for not producing books or not furnishing a statement or information (s. 52A(1A)); up to Rs 10 lakh on an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 (s. 52A(1B)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V (s. 52A(1C)); and up to Rs 1 lakh plus Rs 10,000 a day for any other contravention (s. 52A(1D)). A show-cause notice and hearing come first (s. 52A(2)) and the penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand (s. 52A(3)). As offences, a wilfully false statement carries up to 3 years and fine (s. 49(1)), housing finance without a certificate 1 to 5 years and Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (s. 49(2A)), and taking deposits against a direction up to 3 years and a fine up to twice the deposit (s. 49(3)). The Reserve Bank may also cancel the certificate of registration (s. 29A(6)), prohibit deposits and bar dealing with assets (s. 33A) and debar an auditor for up to 3 years (s. 33C); the National Housing Bank may order a special audit (s. 33(3)) and apply for winding up (s. 33B). HFCs must display on their website any penalty imposed by RBI or NHB (RBI HFC Directions 2025, para 52).
