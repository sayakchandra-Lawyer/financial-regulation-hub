Key concepts

  • Schemes: Regular Refinance (Liberalized Refinance Scheme, LRS), Affordable Housing Fund, LRS fixed rate refinance, special refinance for small ticket loans (with or without rooftop solar), construction finance for affordable housing, and slum redevelopment and low-cost housing
  • Refinance is against individual housing loans for purchase, construction, repair or upgradation; loans against property are not eligible; for HFCs, at least 75% of the loan pool flagged to NHB must be individual housing loans that are priority sector compliant (booklet para 1.2)
  • HFCs must meet the RBI principal business criteria (housing finance at least 60%, and individual housing loans at least 50%, of total assets net of intangible assets)

Key obligations

  • Refinance up to 100% of eligible loans, for 1 to 15 years, repaid in equal quarterly instalments on 1 January, April, July and October after one clear quarter
  • Interest is compounded monthly and paid quarterly; rates are fixed (annual reset) or floating (quarterly reset) and linked to NHB's prime lending rate
  • HFCs secure refinance by a charge on book debts (more security at NHB's discretion); banks give a letter of authority to debit their RBI current account
  • The HFC bears the credit risk and must repay NHB on due dates whether or not its borrowers pay
  • CEO and CFO certify every quarter, within 15 days, that the flagged loan pool conforms to the sanction terms; any adverse balance must be remitted or replenished
  • Refinance returns: NHB-HFC-04 (quarterly), 05 (half-yearly adverse balance), 06, 07, 08 and 09, within the periods the booklet sets
  • Prepayment rules depend on the scheme: free for fund-based schemes with 2 weeks' notice; 2 months' notice and charges for others; LRS fixed rate has a 2-year lock-in and then 2% (up to 5 years) or 1%

Deadlines and cycles

  • Limits are sanctioned for NHB's year (July to June) and are valid for one year from the sanction letter
  • Payment more than 3 working days late: penal charge of 2% on the amount in default for the whole delay

If you breach it

  • Late payment beyond 3 working days attracts a 2% penal charge (para 13.6)
  • NHB may call for additional security if the security becomes inadequate (para 7.2)

General consequences under the statute. National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (Chapter V and s. 52A): the National Housing Bank or the Reserve Bank may impose a penalty on a housing finance company of up to Rs 25,000 for a false statement in a return or document (s. 52A(1)(a)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for carrying on housing finance without a certificate of registration or accepting deposits against a direction (s. 52A(1)(b)); up to Rs 1.5 lakh per default plus Rs 7,500 a day for not producing books or not furnishing a statement or information (s. 52A(1A)); up to Rs 10 lakh on an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 (s. 52A(1B)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V (s. 52A(1C)); and up to Rs 1 lakh plus Rs 10,000 a day for any other contravention (s. 52A(1D)). A show-cause notice and hearing come first (s. 52A(2)) and the penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand (s. 52A(3)). As offences, a wilfully false statement carries up to 3 years and fine (s. 49(1)), housing finance without a certificate 1 to 5 years and Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (s. 49(2A)), and taking deposits against a direction up to 3 years and a fine up to twice the deposit (s. 49(3)). The Reserve Bank may also cancel the certificate of registration (s. 29A(6)), prohibit deposits and bar dealing with assets (s. 33A) and debar an auditor for up to 3 years (s. 33C); the National Housing Bank may order a special audit (s. 33(3)) and apply for winding up (s. 33B). HFCs must display on their website any penalty imposed by RBI or NHB (RBI HFC Directions 2025, para 52).
