Key concepts

  • All returns are filed online on NHB's XBRL-based CRaMIS portal by an official the Board authorises
  • Returns are compiled from the books; Ind AS companies file on Ind AS figures; audit is no reason to delay
  • Schedule I (annual), II (half-yearly, prudential norms), III (quarterly, liquid assets; deposit-taking HFCs), IV (quarterly financials), V (monthly trends), LCR (weekly), ALM-I and ALM-II, 20 major exposures, Broad Liquidity, SARFAESI return, Statutory Auditor's Certificate, FDI and FATF certificates, overseas investment and interest rate futures statements

Key obligations

  • Keep a reporting system that files every return on time; delay is reckoned from the due date and repeated failure is treated as non-submission
  • Keep CRaMIS details current whenever the MD and CEO, directors, statutory auditors, principal, nodal, compliance or grievance redressal officers, branches or corporate office change
  • Schedule V monthly by the 10th of the next month; Schedule IV quarterly (15th of the month after the quarter, 15 May for March); Schedule II half-yearly by 12 May and 12 November; Schedule I annually by 30 June
  • LCR return weekly (as at Friday, by the next Wednesday) for deposit-taking HFCs and non-deposit HFCs with assets of Rs 5,000 crore or more
  • Due dates (Annex): ALM-I, the 20 major exposures return and the Broad Liquidity return within 10 days of each quarter end; Schedule III within 15 days of each quarter end; ALM-II within 20 days of 31 March and 30 September
  • ALM returns for deposit-taking HFCs and non-deposit HFCs with assets of Rs 100 crore or more; Schedule III and the Broad Liquidity return for deposit-taking HFCs
  • Statutory Auditor's Certificate every year confirming the HFC is in housing finance business needing a certificate of registration, advised within one month of finalising the balance sheet
  • Statutory auditors certify the March figures in Schedules I, III and IV and the September and March Schedule II

If you breach it

  • Late or missing returns make the HFC liable to penal action under the NHB Act (see s. 52A(1A) and (1D))
  • NHB will take a serious view of incorrect information

General consequences under the statute. National Housing Bank Act, 1987 (Chapter V and s. 52A): the National Housing Bank or the Reserve Bank may impose a penalty on a housing finance company of up to Rs 25,000 for a false statement in a return or document (s. 52A(1)(a)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for carrying on housing finance without a certificate of registration or accepting deposits against a direction (s. 52A(1)(b)); up to Rs 1.5 lakh per default plus Rs 7,500 a day for not producing books or not furnishing a statement or information (s. 52A(1A)); up to Rs 10 lakh on an auditor who does not comply with a direction under s. 33 (s. 52A(1B)); up to Rs 10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to Rs 1 lakh a day, for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V (s. 52A(1C)); and up to Rs 1 lakh plus Rs 10,000 a day for any other contravention (s. 52A(1D)). A show-cause notice and hearing come first (s. 52A(2)) and the penalty is payable within 30 days of the demand (s. 52A(3)). As offences, a wilfully false statement carries up to 3 years and fine (s. 49(1)), housing finance without a certificate 1 to 5 years and Rs 1 lakh to Rs 5 lakh (s. 49(2A)), and taking deposits against a direction up to 3 years and a fine up to twice the deposit (s. 49(3)). The Reserve Bank may also cancel the certificate of registration (s. 29A(6)), prohibit deposits and bar dealing with assets (s. 33A) and debar an auditor for up to 3 years (s. 33C); the National Housing Bank may order a special audit (s. 33(3)) and apply for winding up (s. 33B). HFCs must display on their website any penalty imposed by RBI or NHB (RBI HFC Directions 2025, para 52).
