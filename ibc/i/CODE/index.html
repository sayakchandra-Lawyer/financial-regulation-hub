Applies to
Companies, LLPs and other corporate persons (Part II), other than financial service providers except those notified under s. 227; Personal guarantors to corporate debtors (Part III, in force for them since 01-12-2019; Part III is not yet in force for other individuals and partnership firms); Creditors, insolvency professionals, insolvency professional agencies, information utilities and registered valuers
Does not apply to
Financial service providers such as banks, NBFCs and insurers, except those the Central Government notifies under s. 227 (s. 3(7))

Key concepts

  • Default of at least Rs 1 crore to start a CIRP (s. 4); no CIRP can ever be started for a default that arose between 25-03-2020 and 24-03-2021 (s. 10A)
  • Homebuyers and other creditors in a class must file jointly: at least 100 of them or 10% of the class (or of allottees in the same project), whichever is less (s. 7(1) provisos)
  • Financial creditor and financial debt (s. 5(7) and (8)); operational creditor and operational debt (s. 5(20) and (21))
  • Corporate insolvency resolution process (CIRP), moratorium and committee of creditors (ss. 6 to 32A)
  • Resolution plan and ineligible resolution applicants (ss. 29A to 31)
  • Liquidation and the order of priority (ss. 33 to 54)
  • Pre-packaged insolvency for MSMEs (ss. 54A to 54P)
  • Voluntary liquidation (s. 59)
  • Avoidance transactions and fraudulent or wrongful trading (ss. 43 to 51 and 66)
  • Insolvency of personal guarantors (Part III)
  • IBBI and service providers (Part IV)
  • Insolvency of notified financial service providers (s. 227)

Key obligations

  • s. 7(5): the NCLT must admit a financial creditor's application within 14 days if a default has occurred, the application is complete and no disciplinary proceeding is pending against the proposed resolution professional; no other ground may be used to reject it, and an information utility record of default is sufficient proof for a financial institution (Explanations I and II, from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 12A: an admitted application may be withdrawn only on the resolution professional's application with 90% of the CoC vote, and not before the CoC is formed or after the first invitation for resolution plans (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 14: on admission, a moratorium bars suits and proceedings against the corporate debtor, transfer of its assets, enforcement of security interests it created (including under SARFAESI) and recovery by an owner or lessor of property in its possession; it does not protect sureties, and since 26-05-2026 it also bars a surety's own action against the corporate debtor under the guarantee
  • s. 21: the CoC comprises the unrelated financial creditors; a related party is excluded from the CoC, except a regulated financial entity that became related only by converting or substituting debt into equity; where there are no financial creditors, the CoC is formed from operational creditors (reg 16); decisions need 51% unless the Code says otherwise; from 26-05-2026 the CoC also supervises the liquidator (s. 21(11))
  • s. 28A: a creditor that has taken possession of a guarantor's asset may transfer it as part of the resolution with prior CoC approval (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 29A: a person who, alone or acting jointly or in concert, is an undischarged insolvent, a wilful defaulter, the holder or promoter of an account classified as NPA for at least a year before the CIRP (unless all overdues are paid before the plan is submitted; a non-related financial entity is exempt), a person convicted of specified offences, a disqualified director, a person barred by SEBI, a promoter or manager of a company in which the NCLT has found avoidance transactions, a guarantor of the corporate debtor whose guarantee has been invoked and remains unpaid, a person under an equivalent disability abroad, or a connected person of any of them (promoters, managers and controllers of the applicant and their related parties, as defined in the section), cannot submit a resolution plan; s. 240A: the NPA and guarantor bars (clauses (c) and (h)) do not apply to applicants for MSMEs
  • s. 30(2)(ba) and (4): a plan must pay financial creditors who do not vote for it at least the lower of (i) what they would get in liquidation under s. 53 and (ii) what they would get if the amount distributed under the plan were shared in the s. 53 order of priority; operational creditors must get at least the higher of their liquidation amount and their s. 53 share of the plan amount (s. 30(2)(b)); the CoC approves a plan with 66% of the vote, records reasons, and may weigh the priority and value of each secured creditor's security (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 31: an approved plan binds the corporate debtor, employees, members, creditors (including government authorities for statutory dues) and guarantors; claims not in the plan are extinguished (s. 31(6)); where the plan involves a combination that needs CCI approval, that approval must be obtained before the plan goes to the NCLT (s. 31(4) proviso)
  • s. 52(2): a secured creditor that wants to realise its security outside liquidation must tell the liquidator within 14 days of liquidation commencing, or it is deemed to have relinquished the security (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 53: liquidation proceeds go (a) to CIRP and liquidation costs, (b) workmen's dues for 24 months and secured creditors who relinquished their security, (c) other employees' wages for 12 months, (d) unsecured financial creditors, (e) government dues for two years and secured creditors' shortfall after enforcing security, (f) remaining debts, (g) preference shareholders and (h) equity shareholders or partners; workmen's provident, pension and gratuity funds are outside the estate (s. 36(4)); from 26-05-2026 government dues rank in clause (e) or (f) even if secured by law or agreement, and a secured creditor that relinquishes security ranks as secured only up to the value of that security
  • s. 227: the Central Government may notify financial service providers, such as NBFCs, for insolvency under modified rules

Deadlines and cycles

  • s. 7(4), 9(5), 10(4): the NCLT decides an admission application within 14 days; an applicant gets 7 days to cure defects
  • s. 12: CIRP to be completed within 180 days, extendable once by up to 90 days on a 66% CoC vote, and ordinarily within 330 days including litigation (the Supreme Court has allowed longer in exceptional cases)
  • s. 8(2): a corporate debtor has 10 days from an operational creditor's demand notice to show a dispute or payment
  • s. 22(1): the first CoC meeting within 7 days of the CoC being formed
  • s. 31(2A), 33(2A), 54(4): the NCLT approves or rejects a plan, and passes liquidation and dissolution orders, within 30 days (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 33(1A): on a 66% CoC request the NCLT may restore the CIRP for up to 120 days instead of ordering liquidation (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 54(1): the liquidator applies for dissolution within 180 days of liquidation commencing, extendable by up to 90 days (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 61: appeal to the NCLAT within 30 days (plus 15); the NCLAT is to decide within three months (s. 61(6), from 26-05-2026); s. 62: appeal to the Supreme Court within 45 days (plus 15)
  • s. 43 and 46: look-back for preferences is one year before the initiation date (two years for related parties) up to the insolvency commencement date; for undervalued transactions one year (two years for related parties)
  • s. 238A: the Limitation Act, 1963 applies to applications and appeals

If you breach it

  • s. 64A and 183A: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore for initiating frivolous or vexatious proceedings (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 66: directors may be ordered to contribute to the assets for fraudulent or wrongful trading
  • s. 67B: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore on officers or creditors who breach the moratorium; for breach of an approved plan, up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher (from 26-05-2026)
  • s. 67C: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore on an operational creditor that hides a dispute or payment
  • s. 68 to 77A: imprisonment and fines for concealing property, defrauding creditors, misconduct during a CIRP, falsifying books and false information
  • s. 235A: at least Rs 1 lakh a day for any contravention, up to three times the loss or unlawful gain, or Rs 5 crore if neither can be quantified

General consequences under the statute. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: the Adjudicating Authority may, on an application by IBBI or the Central Government, penalise any contravention of the Code, rules or regulations with at least Rs 1 lakh for each day it continues, up to three times the loss caused or the unlawful gain made, whichever is higher, or up to Rs 5 crore where neither can be quantified (s. 235A). Contravening the moratorium or an approved resolution plan attracts penalties of Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore, and for a resolution plan up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher (s. 67B). Frivolous or vexatious proceedings attract Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore (ss. 64A and 183A). Offences such as concealment of property, fraud on creditors, falsifying books and false information carry imprisonment and fines (ss. 68 to 77A and 184 to 187), tried by the Special Court (s. 236). IBBI's disciplinary committee may penalise a service provider up to three times the loss or gain, or up to Rs 2 crore where neither can be quantified, suspend or cancel its registration and order disgorgement (s. 220). Appeals against NCLT orders in corporate insolvency lie to the NCLAT within 30 days (extendable by 15) and then to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 45 days (extendable by 15) (ss. 61 and 62); appeals against IBBI disciplinary orders lie to the NCLAT under s. 202.
