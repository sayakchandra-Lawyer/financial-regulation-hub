Key concepts

  • Record of default (reg 2A) and chronology of debt and default with the application (reg 2C)
  • Claims and their verification (regs 7 to 14)
  • CoC meetings and voting (regs 17 to 26)
  • Fair value and liquidation value by two registered valuers (reg 35)
  • Avoidance transactions review (reg 35A)
  • Information memorandum, expression of interest and request for plans (regs 36 to 36B)
  • Mandatory contents of resolution plans (regs 38 and 38A)
  • Real estate projects: separate accounts, RERA compliance and cost-to-complete assessment (regs 4D to 4G)
  • Withdrawal (reg 30A), liquidation decision, dissolution and restoration (regs 40D to 40F)
  • Electronic forms and late fee (reg 40B)

Key obligations

  • reg 2A and 2C: a financial creditor may prove default by certified bankers' book entries or a final court or tribunal order; with a s. 7 or 9 application the creditor files its PAN, email and a chronology of the debt, default, part payments, acknowledgments and limitation
  • reg 2B and 2E (from 02-06-2026): an operational creditor files GST returns, part payments, assignments, guarantees, a statement of account, related-party status and other recovery proceedings; a corporate applicant files three years of bank accounts, assets, receivables, creditors and litigation
  • reg 16E (from 09-06-2026): where creditors other than scheduled banks and public financial institutions hold more than 66% of the CoC vote, the RP invites the five largest unrelated operational creditors, including the three largest statutory authorities, to CoC meetings as non-voting observers and minutes their views
  • reg 13: the IRP or RP verifies claims within 7 days of receipt and, from 02-06-2026, communicates admission or rejection with reasons within 7 days
  • reg 18 and 19: the CoC meets at least once every 30 days unless it decides otherwise (at least once a quarter); members with 33% of the vote can requisition a meeting; notice is 5 days, which the CoC can cut to 24 hours
  • reg 4D to 4G: for real estate projects, a separate bank account for each project, project-wise receipts and payments to the CoC, RERA fund-use rules, and a technical and cost-to-complete assessment by independent professionals with CoC approval
  • reg 35: two registered valuers estimate fair value and liquidation value under the valuation standards IBBI notifies; members get the values only on a confidentiality undertaking
  • reg 35A: the RP forms an opinion on preferential, undervalued, extortionate or fraudulent transactions by day 75 and makes a determination by day 115
  • reg 36A: invitation for expressions of interest no later than day 60; with CoC approval it may cover the company as a whole, particular assets, or each real estate project
  • reg 38: operational creditors are paid in priority to financial creditors, and dissenting financial creditors in priority to assenting ones (pro rata in each stage for staged payment); the plan must state how it deals with all stakeholders and disclose any past failure to implement a plan
  • reg 38(3A): from 23-12-2025 every plan includes a statement of beneficial ownership of the resolution applicant and an affidavit on eligibility for s. 32A protection, in IBBI's formats
  • reg 38A: for real estate projects, the plan gives project-wise units sold and unsold, treatment of allottees (including those who did not claim), delivery timelines and progress reporting to the monitoring committee
  • reg 39: a resolution applicant submits an affidavit of s. 29A eligibility and an undertaking that its information is true; false information makes it ineligible and forfeits its deposit
  • reg 30A: an application to withdraw under s. 12A is made through the IRP or RP in Form FA with a bank guarantee for process costs
  • reg 40E and 40F: the CoC may resolve with 66% to seek dissolution where assets cannot cover costs or cannot be realised, and may apply to restore the CIRP before a liquidation order

Deadlines and cycles

  • Claim verification and communication: 7 days each
  • Avoidance review: opinion by day 75, determination by day 115
  • Expression of interest: by day 60
  • Request for resolution plans: at least 30 days before the last date for plans
  • Dissolution decision: RP informs the NCLT within 10 days of the CoC resolution
  • reg 40B: CIRP forms are filed on IBBI's platform by the stated dates; late filing attracts a fee (from 2026)

General consequences under the statute. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: the Adjudicating Authority may, on an application by IBBI or the Central Government, penalise any contravention of the Code, rules or regulations with at least Rs 1 lakh for each day it continues, up to three times the loss caused or the unlawful gain made, whichever is higher, or up to Rs 5 crore where neither can be quantified (s. 235A). Contravening the moratorium or an approved resolution plan attracts penalties of Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore, and for a resolution plan up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher (s. 67B). Frivolous or vexatious proceedings attract Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore (ss. 64A and 183A). Offences such as concealment of property, fraud on creditors, falsifying books and false information carry imprisonment and fines (ss. 68 to 77A and 184 to 187), tried by the Special Court (s. 236). IBBI's disciplinary committee may penalise a service provider up to three times the loss or gain, or up to Rs 2 crore where neither can be quantified, suspend or cancel its registration and order disgorgement (s. 220). Appeals against NCLT orders in corporate insolvency lie to the NCLAT within 30 days (extendable by 15) and then to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 45 days (extendable by 15) (ss. 61 and 62); appeals against IBBI disciplinary orders lie to the NCLAT under s. 202.
