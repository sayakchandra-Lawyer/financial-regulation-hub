Applies to
All parties to processes under the Code: creditors, corporate debtors, resolution applicants, guarantors and insolvency professionals

Key concepts

  • Mandatory admission of a financial creditor's application once default is shown (s. 7(5))
  • Withdrawal only with 90% CoC approval and only between CoC formation and the first invitation for plans (s. 12A)
  • CoC supervision of liquidation, restoration of CIRP and dissolution without liquidation (ss. 21(11), 33(1A), 33(2), 54(2A))
  • Treatment of dissenting creditors and value of security in resolution plans (ss. 30(2)(ba), 30(4))
  • Government dues rank as government dues even if secured (s. 53 Explanation)
  • No interim moratorium for personal guarantors (ss. 96(4) and 124(4))
  • Penalties for frivolous proceedings and breach of moratorium or plan (ss. 64A, 67B, 183A)
  • Creditor-initiated insolvency resolution process (CIIRP, ss. 58A to 58L) and group insolvency (s. 59A): enacted but not yet in force

Key obligations

  • The amendments to ss. 7, 12A, 14, 16, 18, 21, 26, 28A, 30, 31, 33, 34, 34A, 35, 43, 46, 52, 53, 54, 59, 61, 64A, 66, 67B, 96, 124, 183A, 196 and 220 are in force from 26-05-2026 under S.O. 2625(E)
  • Not yet in force (as on 26-11-2026): Chapter IVA of Part II (CIIRP, ss. 58A to 58L), Chapter VA (group insolvency, s. 59A) and the electronic portal and related provisions (ss. 240B and 240C); these need a separate notification
  • CIIRP as enacted: notified financial institutions holding at least 51% by value of debt owed to such institutions may start the process after 30 days' notice to the corporate debtor; the resolution professional's public announcement starts it; the corporate debtor may object within 30 days; management stays with the board under the resolution professional's oversight
  • s. 31(1) second proviso: the NCLT may approve a plan in two steps, implementation first and distribution within 30 days; s. 31(4) proviso: where the plan involves a combination needing CCI approval, it must be obtained before the plan is submitted to the NCLT

Deadlines and cycles

  • CIIRP as enacted: complete within 150 days, extendable once by up to 45 days on a 66% vote
  • NCLT orders on plans, liquidation and dissolution within 30 days (ss. 31(2A), 33(2A), 54(4))
  • NCLAT to decide appeals within three months (s. 61(6))

If you breach it

  • ss. 64A and 183A: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore for frivolous or vexatious proceedings
  • s. 67B: Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore for breach of moratorium; for breach of a resolution plan up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher

General consequences under the statute. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: the Adjudicating Authority may, on an application by IBBI or the Central Government, penalise any contravention of the Code, rules or regulations with at least Rs 1 lakh for each day it continues, up to three times the loss caused or the unlawful gain made, whichever is higher, or up to Rs 5 crore where neither can be quantified (s. 235A). Contravening the moratorium or an approved resolution plan attracts penalties of Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore, and for a resolution plan up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher (s. 67B). Frivolous or vexatious proceedings attract Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore (ss. 64A and 183A). Offences such as concealment of property, fraud on creditors, falsifying books and false information carry imprisonment and fines (ss. 68 to 77A and 184 to 187), tried by the Special Court (s. 236). IBBI's disciplinary committee may penalise a service provider up to three times the loss or gain, or up to Rs 2 crore where neither can be quantified, suspend or cancel its registration and order disgorgement (s. 220). Appeals against NCLT orders in corporate insolvency lie to the NCLAT within 30 days (extendable by 15) and then to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 45 days (extendable by 15) (ss. 61 and 62); appeals against IBBI disciplinary orders lie to the NCLAT under s. 202.
