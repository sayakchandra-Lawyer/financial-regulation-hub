Liquidators and the committee of creditors in liquidation; Secured and unsecured creditors, workmen and other stakeholders; Bidders for liquidation assets

Key concepts

  • CoC continues in liquidation (reg 8)
  • Compromise or arrangement (reg 2B)
  • Secured creditor's choice to relinquish or realise (reg 21A)
  • Sale by auction or, with CoC approval, private sale (regs 32 and 33)
  • Realisation by a secured creditor (reg 37)
  • Distribution and completion (regs 42 and 44)

Key obligations

  • reg 8: the CoC continues during liquidation without secured creditors that have not relinquished their security; its first meeting is within 7 days of the liquidation commencement date; it may replace the liquidator with 66% of the vote (reg 8B)
  • reg 2B: a compromise or arrangement under s. 230 of the Companies Act may be filed only if the CoC recommended it, within 30 days of liquidation, approved by the required majority and giving creditors more than the liquidation value; it must finish within 90 days, and an ineligible person under s. 29A cannot be a party
  • reg 12: the liquidator makes a public announcement within 5 days of appointment
  • reg 21A: a secured creditor tells the liquidator within 14 days whether it relinquishes or realises its security; silence means the security is deemed relinquished; a realising creditor pays its share of costs and workmen's dues
  • reg 30: claims are verified within 7 days and the decision with reasons is sent within 7 days; claims verified in the CIRP are not re-verified
  • reg 32 and 33: assets are sold standalone, by slump sale, collectively or in parcels, ordinarily by auction; private sale needs 66% CoC approval and specified grounds; no sale to a person ineligible under s. 29A, and sales to related parties need NCLT permission
  • reg 37: if the liquidator finds a buyer at a higher price within 14 days of the secured creditor's proposed price (and tells it within 7 days), the creditor must sell to that buyer
  • reg 42: proceeds are distributed within 15 days of receipt, after the list of stakeholders is filed

Deadlines and cycles

  • Liquidation to be completed within 180 days of the liquidation commencement date (from 02-06-2026), with extension on the CoC's instruction (reg 44)
  • Public announcement: 5 days
  • Secured creditor's decision: 14 days
  • Distribution: 15 days

General consequences under the statute. Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016: the Adjudicating Authority may, on an application by IBBI or the Central Government, penalise any contravention of the Code, rules or regulations with at least Rs 1 lakh for each day it continues, up to three times the loss caused or the unlawful gain made, whichever is higher, or up to Rs 5 crore where neither can be quantified (s. 235A). Contravening the moratorium or an approved resolution plan attracts penalties of Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore, and for a resolution plan up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher (s. 67B). Frivolous or vexatious proceedings attract Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore (ss. 64A and 183A). Offences such as concealment of property, fraud on creditors, falsifying books and false information carry imprisonment and fines (ss. 68 to 77A and 184 to 187), tried by the Special Court (s. 236). IBBI's disciplinary committee may penalise a service provider up to three times the loss or gain, or up to Rs 2 crore where neither can be quantified, suspend or cancel its registration and order disgorgement (s. 220). Appeals against NCLT orders in corporate insolvency lie to the NCLAT within 30 days (extendable by 15) and then to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 45 days (extendable by 15) (ss. 61 and 62); appeals against IBBI disciplinary orders lie to the NCLAT under s. 202.
