IBC REGISTER: Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, its rules, IBBI regulations, notifications and IBBI circulars, for financial institutions (financial creditors, NBFCs/HFCs as financial service providers, corporate debtors, resolution applicants, insolvency professionals) (as of 2026-11-26). One block per instrument. Fields: applies, excluded, concepts, duties, deadlines, breach (stated consequences), penalties (code of the shared statutory penalty text below), refs, repeals, defs (terms the instrument defines: term [para; scope]: meaning).

SHARED STATUTORY PENALTY TEXTS:
P1: IBC: the NCLT may, on an application by IBBI or the Central Government, penalise any contravention of the Code, rules or regulations with at least Rs 1 lakh for each day it continues, up to three times the loss caused or the unlawful gain made, whichever is higher, or up to Rs 5 crore where neither can be quantified (s. 235A). Contravening the moratorium or an approved resolution plan attracts penalties of Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore, and for a resolution plan up to Rs 1 crore or 20% of the amount to be distributed, whichever is higher (s. 67B). Frivolous or vexatious proceedings attract Rs 1 lakh to Rs 2 crore (ss. 64A and 183A). Offences such as concealment of property, fraud on creditors, falsifying books and false information carry imprisonment and fines (ss. 68 to 77A and 184 to 187), tried by the Special Court (s. 236). IBBI's disciplinary committee may penalise a service provider up to three times the loss or gain, or up to Rs 2 crore where neither can be quantified, suspend or cancel its registration and order disgorgement (s. 220). Appeals against NCLT orders in corporate insolvency lie to the NCLAT within 30 days (extendable by 15) and then to the Supreme Court on a question of law within 45 days (extendable by 15) (ss. 61 and 62); appeals against IBBI disciplinary orders lie to the NCLAT under s. 202.

GLOSSARY (plain-language meanings, not statutory text):
IBC (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016): The law for time-bound insolvency resolution and liquidation of companies and LLPs, and of personal guarantors to companies. Its provisions for other individuals and firms are not yet in force.
IBBI (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India): The regulator under the Code. It makes regulations, registers and supervises insolvency professionals, their agencies, information utilities and registered valuers, and issues circulars under s. 196.
NCLT (National Company Law Tribunal): The NCLT for companies and LLPs and for their personal guarantors. It admits applications, approves resolution plans and orders liquidation.
NCLAT (National Company Law Appellate Tribunal): Hears appeals from NCLT orders under the Code; appeals are filed within 30 days, extendable by 15.
CIRP (Corporate insolvency resolution process): The process under Part II in which an insolvency professional runs the company and the creditors decide on a resolution plan within 180 days (outer limit 330 days).
Default: Non-payment of a debt that has become due. A CIRP needs a default of at least Rs 1 crore; a pre-pack needs at least Rs 10 lakh.
Financial creditor (FC): A person to whom a financial debt is owed, such as a bank or NBFC lender or a debenture holder. Homebuyers are financial creditors in a class. Financial creditors make up the CoC.
Operational creditor (OC): A person owed an operational debt, such as a supplier, employee or a government authority for statutory dues.
Corporate debtor (CD): The company or LLP that owes a debt and is in, or may face, insolvency.
IRP / RP (Interim resolution professional / resolution professional): The insolvency professional who takes over management on admission (IRP) and then runs the CIRP for the CoC (RP).
CoC (Committee of creditors): The unrelated financial creditors (operational creditors only where there are no financial creditors), who vote on key decisions: 51% for most matters, 66% for approving a resolution plan, extending the CIRP or replacing the RP, and 90% for withdrawal.
Moratorium: The bar, during the CIRP, on suits and proceedings against the corporate debtor, transfer of its assets, enforcement of security it created (including SARFAESI) and recovery of property it occupies (s. 14). A government licence or permit cannot be suspended or cancelled on the ground of insolvency alone, provided current dues are paid.
Interim moratorium: A moratorium from the date an application is filed. It applies to NBFC/FSP insolvency under the FSP Rules. Since 26-05-2026 it no longer applies to personal guarantors to corporate debtors.
Resolution plan: The proposal to revive the corporate debtor, approved by 66% of the CoC and the NCLT. It binds all stakeholders, including government authorities and guarantors, and wipes out claims not included in it.
Resolution applicant (RA): A person who submits a resolution plan. It must not be ineligible under s. 29A.
Section 29A: The list of persons who cannot submit a resolution plan or buy assets in liquidation, such as wilful defaulters, promoters of long-standing NPAs, undischarged insolvents, certain convicts and invoked guarantors, and persons connected with them. The NPA and guarantor bars do not apply to MSMEs (s. 240A).
Section 32A: Two protections: the corporate debtor is not liable for offences committed before the CIRP once an approved plan passes control to a person who was not involved, and its property cannot be attached for such offences after a plan or a liquidation sale to such a person. The individuals responsible remain liable, and the debtor must keep helping investigators.
Liquidation value: The estimated amount the assets would fetch if the company were liquidated on the insolvency commencement date. A financial creditor that votes against a plan must get at least the lower of its share of this in liquidation and its share of the plan amount in the s. 53 order.
Waterfall (Section 53): The order in which liquidation proceeds are paid: costs; workmen (24 months) and secured creditors who gave up their security; other employees (12 months); unsecured financial creditors; government dues (two years) and secured creditors' shortfall; remaining debts; preference shareholders; and equity shareholders.
Liquidation: The sale of a corporate debtor's assets and distribution of proceeds under s. 53, ordered when no plan is approved or the CoC decides to liquidate. Since 02-06-2026 it is to be completed within 180 days.
Voluntary liquidation: Winding up of a solvent company by its own decision under s. 59, after a declaration of solvency and approval of its members (and two-thirds of its creditors by value, if it has debt).
PPIRP (Pre-packaged insolvency resolution process): A faster, debtor-in-possession process for MSMEs, started with the approval of unrelated financial creditors and a base resolution plan, to finish within 120 days.
CIIRP (Creditor-initiated insolvency resolution process): A process in which notified financial institutions can start resolution out of court with the board kept in place. Enacted in 2026 (ss. 58A to 58L) but not yet in force.
Group insolvency: Coordinated insolvency of companies in a group under s. 59A, inserted in 2026 but not yet in force.
FSP (Financial service provider): A firm authorised to provide financial services. Banks are outside the Code; notified FSPs (currently NBFCs and HFCs with assets of Rs 500 crore or more) go through insolvency under the FSP Rules on the regulator's application.
Administrator: The individual the NCLT appoints, on the regulator's proposal, to act as IRP, RP and liquidator of a notified FSP.
Personal guarantor (PG): An individual who has guaranteed a corporate debtor's debt. Part III applies, with a repayment plan and, if that fails, bankruptcy.
Insolvency professional (IP): A person registered with IBBI and enrolled with an insolvency professional agency, who acts as IRP, RP, liquidator or bankruptcy trustee. Needs an authorisation for assignment.
AFA (Authorisation for assignment): The authorisation an insolvency professional agency issues to an IP; without it an IP cannot take an assignment.
Information utility (IU): A registered entity that stores financial information and issues a record of default after seeking authentication from the debtor.
Registered valuer (RV): A valuer registered with IBBI under the Companies (Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules. Valuations under the Code follow the International Valuation Standards since 01-04-2026.
Avoidance transactions: Preferential, undervalued, extortionate and fraudulent transactions before insolvency that the NCLT can reverse (ss. 43 to 51 and 66). Since 26-05-2026 these proceedings continue after the CIRP ends.
Baanknet: The e-auction platform that liquidators must use for liquidation sales (formerly eBKray).
Form G: The invitation for expressions of interest from prospective resolution applicants, published by day 60 of the CIRP.
Information memorandum (IM): The document the RP prepares on the corporate debtor's assets, liabilities, operations and tax losses for prospective resolution applicants.

IBC CHANGES (2025-12-01 to 2026-11-26):
- 2026-10-30 Circular: Real estate CIRPs: RERA compliance, RERA's views on plans and site notices (IBBI/CIRP/110/2026). Applies: IRPs and RPs of corporate debtors with real estate projects, CoCs and prospective resolution applicants.. Changes: RPs must comply with RERA during the CIRP. RPs may invite RERA's written views on plans and share them with the CoC and bidders. The CIRP must be shown on signboards at each project site and office within 7 days of appointment. Actions: Add RERA portal checks and site notices to real estate CIRP checklists.
- 2026-10-30 Circular: Revised Form CA with a separate part for homebuyers (IBBI/CIRP/109/2026). Applies: Financial creditors in a class, including real estate allottees, and IRPs and RPs.. Changes: Form CA has Part I for other creditors in a class and Part II for allottees. Claims filed earlier need not be refiled. Actions: Update claim invitation templates.
- 2026-10-30 Regulation: CIRP Regulations amended (fifth amendment of 2026) (IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) (Fifth Amendment) Regulations, 2026). Applies: Everyone in a CIRP.. Changes: The CIRP Regulations were amended again; the consolidated text as on 30-10-2026 is the current version. Actions: Read the consolidated CIRP Regulations as on 30-10-2026.
- 2026-09-22 Regulation: Liquidator may modify the list of stakeholders and tells the NCLT within 30 days (IBBI/2026-27/GN/REG154). Applies: Liquidators and stakeholders in liquidation.. Changes: The liquidator may modify an entry in the list of stakeholders on new information and informs the NCLT within 30 days. Actions: Keep a log of changes to the stakeholder list.
- 2026-09-21 Circular: No interim moratorium for personal guarantors, even in pending applications (IBBI/II/106/2026). Applies: Creditors of personal guarantors, resolution professionals and guarantors.. Changes: IBBI drew attention to Bombay and Delhi High Court rulings that the 2026 amendment ended the interim moratorium for personal guarantors from 26-05-2026, including for applications pending then. Actions: Review stayed guarantor recovery actions.
- 2026-09-12 Discussion paper: Discussion paper on safeguards in personal guarantor insolvency (IBBI discussion paper). Applies: Proposal for comment only; not in force.. Changes: IBBI invited comments on proposed changes. Nothing changes until regulations are notified. Actions: Track for final regulations.
- 2026-09-09 Circular: IPs to watch for misuse of insolvency and apply to the NCLT (IBBI/CIRP/105/2026). Applies: Insolvency professionals; creditors and debtors in CIRPs and liquidations.. Changes: IBBI listed signs of misuse, such as a single non-bank creditor dominating the CoC, clusters of connected companies and very low recoveries without proper valuation. An IP who believes on reasonable grounds that a process is fraudulent or malicious must apply to the NCLT. Actions: Build the indicators into case intake reviews.
- 2026-08-14 Discussion paper: Discussion paper on guidance for spotting fraudulent or malicious CIRPs and recourse under ss. 60(5) and 65 (IBBI discussion paper). Applies: Proposal for comment only; not in force.. Changes: IBBI invited comments on proposed changes. Nothing changes until regulations are notified. Actions: Track for final regulations.
- 2026-07-02 Discussion paper: Discussion paper on amendments to the CIRP, Liquidation and personal guarantor regulations (IBBI discussion paper). Applies: Proposal for comment only; not in force.. Changes: IBBI invited comments on proposed changes. Nothing changes until regulations are notified. Actions: Track for final regulations.
- 2026-06-15 Circular: Guidelines and format for valuation reports under the Code (IBBI/RV/103/2026). Applies: Registered valuers and IPs.. Changes: Valuers prepare reports and keep documents in the format IBBI notified. Actions: Share the format with appointed valuers.
- 2026-06-09 Regulation: Observers in CoCs dominated by non-bank creditors (IBBI/2026-27/GN/REG153). Applies: CoCs where creditors other than scheduled banks and public financial institutions hold over 66% of the vote.. Changes: The RP invites the five largest unrelated operational creditors, including the three largest statutory authorities, as non-voting observers and minutes their views. Only unrelated operational creditors count for a CoC made only of operational creditors. Actions: Check CoC composition against the 66% test.
- 2026-06-02 Regulation: IBBI regulations aligned with the 2026 Code amendments (IBBI/2026-27/GN/REG144 to REG152). Applies: All processes under the Code.. Changes: Liquidation to finish within 180 days; secured creditors decide within 14 days; claims verified within 7 days; distribution within 15 days. CIRP claims admitted or rejected with reasons within 7 days; more information required with s. 9 and s. 10 applications. Forms are now notified by IBBI circular (formats circulars of 02-06-2026 and 03-06-2026). Actions: Update liquidation and CIRP calendars. Secured creditors: decide on relinquishment within 14 days of liquidation.
- 2026-05-26 Act: IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 in force (except CIIRP and group insolvency) (Act 6 of 2026; S.O. 2625(E)). Applies: Everyone dealing with processes under the Code.. Changes: Admission of a financial creditor's application is mandatory once default is shown; an IU record of default suffices for a financial institution. Withdrawal needs 90% of the CoC and is barred after the first invitation for plans. CoC supervises liquidation; CIRP can be restored for up to 120 days; dissolution without liquidation. Government dues are not treated as secured in the waterfall. No interim moratorium for personal guarantors. New penalties for frivolous proceedings and breach of moratorium or plan. Actions: Review pending applications and liquidation strategies against the new provisions.
- 2026-04-01 Circular: International Valuation Standards apply to valuations under the Code (IBBI/RV/93/2026). Applies: Registered valuers, IPs and all valuations under the Code.. Changes: IBBI notified the IVSC's International Valuation Standards for all valuations under the Code. Actions: Update valuer engagement letters.
- 2025-12-29 Circular: Beneficial ownership statement and s. 32A affidavit in every resolution plan (IBBI/CIRP/90/2025). Applies: Prospective resolution applicants and RPs.. Changes: Formats for the beneficial ownership statement and s. 32A eligibility affidavit required by reg 38(3A). Actions: Prepare ownership charts for bids.
