RBI powers over stressed assets

The question

Was RBI's circular of 12 February 2018 on resolution of stressed assets (the 'one-day default' framework) within its powers?

What the court held

  • Sections 35AA and 35AB of the Banking Regulation Act are valid. They confer regulatory powers on RBI and are not manifestly arbitrary.
  • RBI can direct banks to start insolvency under the IBC only under section 35AA, and only for specific defaults by specific debtors, with Central Government authorisation. A direction aimed at debtors generally is outside section 35AA.
  • The circular also relied on section 45L of the RBI Act for NBFCs, but nothing showed that section 45L(3) was satisfied. Because banks and NBFCs could not be separated, the circular was declared ultra vires as a whole.

What it means in practice

  • RBI's power to push lenders into insolvency is case-specific. A general framework cannot be used to compel lenders to start insolvency, though lenders can still choose the IBC themselves as part of a resolution.
  • For NBFCs, directions under section 45L must meet that section's conditions. The current stressed-asset rules for NBFCs were issued on this footing.
