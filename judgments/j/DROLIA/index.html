Vidya Drolia v. Durga Trading Corporation

Debt recovery disputes are not arbitrable

The question

When is a dispute not arbitrable, and can claims that fall within the Debts Recovery Tribunal's jurisdiction be referred to arbitration?

What the court held

  • A dispute is not arbitrable where it relates to rights in rem, affects third parties and needs centralised adjudication, concerns sovereign or public functions of the State, or is made non-arbitrable expressly or by necessary implication by a mandatory statute.
  • Matters covered by the Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 are not arbitrable. The Court overruled the Delhi High Court Full Bench in HDFC Bank Ltd v. Satpal Singh Bakshi, which had held them arbitrable.
  • At the stage of sections 8 and 11 the court interferes only where the agreement is plainly non-existent or invalid or the dispute is plainly non-arbitrable: 'when in doubt, do refer'.

What it means in practice

  • Banks and financial institutions entitled to use the DRT cannot take claims that fall within the DRT's jurisdiction (debts at or above the RDB Act s. 1(4) threshold: Rs 10 lakh in the Act, or another amount the Central Government notifies) to arbitration instead. Lenders that cannot use the DRT, such as NBFCs not notified as financial institutions under the RDB Act, commonly use arbitration for recovery.
