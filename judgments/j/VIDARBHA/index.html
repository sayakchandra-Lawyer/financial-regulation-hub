Vidarbha Industries Power Ltd v. Axis Bank Ltd

Must the NCLT admit a lender's insolvency application?

Supreme Court

2022 INSC 710

[2022] 12 SCR 139
Case no.
Civil Appeal No. 4633 of 2021
Bench
Indira Banerjee and J.K. Maheshwari JJ.
Statutes
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, ss. 7(5), 9(5)
Status
Section 7(5) was substituted with effect from 26 May 2026: the NCLT shall admit the application within 14 days if a default has occurred, the application is complete and no disciplinary proceeding is pending against the proposed resolution professional, and no other ground may be used to reject it.

The facts

A power generating company defaulted on bank loans. It asked the NCLT to defer the bank's section 7 application because it held an order of the electricity appellate tribunal in its favour worth about Rs 1,730 crore, more than the bank's claim, which was under appeal. The NCLT and NCLAT held that once debt and default are shown, nothing else is relevant.

The question

Is admission of a financial creditor's application under section 7(5)(a) mandatory once a debt and default are shown?

What the court held

  • The Court read the word 'may' in section 7(5)(a), in contrast to 'shall' in section 9(5)(a), as giving the NCLT a discretion. It could consider relevant factors such as the overall financial health and viability of the corporate debtor before admitting.
  • In a later order on a review petition the Court said these observations were made on the facts of the case. In M. Suresh Kumar Reddy v. Canara Bank (2023 INSC 521) the Court held that Vidarbha was confined to its facts and that, once default is shown, admission must ordinarily follow.

What it means in practice

  • For applications decided under the amended section 7(5), the borrower can no longer rely on Vidarbha to resist admission on grounds such as pending claims or general viability. The ruling remains relevant to older matters and to understanding why the provision was rewritten.
