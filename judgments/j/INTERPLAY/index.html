Unstamped agreements and arbitration

The question

Is an arbitration agreement in an unstamped or insufficiently stamped contract void, and must the court deal with stamping before appointing an arbitrator?

What the court held

  • An unstamped or insufficiently stamped agreement is inadmissible in evidence under section 35 of the Stamp Act, but it is not void, void from the start or unenforceable. Non-stamping is a curable defect, and the Stamp Act itself sets out how it is cured.
  • Admissibility and validity are different questions: a valid agreement can be inadmissible until the duty and penalty are paid.
  • When deciding applications under sections 8 or 11 of the Arbitration Act, the court only examines whether an arbitration agreement prima facie exists. Objections on stamping are for the arbitral tribunal. The five-judge ruling in N.N. Global (2023) was overruled on this point.

What it means in practice

  • A lender's loan agreement or guarantee that was not properly stamped can still be enforced once the deficit duty and penalty are paid, but it cannot be relied on in evidence until then. Stamping at execution remains the safe course, because the penalty for late stamping can be many times the duty.
  • Borrowers cannot block the appointment of an arbitrator by objecting to stamping; the tribunal will deal with it.
