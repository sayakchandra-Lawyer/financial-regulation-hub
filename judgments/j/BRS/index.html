BRS Ventures Investments Ltd v. SREI Infrastructure Finance Ltd

Recovering from both borrower and corporate guarantor

The facts

An NBFC lent Rs 100 crore to a company, with the borrower's holding company as corporate guarantor. In the guarantor's insolvency the NBFC received Rs 38.87 crore under the resolution plan. It then filed for insolvency of the borrower for the balance. The successful resolution applicant of the guarantor argued that the debt stood satisfied and that the borrower's assets were part of the guarantor's plan.

The question

After receiving part payment in the corporate guarantor's insolvency, can the lender proceed against the principal borrower for the balance?

What the court held

  • Yes. Part payment under the guarantor's resolution plan does not extinguish the borrower's liability for the rest. The liability of borrower and surety is co-extensive, and the creditor can proceed against both, together or one after the other.
  • A holding company does not own the assets of its subsidiary. The subsidiary's assets cannot be treated as part of the holding company's resolution plan.
  • The guarantor (or whoever steps into its shoes) is subrogated to the creditor's rights only to the extent of the amount actually paid.

What it means in practice

  • Lenders can file separate or simultaneous section 7 applications against the borrower and the corporate guarantor, and accepting a payout in one does not close the claim in the other, so long as total recovery does not exceed the debt.
  • Buyers of a guarantor company under a plan get subrogation rights only for what was paid, not control over the borrower's assets.
