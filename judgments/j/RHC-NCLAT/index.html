Housing Development Finance Corporation Ltd v. RHC Holding Pvt Ltd

A creditor cannot file for insolvency of an NBFC

Open in Key judgmentsJudgment text ↗

Case details

Court
National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, New Delhi
Date
10 Jul 2019
Case no.
Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 26 of 2019
Bench
S.J. Mukhopadhaya J. (Chairperson) and A.I.S. Cheema J. (Member, Judicial)
Statutes
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, ss. 3(7), 3(8), 3(16), 3(17), 7, 227; Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, ss. 45-I, 45-IA
Status
In November 2019 the Central Government notified rules under section 227: insolvency of NBFCs and HFCs with assets of Rs 500 crore or more can be started only on the Reserve Bank's application.

The facts

A housing finance company filed a section 7 application against a group holding company that held a Reserve Bank certificate of registration as a non-deposit-taking NBFC. The NCLT rejected the application on the ground that an NBFC is a financial service provider outside the Code. The lender argued that the company only invested in and guaranteed the debts of its own group and provided none of the financial services listed in the Code.

The question

Can a financial creditor start corporate insolvency under section 7 against a company registered with the Reserve Bank as an NBFC?

What the tribunal held

  • No. A 'corporate person' under the Code does not include a financial service provider, and a company registered with the Reserve Bank to carry on the business of a non-banking financial institution is a financial service provider.
  • The list of financial services in section 3(16) is inclusive. It is not necessary that the company accepts public deposits.
  • An allegation that the NBFC is breaching the conditions of its registration is a matter for the Reserve Bank. It is not a ground to admit or reject an insolvency application.

What it means in practice

  • A lender to an NBFC or HFC cannot itself use section 7 or 9 against it. Insolvency of a notified financial service provider can be started only by its regulator under the section 227 rules, and other recovery routes remain for the lender.
  • Lenders to group holding and investment companies should check whether the borrower holds an NBFC or core investment company registration before choosing a recovery route.
