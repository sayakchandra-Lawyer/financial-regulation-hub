Magma Fincorp Ltd v. Rajesh Kumar Tiwari

Repossession of a financed vehicle

The facts

A borrower took a vehicle on hire purchase from an NBFC and paid 7 of 35 instalments. The financier repossessed and sold the vehicle. The consumer fora held that repossession without notice was a deficiency in service and ordered a refund of the instalments paid, with compensation.

The question

Can a financier repossess a vehicle under a hire purchase agreement without notice, and what relief can a consumer forum give if it does?

What the court held

  • Under a hire purchase agreement the financier remains the owner until all instalments are paid, and may take back the vehicle on default as the agreement provides.
  • Whether notice is needed before repossession depends on the terms of the agreement, express or implied. If the agreement requires notice, repossessing without it is a breach and a deficiency in service.
  • The remedy for such a breach is damages for the loss actually caused. A refund of all instalments, which were in substance hire charges for use of the vehicle, is not automatic. The refund ordered by the fora was set aside, and the financier was directed to pay a composite Rs 15,000 as damages and costs for not giving a proper notice before repossession.

What it means in practice

  • NBFCs financing vehicles should follow the notice terms of their own agreements and the Reserve Bank's fair practices code, and take possession peacefully. A lapse in the notice leads to modest damages measured by actual loss, not to a refund of what the borrower paid.
