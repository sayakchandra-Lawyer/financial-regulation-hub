Nedumpilli Finance Co Ltd v. State of Kerala

State money-lending laws do not apply to NBFCs

Civil Appeal No. 5233 of 2012
Bench
Hemant Gupta and V. Ramasubramanian JJ.
Statutes
Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934, Chapter III-B, ss. 45-IA, 45JA, 45L, 45Q; Kerala Money Lenders Act, 1958; Gujarat Money Lenders Act, 2011

The facts

NBFCs registered with the Reserve Bank were asked by State authorities in Kerala and Gujarat to take money-lending licences and follow the interest caps in the State money lenders Acts. The Kerala High Court held that the State Act applied to them; the Gujarat High Court held that it did not.

The question

Do State money-lending laws apply to NBFCs registered under the RBI Act and regulated by the Reserve Bank?

What the court held

  • No. Chapter III-B of the RBI Act is a complete code, and the Reserve Bank's control over an NBFC runs 'from the cradle to the grave': registration, capital, deposits, prudential norms, directions and winding up.
  • The Kerala Money Lenders Act, 1958 and the Gujarat Money Lenders Act, 2011 have no application to NBFCs registered under the RBI Act and regulated by the Reserve Bank.
  • The Reserve Bank has power to deal with the interest NBFCs charge, through sections 45JA and 45L of the RBI Act.

What it means in practice

  • A registered NBFC does not need a State money-lending licence and is not bound by State caps on interest. Its pricing is governed by the Reserve Bank's directions on interest rates, fair practices and, for microfinance loans, the pricing framework.
  • The ruling does not cover unregistered lenders, and it does not exempt NBFCs from other State laws such as those on coercive recovery, which need to be checked separately.
