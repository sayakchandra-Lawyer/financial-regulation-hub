Gokal Chand (D) through LRs v. Axis Bank Ltd

Loan-linked life cover: death before the policy is issued

The facts

A home loan was sanctioned on condition that the borrower took life cover. On the day of sanction the bank, through which the cover was arranged, paid a premium of about Rs 6.24 lakh to the insurer out of the loan. The borrower underwent the medical tests five days later, and they showed nothing abnormal. He died of cardiac arrest nine days after the tests. After the death was reported, the insurer sent a back-dated letter postponing the proposal by six months, refunded the premium to the loan account and declined cover. The State and National Commissions dismissed the family's complaint.

The question

Can an insurer avoid liability by postponing or declining a proposal after it learns of the proposer's death, when it has held the premium and the medical tests were clear?

What the court held

  • No. The complaint was allowed, and the insurer was directed to process and pay the claim.
  • Silence alone does not amount to acceptance of a proposal. But where the premium has been received, the medical examination is complete and nothing adverse is communicated, the insurer's conduct can show acceptance.
  • Sending a back-dated postponement letter after learning of the death, on a medical ground the test report did not support, and unilaterally refunding the premium was not bona fide and was a deficiency in service.

What it means in practice

  • Lenders that bundle credit-life cover with home loans, and the insurers they act for, should make sure the proposal is decided and communicated quickly once premium is debited. A borrower who has paid and cleared the medicals is likely to be treated as covered.
  • For a bank or HFC acting as corporate agent, the gap between debiting premium and issue of the policy is a conduct risk that needs a clear process.
