State Bank of India v. Pallabh Bhowmick

Unauthorised electronic transactions: proof of negligence

Open in Key judgmentsJudgment text ↗

Case details

Court
Gauhati High Court
Date
13 Sep 2024
Case no.
Writ Appeal No. 364 of 2022
Bench
Lanusungkum Jamir and Kardak Ete JJ.
Statutes
RBI circular on Customer Protection - Limiting Liability of Customers in Unauthorised Electronic Banking Transactions, 6 July 2017
Status
The Supreme Court dismissed the bank's special leave petition on 3 January 2025.

The facts

After a data breach at an online retailer, a fraudster posing as its customer care persuaded a customer to install a mobile application. Rs 94,204.80 was then taken from his bank account in October 2021. He reported it within a day. The bank refused a refund, alleging that he must have shared his one-time passwords, and the Banking Ombudsman closed his complaint. A single judge directed the bank to refund the amount.

The question

Is a customer who is tricked into installing an application negligent, so that the bank need not refund the loss?

What the court held

  • The Division Bench upheld the direction to refund. The bank has to prove the customer's negligence with cogent material; a general allegation that he must have shared credentials is not enough.
  • The loss arose from a third-party breach and was reported within the time allowed by the Reserve Bank's 2017 circular, so the customer's liability was zero. The bank had also not raised a chargeback.

What it means in practice

  • Read with the Delhi High Court's ruling in Hare Ram Singh, the position is that prompt reporting shifts the loss to the bank unless it can prove the customer's negligence. An Ombudsman closure does not prevent the High Court from granting relief.
