Health claim rejected for non-disclosure at portability (knee replacement)

Non-disclosure at portability unrelated to the claim

An Ombudsman award binds the insurer in that complaint only. It is not a precedent, but it shows how such complaints are decided. The source is the summary of the award published by the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen.

The facts

A family had been continuously insured since 2000 and moved to a new general insurer under portability in 2015. A claim of about Rs 2.06 lakh for the wife's knee replacement was rejected because her ten-year history of hypertension had not been mentioned in the proposal form at portability.

The question

Can a ported health policy claim be rejected for non-disclosure of a condition that has no connection with the treatment claimed?

What the Ombudsman decided

  • The complaint was allowed and the insurer was directed to pay the claim in full.
  • There was no nexus between hypertension and the knee replacement, and the benefits accrued over fifteen years under the earlier policies were available to the insured after portability.

What it means in practice

  • Health insurers should not reject a claim on a non-disclosure that is unrelated to the illness treated, least of all on a ported policy that carries continuity benefits.
