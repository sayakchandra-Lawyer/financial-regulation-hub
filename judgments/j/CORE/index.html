Central Organisation for Railway Electrification v. ECI SPIC SMO MCML (JV)

Unilateral appointment of arbitrators

The question

Is a clause valid if it lets one party appoint a sole arbitrator, or makes the other side choose from a panel curated by that party?

What the court held

  • Equal treatment of parties applies at every stage of arbitration, including the appointment of arbitrators.
  • A clause that lets one party unilaterally appoint a sole arbitrator gives rise to justifiable doubts about the arbitrator's independence and impartiality, and hinders the other party's equal participation. For a three-member tribunal, forcing the other party to pick its arbitrator from a panel curated by one side breaches equal treatment.
  • A party can still expressly waive the objection in writing after disputes arise (proviso to section 12(5)). The Court said the law it laid down applies prospectively to appointments made after 8 November 2024, and that this direction applies to three-member tribunals.

What it means in practice

  • Loan agreements that let the lender alone name the arbitrator are unsafe. Lenders should agree on the arbitrator with the borrower, use an arbitral institution, or apply to the court under section 11.
  • Awards by an arbitrator the lender appointed alone, without an express written waiver by the borrower after the dispute arose, are at risk of being set aside. Bhadra International (2026) confirms that such a sole arbitrator is ineligible.
