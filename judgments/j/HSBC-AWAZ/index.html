Credit card interest rates

Banking Regulation Act, 1949, s. 21A; Consumer Protection Act, 1986

The question

Could the National Consumer Commission cap credit card interest at 30% a year as an unfair trade practice?

What the court held

  • The National Commission's ruling that interest above 30% a year is usurious was set aside. It was contrary to section 21A of the Banking Regulation Act, which bars courts and tribunals from reopening a bank transaction on the ground that the interest is excessive, and it encroached on RBI's domain.
  • Directions and guidelines issued by RBI are an extension of the statute. Interest charged as RBI's circulars allow is not an unfair trade practice in itself.
  • The complaint was not maintainable: the trusts that filed it were not 'persons' or consumers, and no permission to sue in a representative capacity had been obtained.

What it means in practice

  • Interest on credit cards and loans is set by the lender's Board-approved policy within RBI's framework, not by consumer forums. Transparency rules (KFS, APR and disclosure of charges) still apply.
  • The bar in section 21A is written for banking companies, so NBFCs should rely on the RBI framework and fair-dealing rules rather than on section 21A itself.
