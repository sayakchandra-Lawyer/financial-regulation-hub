United India Insurance Co Ltd v. Jai Parkash Tayal

Exclusion of genetic disorders

Open in Key judgmentsJudgment text ↗

Case details

Court
Delhi High Court
Date
26 Feb 2018
Case no.
RFA 610/2016
Bench
Prathiba M. Singh J.
Statutes
Constitution of India, Arts. 14, 21; Insurance Act, 1938
Status
On 27 August 2018 the Supreme Court stayed the High Court's general conclusions and directions (including the findings under Articles 14 and 21 and the direction to the regulator), while leaving the payment to the policyholder undisturbed. The insurer's appeal is pending.

The facts

A policyholder with a heart condition (hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy) was refused a mediclaim payout under an exclusion for 'genetic disorders' that the insurer had added to the policy at a renewal. The trial court decreed his suit, and the insurer appealed.

The question

Is a blanket exclusion of 'genetic disorders' in a health insurance policy valid?

What the court held

  • A broad exclusion of all genetic disorders is too wide, ambiguous and discriminatory. It violates Article 14, and it is contrary to public policy when used to deny claims.
  • The right to avail of health insurance is part of the right to health and healthcare under Article 21.
  • The insurance regulator was asked to look again at exclusion clauses so that claims are not rejected on the basis of genetic disorders. The decree for Rs 5 lakh with interest was upheld.

What it means in practice

  • The regulator afterwards told insurers not to reject claims on the basis of a 'genetic disorder' exclusion and standardised the permitted exclusions. Insurers should follow those regulatory requirements; the High Court's wider constitutional conclusions are stayed.
  • Exclusions added at renewal without clear notice to the policyholder are open to challenge.
