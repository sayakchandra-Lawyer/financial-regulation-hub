IDFC Bank Ltd v. Ruchi Soya Industries Ltd

Lone lender's winding-up petition while JLF restructures

Open in Key judgmentsJudgment text ↗

Case details

Court
Bombay High Court
Date
14 Feb 2017
Case no.
Company Petition No. 570 of 2016
Bench
R.D. Dhanuka J.
Statutes
Companies Act, 1956, ss. 433, 434; RBI Framework for Revitalising Distressed Assets, 2014
Status
Insolvency petitions are now under the IBC, where a lender's right to file does not depend on the majority's wishes.

The facts

IDFC Bank, holding about 1% of Ruchi Soya's debt, filed a winding-up petition. The other lenders, holding about 98% of the debt, were working on a revival plan through a joint lenders' forum under RBI's framework.

The question

Should a winding-up petition by a small lender be admitted while the joint lenders' forum is pursuing revival?

What the court held

  • The petition was dismissed. Winding up is discretionary, and the court considered the wishes of the overwhelming majority of lenders who were pursuing revival under RBI's framework.

What it means in practice

  • Winding up has since been replaced by the IBC for insolvency, where any financial creditor may file on default. Ruchi Soya itself went through the IBC later.
