Pledge of demat shares

Statutes
Indian Contract Act, 1872, ss. 172, 176, 177; Depositories Act, 1996; SEBI (Depositories and Participants) Regulations, 1996, reg. 58

The question

When a lender invokes a pledge of dematerialised shares and is recorded as beneficial owner, has it 'sold' them and must it credit their value against the debt?

What the court held

  • The Depositories Act and its regulations add to, and do not displace, the pledge provisions of the Contract Act.
  • Recording the pledgee as beneficial owner on invocation is not a sale; it only enables the pledgee to sell. The pledge is not discharged, and the pledgee need not account for the shares until an actual sale.
  • The notice under section 176 before sale is mandatory and parties cannot contract out of it. The pledgor may redeem under section 177 until the actual sale. A pledgee cannot sell to itself.

What it means in practice

  • A lender invoking a share pledge must still give reasonable notice and sell to a third party, and should credit the borrower only with the actual sale proceeds. Until then the borrower can redeem.
