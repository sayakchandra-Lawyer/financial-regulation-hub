Sanjabij Tari v. Kishore S. Borcar

Cheque bounce: presumptions and trial directions

The question

How should trial courts apply the statutory presumptions in cheque-dishonour cases, and how should the backlog be handled?

What the court held

  • Courts must give effect to the presumptions in sections 118 and 139 of the NI Act. Treating a section 138 case as a civil recovery suit and asking the complainant to prove the underlying debt is contrary to what Parliament intended.
  • The Court issued directions for all section 138 cases: summons may also be served by the complainant ('dasti') and electronically, with an affidavit of service; district courts are to set up online payment by QR code or UPI so that the accused can pay and the case be compounded or closed; every complaint must carry a synopsis in a set format; and no pre-cognizance summons under section 223 of the BNSS is needed.
  • Trial courts must record reasons before converting a summary trial into a summons trial.

What it means in practice

  • Lenders filing section 138 complaints should use the synopsis format and the faster service routes, and can expect earlier settlement through online payment.
  • The burden is on the drawer to rebut the presumption that the cheque was for a debt or liability.
