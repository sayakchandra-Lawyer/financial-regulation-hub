Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code upheld

The question

Is the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code constitutional, in particular its different treatment of financial and operational creditors?

What the court held

  • The Code is constitutionally valid.
  • Financial creditors are different from operational creditors: they are best placed to assess the viability of the corporate debtor and the resolution plan. Giving only them a vote in the Committee of Creditors is not discriminatory.
  • A financial creditor must prove a 'default', while an operational creditor only claims a debt. The different triggers in sections 7 and 9 are therefore justified.

What it means in practice

  • Financial creditors, including NBFCs and HFCs, vote in the Committee of Creditors and so have a strong say in the outcome. Section 7 is a route to collective resolution of a defaulting company, not a debt collection tool, though lenders often recover through it.
