Unregistered mortgage merged in a decree

The facts

Indian Bank obtained a mortgage decree (giving time to pay before sale) against a company. The mortgage had not been registered with the Registrar of Companies. When the company was wound up, the official liquidator asked the court to declare the decree void.

The question

Is a mortgage decree void against the liquidator because the underlying charge was not registered?

What the court held

  • It depends on the decree. If the decree keeps the unregistered charge alive, section 125 applies and it is void against the liquidator. If the time to pay under the decree has passed and what operates is an order for sale, the matter has passed from contract to judgment and the decree stands.
  • On the facts the time to pay had expired before the liquidator applied, so the decree was valid.

What it means in practice

  • A decree may protect a lender whose charge was not registered, but registration is the only safe course.
