Puneet Kaur v. K V Developers Pvt Ltd

Homebuyers who did not file claims

Case details

Court
National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, New Delhi
Date
1 Jun 2022
Case no.
Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) Nos. 390-394 of 2022
Bench
Ashok Bhushan J. (Chairperson), Shreesha Merla (Member, Technical) and Naresh Salecha (Member, Technical)
Statutes
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, ss. 5(8)(f), 29, 30, 31; IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process for Corporate Persons) Regulations, 2016, regs. 12, 13, 36
Status
The ruling is about homebuyers shown in the developer's own records. For other creditors the Supreme Court has refused a belated claim made after the committee approved the plan, even though the NCLT's approval was pending (RPS Infrastructure Ltd v. Mukul Kumar, 2023 INSC 816).

The facts

In the insolvency of a real estate developer, several homebuyers filed their claims after the resolution plan had been approved by the committee of creditors and was awaiting the NCLT's approval. The resolution professional and the NCLT refused to take the late claims, and it was argued that the claims stood extinguished.

The question

What happens to homebuyers whose payments and allotments are in the developer's own records but who did not file a claim in time?

What the tribunal held

  • Until the NCLT approves the plan, the claims are not extinguished. That does not give late claimants a right to have their claims admitted.
  • The resolution professional is not bound to put late claimants in the list of creditors. But the developer's records show every allottee, the amounts paid and the units allotted, and that liability should be reflected in the information memorandum so that the resolution applicant deals with it.
  • Leaving such homebuyers out is inequitable. The resolution professional was directed to give their details to the resolution applicant, who was to prepare an addendum to the plan for the committee and the NCLT to consider.

What it means in practice

  • Lenders on the committee of creditors of a developer, and bidders, should expect the plan to provide for all allottees shown in the books and not only those who filed claims. That affects the amount available for financial creditors.
  • Home loan lenders can point to this ruling where a borrower's allotment appears in the developer's records but no claim was filed.
