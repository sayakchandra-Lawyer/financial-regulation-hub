Alka Chandewar v. Shamshul Ishrar Khan

Enforcing an arbitral tribunal's interim orders

The facts

A party ignored interim orders made by an arbitral tribunal under section 17 of the Arbitration Act. The tribunal referred the default to the court under section 27(5), but the Bombay High Court held that it had no power to punish for such disobedience.

The question

Can disobedience of an arbitral tribunal's interim order be punished as contempt?

What the court held

  • Yes. Under section 27(5), a person who fails to comply with an arbitral tribunal's interim order is guilty of contempt of the tribunal, and the tribunal may make a representation to the court, which can punish as for contempt of court.
  • From 23 October 2015 section 17(2) also makes the tribunal's interim orders enforceable as if they were orders of the court.

What it means in practice

  • Lenders that get interim relief from an arbitral tribunal (for example, to secure a vehicle or deposit funds) can enforce it through the court.
