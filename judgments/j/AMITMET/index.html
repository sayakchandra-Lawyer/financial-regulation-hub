India Resurgence ARC Pvt Ltd v. Amit Metaliks Ltd

Dissenting secured creditor's payout under a plan

Status
The minimum payout was re-stated in 2026: under the new section 30(2)(ba), dissenting financial creditors must get at least the lower of their liquidation entitlement under section 53 and what they would get if the plan amount were distributed in the section 53 order. It does not apply where, before the amendment, the committee of creditors had already approved a plan or liquidation had been ordered or resolved on. Separately, in DBS Bank Ltd v. Ruchi Soya Industries Ltd (2024 INSC 14) a two-judge Bench doubted this ruling's reasoning on the dissenting secured creditor's minimum entitlement and referred the question to a larger Bench; that reference does not overrule it.

The facts

An asset reconstruction company held security over the corporate debtor's assets which it valued far above what the resolution plan offered it. The plan was approved by about 95 per cent of the committee of creditors. The ARC voted against it and argued that the committee had to take the value of each creditor's security into account.

The question

Can a dissenting secured financial creditor challenge an approved resolution plan on the ground that it gets less than the value of its security?

What the court held

  • No. How the plan money is shared among creditors is a matter for the commercial wisdom of the committee of creditors. A dissenting secured creditor cannot insist on being paid by reference to the value of its security.
  • What the Code assures a dissenting financial creditor is the minimum amount set out in section 30(2)(b). It cannot enforce its security interest outside the plan.
  • Courts review an approved plan only on the limited grounds in sections 30(2) and 61(3).

What it means in practice

  • A secured lender in the minority is bound by the majority's distribution. Its protection is the statutory minimum for dissenting creditors, so its vote and the negotiation inside the committee matter more than a later challenge.
