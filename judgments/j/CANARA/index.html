Canara Bank v. United India Insurance Co Ltd

Insurance of hypothecated goods: bank's and insurer's duties

Statutes
Consumer Protection Act, 1986, s. 2(1)(d); Insurance Act, 1938

The facts

Farmers stored their produce in a cold store and took bank loans against it. The cold store had taken a fire policy covering the stock, at the bank's insistence. A fire destroyed the stock. The insurer rejected the claim, alleging that the fire was not accidental and that the farmers were strangers to the policy; the bank kept charging interest on the loans.

The question

Can persons for whose benefit a policy is taken claim under it as consumers, and what is the position of the bank that required the insurance?

What the court held

  • A beneficiary of a policy is a consumer even though he is not a party to it. The farmers could claim directly against the insurer.
  • An insurer relying on an exclusion must prove it. Where the fire was not caused by the insured, the reason for the fire does not defeat the claim.
  • A bank that insists on insurance of hypothecated goods and is in touch with the insurer has a duty to see that the cover is in place and the insurer is informed. Because of its own deficiency the bank could recover only simple interest at 12 per cent a year and not the contractual rate.

What it means in practice

  • Lenders that require borrowers to insure the security should track that the policy is issued, names the right interests and is renewed. A lapse can cost the lender its contractual interest.
