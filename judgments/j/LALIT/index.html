Lalit Kumar Jain v. Union of India

Personal guarantors after a resolution plan

From 26 May 2026 the interim moratorium on filing an application no longer applies to personal guarantors of corporate debtors (IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026).

The facts

By a notification of 15 November 2019 the Central Government brought the personal insolvency provisions of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code into force only for personal guarantors to corporate debtors. Promoters and directors who had guaranteed company loans challenged the notification in several High Courts, and the cases were transferred to the Supreme Court.

The question

Could the Government bring Part III of the Code into force only for personal guarantors to corporate debtors, and does approval of the company's resolution plan release the guarantor?

What the court held

  • The notification is valid. The Code itself treats personal guarantors to corporate debtors as a separate class, and bringing the provisions into force for that class alone is not excessive delegation.
  • Approval of a resolution plan for the corporate debtor does not by itself discharge the personal guarantor. A discharge of the principal borrower by operation of law, such as in insolvency, does not release the surety.
  • The National Company Law Tribunal dealing with the company's insolvency is also the forum for proceedings against its personal guarantor (section 60(2)).

What it means in practice

  • A lender can pursue the promoter's personal guarantee for the unpaid balance even after the borrower company's resolution plan is approved, and can start insolvency resolution against the guarantor before the NCLT.
  • Whether the guarantee survives still depends on its terms and on the plan: a plan may itself deal with guarantees.
