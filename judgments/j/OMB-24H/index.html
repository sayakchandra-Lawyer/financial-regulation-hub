Eye injection treatment refused as under 24 hours and 'out-patient'

An Ombudsman award binds the insurer in that complaint only. It is not a precedent, but it shows how such complaints are decided. The source is the summary of the award published by the Council for Insurance Ombudsmen.

The facts

An insured under a family floater policy had two intravitreal injection procedures for a retinal condition, costing Rs 50,225 in all. The insurer refused both claims, saying the hospital stay was under 24 hours, the treatment could have been taken as an out-patient and it was 'unproven'.

The question

Can a claim be refused for a stay of less than 24 hours where the procedure is done in an operation theatre by a specialist?

What the Ombudsman decided

  • No. Procedures that once needed longer admission are now done in a few hours because of advances in technology. Eye surgery was covered by the policy's day-care clause with no 24-hour condition.
  • The treatment needed an operation theatre and a specialist and was not an ordinary out-patient treatment. The insurer did not prove that it was unproven. The claim was allowed, less the policy's 10 per cent co-payment.

What it means in practice

  • Insurers should test short-stay claims against the day-care clause and the nature of the procedure, and should state one clear ground of rejection. A different award in the same compilation upheld rejection where the procedure was not in that policy's day-care list, so the wording of the policy decides the outcome.
