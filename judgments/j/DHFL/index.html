Piramal Capital and Housing Finance Ltd v. 63 Moons Technologies Ltd

Resolution of a housing finance company under the IBC

Status
From 26 May 2026 the substituted section 26 expressly provides that avoidance and fraudulent or wrongful trading applications do not affect the insolvency process and continue after it ends.

The facts

The Reserve Bank superseded the board of a large housing finance company and, as the first use of the special insolvency route for financial service providers, started its corporate insolvency. The approved resolution plan gave the benefit of any recoveries from fraudulent trading applications (section 66) to the resolution applicant, valuing them at a notional Re 1. A debenture holder objected, and the NCLAT sent that term back to the creditors. Fixed deposit holders demanded full repayment, and former promoters and directors sought to take part in the process.

The question

Could the appellate tribunal reopen the plan's treatment of section 66 recoveries, are deposit holders entitled to full repayment, and can directors of a superseded board take part in the committee of creditors?

What the court held

  • The plan was restored. How recoveries are dealt with is a commercial decision of the committee of creditors, and the NCLAT could not substitute its own view.
  • Avoidance applications (preferential, undervalued and extortionate transactions) are different from fraudulent and wrongful trading applications under section 66. The plan could assign section 66 recoveries to the resolution applicant, while the NCLT still decides those applications.
  • A vote cast by the authorised representative for a class of creditors binds every member of that class, including those who voted against.
  • Fixed deposit holders cannot claim full repayment by relying on section 45QA of the RBI Act or section 36A of the National Housing Bank Act. Once the plan is approved, they are bound by it.
  • Directors of a board superseded by the Reserve Bank under section 45-IE are not 'suspended directors'. They cannot attend committee meetings or demand copies of the plan.

What it means in practice

  • For lenders and bidders, the ruling confirms that the special route for NBFCs and HFCs works like an ordinary insolvency once it starts, with the Reserve Bank's administrator in charge and the old board out.
  • Depositors and debenture holders of a failed NBFC or HFC are creditors under the plan and take what the plan gives their class.
