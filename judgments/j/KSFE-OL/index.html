Kerala State Financial Enterprises Ltd v. Official Liquidator, High Court of Kerala

Unregistered charge and attachment in winding up

Companies Act, 1956, ss. 125, 446, 537; Companies Act, 2013, s. 77
Status
Decided under the Companies Act, 1956; the registration rule is now in section 77 of the 2013 Act.

The facts

A government-owned chit company lent to a company and attached its properties under the Kerala Revenue Recovery Act. Its charge was never registered with the Registrar of Companies. When the borrower was wound up, the chit company asked for leave to continue the sale.

The question

Does a lender with an unregistered charge, or with an attachment under a revenue recovery law, rank as a secured creditor in a winding up?

What the court held

  • A charge not registered under section 125 of the Companies Act, 1956 is void against the liquidator and creditors.
  • An attachment does not create a charge. A government company's dues are not government dues, so the lender was an unsecured creditor and the Companies Act prevailed.

What it means in practice

  • Registering every charge with the Registrar (now section 77 of the Companies Act, 2013) remains essential. See the NCLAT's rulings in Volkswagen Finance and Home Kraft for how this plays out under the IBC.
