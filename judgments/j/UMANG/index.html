Flat Buyers Association Winter Hills-77, Gurgaon v. Umang Realtech Pvt Ltd

Project-wise insolvency of a real estate company

Open in Key judgmentsJudgment text ↗

Case details

Court
National Company Law Appellate Tribunal, New Delhi
Date
4 Feb 2020
Case no.
Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 926 of 2019
Bench
S.J. Mukhopadhaya J. (Chairperson) and Bansi Lal Bhat J. (Member, Judicial)
Statutes
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, ss. 5(8)(f), 7, 25, 30
Status
This is a tribunal ruling built on the facts of one project. Tribunals have followed the project-wise approach in later cases, but it is not laid down in the Code.

The facts

Two allottees had a real estate developer admitted into insolvency over one housing project. The association of flat buyers of that project appealed, pointing out that an ordinary resolution process would stall construction and that the company had other, separate projects. One of the promoters offered to fund completion of the project.

The question

How should the insolvency of a real estate developer be run where the default relates to one project and the allottees want their homes?

What the tribunal held

  • Insolvency of a real estate company started by allottees or lenders of one project is confined to that project. It does not extend to the company's other projects, and creditors of other projects cannot file claims in it.
  • Allottees are financial creditors whose claim is met by delivery of the flat. A secured lender such as a bank or financial institution cannot be given the flats in preference to the allottees for whom the project was approved.
  • The Tribunal allowed a 'reverse' process: the promoter funds completion from outside as a lender, under the supervision of the resolution professional, and banks and financial institutions are paid alongside. If the promoter fails, the normal process resumes.

What it means in practice

  • Banks, HFCs and NBFCs financing a housing project should expect the tribunal to keep the project going and to protect delivery to allottees; the lender's recovery comes from project cash flows and unsold stock under the resolution professional's control.
  • Project-wise accounts and security matter: a lender's exposure to one project may be dealt with separately from the developer's other projects.
