Om Prakash Ahuja v. Reliance General Insurance Co Ltd

Refusing renewal for an immaterial non-disclosure

The facts

A policyholder claimed the cost of his wife's cancer treatment under a health policy first taken in 2007. The insurer rejected the claims, and later refused to renew the policy, because the proposal had not mentioned her rheumatic heart disease. The consumer fora ordered payment of the claims for the first two years, and the insurer paid. The National Commission, however, set aside the direction to renew and rejected the claims for the later period.

The question

Can a health insurer refuse renewal, and reject later claims, on a non-disclosure that has already been held not to justify rejecting the earlier claims?

What the court held

  • No. The non-disclosed condition had no connection with the illness treated, and the insurer had accepted the finding that it was not a ground to reject the earlier claims. It could not use the same ground to refuse renewal.
  • The insurer had taken, and not refunded, premium for the later years. With a valid policy in place, the treatment expenses for that period were payable. The directions to renew were restored.

What it means in practice

  • A non-disclosure matters only if it is material to the risk. An insurer that loses on that point for a claim cannot fall back on it to refuse renewal or deny later claims.
  • Health insurers should not decline renewal on underwriting grounds that the policy and the regulator's renewal rules do not allow. Renewal can be refused only on limited grounds such as established fraud.
