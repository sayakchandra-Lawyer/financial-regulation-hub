Related party transactions (s. 188)

Companies Act, 2013, s. 188: related party transactions

Applies to
Companies entering into contracts or arrangements with a related party for sale, purchase or supply of goods, property transactions, leasing, services, appointment of agents, office or place of profit, or underwriting (s. 188(1)(a) to (g))
Does not apply to
Transactions in the ordinary course of business on an arm's length basis (s. 188(1) proviso); Loans, guarantees and security to related parties are governed by ss. 185 and 186 rather than the s. 188(1) list; Transactions between a holding company and its wholly owned subsidiary whose accounts are consolidated and placed before the shareholders do not need the shareholder resolution; Board consent is still needed (s. 188(1) proviso)

Key concepts

  • Board consent by resolution at a meeting (s. 188(1))
  • Prior shareholder resolution where the rule 15(3) thresholds are crossed, for example sale, purchase or supply of goods amounting to 10% or more of turnover, or property transactions amounting to 10% or more of net worth
  • A member who is a related party cannot vote on the resolution approving that transaction (s. 188(1) second proviso), except in a company where 90% or more of the members, in number, are relatives of promoters or related parties (third proviso)
  • Audit Committee approval and omnibus approval under s. 177 and rule 6A, for companies that must have an Audit Committee

Key obligations

  • Obtain Board consent at a meeting; interested directors stay out of the discussion (rule 15(2))
  • Obtain prior approval of the company by resolution where the rule 15(3) thresholds apply
  • Disclose the contracts or arrangements in the Board's report with justification (s. 188(2)) and record them in the register under s. 189 (Form MBP-4)

If you breach it

  • s. 188(3): a contract entered into without the required consent and not ratified within three months is voidable at the option of the Board or the shareholders; a director who authorised it indemnifies the company for any loss
  • s. 188(5): any director or employee who entered into or authorised the contract in contravention: for a listed company, penalty Rs 25 lakh; for any other company, penalty Rs 5 lakh

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
