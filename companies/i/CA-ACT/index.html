Key concepts

  • Charge: an interest or lien created on the property or assets of a company or any of its undertakings or both as security, and includes a mortgage (s. 2(16))
  • Free reserves: reserves available for distribution as dividend as per the latest audited balance sheet, excluding unrealised or notional gains, revaluation, and fair-value changes recognised in equity (s. 2(43))
  • Related party: includes a director or KMP or their relative, a firm in which a director, manager or relative is a partner, a private company in which a director, manager or relative is a member or director, a public company in which a director or manager is a director and holds with relatives more than 2% of paid-up capital, and holding, subsidiary and associate companies (s. 2(76))
  • Relative: members of a Hindu Undivided Family, spouses, and persons related as prescribed (s. 2(77) read with the definitions rules)
  • Small company: a company other than a public company whose paid-up capital does not exceed Rs 10 crore and turnover does not exceed Rs 100 crore (s. 2(85) read with rule 2(1)(t), as substituted on 01-12-2025); a holding or subsidiary company, a Section 8 company and a company governed by a special Act cannot be a small company
  • s. 462: the Central Government may direct that any provision of the Act does not apply, or applies with exceptions, modifications or adaptations, to a class of companies; the exemption notifications for private, Government, Section 8, Nidhi and IFSC companies are made under it

Key obligations

  • Read the s. 185, 186 and 180 limits with these definitions: free reserves and securities premium set the s. 180(1)(c) borrowing limit and the s. 186(2) lending limit
  • Check whether a s. 462 exemption notification modifies a section before relying on it for a private, Government or IFSC company

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
