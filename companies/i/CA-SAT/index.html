Applies to
Companies whose registered charges are repaid or satisfied; Charge-holders; Persons who obtain an order appointing, or themselves appoint, a receiver or manager of charged property

Key concepts

  • Memorandum of satisfaction entered by the Registrar (Form CHG-5)
  • Registrar's own entry of satisfaction or release on evidence (s. 83)
  • Notice of appointment and cessation of a receiver or manager (s. 84, Form CHG-6)
  • Company's register of charges (s. 85, Form CHG-7)
  • Rectification and extension of time by the Central Government (s. 87, Form CHG-8)

Key obligations

  • s. 82(1): intimate payment or satisfaction in full of a registered charge to the Registrar within 30 days (Form CHG-4); the Registrar may allow intimation within 300 days on payment of additional fees, on an application by the company or the charge-holder
  • s. 82(2): the Registrar gives the charge-holder up to 14 days to show cause; no notice is needed if the intimation is in the specified form and signed by the charge-holder; if no cause is shown, the Registrar enters a memorandum of satisfaction
  • s. 83: the Registrar may enter satisfaction or release on evidence even without the company's intimation, and must inform the affected parties within 30 days
  • s. 84: a person who obtains an order appointing, or appoints, a receiver or manager of charged property gives notice to the company and the Registrar within 30 days, and again on ceasing to act
  • s. 85: the register of charges and instruments are open to members and creditors free of charge, and to others for a fee
  • s. 87: the Central Government may, on being satisfied that the omission or misstatement was accidental, inadvertent or for other sufficient cause, or does not prejudice creditors or shareholders, extend the time for intimating satisfaction or rectify an omission or misstatement in any earlier charge filing

Deadlines and cycles

  • 30 days from payment or satisfaction to file CHG-4, extendable by the Registrar to 300 days with additional fees (s. 82(1))
  • Up to 14 days for the charge-holder to show cause (s. 82(2))
  • 30 days to notify appointment of a receiver or manager (s. 84(1))
  • Registrar informs affected parties within 30 days of a s. 83 entry

If you breach it

  • s. 86(1): company Rs 5 lakh and every officer in default Rs 50,000 for any default under Chapter VI
  • s. 86(2): false or incorrect information, or suppression of material information, required to be registered under s. 77 attracts s. 447

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
