Applies to
Every company creating, modifying or satisfying a charge, and every charge-holder
Does not apply to
Rule 3 does not apply to a charge that a banking company must create in favour of RBI for a loan or advance under s. 17(4)(d) of the RBI Act, 1934 (rule 3(5), inserted on 27-04-2022)

Key concepts

  • CHG-1: creation or modification of a charge (other than for debentures)
  • CHG-9: charge for debentures, including rectification
  • CHG-2 and CHG-3: Registrar's certificates of registration and modification, which are conclusive evidence of compliance (rule 6(3))
  • CHG-4: satisfaction of charge; CHG-5: certificate of satisfaction
  • CHG-6: appointment or cessation of a receiver or manager
  • CHG-7: the company's own register of charges
  • CHG-8: application to the Central Government for rectification or extension of time for satisfaction
  • The copy of the instrument is certified by a director, company secretary or authorised officer of the charge-holder (rule 3(4))

Key obligations

  • Rule 3(1): file CHG-1 or CHG-9, signed by the company and the charge-holder, with a copy of the instrument, within 30 days of creation or modification
  • Rule 3(2) and rule 4: after 30 days, file within the s. 77 periods with additional or ad valorem fees; a delayed filing needs a declaration by the company secretary or a director that it will not adversely affect intervening creditors
  • Rule 3(3): where the charge-holder registers the charge, it may recover the fees from the company
  • Rule 8: intimate satisfaction in CHG-4 within 300 days of payment or satisfaction in full
  • Rule 10: keep the register of charges in CHG-7 at the registered office, make entries forthwith, preserve it permanently, and keep each instrument for eight years after satisfaction
  • Rule 12: apply to the Central Government in CHG-8 for rectification of an omission or misstatement, or for more time to file satisfaction after 300 days
  • Rule 13: for a company in insolvency resolution or liquidation, CHG-1, CHG-4, CHG-8 and CHG-9 are signed by the resolution professional or liquidator

Deadlines and cycles

  • 30 days for CHG-1 or CHG-9 (rule 3(1))
  • Up to 60 days with additional fees, and a further 60 days with ad valorem fees (s. 77 provisos, rules 3(2) and 4)
  • 300 days for CHG-4 (rule 8)

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
