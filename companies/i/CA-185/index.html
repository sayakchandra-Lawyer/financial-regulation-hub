Loans to directors (s. 185)

Companies Act, 2013, s. 185: loans, guarantees and security to directors and persons in whom directors are interested (as substituted from 07-05-2018)

Applies to
Every company advancing a loan (including a book debt), or giving a guarantee or security for a loan, to or for a director or a person in whom a director is interested; The director or other person who receives the loan, guarantee or security (s. 185(4)(iii))
Does not apply to
A company that, in the ordinary course of its business, provides loans or gives guarantees or security for repayment of loans and charges interest at not less than the yield of the 1, 3, 5 or 10-year Government security closest to the tenor (s. 185(3)(b)): this covers lending NBFCs and HFCs; Loans by a holding company to its wholly owned subsidiary, and guarantees or security by a holding company for loans made to its wholly owned subsidiary (s. 185(3)(c)), and guarantees or security by a holding company for loans by a bank or financial institution to any subsidiary (s. 185(3)(d)), if the subsidiary uses the loan for its principal business; Loans to a managing or whole-time director under conditions of service extended to all employees or a scheme approved by special resolution (s. 185(3)(a)); A private company in whose share capital no other body corporate has invested money, whose borrowings from banks, financial institutions or bodies corporate are below twice its paid-up share capital or Rs 50 crore (whichever is lower), and which has no subsisting default on those borrowings (G.S.R. 464(E), 05-06-2015, entry 14), provided it is not in default in filing its financial statements or annual return (para 2A inserted by G.S.R. 583(E), 13-06-2017)

Key concepts

  • Absolute bar (s. 185(1)): a director of the company or of its holding company, any partner or relative of such a director, and any firm in which such a director or relative is a partner
  • Conditional route (s. 185(2)): any person in whom a director is interested may receive a loan, guarantee or security with a special resolution, if the borrower uses it for its principal business
  • 'Person in whom a director is interested': a private company of which the director is a director or member; a body corporate where the director (or two or more directors together) controls at least 25% of voting power; a body corporate whose Board or managers are accustomed to act on the lending company's Board or directors' instructions (Explanation to s. 185(2))
  • 'Directly or indirectly': structures routed through others are caught

Key obligations

  • s. 185(1): do not lend to, or give a guarantee or security for a loan taken by, a director, a director of the holding company, their partners or relatives, or a firm in which any of them is a partner
  • s. 185(2)(a): for a person in whom a director is interested, pass a special resolution; the explanatory statement must give full particulars of the loan, guarantee or security, the purpose and any other relevant fact
  • s. 185(2)(b): the borrower must use the loan for its principal business activities
  • Board approval at a meeting is also needed (s. 179(3)(f)), and the s. 186 limits and conditions apply where the borrower is a body corporate or other person

Deadlines and cycles

  • MGT-14 for the special resolution within 30 days (s. 117)

If you breach it

  • s. 185(4)(i): company fine Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 lakh
  • s. 185(4)(ii): every officer in default imprisonment up to 6 months or fine Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 lakh
  • s. 185(4)(iii): the director or other person who received the loan, guarantee or security: imprisonment up to 6 months or fine Rs 5 lakh to Rs 25 lakh, or both

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
