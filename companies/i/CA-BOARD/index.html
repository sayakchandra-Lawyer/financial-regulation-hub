Board and shareholder approvals (ss. 179-180)

Companies Act, 2013: powers of the Board (s. 179) and restrictions needing a special resolution (s. 180)

Applies to
The Board of every company borrowing money, issuing debentures, investing, granting loans or giving guarantees or security; Lenders taking security or relying on the company's borrowing authority
Does not apply to
s. 180 does not apply to private companies (G.S.R. 464(E), 05-06-2015, entry 12), if the private company is not in default in filing its financial statements or annual return (para 2A, G.S.R. 583(E), 13-06-2017); Acceptance of deposits by a banking company in the ordinary course is not 'borrowing' (s. 179(3) second proviso and s. 180(1)(c) proviso)

Key concepts

  • Powers exercisable only by resolution at a Board meeting: issue securities including debentures, borrow money, invest funds, grant loans or give guarantees or security (s. 179(3)(c) to (f))
  • Delegation of (d) to (f) to a committee of directors, the managing director, manager or principal officer, by Board resolution at a meeting (s. 179(3) first proviso)
  • Borrowing limit: paid-up share capital, free reserves and securities premium; temporary loans from the company's bankers are excluded (s. 180(1)(c))
  • Temporary loans: repayable on demand or within six months, such as cash credit, bill discounting and seasonal short-term loans, but not loans for capital expenditure (Explanation to s. 180(1)(c))
  • Sale, lease or other disposal of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking (s. 180(1)(a)), with the 20% tests for 'undertaking' and 'substantially the whole'
  • Remitting or giving time to repay a debt due from a director (s. 180(1)(d))
  • Commencement: s. 180 from 12-09-2013; s. 179 from 01-04-2014

Key obligations

  • s. 179(3)(c) to (f): pass a Board resolution at a meeting (not by circulation) to issue debentures, borrow, invest, or grant loans, guarantees or security; the Board may delegate (d) to (f) by a resolution at a meeting on conditions it sets
  • s. 179(3) Explanation II: for bank facilities, the Board approves the arrangement (overdraft, cash credit or otherwise), not each day-to-day drawing
  • s. 180(1)(c): obtain a special resolution before borrowing if the new borrowing plus outstanding borrowings (excluding temporary loans from bankers) will exceed paid-up share capital, free reserves and securities premium; the resolution must state the total amount up to which the Board may borrow (s. 180(2))
  • s. 180(1)(a): obtain a special resolution to sell, lease or otherwise dispose of the whole or substantially the whole of an undertaking; the resolution may set conditions on use of proceeds (s. 180(4))
  • s. 117(3): file the special resolutions under s. 180, and Board resolutions under s. 179(3), with the Registrar in Form MGT-14 within 30 days (subject to the exemptions in s. 117(3)(g))
  • s. 62(3): a loan or debenture with an option to convert into shares needs its terms approved by a special resolution before the loan is raised or the debentures are issued
  • s. 10A: a company with share capital incorporated after the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2019 may not exercise borrowing powers until a director files the declaration that subscribers have paid for their shares (within 180 days of incorporation)

Deadlines and cycles

  • MGT-14 within 30 days of passing the special resolution or the s. 179(3) Board resolution (s. 117(1))

If you breach it

  • s. 180(5): a debt incurred beyond the s. 180(1)(c) limit is not valid or effectual unless the lender proves it lent in good faith without knowledge that the limit was exceeded
  • s. 180(3): a s. 180(1)(a) disposal does not affect the title of a good-faith buyer or lessee, or sales and leases in the ordinary course of a business of selling or leasing

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
