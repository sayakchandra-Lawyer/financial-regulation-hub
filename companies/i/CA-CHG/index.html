Applies to
Every company creating a charge, within or outside India, on its property, assets or undertakings, tangible or otherwise, in or outside India; Charge-holders (banks, NBFCs, HFCs, debenture trustees and other secured creditors); A company acquiring property already subject to a charge, and any modification of a registered charge (s. 79)
Does not apply to
Charges that the Central Government prescribes, in consultation with RBI, as outside s. 77 (s. 77(1) last proviso): see the 2022 rule exempting charges created by banking companies in favour of RBI

Key concepts

  • Charge: an interest or lien created on the property or assets of a company or any of its undertakings or both as security, including a mortgage (s. 2(16))
  • Joint filing: the particulars are signed by the company and the charge-holder (s. 77(1))
  • Certificate of registration (Form CHG-2) and modification (CHG-3) issued by the Registrar (s. 77(2), rule 6)
  • Unregistered charge is not taken into account by a liquidator (under the Act or the IBC) or any other creditor (s. 77(3)); the debt itself survives (s. 77(4))
  • Constructive notice from the date of registration to anyone acquiring the property (s. 80)
  • Registrar's register of charges is the MCA portal record, open to inspection (s. 81, rule 7)

Key obligations

  • s. 77(1): register the particulars of the charge, signed by the company and the charge-holder, with the instrument creating it, with the Registrar within 30 days of creation (Form CHG-1, or CHG-9 for debentures)
  • s. 77(1) first and second provisos: for charges created on or after 02-11-2018, the Registrar may allow registration within 60 days of creation on payment of additional fees, and on a further application within a further 60 days on payment of ad valorem fees
  • s. 77(1) third proviso: late registration does not prejudice rights acquired in the property before the charge is actually registered
  • s. 78: if the company does not register the charge within 30 days, the charge-holder may apply; the Registrar gives the company 14 days' notice and then registers unless the company registers it or shows sufficient cause; the charge-holder may recover the fees from the company
  • s. 79: s. 77 applies to property acquired subject to a charge and to any modification of the terms, extent or operation of a registered charge
  • s. 85 (read with rule 10): keep the company's own register of charges (Form CHG-7) and a copy of each instrument at the registered office

Deadlines and cycles

  • 30 days from creation or modification to file CHG-1 or CHG-9 (s. 77(1), rule 3)
  • Up to 60 days from creation with additional fees, then a further 60 days with ad valorem fees, on application to the Registrar (s. 77(1) provisos, for charges created on or after 02-11-2018)
  • Registrar's notice to the company on a charge-holder's application: 14 days (s. 78)

If you breach it

  • s. 77(3): an unregistered charge is ignored by the liquidator and other creditors, so the lender ranks as unsecured in liquidation or insolvency
  • s. 86(1): company Rs 5 lakh and every officer in default Rs 50,000 for any default under Chapter VI
  • s. 86(2): wilfully furnishing false or incorrect information, or knowingly suppressing material information, in a charge filing is punishable under s. 447 (fraud)

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
