Applies to
Companies raising money through debentures or private placement, and companies receiving loans or other money that could be a 'deposit'; Debenture trustees and debenture holders
Does not apply to
The deposit rules do not apply to banking companies, RBI-registered NBFCs, NHB-registered HFCs and companies specified under the s. 73(1) proviso (rule 1(3) of the deposit rules); Many receipts are not 'deposits', including loans from banks, public financial institutions, insurance companies and scheduled banks, amounts received from any other company, amounts from a director (and, for a private company, a director's relative) who declares the money is not borrowed, bonds or debentures secured by a first charge (or one ranking pari passu with it) on tangible assets, not exceeding the assets' market value as assessed by a registered valuer, debentures compulsorily convertible within ten years, listed non-convertible debentures that create no charge, and promoter loans brought in at a lender's stipulation (rule 2(1)(c) of the deposit rules)

Key concepts

  • Secured debentures: charge on the company's (or its holding, subsidiary or associate company's) assets sufficient to cover principal and interest; debenture trustee appointed before the offer, and a debenture trust deed executed within 60 days of allotment (s. 71 and rule 18 of the share capital and debentures rules)
  • Debenture redemption reserve: not required for RBI-registered NBFCs and NHB-registered HFCs on privately placed debentures, or for listed companies; 10% for other unlisted companies, plus a 15% investment or deposit of debentures maturing in the next year where rule 18(7) requires it
  • Private placement: special resolution for each offer, except that for non-convertible debentures within the s. 180(1)(c) limit a Board resolution under s. 179(3)(c) is enough, and above that limit one special resolution a year covers all offers (rule 14(1) of the prospectus and allotment rules)
  • Return of deposits and of money received but not treated as deposits: Form DPT-3 by 30 June each year (rule 16 of the deposit rules)

Key obligations

  • s. 71 and rule 18: for secured debentures, create the charge in favour of the debenture trustee and register it with the Registrar in Form CHG-9 within 30 days
  • s. 42 and rule 14: issue the private placement offer letter (PAS-4) to identified persons, not more than 200 in a financial year (excluding QIBs and ESOP allottees), and file the return of allotment (PAS-3) within 15 days of allotment
  • s. 42(4) and (6): take subscription money only through banking channels, not cash; keep it in a separate bank account in a scheduled bank; do not use it until allotment is made and PAS-3 is filed; allot within 60 days of receiving the money, or refund within the next 15 days, failing which interest at 12% a year runs from the 60th day
  • s. 73: accept deposits from members only as permitted by s. 73(2) and the deposit rules; s. 76 applies to eligible public companies accepting public deposits
  • Rule 16: file DPT-3 by 30 June each year with the position as on 31 March, covering deposits and particulars of money received that is not a deposit

Deadlines and cycles

  • CHG-9 within 30 days of creating the charge for debentures (s. 77, rule 3)
  • PAS-3 within 15 days of allotment on private placement (rule 14(6))
  • Debenture trust deed within 60 days of allotment (rule 18(1)(c))
  • DPT-3 by 30 June each year (rule 16)

If you breach it

  • s. 42(9): late PAS-3 makes the company, its promoters and directors liable to a penalty of Rs 1,000 a day, up to Rs 25 lakh
  • s. 42(10): if the company makes an offer or accepts money in contravention of s. 42, the company, its promoters and directors are liable to a penalty up to the amount raised or Rs 2 crore, whichever is lower, and the company must refund the money with interest within 30 days of the order
  • s. 74(3) and s. 76A: penalties for accepting or failing to repay deposits in contravention of Chapter V

General consequences under the statute. Companies Act, 2013: where no specific penalty is provided, the company and every officer in default are liable to a penalty of Rs 10,000 and Rs 1,000 a day for a continuing contravention, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 50,000 for an officer (s. 450). Penalties are imposed by an adjudicating officer (the Registrar) after a hearing, with an appeal to the Regional Director within 60 days; failing to comply with the order within 90 days is an offence (s. 454). One Person Companies, small companies, start-ups and producer companies pay at most half the specified penalty, up to Rs 2 lakh for the company and Rs 1 lakh for an officer (s. 446B). Offences not punishable with imprisonment only, or with imprisonment and fine, may be compounded by the NCLT or, where the maximum fine does not exceed Rs 25 lakh, by the Regional Director (s. 441). A false statement or knowing omission of a material fact in a filing is punishable under s. 447 (s. 448).
