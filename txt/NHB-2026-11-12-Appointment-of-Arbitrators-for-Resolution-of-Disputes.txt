                        Appointment of Arbitrators for Resolution of Disputes

Please refer to the order passed by the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi dated September 30, 2026
in FAO (COMM) 91/2025 & CM APPL. 20542/2025 (Shikha Ghosh v Axis Bank & Anr.) on the
captioned subject (copy enclosed).

2. The Hon'ble High Court of Delhi has examined the issue of unilateral appointment of
arbitrators by a bank in a large number of disputes and expressed serious concerns regarding
the independence and impartiality of arbitrators appointed repeatedly by a single party. It also
referred to the relevant provisions of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, and the judicial
pronouncements governing the neutrality and independence of arbitrators. It also directed the
Reserve Bank of India, being the sectoral regulator, to caution all banks in this regard, so that
any remedial measures required to be undertaken by banks and financial institutions in respect
of unilateral appointments of arbitrators may also be examined and appropriate guidance
issued.

3. Accordingly, it is advised that banks and NBFCs shall take note of the observations made by
the Hon'ble Court in respect of unilateral appointment of arbitrators and ensure that the
appointment of arbitrators in disputes is undertaken in accordance with the provisions of the
Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, and the applicable judicial pronouncements governing
the independence, impartiality and neutrality of arbitrators.

4. Banks and NBFCs shall report the action taken to their respective supervisor/Senior
Supervisory Manager (SSM).

1. This hearing has been done through hybrid mode.
2. The issue that has arisen in this case is with respect to enforcement of
Awards wherein the Respondent has not been properly served as also
unilateral appointment of the same arbitrators made by Banks in thousands of
disputes.
3. The present appeal has been filed challenging the impugned order dated
30th January, 2025 passed by the ld. District Judge (Commercial Court)-05,
Tis Hazari, Delhi. Vide the impugned order the petition under Section 34 of

FAO (COMM) 91/2025                              Page 1 of 10
the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 (hereinafter "the ACA, 1996") has
been dismissed on the ground of limitation.
4. The brief facts of the case are that sometime in January, 2019 the
Respondent Bank had disbursed a loan for a sum of Rs. 16,66,795/- to the
Petitioner. The said loan was allegedly not repaid by the Appellant and
accordingly the Respondent Bank initiated arbitration proceedings after
communicating the same to the Appellant on 18th January, 2023. However,
receipt of this communication is disputed by the Appellant.
5. On 6th February, 2023, the ld. Sole Arbitrator is stated to have accepted
the reference of dispute to arbitration and allegedly issued notice to the
Appellant. The receipt of this notice is, again, disputed by the Appellant.
Accordingly, the matter proceeded ex-parte as against the Appellant in terms
of order dated 16th May, 2023 passed by the ld. Sole Arbitrator.
6. Thus, the case of the Appellant is that the Award dated 26th May, 2023
was passed without the knowledge of the Appellant as to the existence of the
said arbitral proceedings.
7. A copy of the said Award was received by the Appellant on 2nd June,
2023 and upon the award coming to the knowledge of the Appellant, a petition
under Section 34 of the ACA, 1996 was filed before the Commercial Court.
8. This petition was dismissed vide the impugned order as being time
barred.
9. The present appeal was considered on 7th April, 2025, on which date
notice was issued and the TCR was called for. Thereafter, on 5th August,
2025, upon joint submission of the parties the matter was referred to the Delhi
High Court Mediation and Conciliation Centre for amicable resolution

through mediation, but the same did not fructify.
10. On 3rd September, 2026, after hearing some arguments in this matter,
the Court had put the following queries to the Respondent Bank:

            "2. The Court has considered the matter and heard the
            ld. Counsels for the parties. Before proceeding further.
            Respondent Bank is directed to file an affidavit giving
            the following information:
            i) Whether there is any proof of service of the Appellant
            in the arbitral proceedings?

            ii) Whether Shri. Vikrant Bhardwaj, who was the Sole
            Arbitrator, is connected to the Respondent Bank in any
            manner?

            iii) Number of arbitrations handled by Shri. Vikrant
            Bhardwaj, the Sole Arbitrator on behalf of the
            Respondent Bank.

            iv) Whether any declaration in terms of Schedule VI and
            VII of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 was
            given by the said Arbitrator."

11. Thus, an affidavit was directed to be filed by the Respondent Bank in
respect of the above queries. The said affidavit has been filed by the
Respondent Bank answering the queries raised by the Court as under:

            "3. In compliance with the above directions, the

12. A perusal of the affidavit, as extracted above, would show that there is
no proof of service except what is recorded by the ld. Sole Arbitrator in the
Award dated 26th May 2023. Further, in respect of the ld. Sole Arbitrator -
Shri Vikrant Bhardwaj the Respondent Bank claims that he does not have any
relationship with the Bank of the nature as provided under Schedule 7 of the
ACA, 1996. However, upon querying the official of the Respondent Bank,
who is present in Court, it is stated that the said Arbitrator was paid his fees

FAO (COMM) 91/2025  Page 5 of 10
through the concerned lawyer for the Respondent Bank.
13. The most shocking aspect uncovered from the affidavit of the
Respondent Bank is that in a span of four to five years the said Arbitrator -
Shri Vikrant Bhardwaj has acted as an arbitrator in a whopping 4155 cases
concerning the Respondent Bank.
14. This would clearly be in violation of various provisions of the ACA,
1996, especially those introduced through amendments in 2015 via the
Arbitration and Conciliation (Amendment) Act, 2015. It is a matter of fact
that Section 12 of the ACA, 1996 was amended in order to deal with such a
situations, albeit the scale of conflict contemplated under the Fifth Schedule
was of a far lower level than what has been unearthed here. Under the Fifth
Schedule to the ACA, 1996, in total 34 grounds/circumstances have been
mentioned which would give rise to a justifiable doubt as to the independence
or impartiality of the arbitrator. The said grounds have been categorised under
the following heads:

   (i) Arbitrator's relationship with the parties or counsel;
   (ii) Relationship of the arbitrator to the dispute;
   (iii) Arbitrator's direct or indirect interest in the dispute;
   (iv) Previous services for one of the parties or other involvement in the

           case;
   (v) Relationship between an arbitrator and another arbitrator or counsel;
   (vi) Relationship between arbitrator and party and others involved in the

           arbitration; and
   (vii) Other circumstances.
15. It is seen that the grounds/circumstances mentioned in respect of the

FAO (COMM) 91/2025  Page 6 of 10
category (iv) above i.e., previous services for one of the parties or other
involvement in the case, the following have been expressly contemplated by
the Legislature:

           "22. The arbitrator has within the past three years
           been appointed as arbitrator on two or more occasions
           by one of the parties or an affiliate of one of the parties.

           xxx

           24. The arbitrator currently serves, or has served
           within the past three years, as arbitrator in another
           arbitration on a related issue involving one of the
           parties or an affiliate of one of the parties."

16. The above amendments came into effect from 23rd October, 2015,
however, the Court has been informed that up until 2023 such unilateral
appointments were continued to be made by the Respondent bank.
17. Mr. Dayan Krishnan, ld. Senior Counsel appearing for the Respondent
Bank submits that the Bank concedes that such arbitral awards, where the
Respondent Bank had made unilateral appointment of arbitrators, are void ab
initio, in view of the settled principle of necessity of neutrality at all stages of
arbitration as settled by the Supreme Court in Perkins Eastman Architects
DPC v. HSCC (India) Ltd., (2020) 20 SCC 760 and Central Organisation
for Railway Electrification v. ECI SPIC SMO MCML (JV), (2025) 4 SCC
641.
18. In fact, in similar matters, this Court has also rendered judgments
setting aside arbitral awards on the ground of unilateral appointment of
arbitrators, including in M/s. Satyaprakash and Brothers (P) Ltd. v. Union

FAO (COMM) 91/2025  Page 7 of 10
of India [2025:DHC:6615-DB], M/s. Sidharth Construction Co. v. Indian
Tourism Development Corporation & Anr. [2025:DHC:2313-DB]. In
addition, the Coordinate Bench of this Court in Ram Kumar and Anr. v.
Shriram Transport Finance Co. Ltd., [2022 SCC Online Del 4268] has also
held similarly.
19. Mr. Krishnan, ld. Senior Counsel further concedes that out of the 4155
cases, where Shri Vikrant Bhardwaj was an arbitrator, in almost 65 per cent
of the awards settlements have been effected, i.e., the disputes have been
resolved in about 2700 cases. Further, in about 1264 cases no execution
petition has been filed and no fresh arbitral appointment has also been made.
20. On instructions from the bank, it is further submitted that from 2023 no
unilateral appointment has been made and upon being queried, it is submitted
that there are a total of 76,000 awards which have been passed by such sole
arbitrators pan India concerning the Respondent Bank.
21. Heard. The clear impression that the Court gathers from the above is
that this is nothing but the tip of an iceberg.
22. Arbitrators who were appointed in thousands of matters like this
without filing proper declarations cannot be said to be independent and
impartial arbitrators. Even judging by the unamended Section 12(1) of the
ACA, 1996, handling thousands of the arbitrations by the very same arbitrator
appointed by one single party would give rise to justifiable doubts as to
independence and impartiality. Clearly, such lawyers who are appointed as
arbitrators cannot be considered as lacking bias. Repeated appointments of
the same arbitrators in thousands of cases goes against the core of Neutrality
which is at the soul of any arbitral process.

FAO (COMM) 91/2025  Page 8 of 10
23. The Respondent Bank - Axis Bank which is the party in the present
case is answerable as to how this situation could be permitted and the Bank
also requires to take some remedial measures without doubt.
24. Accordingly, let a fresh affidavit be filed by the Respondent Bank
giving the reasons as to why thousands of arbitration cases were being given
to the same arbitrator. The Bank shall also file an affidavit as to what is the
future course of action that the bank wishes to adopt in respect of such awards
which have been passed by unilaterally appointed arbitrators, including
enforcement of such awards and steps taken for recovery of loan amounts.
25. Banks being the custodian of the public money cannot avail remedies
which are contrary to law to recover loans. There can be no doubt that the
defaulting borrowers also cannot be allowed to go scot free.
26. Notwithstanding the public interest in recovery of the loans, the route
adopted by the Respondent Bank for the same which is in complete lack of
adherence to statutory provisions has come as a double blow to the
Respondent Bank - on the one hand Awards which are passed in unilateral
appointments are void and unenforceable, while on the other hand the
Respondent Bank's hands are tied as it cannot enforce such awards or recover
such monies. Thus, the Respondent Bank is totally in a fix as to how to recover
the monies or even invoke fresh arbitration.
27. A solution would need to be carved at an institutional level in a
centralised manner by the Respondent Bank failing which substantial amounts
of public money will go unrecovered and a significant number of awards
would be rendered void and unenforceable.
28. Under these circumstances, let a fresh affidavit be filed as to the manner

FAO (COMM) 91/2025  Page 9 of 10
in which the Respondent Bank wishes to proceed in such cases.
29. Notice was issued to the Reserve Bank of India which is today
represented before the Court. The RBI being the sector regulator, shall also
caution all banks in this regard, so that if any remedial measures need to be
taken by other banks and financial institutions in respect of unilateral
appointments, the same can also be looked into and some guidance issued in
respect thereof.
30. The RBI also to file its affidavit by 16th November, 2026.
31. Coming to the merits of this case, since on behalf of the Respondent
Bank the stand is that the award is void ab initio, therefore, it is declared that
the award dated 26th May, 2023 is unenforceable against the Appellant.
32. The appeal accordingly succeeds, however, in order to take this issue
to a logical conclusion, let the Respondent bank file its affidavit by 16th
November, 2026.
33. In respect of recovery of the loan disbursed to the Appellant, the
Respondent Bank is free to take steps afresh, in accordance with law, if so
permissible.
34. List on 17th November, 2026.
35. This matter shall be treated as part-heard.
