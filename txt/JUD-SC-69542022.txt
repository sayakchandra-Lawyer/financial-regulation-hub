       FACTUAL BACKGROUND

3. The facts giving rise to the present writ petition are as
       follows:

   3.1) The petitioner is a national award-winning journalist
           and the Managing Editor of Moneylife magazine, who
           has been actively engaged in matters concerning
           investor protection and financial accountability.
           Through her writings and public advocacy, she has
           consistently raised concerns regarding the
           accumulation of unclaimed deposits and investments
           with banks and other financial institutions, and the
           difficulties faced by depositors and their legal heirs in
           securing their recovery.

WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO.185 OF 2022  2
3.2) The present petition primarily concerns three
       statutory funds established for dealing with unclaimed
       financial assets. Their respective legal frameworks are
       briefly set out below:
       i) DEAF was established by RBI under Section 26A
               of the Banking Regulation Act, 1949 (hereinafter
               referred to as the “Banking Regulation Act”) and
               is governed by the Depositor Education and
               Awareness Fund Scheme, 2014. Under this
               framework, amounts lying in bank accounts
               which have remained inoperative for ten years, or
               deposits which have remained unclaimed for a
               similar period, are transferred to DEAF. Such
               transfer does not extinguish the entitlement of
               the depositor or other lawful claimant to seek
               repayment through the concerned bank.
       ii) IEPF is established under Section 125 of the
               Companies Act, 2013 (hereinafter referred to as
               the “Companies Act”) and is administered by the
               IEPF Authority under the Ministry of Corporate
               Affairs. The Fund receives unclaimed dividends,
               amounts relating to matured deposits and
               debentures, and other specified sums, while
               shares liable to be transferred under Section
               124(6) of the Companies Act are credited to the
               demat account of the IEPF Authority in
               accordance with the applicable Rules. The

WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO.185 OF 2022  3
               procedure for claiming refunds and restoration of
               securities is governed by the Investor Education
               and Protection Fund Authority (Accounting,
               Audit, Transfer and Refund) Rules, 2016.
       iii) SCWF was established under the Finance Act,
               2015 and is administered by the Ministry of
               Social Justice and Empowerment. It receives
               specified unclaimed amounts from certain
               savings and financial instruments, including
               post-office and provident-fund accounts, subject
               to the conditions prescribed under the applicable
               statutory framework. The Fund is intended to
               support schemes promoting the welfare of senior
               citizens.
3.3) According to the petitioner, substantial amounts had
       accumulated in these funds without being claimed by
       the persons lawfully entitled thereto. It was pointed
       out that DEAF had accumulated approximately Rs.
       39,264.25 crores (Rupees thirty-nine thousand two
       hundred and sixty-four crore and twenty-five lakh) as
       on 31.03.2021, as compared to Rs. 18,381 crores
       (Rupees eighteen thousand three hundred and eighty-
       one crore) as on 31.03.2019. Similarly, the amount
       lying with IEPF was stated to have increased to
       approximately Rs. 4,100 crores (Rupees four thousand
       one hundred crore) by 31.03.2020. The petitioner
       attributed this accumulation, inter alia, to the lack of

       the existence of such assets and the difficulties

3.4) The petitioner relied upon various circulars issued by
       RBI from 2005 onwards, requiring banks to adopt
       simplified procedures for settlement of claims
       concerning deceased depositors and to take proactive
       measures for identifying holders of unclaimed
       deposits. These directions included publication of
       information regarding inoperative accounts on the
       websites of banks and periodic updating of such
       information. It was alleged that despite repeated
       regulatory directions, several banks had failed to
       implement these measures effectively.
3.5) The petitioner further contended that banks,
       depositories and other financial institutions frequently

       centralised mechanism for identifying unclaimed
       financial assets, simplification of claim procedures and

effective enforcement of the existing regulatory

RELEVANCE OF THE ISSUE AT HAND

4. We have heard Mr. Prashant Bhushan, learned counsel
       appearing for the petitioner, Mr. N. Venkataraman, learned
       Additional Solicitor General appearing for Respondent No.
       1, Union of India through the Department of Financial
       Services, and Respondent No. 3, Ministry of Corporate
       Affairs, Mr. Dama Seshadri Naidu, learned senior counsel
       appearing for Respondent No. 2, RBI, and Mr. Amarjit Singh
       Bedi, learned counsel appearing for Respondent No. 4, the
       Securities and Exchange Board of India (hereinafter
       referred to as “SEBI”). We have also perused the pleadings,
       affidavits and other material placed on record by the
       respective parties, including the subsequent developments
       brought to our notice during the pendency of these
       proceedings.

5. The issues raised in the present writ petition are of
       considerable public importance. Savings, deposits and
       investments represent the hard-earned money of
       individuals, often accumulated over several years to provide
       financial security to themselves and their families. The right
       to property, though no longer a fundamental right,
       continues to enjoy constitutional protection under Article
       300A of the Constitution, which provides that no person

WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO.185 OF 2022  6
       shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law.
       The transfer of unclaimed financial assets to statutory
       funds does not, by itself, extinguish the rights of persons
       lawfully entitled thereto. The State and its regulatory
       authorities, therefore, have an important responsibility to
       protect such financial interests and facilitate the recovery
       of these assets by their rightful claimants.
6. This responsibility must be discharged consistently with
       the constitutional principles of fairness, reasonableness
       and non-arbitrariness embodied in Article 14 of the
       Constitution. Where statutory mechanisms have been
       established for the custody, administration and recovery of
       unclaimed financial assets, the concerned authorities must
       ensure that such mechanisms remain effective, transparent
       and reasonably accessible. The existence of a statutory
       right to reclaim money would be of little practical value if
       the persons entitled thereto are unable to trace the assets
       or are confronted with unnecessarily cumbersome
       procedures for establishing their claims. The difficulties
       faced by nominees and legal heirs, particularly those who
       may be unaware of the financial assets left behind by
       deceased family members, are therefore legitimate matters
       of public concern deserving appropriate attention.
7. At the same time, the need to facilitate recovery of
       unclaimed financial assets must be balanced with
       appropriate safeguards against fraudulent or competing
       claims. The object must be to ensure that the procedures

WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO.185 OF 2022  7
       prescribed by financial institutions are neither
       unnecessarily burdensome nor so relaxed as to compromise
       the interests of rightful claimants. Viewed in this
       perspective, the concerns brought before this Court by the
       petitioner are both genuine and deserving of consideration.
       It is in this background that we proceed to examine the
       measures undertaken by the respondents during the
       pendency of these proceedings and the extent to which they
       address the issues raised in the present writ petition.
8. During the pendency of the present writ petition, the
       respondents have placed on record various affidavits
       detailing the steps undertaken to address the concerns
       raised by the petitioner. These measures include the
       introduction of centralised search facilities, simplification of
       procedures for settlement of claims, strengthening of
       nomination and transmission mechanisms, and initiatives
       for identifying and reaching out to the rightful owners of
       unclaimed financial assets. We consider it appropriate to
       examine these developments separately with reference to
       the measures undertaken by the respective respondents.

MEASURES UNDERTAKEN BY RBI

9. RBI, in its counter affidavit dated 27.01.2023, referred to
       the various regulatory directions issued to banks
       concerning unclaimed deposits and settlement of claims of
       deceased depositors. These included the requirement to

       publish details of unclaimed deposits on the websites of
       banks, periodically update such information, and adopt
       simplified procedures for settlement of claims. RBI further
       informed this Court that a centralised web portal was under
       development to enable members of the public to search for
       unclaimed deposits across different banks through a
       common platform.
10. Subsequently, in its additional affidavit dated 28.04.2026,
       RBI placed on record the establishment of UDGAM, a
       centralised web portal launched on 17.08.2023 for
       facilitating the identification of unclaimed deposits across
       multiple banks. The object of the portal was explained in
       the press release dated 17.08.2023 in the following terms:

    “The launch of the web portal will aid users to identify
    their unclaimed deposits/accounts and enable them
    to either claim the deposit amount or make their
    deposit accounts operative at their respective banks.”

The portal was initially launched with seven participating
banks. Thereafter, by press release dated 05.10.2023, RBI
informed the public that the search facility had been
extended to thirty banks with effect from 28.09.2023,
covering approximately ninety per cent of the unclaimed
deposits, in terms of value, lying with DEAF.

11. It was further submitted before us that UDGAM enables
       individuals as well as non-individual entities to search for

unclaimed deposits by furnishing relevant particulars
concerning the account holder. The search results provide
the name of the account holder, the concerned bank and a
unique Unclaimed Deposit Reference Number, which
facilitates identification of the deposit and enables the
claimant to approach the bank concerned. RBI has also
clarified, through its Frequently Asked Questions dated
05.03.2024, that the portal serves as a centralised search
facility and that the actual settlement of claims continues
to be undertaken by the respective banks. The relevant
clarification reads as follows:

“No, the UDGAM portal facilitates only (a) the search
of unclaimed deposits/accounts across multiple

12. RBI has further stated that the functioning of UDGAM has
       been improved on the basis of user feedback. Mandatory
       registration has been discontinued, the search facility has
       been made more accessible, and restrictions concerning the
       number of searches and logins have been relaxed. As on
       01.04.2026, approximately twenty lakh users were
       registered on the portal and nearly forty-four lakh searches
       had been conducted. These developments demonstrate that
       the centralised search mechanism, which was only under

MEASURES UNDERTAKEN BY SEBI

16. SEBI has filed a detailed affidavit in May 2026 setting out
       the measures adopted for protecting investors and
       facilitating the transmission of securities to nominees and
       legal heirs. The affidavit refers to circulars issued from 2021
       onwards concerning nomination facilities for trading
       accounts, demat accounts, securities held in physical form
       and mutual fund folios. The regulatory framework has
       subsequently been revised through circulars dated
       10.01.2025 and 28.02.2025. These measures are intended
       to ensure that investors either register their nominees or
       expressly opt out of nomination, and that the relevant
       particulars of nominees are available to facilitate
       transmission of investments upon the death of the account
       holder.

17. SEBI has also undertaken measures for simplifying the
       documentary requirements governing transmission of

securities. By circular dated 18.05.2022, issued pursuant

“The LODR Amendment Regulations has inter alia

       prescribed identity-verification requirements, without
       insisting upon additional affidavits, indemnities or

       procedural difficulties while retaining the necessary
       safeguards concerning identification and entitlement.
18. Another important measure concerns the establishment of
       a centralised mechanism for reporting and verifying the
       death of an investor. By circular dated 03.10.2023, which

       communicating verified information concerning the death of

CONSIDERATION OF THE REMAINING GRIEVANCES OF
THE PETITIONER AND THE NEED FOR FURTHER
DIRECTIONS

26. Having examined the measures undertaken by the
       respondents, we find that the principal concerns which
       prompted the institution of the present writ petition have
       been substantially addressed. The petitioner had
       approached this Court highlighting the difficulties in tracing

WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO.185 OF 2022  19
       unclaimed financial assets, the procedural obstacles faced
       by nominees and legal heirs, and the need for greater
       accountability on the part of financial institutions. The
       subsequent establishment of centralised search facilities,
       simplification of claim procedures, strengthening of
       nomination and transmission mechanisms, and
       implementation of public awareness initiatives constitute
       significant progress towards addressing these concerns.
27. Mr. Prashant Bhushan, learned counsel appearing for the
       petitioner, has nevertheless submitted that the measures
       undertaken remain inadequate. In particular, it has been
       contended that there is still no single comprehensive
       platform through which individuals or legal heirs can
       identify and reclaim financial assets held across different
       banks and financial institutions. It has further been
       submitted that unclaimed amounts relating to insurance
       policies, post-office deposits, provident funds and other
       financial instruments have not been adequately addressed.
       We have considered these submissions in the light of the
       material placed on record.
28. Insofar as unclaimed bank deposits are concerned, RBI has
       already established UDGAM, which provides a centralised
       search facility across participating banks. As stated in its
       affidavit dated 28.04.2026, the participating banks
       accounted for approximately ninety per cent of the
       unclaimed deposits lying with DEAF in terms of value at the
       relevant time. Although the facility does not cover every

WRIT PETITION (CIVIL) NO.185 OF 2022  20
       bank, it substantially addresses the demand for a common
       searchable database. The fact that claims continue to be
       settled by the respective banks does not render the
       mechanism inadequate, since the concerned banks remain
       responsible for verifying entitlement and effecting
       repayment in accordance with law. We therefore find no
       justification for directing the establishment of another
       centralised mechanism for this purpose.
29. As regards the broader demand for a single portal covering
       all categories of financial assets, we are unable to accept
       that its absence warrants further intervention by this Court.
       Banking, securities, insurance and other financial sectors
       operate under distinct statutory and regulatory frameworks,
       with different requirements concerning ownership,
       verification and transmission. While greater coordination
       between these mechanisms may be desirable, the
       establishment of a unified platform involves questions of
