         Com. App. (AT) (Ins) No. 1804 OF 2026 has been preferred under Section 61
of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 ("Code") by Ms. Sangeeta Aggarwal
("Appellant") challenging the order dated 31.08.2026 passed by the Ld. National
Company Law Tribunal, New Delhi, Bench - II ("Ld. Adjudicating Authority") in I.A.
(IBC) No. 798 filed under Company Petition (IB) No. 651 of 2025.

2. The relevant facts with regard to this appeal are that on 06 December 2024,
Canara Bank instituted proceedings against the Appellant Ms. Sangeeta Aggarwal
under Section 95 of the Code, being CP (IB) No. 23 (AHM) of 2024, before the Ld.
National Company Law Tribunal, Ahmedabad Bench ("Ld. Adjudicating Authority").
Mr. Sunil Kumar Kabra was appointed as the Resolution Professional in the said
proceedings on 22 January 2025.
3. It is further reflected that the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process against
the Corporate Debtor was also initiated vide order dated 01 April 2025, passed by
the Ld. National Company Law Tribunal, New Delhi, Bench V, in CP (IB) No. 413 (ND)
of 2025 and Respondent No.1, Equentia filed its claim in Form C, for INR 13.40
crores, in the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor.
4. It is further evident that on 05 November 2025, Respondent No. 1- Equentia
instituted a fresh proceeding under Section 95 against the Appellant, being CP (IB)
No. 651 of 2025, before the Ld. Adjudicating Authority and vide order dated 29
January 2026, the Ld. Adjudicating Authority appointed Mr. Loveneet Handa
("Respondent No. 2") as the RP, who submitted report on 17 February 2026, bearing
I.A. No. 798 of 2026 under Section 99 of the Code, recommending initiation of the
personal insolvency resolution process against the Appellant. The Adjudicating

Authority by passing impugned Order initiated personal insolvency process against
Appellant Sangeeta Aggarwal.
5. It is further reflected that on 21 November 2025, i.e., subsequent to the filing
of Equentia's petition, Canara Bank withdrew its proceedings pending before the
Adjudicating Authority, Ahmedabad Bench.
6. Com. App. (AT) (Ins.) No. 1805 OF 2026 has been preferred under Section 61
of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 ("Code") by Sri Narain Aggarwal
("Appellant") challenging the order dated 31.08.2026 passed by the Ld. National
Company Law Tribunal, New Delhi, Bench - II ("Ld. Adjudicating Authority") in I.A.
(IBC) No. 972 filed under Company Petition (IB) No. 653 of 2025.
7. The relevant facts with regard to this appeal are that on 06 December 2024,
Canara Bank instituted proceedings against the Appellant under Section 95 of the
Code, being CP (IB) No. 22 (AHM) of 2024, before the Ld. National Company Law
Tribunal, Ahmedabad Bench ("Ld. Adjudicating Authority, Ahmedabad") and Mr.
Sunil Kumar Kabra was appointed as the Resolution Professional in the said
proceedings. The Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process against the Corporate
Debtor was also initiated vide order dated 01 April 2025, passed by the Ld. National
Company Law Tribunal, New Delhi, Bench V, in CP (IB) No. 413 (ND) of 2025 and on
15 April 2025, respondent No.1-Equentia filed its claim in Form C, for INR 13.40
crores, in the CIRP of the Corporate Debtor.
8. It is further reflected that on 07 November 2025 Respondent No.1-Equentia
instituted a fresh proceeding under Section 95 against the Appellant, being CP (IB)
No. 653 of 2025, before the Ld. Adjudicating Authority. On 21 November 2025, i.e.,
subsequent to the filing of Equentia's petition, Canara Bank withdrew its proceedings
pending before the Hon'ble Adjudicating Authority, Ahmedabad Bench. However, on
19 December 2025, Canara Bank filed a fresh petition, being CP (IB) No. 704 of 2025,
                                                             4

under Section 95 against the Appellant before the Hon'ble National Company Law
Tribunal, Bench V, at New Delhi, which is stated to be pending.
9. Ld. Adjudicating Authority vide order dated 19 December 2025 appointed Mr.
Amit Talwar, as the Resolution Professional in the Equentia's petition. However, vide
order dated 10 February 2026, Ld. Adjudicating Authority, replaced Mr. Amit Talwar

10. Aggrieved by the aforesaid impugned orders the appellants/ Personal
Guarantors have filed the above appeals. Keeping in view the identical issues
involved, both Appeals are being disposed of passing this common judgment.
11. Learned Counsel for the Appellant submit that admitted facts are that, on
6.12.2024, the Canara Bank has instituted proceedings against the Appellant under
Section 95 of the Code, and an interim moratorium in pursuance of Section 96 of the
Code commenced on this date. The respondent No. 1, Equentia, has filed a petition
under Section 95 of the Code on 5.11.2025 during the existence of the moratorium
initiated by the proceedings instituted by the Canara Bank on 6.12.2024. Therefore,
the proceedings initiated by Equentia are non-est.
12. It is further submitted that the fact that Canara Bank has withdrawn its
petition on 21.11.2025 and filed a fresh petition on 19.12.2025 is of no consequence
as on 5.11.2025. The interim moratorium commenced on 6.12.2024 by the filing of
the proceedings by the Canara Bank was in force.
13. It is further submitted that the learned adjudicating authority has taken
shelter of the amendment made under Section 96 of the Code. However, the said
                                                             5

amendment is not retrospective and would not be applicable to the facts of the
instant case. Therefore, the proceedings filed within the course of moratorium
initiated by proceedings instituted earlier are nullity. Reliance in this regard is placed
on Alchemist Asset Reconstruction Company Limited versus Hotel Gaudavan Private
Limited, 2018 (16) SCC 94 which has been followed in Sushant Chhabra versus
Catalyst Trusteeship Limited (Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) No. 443 of 2026).
Learned Counsel for the Appellant have also cited Union Bank of India versus P.K.
Balasubramanian, Company Appeal (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 293 of 2022.
14. The Learned Counsel for the Appellant further submits that the main
controversy in this appeal is pertaining to the impact of Section 96(4), which was
introduced on 26 May 2026. It says that the provisions of this section shall not apply
where an application is filed for initiating an insolvency resolution process in respect
of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor. It is submitted that the reliance of the
learned adjudicating authority on Tata Capital Financial Services Limited versus Neel
Motors LLP, Commercial Arbitration Petition No. 620 of 2021 is not correct in view
of the explanation given by the Hon'ble Bombay High Court in paragraph number 14
of the report, as it is verified that the amendment is retroactive and is not
retrospective, and therefore would apply prospectively to existing facts such as the
existing arbitration petition.
15. Elaborating further, it is submitted that a retroactive amendment applies
prospectively to events that have happened earlier. If Section 96(4) had been a pure
prospective amendment, the result would be that 96(4) would only apply to those 95
petitions filed after 26 May 2026. The import of retroactivity is merely that, even in
cases where the Section 95 petition was filed before 26 May 2026, the interim
moratorium was in force prior to the amendment.
16. It is further submitted that the judgment of the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in
IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited versus Manish Jain is merely a judgment deciding
                                                             6

an application under Order VII, Rule 11. In fact, it explicitly states in paragraph
number 21 that it has not considered whether the defendant number 2 is deprived
of the interim moratorium enjoyed by it. Therefore, it cannot be relied upon by the
respondent in the instant case.
17. It is further submitted that the IBBI circular dated 21 September 2026, issued
under Section 196 of the Code, has been issued in contravention of paragraph
number 13 of the judgment of Sundaresh Bhat, Liquidator of ABG Shipyard Limited
Company Appeal (AT) (INS) No. 398 of 2021. Therefore, the petition filed by the
respondent No. 1 during the interim moratorium initiated by the filing of the petition
by the Canara Bank on 06 December 2024 is non-est and therefore the impugned
order may kindly be set aside.
18. Learned Counsel for the Respondent No. 1 and RP submits that the Appeal
rests on a single premise that the application under Section 95 of the Code filed by
Equentia Financial Services Private Limited (the “Financial Creditor”) on 05.11.2025
was barred by the interim moratorium under Section 96 of the Code, and thereby
became non-est for all time, however, the said premise no longer survives after the
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026, as sub-section (4) was
inserted in Section 96, which expressly provides that Section 96 shall not apply
where an application is filed for initiating an insolvency resolution process in respect
of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor.
19. It is further submitted that the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in Tata Capital
Financial Services Ltd. v. Neel Motors LLP & Ors., IBC Law 4165 and the Hon’ble
Delhi High Court in IDBI Trusteeship Services Ltd. v. Manish Jain & Ors., CS(COMM)
800/2025 have held that Section 96(4) applies to pending proceedings. The
Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (“IBBI”) has, by Circular No.
IBBI/II/106/2026 dated 21.09.2026 has also clarified the effect of these judgments.
                                                             7

Thus, there was no interim moratorium when the petition was filed by the
Respondent No. 1.
20. It is further submitted that Section 96 of the Code was amended by Section
51 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code (Amendment) Act, 2026, whereby, after
sub-section (3), the following sub-section was inserted:

               “51. In section 96 of the principal Act, after sub-section (3). the
               following sub-section shall be inserted, namely: —
               “(4) The provisions of this section shall not apply where an
               application is filed for initiating an insolvency resolution process
               in respect of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor.”

21. Therefore, the Appellants are admittedly personal guarantors to a corporate
debtor, and the proceedings against them are proceedings of the very kind to which
Section 96 now longer applies. The Appellant’s contention regarding the interim
moratorium under Section 96 is, therefore, untenable in view of the express statutory
exclusion. The said amendment came into force with effect from 26.05.2026, and
from that date the interim moratorium ceased to operate in respect of applications
concerning personal guarantors to corporate debtors. The Impugned Order dated
31.08.2026 was passed after the said date.
22. It is further submitted that the ratio of the above cases is directly applicable
to the facts of this case. The application of Section 96(4) to the pending Section 95
proceedings is a prospective application of a newly introduced provision to facts that
continue to exist, and does not become retrospective merely because the application
was filed earlier. The IBBI, vide Circular No. IBBI/II/106/2026 dated 21.09.2026.
has clarified the effect of the judgments of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court and the
Hon’ble Delhi High Court regarding the cessation of the interim moratorium in
respect of personal guarantors to corporate debtor.
23. It is also submitted that the reliance placed by the Appellant on the judgment
of this Hon’ble Tribunal in Sushant Chhabra v. Catalyst Trusteeship Limited,
                                                             8

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 443 of 2026, is misplaced and that judgment
cannot be read to mean that a provision subsequently enacted by the Legislature,
expressly excluding personal guarantors to corporate debtors from Section 96, can
have no application to pending proceedings. The subsequent amendment has
materially altered the statutory position. Similarly, the reliance placed by the
Appellant on Union Bank of India v. P.K. Balasubramanian, Comp. App. (AT) (CH)
(Ins.) No. 293 of 2022, is also misplaced, and cannot override the express exclusion
now contained in Section 96(4) of the Code as interpreted by the Hon’ble Bombay
High Court and the Hon’ble Delhi High Court.
24. It is further submitted by the Counsel for the RP that the RP filed their Reports
under Section 99 of the Code, after satisfying the requirements of Section 95 read
with Section 78 and Rule 7(1) of the Personal Guarantors Rules, and complied with
Sections 99(1), 99(2), 99(3), 99(4) and 99(7) of the Code. However, the Appellants
deliberately concealed the material facts that an application under Section 95 of the
Code was earlier pending before the Hon’ble NCLT, Ahmedabad Bench, which was
subsequently withdrawn. Despite having withdrawn it, the Appellant has sought to
rely upon the pendency of the earlier proceedings as a ground to derail the present
proceedings and has thereafter filed an application before the Hon’ble NCLT, Bench-
V, bearing C.P. (IB) No. 707 of 2025. The Appellant also failed to intimate the RP of
any ongoing litigation or any pending Section 95 application. Such conduct is
misleading and amounts to suppression of material facts.
25. It is also submitted that the Adjudicating Authority has considered all the
facts and material on record as well as the impact of the amended Section 96 and
the judgments of the Hon’ble Bombay High Court, and rightly allowed the application
under Section 95 and the Appellant has failed to point out any error of law or
jurisdictional infirmity in the Impugned Order. Therefore, the Appeals are
misconceived, devoid of merit and liable to be dismissed.

26. Having heard learned counsel for the parties and having perused the record,
it is reflected that there are certain facts which are not disputed by the parties. It is
admitted to the parties that on 6/12/2024, the Canara Bank has instituted
proceedings against the Appellants, being CP (IB) No. 22 and 23 of 2024 under
Section 95 of the Code before the NCLT, Ahmedabad, and the interim moratorium in
view of Section 96 of the code commences and on 22-1-2025, the adjudicating
authority, Ahmedabad, appointed Mr. Sunil Kumar Kabra as the resolution
professional in the aforesaid proceedings.
27. It is also admitted to the parties that respondent No. 1, Equentia, instituted
proceedings under Section 95 of the Code on 5.11.2025, CP (IB) No. 651 of 2025,
and on 7.11.2025, CP (IB) No. 653 of 2025, against the Appellants before the NCLT,
New Delhi, admittedly, after the institution of the proceedings by the Canara Bank
against the Appellant on 06.12.2024 and after commencement of the Interim
Moratorium and also that Mr. Loveneet Handa was appointed as the resolution
professional on 29/1/2026. Reports under Section 99 of the Code was accordingly
filed by the RP, being IA No. 798 and 972 of 2026, on 17.02.2026 and 27.02.2026,
respectively.
28. It is also admitted to the parties that on 21.11.2025, the Canara Bank
withdraw the proceedings instituted against the Appellant at NCLT Ahmedabad and
on 19.12.2025, Canara Bank filed its fresh petitions under Section 95 of the Code
against the Appellants before NCLT, New Delhi.
29. It is also admitted to the parties that on 26 May 2026, Section 96 of the Code,
as amended by the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code Amendment Act, 2026, came
into force, whereby a new subsection i.e. subsection (4), was inserted therein.
30. It is also an admitted fact that on 31st August 2026, the impugned orders
were passed whereby the petitions filed under Section 95 of the Code by the
respondent No. 1, Equentia Financial Services Private Limited, were accepted, and a
                                                            10

personal insolvency resolution process was initiated against the appellant under
Section 95 of the Code.
31. The main contention of the Appellant before us is that, since the petitions
under Section 95 of the Code were filed by the Canara Bank against the Appellants
on 06/12/2024, well before the coming into force of the Amendment under Section
96 of the Code (i.e., on 26 May 2026) and the Interim moratorium as provided under
Section 96 of the Code commences and the petitions under Section 95 of the Code
have been filed by the Respondent Equentia on 05/11/2025, during the interim
moratorium commenced by the filing of the petitions under Section 95 of the Code
by the Canara Bank on 06.12.2024, the same having regard to the law laid down by
the Honourable Supreme Court in Alchemist Asset Reconstruction Company Limited
(supra) and by this Appellate Tribunal in Sushant Chhabra and Union Bank of India
vs. P.K. Balasubramanian (supra) are non-est proceedings and therefore, the
impugned orders are liable to be set aside.
32. It is also the case of the appellant that the amendment made under Section
96 of the Code, with effect from 26 May 2026, is not retrospective. Therefore, it cannot
be implemented and taken into compliance with regard to the proceedings already
pending before the amendment came into existence.
33. Ld. counsel for the Respondent No. 1 and Resolution Professional on the other
hand, submits that, as held by the Hon’ble Bombay High Court in Tata Capital
Financial Services Limited (supra) and by the Hon’ble Delhi High Court in IDBI
Trusteeship Services Limited (Supra), the amendment made under Section 96 of the
Code with effect from 26 May 2026 is a retroactive amendment and is applicable to
the proceedings pending before any forum. By the insertion of subsection 4, it has
been clarified that the provisions of this section shall not apply where an application
is filed for initiating an insolvency resolution process in respect of a personal
                                                            11

guarantor to a corporate debtor. Thus, there was no interim moratorium in effect
when impugned orders were passed by the Learned Adjudicating Authority.
34. Ld. counsel for the respondents have also relied on a circular issued by the
IBBI on 21/09/2026 in pursuance of the law laid down by the Hon'ble Bombay High
Court and Hon'ble Delhi High Court in Tata Capital Financial Services Limited
(supra) and IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited (supra), respectively.
35. In response to the submissions made by the learned counsel for the
respondents, the learned counsel for the appellant, in rebuttal, submits that the
cases of Tata Capital Financial Services Limited and IDBI Trusteeship Services
Limited (supra) are, on their own facts and circumstances, and may not be used as
a precedent with regard to the case at hand. Further, it is also submitted that the
IBBI was not having any power to issue the circular relied on by the appellant, as
the same has been issued in contravention of the law laid down by this Appellate
Tribunal in Sundaresh Bhat (supra).
36. Perusal of the impugned judgments would reveal that the learned adjudicating
authority has relied on the law laid down by the Hon Bombay High Court in Tata
Capital Financial Services Limited (Supra) and came to the conclusion that the
petitions filed by the respondent Equentia are not hit by the interim moratorium.
Thus, the sole issue before this Appellate Tribunal is as to whether the amendment
made in Section 96 (vide Amendment Act, 2026), whereby Subsection 4 has been
inserted in Section 96 (whereby the provision of interim moratorium with regard to
the personal guarantors of the corporate debtor has been abolished), would apply to
the proceedings, wherein the impugned orders have been passed by the adjudicating
authority.
37. We notice that before the amendment made in Section 96 of the Code with
effect from 26 May 2026, Section 96 stood as under:
                                                            12

               “96. Interim-moratorium.

               (1) When an application is filed under section 94 or section 95-

               (a) an interim-moratorium shall commence on the date of the
               application in relation to all the debts and shall cease to have
               effect on the date of admission of such application; and

               (b) during the interim-moratorium period—

               (i) any legal action or proceeding pending in respect of any debt
               shall be deemed to have been stayed; and

               (ii) the creditors of the debtor shall not initiate any legal action or
               proceedings in respect of any debt.

               (2) Where the application has been made in relation to a firm, the
               interim-moratorium under sub-section (1) shall operate against all
               the partners of the firm as on the date of the application.

               (3) The provisions of sub-section (1) shall not apply to such
               transactions as may be notified by the Central Government in
               consultation with any financial sector regulator.”

               Following Amendment has been made w.e.f. 26.05.2026;

               “(4) The provisions of this section shall not apply where an
               application is filed for initiating an insolvency resolution process
               in respect of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor.”

38. In Union Bank of India vs. P.K. Balasubramanian decided on 29.03.2023

(Supra), relied on by the Appellants, following was held:

               “12. Section 96(1)(a) provides that an interim-moratorium, shall
               commence on the date of the `Application’, in relation to all the
               debts. Section 96(1)(b) of the `Code’, also specifies that during the
               `Moratorium’ period (i) any legal action or proceeding pending in
               respect of any `debt’ shall be deemed to have been stayed; and
               (ii) the `Creditors of the Debtor’, shall not initiate any legal action
               or proceedings in respect of any `debt’. The use of expression
               ‘Creditors of the Debtor’, means that all other `Creditors of the
               Debtor’, apart from the `Creditor’, on whose `Application’, `interim
               Moratorium’, has commenced. Once `Application’ under Section
               100 is admitted, `Moratorium’, commences with respect to all
               `Debts’, under Section 101 and thereafter `Public Notice’, is
               issued and ‘Claims’ from `Creditors’, are invited under Section
               102. Section 103 provides for registering of `Claims’ by Creditors.
               Section 104 provides for preparation of list of Creditors and
               thereafter repayment Plan is contemplated under Section 105.
               Thus, when an `Insolvency Resolution Process’, commences
               against the `Personal Guarantor’, all `Creditors’ of the ‘Personal
               Guarantor’, are taken care of in the proceedings under Chapter-
               III. The Code does not contemplate multiplicity of `Applications’,
               against the same `Personal Guarantor’. This `Tribunal’, is of the
               earnest view that when the `Insolvency Resolution Process’,
               commences against a `Personal Guarantor’, `Claims of all
                                                            13

               `Creditors’, are taken care of under the scheme of the I & B Code,
               2016.”

39. In Civil Appeal No.16929 of 2017, Alchemist Asset Reconstruction

Company Ltd. vs. M/s. Hotel Gaudavan Pvt. Ltd. & Ors. decided on 23.10.2017

(Supra), another case relied by the Appellants, Hon’ble Supreme Court considered

the effect of moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC with respect to an arbitration

proceeding. The Hon’ble Supreme Court held that the effect of Section 14(1)(a) is that

the arbitration proceedings instituted after the aforesaid moratorium is non est in

law and set aside the order of the District Judge dated 06.07.2017 and further state

that the effect of Section 14(1)(a) is that the arbitration that has been instituted after

the aforesaid moratorium is non-est in law.

40. Ld. Counsel for the Appellants have also relied on Sushant Chhabra (Supra)

decided on 27.03.2026 wherein this Appellate Tribunal again considered the effect

of interim moratorium commences vide section 96 of the Code. This Appellate

Tribunal captured the facts of the case in para no. 9 and 10 of the judgment, as

under: -

               “9. The only submission, which has been raised by the
               Appellants challenging the order dated 17.02.2026 is admitting
               Section 95 application filed by Catalyst Trusteeship Ltd. against
               the Appellants during the period when interim moratorium had
               already been triggered on Section 95 applications filed by Canara
               Bank and was subsisting on the date of filing of Section 95
               application by Catalyst Trusteeship Ltd. The applications under
               Section 95 sub-section (1) were filed by Canara Bank against
               both the Appellants, which were CP(IB)122/ND/2025 and
               CP(IB)121/ND/2025, which were filed on 12.01.2025. Section 96
               of the IBC contemplates issuance of interim moratorium when
               application is filed under Section 94 or Section 95.
               10. There is no dispute between the parties that on filing of
               Section 95 application by Canara Bank, interim moratorium was
               triggered on 12.01.2025 and on the date when applications were
               filed by Catalyst Trusteeship Ltd. against the Appellants, i.e.
               CP(IB)450/ND/2025 and CP(IB)446/ND/2025 on 05.08.2025,
               interim moratorium was in operation. The submission, which has
               been made by learned Counsel for the Respondent to meet the
               argument of the Appellant is that in view of withdrawal of Section
               95 application filed by Canara Bank on 10.11.2025, the
                                                            14

               proceedings stood obliterated and, on the date, when admission
               order was passed on 17.02.2026, interim moratorium was no
               longer continuing. Hence, there was no prohibition in admitting
               Section 95 application filed by Catalyst Trusteeship Ltd.
               (Financial Creditor).”

Thereafter this Appellate Tribunal after considering various authorities opined as

under: -

               “23. From the facts noted above, it is clear that although on the
               date when Section 95 application was filed by the Catalyst
               Trusteeship Ltd. against the Appellant herein on 05.08.2025,
               interim moratorium was already operating in Section 95
               application filed Canara Bank against the Appellant. The
               judgment referred above, clearly prohibit any such institution.
               The distinguishing feature, which Respondent is contending
               herein is that even though application was filed by Canara Bank,
               prior in time and moratorium had triggered, but the application
               having been withdrawn on 10.11.2025, there was no inhibition
               in admitting Section 95 application filed by Financial Creditor
               against the Appellants herein. The real question to be considered
               and answered is whether due to withdrawal of Section 95
               application by Canara Bank, the prohibition which triggered due
               to interim moratorium shall come to an end. When application

               Canara Bank was withdrawn. Section 101 of the IBC provides

                                                            15

               moratorium under Section 14 of the IBC, any arbitration

100. In Vineeta Sharma v. Rakesh Sharma [Vineeta Sharma v.
Rakesh Sharma, (2020) 9 SCC 1: (2021) 1 SCC (Civ) 119] this
Court described the nature of prospective, retrospective, and
retroactive laws: (SCC p. 53, para 61)

“61. The prospective statute operates from the date of its
enactment conferring new rights. The retrospective statute
operates backwards and takes away or impairs vested rights
acquired under existing laws. A retroactive statute is the one that
does not operate retrospectively. It operates in futuro. However,
its operation is based upon the character or status that arose
earlier. Characteristic or event which happened in the past or
requisites which had been drawn from antecedent events.”

101. The terms “retrospective” and “retroactive” are often used
interchangeably. However, their meanings are distinct. This
Court succinctly appreciated the difference between these
concepts in State Bank’s Staff Union (Madras Circle) v. Union of
India [State Bank’s Staff Union (Madras Circle) v. Union of India,
(2005) 7 SCC 584: 2005 SCC (L&S) 994]:

“Retroactivity” is a term often used by lawyers but rarely defined.
On analysis it soon becomes apparent, moreover, that it is used
to cover at least two distinct concepts. The first, which may be
called “true retroactivity”, consists in the application of a new rule
of law to an act or transaction which was completed before the
rule was promulgated. The second concept, which will be referred
to as “quasi-retroactivity”, occurs when a new rule of law is
applied to an act or transaction in the process of completion….
The foundation of these concepts is the distinction between
completed and pending transactions….” [T.C. Hartley, The
Foundations of European Community Law 129 (1981).]”

Tata Capital Financial Services Ltd. v. Neel Motors LLP and Ors.,
(2026) ibclaw.in 4165 HC

“102. Many decisions of this Court define “retroactivity” to mean
laws which destroy or impair vested rights. In real terms, this is
the definition of “retrospectivity” or “true retroactivity”. “Quasi-
retroactivity” or simply “retroactivity” on the other hand is a law
which is applicable to an act or transaction that is still underway.
Such an act or transaction has not been completed and is in the
process of completion. Retroactive laws also apply where the
status or character of a thing or situation arose prior to the
passage of the law. Merely because a law operates on certain
circumstances which are antecedent to its passing does not mean
that it is retrospective.

103. In the present case, RCFL issued the debentures and
defaulted on the payments to the debenture-holders prior to the
issuance of the SEBI Circular. However, as of 13-10-2020 (the
date on which the SEBI Circular came into force), a compromise
or agreement on the restructuring of the debt owed by RCFL did
not exist. The debenture-holders were not vested with any rights
with respect to the resolution of RCFL’s debt. The existence of the
debt and the subsequent default by RCFL was the status of
                                                            19

               events, which existed prior to 13-10-2020. Once it came into force,
               the SEBI Circular applied to the manner of resolution of debt, as
               specified therein.”

                                                                              [Emphasis Supplied]”

               14. A plain reading of the foregoing would make it clear that the
               term “retroactive”, while being used differently in different
               judgements is actually a prospective application of a newly
               introduced requirement of a law to facts existing as of the date of
               the introduction of the requirement. The mere reason that some
               facts may have already been into existence but continue to exist
               would not mean that the application becomes retrospective.

               15. Applying the same principles to Section 96(4) of the IBC, in

               applies to such applications already filed and pending or that it
               would apply only to applications filed after the aforesaid sub-
               section (4) is added by way of amendment to Section 96 of the
               IBC.”

               “2. The parties in support of the proposition that the amendment
               applies retroactively emphasize that the amendment was brought
               about to address the mischief of rampant misuse of the interim
               moratorium under Section 96 of the IBC triggered upon filing of
                                            22

applications under Sections 94 and 95 thereof. The parties
resisting such an interpretation insist that sub-section (4) of
Section 96 of the IBC introduced by way of amendment with
effect from 26.05.2026 applies prospectively i.e. to applications
filed under Sections 94 and 95 of the IBC after the amendment
has come into force.”

40. We are of the opinion that the aforesaid material, particularly
the report of the Select Committee brought to our notice, clearly
indicates that the very purpose of bringing about the amendment
for adding sub-section 4 to Section 96 of the IBC, was to address
the mischief of rampant and deliberate misuse of the interim
moratorium triggered under Section 96 of the IBC, merely by filing
of applications under Sections 95 and/or 96 of the IBC.

41. As a matter of fact, judicial notice of such gross misuse was
also taken by the Courts. It was found that the route of filing
applications under Sections 94 and/or 95 of the IBC, was being
taken, not with the purpose of triggering the procedure for
examination of applications and preparation of report by the
resolution professional, for the applications to be considered for
admission or rejection by the adjudicating authority, but only
with the sole intention of triggering and enjoying interim
moratorium under Section 96 of the IBC. It was found that the
applications under Section 94 and 95 were being indiscriminately
filed, certain papers being collected and eventually thrown into
the Registry of the NCLT, to claim that the moment such
applications were filed, interim moratorium was triggered under
Section 96 of the IBC. It came to notice that creditors had started
adopting this path at stages, when the creditors were on the
verge of completion of lawful methods undertaken for realization
of their dues.”

“47. It is crucial that the resolve of the legislature for remedying
the mischief is given its full effect, so that the mischief is
suppressed and the remedy is advanced. The Courts must
interpret such amendments to add life and force to the cure and
remedy, rather than adopting an interpretation that fails to take
forward the cure and remedy in its true spirit. The said Mischief
Rule/Heydon’s Rule has been applied consistently in our legal
system and hence, it is an accepted Rule of interpretation. We
find that the said Rule applies in the facts of the present case, to
advance the proposition that sub-section 4 to Section 96 of the
IBC introduced by way of amendment, must apply retroactively
to pending proceedings also. This would advance the cure and
remedy of addressing the mischief and it would not be
appropriate to apply the same only prospectively, in the context
of applications filed on or after 26.05.2026.

48. In this context, an attempt was made on behalf of the parties
in these cases, advancing the proposition that the amendment
ought to be applied prospectively, to claim that the applications
filed bona fide, under Sections 94 or 95 of the IBC for insolvency
resolution of personal guarantors to a corporate debtor, should
not be adversely affected and painted in the same brush as
mischievously initiated applications. We are of the opinion that
                                            23

when the legislature has acted, specifically taking note of the
rampant mischief and misuse of the said provisions and larger
and considerable public interest is involved in bringing about the
said amendment, it ought to be applied in such a manner that it
takes forward the dominant intention and purpose of the
amendment.

49. The parties whose proceedings are still pending, cannot claim
that the amendment should not apply and in the process, call
upon the Courts to go into the question as to whether such
applications for insolvency process of personal guarantors to
secured creditors, have been filed mischievously or not. As noted
hereinabove, in the context of the procedural nature of the
mechanism between Sections 94 to 99 of the IBC, such parties
cannot claim a vested right to such interim moratorium under
Section 96 of the IBC.”

“54. We find that the contentions raised by the
creditors/petitioners in these petitions, concern retroactive
operation of amendment and hence, the said contention that
literal reading would show that the amendment cannot apply
retrospectively, is without any substance. The said submission
made on the basis that use of the words ‘is filed’ must necessarily
mean that the amendment can only apply prospectively, is found
to be without any substance. There is nothing to indicate that use
of the words in sub-section 4 of Section 96 of the IBC, would
necessarily mean that it would apply to the applications to be
filed in future. If that was the case, the legislature would have
used words such as ‘to be filed’ or other such similar phrases. In
our opinion, a proper and even literal reading of the said provision
added by way of amendment, in the backdrop in which it has
been inserted, clearly demonstrates that the words ‘is filed’
necessarily include applications / proceedings under Sections 94
or 95 of the IBC, that were pending on the date on which the
amendment was brought into effect. Hence, we do not find any
substance in reliance placed on behalf of the respondents on the
judgment in the case of B. Premanand Vs. Mohan Koikal (supra).”

“60. At this stage, it would be relevant to note that Section
96(1)(b)(ii) of the IBC provides that the creditors of the debtor shall
not initiate any legal action or proceeding in respect of any debt.
Initiation of legal action or proceeding in respect of any debt does
not mean that the creditor cannot even file a proceeding. The
effect of the said provision would be that any such proceeding
would remain inchoate and ineffective. We find that Section
96(1)(b)(ii) of the IBC cannot be read to oust the jurisdiction of a
Court or tribunal which would otherwise have jurisdiction. It only
prevents a proceeding filed to have any effect during the interim
moratorium and obviously such a proceeding if filed cannot be
labelled as void ab initio.”

CONCLUSION & ANSWER TO THE QUESTION

64. In view of the above, we answer the question framed at the
outset in this judgment by holding that sub-section (4) of Section
96 of the IBC added by way of amendment with effect from
26.05.2026, applies retroactively and hence, even to pending
                                                            24

               proceedings. Although it operates from the said date in futuro, its
               effect is equally on proceedings pending on the said date under
               Sections 94 and/or 95 of the IBC.
               65. In the light of the conclusion rendered hereinabove, we are in
               agreement with the findings rendered by the learned Single
               Judge of this Court in the judgment in the case of Tata Capital
               Financial Services Limited Vs. Neel Motors LLP and others [(2026)
               ibclaw.in 4165 HC] (supra) and order of the learned Single Judge
               of Delhi High Court in the case of IDBI Trusteeship Services
               Limited vs. Manish Jain & Ors. [(2026) ibclaw.in 4732 HC]
               (supra).”

               Section 96 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (for
               short, ‘the IBC’) introduced by way of amendment with effect from
               26.05.2026 would operate retroactively or not. In other words,
               whether Section 96 (4) of the IBC would apply where an
               application is filed under Sections 94 or 95, respectively thereof
               for initiating an insolvency resolution process in respect of a
               personal guarantor to a corporate debtor to applications already
               filed and pending or that it would apply only to applications filed
               after the aforesaid Sub-Section 4 came to be added by way of
               amendment to Section 96 of the IBC.

               3.The High Court, after due consideration of all the relevant
               aspects of the matter thought fit to answer the question in the
               following terms: -

                   “64. In view of the above, we answer the question framed at
                   the outset in this judgment by holding that sub-section (4) of
                   Section 96 of the IBC added by way of amendment with effect
                   from 26.05.2026, applies retroactively and hence, even to
                   pending proceedings. Although it operates from the said date
                   in future, its effect is equally on proceedings pending on the
                   said date under Sections 94 and/or 95 of the IBC.
                   65. In the light of the conclusion rendered hereinabove, we are
                   in agreement with the findings rendered by the learned Single
                   Judge of this Court in the judgment in the case of Tata
                   Capital Financial Services Ltd. vs. Neel Motor LLP and
                                                            25

                   Ors. (supra) and order of the learned Single Judge of Delhi
                   High Court in the case of IDBI Trusteeship Services Ltd. vs.
                   Manish Jain & Ors. (supra).”
               4. We are of the view that no error, not to speak of any error of
               law could be said to have been committed by the High Court in
               passing the impugned judgment and order.
               5.The petitions fail and are accordingly, dismissed.

47. We also notice that the IBBI has also issued a circular, being Circular No.
IBBI/II/106/2026, dated 21 September 2026, whereby, in pursuance of the law laid
down by the Hon'ble Bombay High Court in Tata Capital Financial Services Limited
and by the Hon'ble Delhi High Court in IDBI Trusteeship Services Limited, it has
been directed that the interim moratorium under Section 96 of the Code in respect
of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor has ceased to operate with effect from
26 May 2026 in respect of applications pending before the adjudicating authority as
on that date. The instant circular has been issued in exercise of the powers conferred
on the IBBI under Section 196 of the Code.
48. We also notice that in Shabbir Abbas Patel Supra) Hon’ble Bombay High Court
has considered the relevant part of the Report of the Select Committee on the IBC
(Amendment) Bill, 2025 in order to assess the purpose of the amendment and
observed that the purpose for which the amendment was introduced can be gathered
from the material that triggered the legislature to introduce sub-section 4 in Section
96 of the IBC. The reasons and justification given by the Ministry for the proposed
Amendment in para 47.3 reveals that the interim moratorium under section 96
provides that upon the filing of an application, all legal actions or proceedings
pending in respect of the concerned debt shall remain stayed, and creditors shall not
initiate any legal action or proceeding in respect of such debt. The Adjudicating
Authority (AA) has, on occasion, expressed concerns regarding the misuse of
initiation of the individual insolvency resolution process by personal guarantors to
take advantage of the interim moratorium. Clause 47 therefore seeks to insert a new
                                                            26

sub-section (4) to section 96 of the Code to provide that provisions of section 96 of
the Code will not apply where an application to initiate an insolvency resolution
process in respect of a personal guarantor to a corporate debtor is filed by a creditor
or the debtor itself.
49. In the report it is highlighted that the Bill seeks to introduce a new sub-section
excluding application of interim moratorium in respect of personal guarantor to a CD
to prevent wrongful disposal of assets by PG as the interim moratorium under section
96 provides that upon the filing of an application, all legal actions or proceedings
pending in respect of the concerned debt shall remain stayed, and creditors shall not
initiate any legal action or proceeding in respect of such debt. Concerns have also
been expressed regarding the misuse of initiation of the individual insolvency
resolution process (IIRP)by PGs to take advantage of the interim moratorium. To
remove any perverse incentives to initiate IIRP, the proposed amendment seeks to
dispense with the concept of interim moratorium for the insolvency of PG to CD.
50. We are also of the considered view and may say with our experience that
whenever recovery proceedings begin against a personal guarantor, the guarantor
rushes to file an application before the Adjudicating Authority, invoking interim
moratorium and this provision has been widely misused and this Appellate Tribunal
has also flagged the issue in many of its judgments.
51. Thus, it is evident that in the aforesaid case law, it has been held that the
amendment incorporated under Section 96 of the Code, with effect from 26 May
2026, would apply retroactively, i.e. to the proceedings which were pending even
before the amendment was made under Section 96 of the Code and it is also crystal
clear that by virtue of the aforesaid amendment made under Section 96 of the Code,
there was no moratorium after the effective date, i.e., 26 May 2026. The impugned
orders are admittedly passed on 31st August 2026, when there was no moratorium
in operation.
                                                            27

52. Therefore, having regard to the law placed hereinbefore and the reasons given,
we are of the considered view that the amending provisions made under Section 96
of the Code would apply retroactively, and also to the pending proceedings. Since the
impugned order has been passed on 31st August 2026, when there was no interim
moratorium, in our considered opinion, no illegality has been committed by the
adjudicating authority in passing impugned orders. For the reasons aforesaid, the
appeals, in our considered opinion, lack merit and are dismissed as such.
