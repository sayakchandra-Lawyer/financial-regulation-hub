Per: Barun Mitra, Member (Technical)
         The present appeal filed under Section 61 of the Insolvency and
Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (‘IBC’ in short) by the Appellant-Suspended Director
of Dharan Infra-EPC Ltd. arises out of the Order dated 16.10.2026
(hereinafter referred to as the ‘Impugned Order’) passed by the Adjudicating
Authority (National Company Law Tribunal, Mumbai Bench, Court-V) in
C.P.(IB)/729(MB)2025. By the said impugned order, the Adjudicating
Authority has dismissed I.A No. 166 of 2026 filed by the Interim Resolution
Professional (‘IRP’ in short) under Section 12A of the IBC seeking withdrawal
of the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (‘CIRP’ in short) initiated
against Dharan Infra-EPC Ltd.-Corporate Debtor.
2. The salient facts of the present case which are relevant to be noticed for
consideration of the matter at hand is that the Corporate Debtor had been
admitted into CIRP on 12.12.2025 following a Section 7 petition filed by Tata

                                                   Page 3 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
Capital Housing Finance Ltd.-Financial Creditor. The IRP appointed for
conduct of CIRP proceedings published Form A on 15.12.2025 inviting claims
from the creditors of the Corporate Debtor. The last date to constitute the
Committee of Creditors (‘CoC’ in short) was 04.01.2026. However, before the
CoC was constituted, the Appellant offered a settlement proposal to the
Respondent No.1-Financial Creditor which settlement proposal was accepted
by the Respondent No. 1. On full and final settlement amount having been
received, Respondent No. 1 furnished Form FA to the IRP on 01.01.2026
requesting the withdrawal of CIRP of the Appellant-Corporate Debtor.
Accordingly, I.A No. 166 of 2026 was filed through the IRP on 06.01.2026
before the Adjudicating Authority seeking withdrawal of
C.P.(IB)/729(MB)2025. The Adjudicating Authority took notice that six other
financial creditors had submitted their respective claims to the IRP and also
opposed the withdrawal of the CIRP by way of filing intervention petitions. The
Adjudicating Authority declined the Section 12A proposal, and aggrieved by
the impugned order, the present appeal has been preferred by the suspended
Director.

3. Making submissions on behalf of the Appellant, Shri Shikhil Suri, Ld.
Sr. Counsel for the Appellant, submitted that in the present facts of the case,
the CIRP admission of the Corporate Debtor occurred on 12.12.2025; Form
FA was submitted by the Financial Creditor-Respondent No. 1 on 01.01.2026
and Section 12A withdrawal application vide I.A No. 166 of 2026 was filed on
06.01.2026. The CoC had not yet been constituted at the time when the
withdrawal application was filed. Furthermore, all the three events viz. CIRP

                                                   Page 4 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
admission, submission of Form FA and filing of withdrawal application had
preceded the coming into operation of the amended Section 12A. Submission
was pressed that since the settlement with the original Financial Creditor-
Respondent No. 1 had reached its logical conclusion even before the CoC was
constituted, the debt stood extinguished and there was no pending issue of
debt or default qua the Respondent No. 1.

4. It was pressed strongly that a Corporate Debtor company can be
admitted into CIRP only on a judicial finding returned by the Adjudicating
Authority of a default as defined under the IBC. In the present case, when
there is no subsisting debt and default, the Adjudicating Authority committed
an error in disallowing the Section 12A withdrawal application. Mere receipt,
verification or admission of claims of other Financial Creditors by the IRP was
not a cogent ground for rejecting the Section 12A application. Once the default
amount relied upon by the original Financial Creditor-Respondent No. 1
forming the basis of the Section 7 petition stood fully and finally settled, the
withdrawal application should have been allowed. Even if there existed
outstanding claims of other Financial Creditors which were yet to be settled,
any such purported debt and default relied upon by other objecting Financial
Creditors opposing the Section 12A withdrawal could not have been
entertained by the Adjudicating Authority for it required independent judicial
determination that atleast one of their claims constituted a default as defined
under the IBC. In the absence of an adjudicated default at the time of
consideration of the withdrawal application, the Corporate Debtor has to be
automatically released from the rigours of CIRP. It was emphatically asserted

                                                   Page 5 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
that there has to be a proper application of judicial mind by the Adjudicating
Authority for determination and consideration of the issue of subsistence of
any debt and default. It was contended that the Adjudicating Authority erred
in failing to carry out the said adjudicatory exercise and to reject the
withdrawal application.

5. Heard. We have also perused the records carefully.

6. Coming to the statutory framework, it is Section 12A of the IBC which
deals with withdrawal of applications admitted under Sections 7, 9 and 10 of
the IBC. Section 12A of the IBC was amended vide Insolvency and Bankruptcy
Code (Amendment) Act, 2026 and the amended provision came into operation
w.e.f. 26.05.2026. When we look at the impugned order, we find that it has
been duly considered by the Adjudicating Authority as to whether Section 12A
was applicable in its pre-amended version or as per the amended version in
the present matter. To arrive at its finding, the impugned order has taken
cognisance of the chronological sequence of three critical events and taken
notice that the Corporate Debtor was admitted into CIRP on 12.12.2025; the
Financial Creditor had submitted claims in Form FA on 01.01.2026 and I.A.
No. 166 of 2026 seeking withdrawal of the CIRP was filed on 06.01.2026. It
also concluded that these developments clearly preceded 26.05.2026 being
the date from which amended Section 12A came into operation. Hence, in our
considered view, the Adjudicating Authority has rightly held that the present
application is to be considered in accordance with the pre-amended Section
12A of the IBC.

                                                   Page 6 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
7. For ease of convenience, we would now like to advert our attention to
the pre-amended Section 12A which is as extracted below:

         “12A. The Adjudicating Authority may allow the withdrawal of
         application admitted under section 7 or section 9 or section 10,
         on an application made by the applicant with the approval of
         ninety per cent. voting share of the committee of creditors, in such
         manner as may be prescribed.”

8. This now brings us to the CIRP Regulation 30A which deals with
applications for withdrawal of Section 12A. In the present case, admittedly
the CoC had not been constituted at the time when the Section 12A
application was filed. The IRP had also admittedly filed Section 12A
application under CIRP Regulation 30A(1)(a) of the CIRP Regulations which is
as extracted below:

     “30A. (1) An application for withdrawal under section 12A may be made
     to the Adjudicating Authority –
     (a) before the constitution of the committee, by the applicant through the
     interim resolution professional;”
When we peruse the above CIRP Regulation 30A(1)(a), it clearly provides that
an application for withdrawal under Section 12A can be made before the
constitution of the CoC through the IRP.

9. When the pre-amended Section 12A of IBC is read in conjunction with
Regulation 30A(1)(a) of the CIRP Regulations, we have no ambiguity in our
minds that the statutory framework permits withdrawal of the CIRP through
the IRP with the approval of 90% voting share of the CoC.

10. Coming to the impugned order, we find that the Adjudicating Authority
rejected the Section 12A application on the grounds as excerpted hereunder:

                                                   Page 7 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
11.13 In the present case, six other Financial Creditors have not only
submitted their respective claims to the IRP but have also filed
Intervention Petitions opposing withdrawal of the CIRP and seeking its
continuation. The settlement in question is confined to one Financial
Creditor for Rs. 10 Crore against the claimed default of Rs.
28,04,91,115/-. The claims of the Intervenors are substantial, one of
them being approximately Rs. 183 Crore. Further, the IRP had informed
the Hon’ble NCLAT that claims aggregating to approximately Rs. 600
Crore had been received. The other Financial Creditors are not parties to
the settlement, and no material has been placed on record to establish
that their claims have also been settled or that any such settlement is
proposed. The ability of the Corporate Debtor to resolve its financial
liabilities vis-a-vis its creditors is a relevant consideration while
determining an application for withdrawal of CIRP. A settlement with
one Financial Creditor, in the wake of significant larger amount
pertaining to the other creditors remaining outstanding, does not, by
itself, establish the Corporate Debtor’s ability to resolve its financial
distress. Furthermore, it is also relevant to note that the higher threshold
of approval by 90% of the voting share of the CoC under Section 12A of
the Code is intended to safeguard the interests of the significant majority
of creditors and ensure that withdrawal of CIRP is not permitted solely
on the basis of a settlement with an individual creditor or some creditors.

12.  Having considered the facts and circumstances of the present

case, the implications of withdrawal, and the objectives of the Code, we

are of the considered opinion that the CIRP initiated by admitting Petition

No. 729 of 2025, cannot be permitted to be withdrawn solely on the basis

of a settlement with one Financial Creditor. Accordingly, I.A. No.

166/2026 is dismissed.

11. Further when we peruse Para 11.11 of the impugned order, it is clear

that the Adjudicating Authority has placed reliance on the judgment passed

by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in GLAS Trust Company LLC Vs. BYJU

Raveendran & Ors. in Civil Appeal No. 9986 of 2024 wherein the Hon’ble

Apex Court had considered the issue of withdrawal of CIRP prior to

constitution of the CoC and held that that after initiation of CIRP, the

proceedings assume the character of proceedings in rem and no longer remain

                                                   Page 8 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
the preserve of the original applicant. It is also pertinent to add here that

GLAS Trust judgement is dated 23.10.2024 and hence falls in the era of pre-

amended Section 12A and therefore squarely applicable in the present case.

12. The Adjudicating Authority has thereafter at para 11.13 of the

impugned order taken notice that in the present case, six other financial

creditors submitted their respective claims to the IRP and opposed the

withdrawal of the CIRP by way of filing intervention petitions. It has also

noticed that the IRP had earlier informed this Tribunal that claims aggregating

to approximately Rs. 600 Cr. had been received by him. The Adjudicating

Authority has therefore held that CIRP cannot be permitted to be withdrawn

solely on the basis of a settlement with one Financial Creditor or some

creditors since the approval of 90% of the CoC members warranted by Section

12A is to safeguard majority of the creditors.

13. At this stage, we must advert our attention to the relevant portions of

the GLAS Trust judgement supra as reproduced below:

   “42. From this scheme of Chapter II of the IBC, it appears that the admission
   of an application is a significant event that alters the nature of the
   proceedings, and the stakeholders involved. Initially, when the petition is
   filed by the financial creditor, operational creditor or corporate applicant, as
   the case may be, the proceedings are in personam and the only relevant
   stakeholders are the applicant creditor and the corporate debtor. However,
   once the petition is admitted and CIRP is initiated, several significant
   changes take place, including the transfer of the management of the affairs
   of the corporate debtor to the IRP, the declaration of the moratorium, and the
   collation of the claims against the corporate debtor. Therefore, the
   proceedings now change character – they become in rem and are no longer
   the preserve of only the applicant creditor and the corporate debtor and even
   creditors who were not the original applicants, become necessary
   stakeholders.

   Regulations lays down an exhaustive procedure for the withdrawal of an
   application filed by creditors under Sections 7, 9, or 10 of the IBC.
   Withdrawal may be sought at four stages, all of which have a procedure

14. When we peruse the GLAS Trust judgement supra, we find that the

Hon’ble Apex Court identified four different stages during which a procedure

for the withdrawal of CIRP or settlement of claims is contemplated in the IBC

framework under the pre-amended Section 12A provisions. The situation

applicable in the present case is the second stage outlined at para 63(ii) of the

                                                   Page 10 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
GLAS Trust judgement supra where the Section 7 application of a Financial
Creditor has been admitted and CIRP initiated, though, the CoC has not yet
been formed. The proceedings had clearly turned into in rem proceedings
involving all creditors as necessary stakeholders.

15. We notice that in the present factual matrix, upon admission of the
Corporate Debtor into CIRP, six other Financial Creditors had filed
intervention petitions before the Adjudicating Authority. These intervenors
had also submitted that withdrawal of CIRP be not permitted solely on the
basis of settlement between the Appellant and Respondent No. 1. It is also
noticed that the IRP had admitted that claims amounting to approximately
Rs. 600 Cr. had been received.

16. In terms of the GLAS Trust judgment, at the post-CIRP admission but
pre-CoC stage, the Adjudicating Authority is required to hear the concerned
parties, consider all relevant factors and apply its judicial mind. Once CIRP is
initiated, the proceedings change character from in personam to in rem
proceedings and are no longer the preserve of the original Section 7 Financial
Creditor- Applicant. Merely because the settlement with the original Financial
Creditor-Respondent No. 1 had materialised, does not make it incumbent
upon the Adjudicating Authority to allow the withdrawal application without
hearing the contentions of all other concerned parties including creditors who
were not original applicants but were opposed to the withdrawal. The
Adjudicating Authority after having taken cognisance of the objections of
other creditors to the withdrawal proposal, who are stake-holders in the
present proceedings which has since assumed the character of in rem

                                                   Page 11 of 12

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 2062 of 2026
proceedings, no infirmity is found in the impugned order in rejecting the
application for withdrawal.

17. In result, we find the Appeal to be devoid of merit. The impugned order
does not warrant any interference. The Appeal is dismissed with no costs.
