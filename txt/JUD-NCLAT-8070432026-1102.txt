JUSTICE YOGESH KHANNA, OFFICIATING CHAIRPERSON

         This Company Appeal (At)(Ins) No.1175/2026 is filed under Section 61 of
IBC challenging the order dated 19.06.2026 passed by Ld. NCLT, Mumbai
Bench, Mumbai in CP(IB)/640(MB)/2026 whereby in an application under
Section 95 of the Code filed by Respondent No.1, the Adjudicating Authority has
appointed Resolution Professional under Section 97, issued the interim
moratorium under Section 96, and has directed the Resolution Professional to
examine the application, including limitation and invocation of the personal
guarantee and to submit a report under Section 99(I).

2. It was argued by the learned senior counsel for appellant the Respondent
No.1 has no locus to file the proceedings under Section 95 of the Code as there
was no contract of guarantee executed by the appellant and Respondent No.1
and the personal guarantee was furnished only in favour of the Debenture
Trustee (IDBI Trustiship Services Ltd) for the benefit of all debenture holders and
under the Debenture Trust Deed, the power to declare an event of default,
accelerate, invoke the guarantee and initiate proceedings vest exclusively in the
Debenture Trustee, acting only on the written instructions of the majority
debenture holders representing not less than 75% outstanding debentures.

3. Respondent No. 1 (IIFL One Value Fund - Series B) is, on the admitted
record of the TDPL Committee of Creditors, only a minority debenture holder -
its voting share is 24.51% (claims as on commencement tabulated at the 20th
CoC Meeting dated 13.06.2026), whereas Rare Asset Reconstruction Ltd. holds
                                                               4

65.77% and Renaissance Fiscal Services Pyt. Ltd. 4.49%, the TDPL debentures
having been assigned to Rare ARC (30.03.2024) and to Renaissance
(29.06.2024). Respondent No. 1 thus cannot constitute and has not obtained the
instructions of, the Majority Debenture Holders, and the Debenture Trustee has
neither authorised nor filed the Petition. The invocation and the Petition are
therefore without authority and non-est.

4. It was argued this very objection was raised before the Ld. Adjudicating
Authority, which by its Order dated 12.06.2026, the Bench recorded that counsel
for the Personal Guarantor had taken it through the stipulations of the
Debenture Trust Deed and contended the petition is not maintainable for non-
compliance with those stipulations and reserved the matter. Yet by the Impugned
Order dated 19.06.2026 the Adjudicating Authority appointed the Resolution
Professional without deciding that threshold question of locus, instead remitting
the issue of “invocation” to the Resolution Professional’s examination.

5. It was argued the recitals to the Supplementary Debenture Trust Deed
confirm this scheme, wherein* Recital (C) records even the amendment of the
Principal DTD was effected by the Debenture Trustee acting on the instructions
of the Majority Debenture Holders a fortiori, the graver steps of default,
invocation and the initiation of insolvency proceedings could not be taken by a
minority holder acting unilaterally.

6. The Appellant submits the Adjudicating Authority ought to have
determined the threshold question of locus before setting the Section 99

9. Though the Counsel for Personal Guarantor tried to make his

regard, we refer to judgement of the Hon'ble Supreme Court, in

         envisaged in Section 95 to Section 99 of the !BC;

         (ii) The Resolution Professional appointed under Section
         97 serves a facilitative role of collating all the facts
         relevant to the examination of the application for the
         commencement of the insolvency resolution process
         which has been preferred under Section 94 or Section 95.
         The report to be submitted to the Adjudicating Authority
         is recommendatory in nature on whether to accept or
         reject the application."

23. We have also heard the learned senior counsel for the Respondent also

and we are of the considered view the issue of locus and maintainability cannot

be adjudicated prior to the stage of Section 100 of the IBC and our this view is

supported by the law laid down in Dilip B Jiwarajka V. Union of India & Ors

(2024) 5 SCC 435, wherein the Hon’ble Supreme Court held:-

         “Para 86.6 No adjudication is involved at the stages envisaged in Section 95
         to Section 99 of the IBC” and “no judicial determination takes place until the
         adjudicating authority decides under Section 100 whether to accept or reject
         the application.”
24. Further in Bank of Baroda Vs Farooq Ali Khan (2025) SCC OnLine 374 it

was held:-

         9. The Adjudicating Authority, by its order dated 16.02.2024, has followed the
         procedure envisaged under Sections 95 to 100 of the IBC, and has also relied on
         the afore-stated principles in Jiwrajka (supra). It specifically observed that
         respondent no. 1’s objections regarding limitation and waiver of the guarantee will
         be considered once the resolution professional submits his report. This is the correct
         approach as the appointment of a resolution professional at the very threshold is
         statutorily mandated under Section 97 of the IBC. As has been held by this Court
         in Jiwarajka (Supra) the Adjudicating Authority does not adjudicate any point at
         this stage and need not decide jurisdictional questions regarding existence of the
         debt before appointing the resolution professional.
25. In Canara BANK Vs Uma Kumari Singh 2025 SCC OnLine NCLAT 1514 decided

by three judges bench, this Tribunal held:

         “Para 9-11. We have heard Counsel for the Parties and after examining the record,
         are of the considered opinion that there is an error committed by the Ld. Tribunal
         in dismissing the application, in all the three cases, filed by the Bank on the first
         the date of hearing on the ground of limitation because it has been concluded by
         the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the case of Dilip B Jiwrajka (Supra) that no judicial
         adjudication is involved at the stages in Section 95 and 99 of the Code.
         10.Thus in view of the aforesaid facts and circumstances the present appeals
         succeeds and the impugned order passed in all three appeals are hereby set aside.
         The application filed under Section 95 are hereby restored to its original number.
         The matter(s) are remanded back to the Learned Tribunal to decide the same in
                                                              15

         accordance with the law. The parties are directed to appear before the Tribunal on
         23.09.2025.

         11.Before parting, we would also like to add that the Respondents shall have a
         right to raise the issue of limitation when the Tribunal would exercise its
         adjudicatory jurisdiction.

26. In CL Sharma V Bank of Maharashtra 2024 SCVC OnLine NCLAT 279 this

Tribunal held:-

         “11.The law declared by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in the above
         judgement (Jiwrajka) is very clear. The Adjudicating Authority
         should consider the issue pertaining to jurisdictional fact at the stage
         of Section 100 and there is no adjudication contemplated at the stage
         when RP is appointed under Section 97 sub-section (5). The
         conclusion recorded in paragraph 86(iii) clearly lays down the
         aforesaid. The submission of the Appellant(s) that applications were
         barred by time, ought to have been considered at the time when
         Adjudicating Authority appointed the RP, thus, cannot be accepted.

27. In Central Bank of India Vs Deepen Arun Parekh, this Tribunal held as under:-

         36. We are of the considered opinion that Adjudicating Authority committed error
         in considering the objection raised by the Respondent on the merits of the
         application under Section 95 filed by the Central Bank of India at the stage when
         RP was not even appointed. The Adjudicating Authority proceeded to enter into
         adjudicatory issues, which can be taken by the Adjudicating Authority only at the
         time of hearing of Section 95 application under Section 100, as is now the law
         declared by the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Dilip B Jiwrajka’s case, as noted above.

28. Further in Prabhaben Ravjibhai Harkani Vs Bank of Baroda, Company Appeal

(AT) (Ins) No.1002/2025 decided on 07.08.2026 this Tribunal held:-

         This judgement applies to applications filed under Section 94 of the IBC i.e.
         applications filed by the debtor itself. It will not apply to applications filed under
         Section 95 of the IBC i.e. applications filed by creditors/personal guarantors.

         This judgement itself recognizes that different procedures apply to proceeddings
         under Section 94 and Section 95.

         39. The learned counsel for the appellant has also relied upon the judgement of the
         Hon’ble Supreme Court in Di8lip Jiwrajka V Union of India (2024) 5 SCC 435 to
         contend that once an application under Section 94 is filed, the Adjudicating
         Authority is required to appoint a Resolution Professional before examining the
         application on merits. It is, therefore, necessary to consider whether the said
         judgment has an application to the facts of the present case.

29. Thus in view of the law laid down by the Hon’ble Supreme Court and also

three judges bench of this Tribunal, we are not inclined to interfere with
                                                              16

impugned order of the Ld. NCLT and dismiss the appeal filed by the appellant as
per settled law viz the adjudicatory process shall start only after the report is
filed by the Resolution Professional per Section 100 of the IBC.
30. The appeals are accordingly dismissed. Pending applications are also
disposed of.
