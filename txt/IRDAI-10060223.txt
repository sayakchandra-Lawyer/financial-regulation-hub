1. The Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India (“the Authority”)
     issued a public consultation paper titled “Recalibrating Economics of Insurance
     Distribution” on 23 September 2026. The paper proposed reforms covering the
     structure of insurance distribution, expenses of management and commission,
     market conduct, transparency and the use of digital infrastructure. Stakeholders’
     views on these proposals were sought through 32 questions.

2. The public consultation received an overwhelming response. By the extended last
     date of 31 October 2026, about 2.18 lakh answers to the 32 questions were
     received from over 13,000 persons. The responses reflect the breadth of the
     insurance ecosystem and the seriousness with which participants have engaged
     with the proposals.

3. The reforms' objectives remain to lower the cost of insurance, improve
     policyholder outcomes, widen coverage, and support a sustainable and
     professional distribution ecosystem. Insurance should be accessible, affordable
     and fairly sold, and distribution should reward quality advice, service and long-
     term customer relationships. These goals are central to IRDAI's mandate of
     protecting policyholders' interests and promoting the orderly growth of the sector,
     in line with the national vision of insurance for all.

4. IRDAI hereby constitutes an Expert Committee of independent economists and
     insurance professionals to advice further on strengthening the design and
     implementation of its distribution reforms. The Committee's role is to help make
     the framework as robust, clear and effective as possible. Drawing on the depth of
     expertise available across the sector and beyond, it will advise on how the
     proposals can be further enhanced and refined, so that they are implemented
     smoothly and deliver their intended benefits. Its work will complement IRDAI’s
     ongoing engagement with all segments of the insurance industry.

5. The Terms of Reference (ToR) of the Committee are proposed as under:

        (i) To examine the proposed reforms and recommend additional measures
                  or refinements, if any, that may be incorporated to achieve a fair and
                  sustainable balance between the interests of policyholders, insurers and
                  distributors.

        (ii) To assess whether the proposed measures carry the potential of adverse
                  impact on employment in the insurance sector and on the income of
                  individual agents and other distribution persons and, if so, to suggest
                  suitable refinements in the proposed reform measures to mitigate such
                  impact.

        (iii) To suggest appropriate transition pathways, including phasing, glide
                  paths and supportive measures, to address the concerns of smaller
                  distributors and smaller insurers.

        (iv) To recommend an appropriate framework, including principles,
                  parameters and a review mechanism, for determining the commission
                  limits applicable to smaller premium policies.

        (v) To examine whether additional measures are required to expand
                 insurance coverage in smaller towns, rural areas and underserved
                 segments, and to recommend such measures.

        (vi) To examine whether the large commercial risk segment would be better
                 served by enhanced transparency and a gradual shift towards fee-based
                 services, and to recommend the manner and pace of such transition, if
                 found appropriate.

        (vii) Any other matter incidental or related to the above, as the Committee
                   may consider relevant, or as may be referred to it by the Competent
                   Authority.
