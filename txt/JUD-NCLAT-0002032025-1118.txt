18.11.2026         We have heard Shri Anuj Kumar Khanolkar, Learned Counsel

appearing for the Appellant, as well as Ms. Honey Satpal, Learned Counsel

appearing for the RP and perused the record.

         The instant appeal has been preferred by the Appellant, being one of the
secured creditors of the Corporate Debtor, aggrieved by the impugned order dated
12.12.2024, whereby an application moved by the Resolution Professional (RP) of
the Corporate Debtor (CD) - M/s. Yug Weave Tech Private Limited being I.A. No.
335 of 2023, has been disposed of with the directions that the pending CIRP
cost/professional fees of the Applicant/Resolution Professional amounting to Rs.
16,48,000/-, shall be paid to him within 15 days from the order.

         Learned Counsel for the Appellant submits that the Learned Adjudicating
Authority has committed a mistake by passing the impugned order, as the
Appellant was only obliged to pay such fee to the Resolution Professional which it
has ratified. In this regard, the attention of this Appellate Tribunal has been drawn

                                                                                               Page 1|5
on the 6th CoC meeting held on 02.11.2021, in order to show that in this CoC
meeting the fee of the Resolution Professional up to the month of October 2021,
has been ratified and approved for payment and therefore, the Appellant was not
obliged to pay any fee to the Resolution Professional beyond what has been
approved in this meeting i.e. till October 2021.

         The attention of this Court has also been drawn to Regulation 34 of CIRP
Regulations, 2016, and schedule II of the same in order to show that the fees of the
Resolution Professional could only be paid until the closure of the CIRP, and
therefore this aspect of the matter has not been considered by the Learned
Adjudicating Authority.

         It is further submitted that the Learned Adjudicating Authority has
committed a mistake by allowing the application moved by the Resolution
Professional and therefore, the impugned order may kindly be set aside

         Learned Counsel appearing for the RP vehemently submits that, in the 1st
CoC meeting held on 25.11.2020, the fee of the Appellant/Resolution Professional
was fixed at Rs. 1 lac in accordance with Regulation 34 of the CIRP Regulations,
2016, and schedule II of the regulations.

         She further submits and has drawn our attention towards the 6th CoC
meeting minutes held on 02.11.2021 to show that in this meeting the Authorised
Representative of the sole member of the CoC has asked the RP to reduce the
outstanding fee and the RP had informed him that all the works related to CIRP
and compliances with regard to the Corporate Debtor are to be done by him timely
and there are other works also which is to be handled by him regularly and the fee
is already less and he will continue his fee of Rs. 1 lac per month till the
appointment of liquidator by the Learned Adjudicating Authority.

                                                                                               Page 2|5
         It is further submitted that, since the aforesaid fee was never modified either
by the CoC or by the Learned Adjudicating Authority, the Appellant was obliged to
pay the same until the CD had gone into liquidation vide order dated 06.07.2022.

         It is further submitted that the reliance of the Appellant on schedule II of the
CIRP Regulations, 2016, is patently wrong, as the said schedule only provides the
minimum fixed fee, and it says that the minimum fixed fee shall be applicable for
the period from appointment as Interim Resolution Professional or the Resolution
Professional, as the case may be, till the time of either submission of application for
approval of the Resolution Plan or to liquidate the Corporate Debtor or submission
of application under Section 12A of the Code, or till order for closure of CIRP
(whichever is earlier) and since in this case the liquidation order has been passed
on 06.07.2022 the Appellant was duty-bound to pay the fees up to the period of
06.07.2022.

         We have heard Learned Counsel for the Parties and have perused the record.

         It is reflected that in the 1st meeting of the CoC held on 25.11.2020 the fee of
the Resolution Professional was decided to be fixed as Rs. 1 lac per month, plus
professional charges. It is not in dispute that the RP has continued and has
discharged his obligations throughout the CIRP. The only objection which has been
raised by the Appellant is that the RP was not having any work to do and therefore
after October 2021 the Appellant is not obliged to pay the fee of the RP, as the
Appellant has ratified his fee till October 2021.

         There are certain other objections which have been raised by the Appellant
with regard to the allegations that the RP has not performed his duties up to the
mark, and that some theft has been taken placed in the premises of the Corporate
Debtor during the tenure of the RP and the same has not been properly pursued by

                                                                                               Page 3|5
the RP. The defence of the counsel for the RP on this is that the RP had continued
the services of the same security agency which was appointed by the Appellant and
the FIR was also filed by the RP with regard to the said theft referred to by Learned
Counsel for the Appellant.

         Be that as it may, we notice that the Learned Adjudicating Authority, in the
impugned order, has taken note of the fact that, in the CoC meeting held on
21.11.2020 the CoC resolved to appoint the IRP and his fee of Rs. 1 lac per month,
plus (applicable taxes) was fixed and thereafter nothing is available on record which
may suggest that the fee of the RP was ever curtailed and made subject to any
specific function to be performed by him.

         The reference of the 6th CoC meeting, which has been given by Learned
Counsel for the Appellant, in our considered opinion, would not be of any help to
the Appellant, as in the said meeting it has only been resolved that the fee of the RP
up to the month of October 2021, is hereby ratified and approved for payment.

         We are of the considered view that once the fee of the RP has been fixed by
the CoC the same could only be altered by the CoC by considering a specific agenda
for the same or by the Learned Adjudicating Authority and not otherwise.

         We also notice that, in the 6th CoC meeting itself, when an objection has
been taken by the representative of the Appellant with regard to continuance of
payment of the fee to the RP, the RP has categorically replied that he has performed
various statutory functions on behalf of the Corporate Debtor and thereafter, we
notice that the matter has not been taken any further by the Appellant.

         Having regard to all the facts and circumstances of this case, we are of the
considered view that once the RP was appointed by the CoC and his fee has been
approved as Rs. 1 lac per month and there is no change in that fee, the said fee is

                                                                                               Page 4|5
to be paid in accordance with the eventualities described under schedule II of the
CIRP, 2016.

         In the instant case, by passing the impugned order, the Learned
Adjudicating Authority had directed that the fee up to the initiation of the
liquidation process against the Corporate Debtor i.e., till 06.07.2022 be paid to the
RP.

         We do not find any illegality or to say any irregularity in the
observations/conclusions drawn by the Learned Adjudicating Authority and thus,
in view of the aforesaid facts and circumstance, the appeal, in our considered
opinion lacks merit and is hereby dismissed.

         There is no order as to cost. The pending I.A., if any, is also disposed of.

         We further direct the Appellant to immediately pay the balance fee of the RP,
as determined by the Learned Adjudicating Authority, within a period of one month
from today.
