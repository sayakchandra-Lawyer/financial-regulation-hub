[Per: Justice N. Seshasayee, Member (Judicial)]

05.11.2026: These twin appeals were preferred by the personal
guarantors who face a proceeding under Section 95 of IBC.

2. These petitions under Section 95 of IBC are filed through a
resolution professional as has been provided in Section 95 and his
appointment was eventually confirmed under Section 97 of IBC. The
resolution professional also has filed his report under Section 99 of IBC.
In his report, the resolution professional has recommended initiation of
PIRP against the appellants herein. The appellants have filed separate
replies in their separate petitions and the petitions are being heard by
the Adjudicating Authority.

3. Be that as it may. On 16.03.2025 the resolution professional
passed away and for few months this fact was not brought to the notice

Comp App (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 591 & 592 / 2026          Page 2 of 6
of the Tribunal. Indeed, according to the appellants, it was they who
informed the tribunal about the passing away of the resolution
professional. It is in these circumstances, the financial creditor have
moved IA No.396/2024 proposing a new resolution professional to be
appointed. Even though, the Adjudicating Authority posted the matter
on few days in January - February, 2026, no final decision has been
taken on the appointment of resolution professional to replace the one
who has passed away. Indeed, the appellants resists the appointment
of the successor resolution professional who Bank of Baroda, the
financial creditor, has recommended, essentially because according to
him this resolution professional has been suspended by IBBI. He adds
that even though this order of suspension has been stayed by the
Hon’ble High Court of Gujarat yet he has not come clean as yet.

4. The learned counsel for the appellant would submit that without
a resolution professional to defend the action, the Adjudicating
Authority has kept that issue aside and is focusing in concluding the
hearing on the matter. Given the fact that it is the resolution
professional who has initiated the proceeding, on his demise there is
none to prosecute the petition. Therefore, the Adjudicating Authority
has seriously prejudiced with the hearing on Section 95 petition without
there being anybody to represent the petitioner.

5. Mr. Edward James takes notice for the Bank of Baroda / the sole
respondent herein. Arguing for the respondent, Mr. P.H. Arvindh
Pandian, the learned senior counsel, would submit that the role of the
resolution professional in the context is no more than an authorisee of
the Bank of Baroda. Even in the petition filed by Bank of Baroda
through the resolution professional, and it is not the resolution
professional who has filed the petition in his name. Again, in the
verification portion, the resolution professional states that he has been
authorized by the Bank of Baroda to prefer the petition. He adds that
given the circumstances, the resolution professional is only an
authorisee of the financial creditor and he has no personal interest in
the action he has initiated. He submits that the entire exercise now
staged before this tribunal is intended to delay the conclusion of the
proceedings.

6. We have little hesitation in holding that we are least impressed by
the stance adopted by the appellants that the financial creditor has
chosen to institute the petition under Section 95 through the resolution
professional is but a statutory facility provided to it. At no point the
resolution professional claims any interest in the cause of action that
has given rise to the institution of the petition under Section 95. This
apart, the resolution professional himself makes a statement in the
verification column that he has been authorized by Bank of Baroda, and

Comp App (AT) (CH) (Ins) No. 591 & 592 / 2026  Page 4 of 6
Bank of Baroda also makes a statement at the opening page of the
petition that it is filing the petition through the resolution professional.

7. Secondly, a report of the resolution professional under Section 99
of the IBC is no more than recommendatory in its utility. At the end of
the day, the appellants have to defend the action proposed against them
on the strength of their defence.

8. Turning to the appointment of a resolution professional with
suspected integrity or whatever of which we do not have much clarity,
we wonder why Bank of Baroda should insist and persist with the one
whose name apparently has not been cleared by the court. There are
any number of resolution professionals, and the Bank of Baroda should
have considered the issue even when the resistance has come in the
first instance. After all, it is the stance adopted by the Bank of Baroda
that has given rise to this situation in a way.

9. The learned counsel for Bank of Baroda would now submit that
he would advise his client appropriately not to insist on someone whose
conduct has not yet been cleared by the courts of law in this country. If
Bank of Baroda propose any new resolution professional, the
Adjudicating Authority may consider the same, and if not we require the
Adjudicating Authority to appoint a resolution professional of his choice
so that situation such as this do not arise.
