Brief Facts:-
2) United Bank of India - the assignor, predecessor-in-
interest of the appellant-Company, sanctioned and extended
loan facility of Rs.12,26,00,000/- to M/s Kalpataru Cold Storage
Private Limited, the Corporate Debtor (CD), vide Loan
Sanction Letter dated 28.03.2013.
3) According to the appellant-Company, the first default
occurred on 31.12.2013 in the repayment of loan to the United
Bank of India.
4) On 27.02.2014, the Corporate Debtor made a payment of
Rs. 18,00,000/- to the United Bank of India.
5) On 31.03.2014, the account of the Corporate Debtor was
declared as a Non-Performing Asset (NPA).

                                                                                                                2
6) The appellant-Company claims that on 08.10.2014, the
Corporate Debtor submitted a One-Time Settlement (OTS)
proposal to the United Bank of India acknowledging the debts
due.

7) On 10.10.2014, the United Bank of India informed the
Corporate Debtor that the OTS proposal was not acceptable
and sought for an upgradation of the proposal.

8) On 16.10.2014, the Corporate Debtor requested
reconsideration of the earlier OTS proposal.

9) The appellant-Company submits that in the Balance
Sheet of the Corporate Debtor for the Financial Year 2015-16,
the loan amount was reflected as an outstanding liability. It is
the claim of the appellant-Company that the OTS proposal and
the balance sheet entries constituted acknowledgement of
debt. When the matter stood thus, on 29.03.2017, by a
registered Assignment Agreement, executed between the
United Bank of India and the appellant-Company, the debts of
the United Bank of India, pertaining to M/s Kalpataru Cold

                                                                                                                3
Storage Pvt. Ltd., were assigned in favour of the appellant-
Company.
10) It is the appellant’s further case that such
acknowledgement in the balance sheet entries were available
in the Balance Sheet for the Financial Year 2016-17 also.
11) Further, the appellant-Company submits that, on
30.06.2017, the OTS offer was repeated whereby the
Corporate Debtor agreed to offer an amount of
Rs.10,50,00,000/-
12) According to the appellant-Company, further, the
Balance Sheet for the Financial Year 2017-18, also
acknowledged the debt.
13) It was at this stage that the appellant-Company filed a
petition on 26.10.2018 under Section 7 of the IBC in C.P. (IB)
No. 1498/KB/2018 before the National Company Law Tribunal,
Kolkata Bench (hereinafter ‘Adjudicating Authority’) setting
out total outstanding debt of Rs. 21.61 crores.

                                                                                                                4
14) The Adjudicating Authority set out two questions for

consideration; (1) whether the applicant-(the appellant-

Company herein) can be said to be financial creditor on the

basis of the Deed of Assignment dated 29.03.2017; and, (2)

whether the debt was time-barred.

15) The Adjudicating Authority answered question no. 1 in

the affirmative and held the appellant-Company to be a

financial creditor. On the contention of the debt being time-

barred, it held as follows: -

       “16. However, there is one more dimension as far as case
       in hand is concerned. The financial creditor and even
       corporate debtor have produced on record the corporate
       debtor’s balance-sheet for the year ending March, 2016
       (page no.300 onwards in paper book). From the perusal of
       those financial papers of the corporate debtor, it is seen
       that corporate debtor noted that they have to pay the debt
       of United Bank of India. Their account is declared as NPA.
       In short, in the year 2016, the corporate debtor in its
       balance-sheet acknowledged the debt to be payable by
       them which is claimed herein by the financial creditor. This
       acknowledgement of debt in its balance-sheet by the
       corporate debtor in the year 2016 has brought the claim of
       financial creditor within period of limitation. It is filed well
       within period of limitation. We hold that the debt is not
       time-barred. We answer point no.2 in the negative.”

Having found that the debt was not time-barred, the

16) Respondent No.1 carried the matter in appeal to the

held that there was no acknowledgement in the financial

the impugned order (i.e. NCLT order dt. 30.09.2019) was
quashed and set aside.

27) At this juncture, it is apt to refer to Section 18 of the

Limitation Act, 1963, which reads as under:-

       “18. Effect of acknowledgment in writing.—(1) Where,
       before the expiration of the prescribed period for a suit or
       application in respect of any property or right, an
       acknowledgment of liability in respect of such property or
       right has been made in writing signed by the party against
       whom such property or right is claimed, or by any person
       through whom he derives his title or liability, a fresh period
       of limitation shall be computed from the time when the
       acknowledgment was so signed.
       (2) Where the writing containing the acknowledgment is
       undated, oral evidence may be given of the time when it
       was signed; but subject to the provisions of the Indian
       Evidence Act, 1872 (1 of 1872), oral evidence of its contents
       shall not be received.
       Explanation.—For the purposes of this section,—

               (a) an acknowledgment may be sufficient though it
               omits to specify the exact nature of the property or
               right, or avers that the time for payment, delivery,
               performance or enjoyment has not yet come or is
               accompanied by a refusal to pay, deliver, perform or
               permit to enjoy, or is coupled with a claim to set off,
               or is addressed to a person other than a person
               entitled to the property or right,
               (b) the word “signed” means signed either
               personally or by an agent duly authorised in this
               behalf, and
               (c) an application for the execution of a decree or
               order shall not be deemed to be an application in
               respect of any property or right.”

                                                                                                              15
28) In Dena Bank (Now Bank of Baroda) vs. C. Shivakumar

Reddy and Another, (2021) 10 SCC 330, this Court held as

under: -

       “139. Section 18 of the Limitation Act cannot also be
       construed with pedantic rigidity in relation to
       proceedings under the IBC. This Court sees no reason
       why an offer of one-time settlement of a live claim, made
       within the period of limitation, should not also be
       construed as an acknowledgment to attract Section 18 of
       the Limitation Act. In Gaurav Hargovindbhai Dave v. Asset
       Reconstruction Co. (India) Ltd., (2019) 10 SCC 572 cited by
       Mr Shivshankar, this Court had no occasion to consider
       any proposal for one-time settlement. Be that as it may,
       the balance sheets and financial statements of the
       corporate debtor for 2016-2017, as observed above,
       constitute acknowledgment of liability which extended
       the limitation by three years, apart from the fact that a
       certificate of recovery was issued in favour of the
       appellant Bank in May 2017. The NCLT rightly admitted
       the application by its order dated 21-3-2019.”

                                                          [Emphasis supplied]

This Court held that Section 18 of the Limitation Act cannot be

construed with pedantic rigidity in relation to proceedings

under the IBC.

29) In Vidyasagar Prasad vs. UCO Bank and Another, 2024

SCC OnLine SC 2993, this Court observed:-

       “10. Having considered the specific facts and
       circumstances of this case, the Adjudicating Authority

                                                                                                              16
       as well as the National Company Law Appellate
       Tribunal have concurrently held that the entries in the
       balance-sheets amount to clear acknowledgment of
       debt. We agree with the findings. Further, note 3.4
       appended to said balance-sheet entry dated March 31,
       2017 mentions that “company has made certain defaults in
       the repayment of term loans and interest.” It further
       mentions of a continuing default. The entry also mentions
       long-term borrowings. The conclusions of the National
       Company Law Tribunal and National Company Law
       Appellate Tribunal that there is acknowledgment of debt
       are unimpeachable.

               10.1. Following the principles as expounded in the
               case of Asset Reconstruction Co. (India) Ltd. v. Bishal
               Jaiswal, (2021) 6 SCC 366, the Adjudicating Authority
               as well as the National Company Law Appellate
               Tribunal have examined the case in detail and have
               come to the conclusion that the entry made in the
               balance-sheet coupled with the note of the auditor of
               the appellant clearly amounts to acknowledgment of
               the liability. We see no reason whatsoever to take a
               different view of the matter. Their findings are
               fortified when we examine the matter from another
               perspective.”

                                                          [Emphasis supplied]

30) In IL & FS Financial Services Limited vs. Adhunik

Meghalaya Steels Private Limited, (2026) 3 SCC 794, this

Court, after an exhaustive consideration of the law, held: -

       “33. It was not disputed before us that entries in
       balance sheets could constitute a valid
       acknowledgement and in fact it could not have been
       disputed, in view of the categoric pronouncement of
       this Court in Asset Reconstruction Co. (India) Ltd. v.
       Bishal Jaiswal, (2021) 6 SCC 366. The only dispute was
       whether the entry in FY 2019-20 did or did not constitute
       a valid acknowledgement. Among the grounds

                                                                                                              17
canvassed was the aspect that the name of the
appellant was not mentioned in the balance sheet of FY
2019-20.

35. The other aspect which remains to be examined is the
contention of the respondent that the name of the appellant
is nowhere mentioned in the balance sheet of FY 2019-20
and as such the balance sheet of FY 2019-20 cannot be
construed as an acknowledgement of any jural relationship
between the parties. To counter this aspect, the appellant
has drawn attention to the judgment of this Court in
Vidyasagar Prasad v. UCO Bank, (2025) 17 SCC 234. In
Vidyasagar Prasad this Court, at the outset, dealt with the
earlier judgments in Laxmi Pat Surana v. Union Bank of India,
(2021) 8 SCC 481, Dena Bank v. C. Shivakumar Reddy,
(2021) 10 SCC 330 and Rajendra Narottamdas Sheth v.
Chandra Prakash Jain, (2022) 5 SCC 600 to reiterate that
Section 18 of the Limitation Act dealing with
acknowledgment of debt applies to proceedings under the
IBC in view of Section 238-A.

37. It will be noticed that even in Vidyasagar Prasad v. UCO
Bank, (2025) 17 SCC 234 a similar argument about the name
of creditor not being mentioned was repelled and
additionally the aspect of the proposal given by the
corporate debtor therein for a one-time settlement was
taken into account as an additional aspect in favour of
acknowledgment of debt.

39. We have independently examined the facts of the
present matter to construe whether the entries in the
balance sheet of FY 2019-20 constitute a valid
acknowledgement. As to whether a certain document in a
given case constitutes a valid acknowledgement would
depend on the facts and circumstances of each case. We
do no better than recall the observations of this Court in
Khan Bahadur Shapoor Fredoom Mazda v. Durga Prasad
Chamaria, 1961 SCC OnLine SC 147 wherein it was
observed as under: (SCC OnLine SC para 7)

       “7. It is often said that in deciding the question as to
       whether any particular writing amounts to an
       acknowledgment as in construing wills, for instance, it
       is not very useful to refer to judicial decisions on the

                                                                                                     18
       point. The effect of the words used in a particular
       document must inevitably depend upon the context in
       which the words are used and would always be
       conditioned by the tenor of the said document, and so
       unless words used in a given document are identical
       with words used in a document judicially considered it
       would not serve any useful purpose to refer to judicial
       precedents in the matter.”

40. Having said that, the legal principles as to what
constitutes a valid acknowledgment as laid down in the
precedents, have to be rigorously applied. It should
also not be forgotten that this Court in Khan Bahadur
Shapoor Fredoom Mazda v. Durga Prasad Chamaria,
1961 SCC OnLine SC 147 has held that surrounding
circumstances could be considered and that a liberal
construction should be favoured, though the process of
reasoning should not be involved or far-fetched. This
Court in Khan Bahadur Shapoor Fredoom Mazda v. Durga
Prasad Chamaria, 1961 SCC OnLine SC 147 had considered
the general tenor and context of the document. Further, as
noticed in Lakshmirattan Cotton Mills Co. Ltd. v. Aluminium
Corpn. of India Ltd., (1971) 1 SCC 67, the previous
correspondence and the surrounding circumstances were
also taken into consideration. In Asset Reconstruction Co.
(India) Ltd. v. Bishal Jaiswal, (2021) 6 SCC 366, this Court
held that a case-to-case examination will be made with
regard to entries made in balance sheets to decide the
question of acknowledgment. In Dena Bank v. C.
Shivakumar Reddy, (2021) 10 SCC 330, this Court held that
in relation to proceedings under the IBC, Section 18 of the
Limitation Act cannot be construed with pedantic rigidity.
In Vidyasagar Prasad v. UCO Bank, (2025) 17 SCC 234,
this Court affirmed the finding of NCLAT in that case
wherein NCLAT had held that the company's balance
sheet is prepared in the statutory format as per
Schedule III of the Companies Act which did not
provide for giving the specific name of every secured
or unsecured creditor.

42. Keeping all these principles in mind, if we examine
the facts of the present case, it will be clear that the
balance sheet of FY 2019-20, viewed in the background

                                                                                                     19
       of the other admitted documents, including the
       financial statements of the previous years, clearly
       constitutes a valid acknowledgment of a subsisting
       liability and indicated the existence of a jural
       relationship and an admission as to the existence of
       such relationship. We say so for the following reasons:

       42.1. The general tenor and context of the balance sheet
       of FY 2019-20 considered in the background of
       surrounding circumstances arising from the balance
       sheets of FY 2015-16, 2016-17 and 2017-18 clearly points
       to the fact that the entry in the balance sheet of FY 2019-
       20 constitutes a valid acknowledgement and pertains to
       the same borrowing as was reflected in the balance
       sheet of FY 2015-16, 2016-17 and 2017-18.

       42.2. Under the Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) 7,
       a cash flow statement is appended to the financial
       statement. The cash flow statement indicates that in FY
       2018-19 there was proceeds from borrowings of Rs
       72,30,902 and added to Rs 23,68,91,933, a figure of Rs
       24,41,22,835 is arrived at.

       42.3. More importantly, in the cash flow statement it
       was indicated that no part of cash flow proceeds was
       utilised in the repayment of existing borrowings under
       the financial activities since the amount under the head
       “cash flows from (used in) financial activities” is nil.
       This clearly indicates that the debt remained unpaid
       even in 2019-20.”

                                                          [Emphasis supplied]

31) Applying the law to the present set of facts, the following

scenario emerges. The Balance Sheet for the Financial Year

2015-16 ending 31.03.2016 clearly reflects long term

borrowings to the tune of Rs.6,17,32,832/-. Further, it is shown

                                                                                                              20
that long-term borrowings as on 31.03.2016 and borrowings
from the bank are to the tune of Rs. 4,11,62,832/-.

32) We are satisfied that there is a clear acknowledgement of
liability in terms of long-term borrowings in the balance
sheets for the Financial Years 2015-16, 2016-17 and 2017-18.
Respondent No. 1 does not dispute that what is on record is
the correct Balance Sheet for the Financial Year 2016-17.
Hence, the argument that Balance Sheet for the Financial Year
2016-17 was not placed before the Courts below, does not
impress us. The Balance Sheets for the Financial Years 2015-
16 and 2017-18 were admittedly on record.

33) The Notes relied upon also do not carry the case of the
Respondent any further insofar as the acknowledgement of
liability is concerned. In fact, there is a contradiction in the
Notes. While Note 5 speaks of no default continuing or
otherwise as on the date of Balance Sheet, Note 29 has a
different story to tell, if the following para from the Note is
perused: -

                                                                                                              21
       “….Company’s accounts were declared non
       performing assets (NPA) earlier on certain defaults in
       repayment of loans installments and interest dues
       thereon to the Bank. A Notice under Section 13(2) of the
       SARFAESI Act,2002 has been served by Bank on
       03.04.2014 and date of declaration of NPA is
       31.03.2014, the matter is sub judice before the Debts
       Recovery Tribunal (DRT).”

34) In view of the above, we are clearly of the opinion that
Section 7 application was clearly within time. The NCLAT
clearly erred in setting aside the order of the Adjudicating
Authority. If the acknowledgements of the balance sheets of
2015-16 2016-17 and 2017-18 are taken, the Section 7
application filed on 26.10.2018 is clearly within time. The
default admittedly occurred originally on 31.12.2013 and the
account was declared NPA on 31.03.2014. The jural
relationship subsisted when the balance sheets
acknowledged the liability. The impugned judgment of the
NCLAT is dated 25.02.2020. Much water has flown on the
aspect of the entries in the balance sheets constituting valid
acknowledgement of debt in law. Even on facts, we are

                                                                                                              22
convinced that the NCLAT misread the balance sheets and the
notes appended thereto.
35) In any event, by this judgment, we are only holding that
the application is within time. As to whether as on the date of
the application under Section 7, there was any default, is a
matter which can be decided once the matter is remitted.
Before the Adjudicating Authority, we find that only two
contentions were raised viz., one with regard to the appellant-
Company not being a financial creditor and the other as to
whether the debt was time-barred. No argument was raised to
the effect that assuming the debt to be within time, there was
no default, as required under Section 7 of the IBC. However,
since the same was canvassed before us, on the special facts
of the case, we grant one opportunity to the respondent to
place his case before the Adjudicating Authority on the aspect
of default alone.

                                                                                                              23
36) For the reasons stated above, the appeal is allowed and
the impugned order passed by the NCLAT dated 25.02.2020
is set aside and we remit the matter to the Adjudicating
Authority to consider the merits of the plea of the respondent
on the aspect of occurrence of default under Section 7(5) of the
IBC. We make it clear that all other aspects including the
aspect of limitation in filing the Section 7 application stands
concluded by the present judgment. In view of our holding on
the aspect of valid acknowledgement in the balance sheets,
we are not considering the aspect with regard to the OTS. No
order as to costs.
