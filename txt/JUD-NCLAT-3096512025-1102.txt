JUSTICE YOGESH KHANNA, OFFICIATING CHAIRPERSON

         This appeal is filed against an impugned order dated 09.09.2025 passed
by the Ld. Adjudicating Authority (National Company Law Tribunal, Cuttack
Bench) in C.P. (IB) No.18/CB/2024. Vide the impugned order, the petition
filed under Section 7 of the Insolvency & Bankruptcy Code, 2016 (for short
the “IBC”) on 10.04.2024 was accepted and M/s. SS Aluminium Private
Limited was admitted into insolvency for default of Rs.15,88,33,394.26/-.

2. As per the averments made in the application under Section 7 of the
IBC, numerous credit facilities were given to the corporate debtor from time
to time and the loan of the company was declared Non-Performing Asset
(“NPA”) on 11.05.2021. Further, the recall notice was issued on 04.10.2021
for an outstanding amount of Rs.13,48,00,000/-. Thereafter a notice dated
17.05.2021 under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI Act, 2002, was issued and
the appellant had given the OTS dated 23.11.2021, 01.12.2021 and
13.01.2022 to the bank, but did not make any payment and ultimately on
23.12.2021, notice under Section 13(4) of the SARFAESI Act, 2002, was
issued and the proceedings were initiated against the asset of the corporate
debtor.

3. The application under Section 7 of the IBC was contested by the
appellant herein, primarily, on 2 grounds viz. (a) limitation and (b) the
procedure under the Notification dated 29.05.2015 issued by the Ministry of
Micro, Small and Medium Enterprises, annexed as Annexure A-2 to the
appeal, was never followed. It was argued, as per the Notification, the banks
were obliged to identify the incipient stress in the account of the Micro, Small
and Medium Enterprises (“MSME”) and after identification of the MSME,
could voluntarily initiate proceedings under this framework, if enterprise
reasonably apprehends failure of its business or its inability or likely inability

4. We have gone through the impugned order, which had dealt with the
issue of limitation and had rejected such contention saying not only there
were 3 OTS submitted by the appellant before the bank, but also on
13.07.2021, the appellant had executed an instrument of acknowledgement
of debt. It was observed by the Ld. Adjudicating Authority that on multiple
occasions, the settlement was sought by sending various OTS proposals and
thus the appellant had admitted its liability towards debt and the existence
of debt and default was purely established and the petition was within
limitation. We agree with this finding.

Notification, the Ld. Adjudicating Authority held as under:

6. The learned counsel for the appellant has referred to the additional
documents filed by him and referred to the I.A. No.4708/2026 filed for taking
on record the additional documents, wherein he annexed a letter dated
30.10.2023 written to General Manager, Canara Bank asking for revival of his
sick unit under Rehabilitation Scheme and such letter was duly answered on
10.11.2023, wherein the District Industry Centre, Balasore had requested the
General Manager, Canara Bank, Bhubaneswar to provide him cooperation
and re-finance for sustainable revival of the unit under Sick Rehabilitation
Scheme of RBI at the earliest. However, it was never done.

7. The said letters have been challenged by the learned counsel for the
respondent as are produced in this appeal for the first time. Nevertheless, it
was argued the MSME status of the appellant was valid only upto 31.03.2021
and thereafter the appellant ceased to be a MSME unit. It is also evident from
the record, no application was filed under Section 13(3-A) of the SARFAESI
Act, 2002, asserting, it was an MSME or intending to claim benefit of
framework. Further, if one peruse the Notification dated 29.05.2015, its
paragraph 4 says an application needs to be filed by the MSME to the
Committee for Stressed MSME, in the manner as may be specified by the
Bank for a corrective action plan and terms thereof. No such application was
ever filed when notice dated 17.05.2021 under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI
was given. Rather no reply was ever given to such notice.

9. In ‘Shri Swami Samarth Construction & Finance Solution & Ors.’
Vs. ‘The Board of Directors of NKGSB Co-op. Bank Ltd. & Ors.’, reported
in [MANU/SC/0997/2025], the Hon’ble Supreme Court has held:

                 “5. The Notification detailing the FRAMEWORK, more
                 particularly paragraph 1 and its sub-paragraphs, have
                 to be read together to make its terms effective and
                 meaningful. Although, in the sequence of the
                 FRAMEWORK “Identification by Banks or Creditors”
                 comes first, it is immediately followed by
                 “Identification by the Enterprise”. In terms of sub-
                 paragraph 2, any MSME may choose to voluntarily
                 initiate proceedings under the FRAMEWORK if it
                 “reasonably apprehends failure of its business or its
                 inability or likely inability to pay debts and before the
                 accumulated losses of the enterprise equals to half or
                 more of its entire net worth” (emphasis ours). The
                 obligation of the MSME does not end there. For
                 initiation of proceedings under the FRAMEWORK, the
                 application has to be verified by an affidavit of an
                 authorised person and upon receipt of a request, the
                 lending bank/secured creditor is mandatorily bound to
                 proceed in terms of the FRAMEWORK and to constitute
                 a committee to identify incipient stress in the account.

                 6. The way Mr. Nedumpara urges us to read the
                 Notification and the terms of the FRAMEWORK, if
                 accepted, would lead to the conclusion that every
                 lending bank/secured creditor under the SARFAESI
                 Act would be obliged to find out in every event of
                 continuing default, likely to give rise to classification of
                 the relevant account as NPA, whether the borrower is
                 an MSME to which the FRAMEWORK applies, whether
                 its business has failed or whether it is suffering from
                 any disability to pay its debts; and upon receiving a
                 response, to apply the terms thereof by, inter alia,
                 including the account in the Special Mention Account
                 for the claim for a corrective action plan to be
                 considered by the Committee for stressed MSMEs. This
                 could not have been the intention behind introduction
                 of the FRAMEWORK to aid the MSMEs which, for

Comp. App. (AT) (Ins.) No. 174 of 2026

                                                                               5 of 8
                 reasons personal to them, is unable to clear its debt
                 and require revival and rehabilitation that the
                 FRAMEWORK envisages. If indeed it is only the
                 obligation of the lending bank/secured creditor to
                 identify incipient stress in the account, sub paragraphs
                 2 and 3 of paragraph 1 would be rendered redundant.
                 An MSME, despite finding that its business is failing or
                 that it is unable to pay its debts or accumulation of
                 losses equals to half or more of its entire net worth and
                 classification of its account as NPA is imminent, it
                 would rest on its oars believing that it has no
                 responsibility and that its account will not be classified
                 as NPA because it is the entire obligation of the lending
                 bank/secured creditor to do what the FRAMEWORK
                 requires. We would read and interpret the seemingly
                 confusing terms of the FRAMEWORK harmoniously to
                 ensure that a right under the MSME Act is not
                 destroyed by the SARFAESI Act or vice versa. In our
                 reading, the terms of the FRAMEWORK do not prohibit
                 the lending bank/secured creditor (assuming that it
                 has no conscious knowledge that the defaulting
                 borrower is an MSME) to classify the account of the
                 defaulting MSME as NPA and to even issue the
                 demand notice under Section 13(2) of the SARFAESI
                 Act without such identification of incipient stress in the
                 account of the defaulting borrower (MSME); however,
                 upon receipt of the demand notice, if such borrower in
                 its response under Section 13(3-A) of the SARFAESI Act
                 asserts that it an MSME and claims the benefit of the
                 FRAMEWORK citing reasons supported by an affidavit,
                 the lending bank/secured creditor would then be
                 mandatorily bound to look into such claim keeping
                 further action under the SARFAESI Act in abeyance;
                 and, should the claim be found to be worthy of
                 acceptance within the framework of the FRAMEWORK,
                 to act in terms thereof for securing revival and
                 rehabilitation of the defaulting borrower.”

and it is incumbent upon the concerned MSME to produce authenticated and

verifiable documents/material for substantiating its claim as being MSME. In

Para 17 the Court further records on failure to bring to notice of banker that

11. Section 7 IBC, proceedings are primarily concerned with Debt and
Default and the RBI circular which is applicable to SARFAESI Proceedings
shall not stand as hurdle to decide the present applicationas held in ‘Amit
Narang’ Vs. ‘Aditya Birla Finance Limited & Ors.’ [(Para 20) (2024] 252
CompCas 781].

12. The Adjudicating Authority had rather considered, the Debt & Default
and Form C and Form-D of the NeSL Certificate and the loan account
statements as maintained by the Respondent Bank. It found, the default
exceeded Rs.one crore and the statutory threshold for initiation of proceedings
under Section 7 had been satisfied. The Adjudicating Authority has expressly
observed, the precise quantification of the claim may be examined by the
Resolution Professional during CIRP.

13. Thus, considering the law stated above, we find no reason to upset a
reasoned order passed by the Ld. Adjudicating Authority; firstly as the M/s.
S.S. Aluminium Pvt. Ltd. had lost its status of MSME on 31.03.2021 and
secondly it never send a response under Section 13(3A) of SARFAESI Act for
reconstruction of its debt and thirdly it was also the responsibility of the
corporate debtor/appellant to apply in terms of notification dated 29.05.2025
at the earliest, which it never did. Thus, we are not inclined to interfere with
the impugned order. Even the letters dated 30.10.2023 and dated 10.11.2023
(supra); relied upon by the appellant, were never produced before the Ld.
Adjudicating Authority, hence no benefit qua such letters, even otherwise, be

was claimed; hence these 2 letters filed by the appellant would not upset the

16.03.2026, last date of EOI was 31.03.2026, submission of plan was by
18.5.2026. The Tribunal itself has recorded in its order dated 14.05.2026
that there was no stay. Resolution Plan is filed and is awaiting approval of
the Ld. Adjudicating Authority. Non-participation by the Appellant in the
Resolution process evince casual nature of appeal. The appeal thus has no
merit and is accordingly dismissed. Pending applications are also disposed
of.
