Bankruptcy Code, 20163. Section 61(2) provided for an appeal being filed
within 30 days of the date of the order with a condonable delay of 15 extra

within the 30 day-period that expired on 25.04.2025 or at least within the

that the 45th day, being the last day of the extendable 15-day period under

condonation of the delay of three days in filing the said recall applications.

7. The specific case of the appellant in its recall applications was that
the decision of this Court in Tata Steel Ltd. vs. Raj Kumar Banerjee and
others5 barred the application of Section 4 of the Act of 1963 when an
appeal was filed beyond the prescribed period of 30 days under the IBC.
This Court categorically held therein that the expression ‘prescribed
period’ appearing in Section 4 of the Act of 1963 could not be construed
to mean anything other than the ‘period of limitation’ and any period
beyond that prescribed period, during which the Court or Tribunal has the
discretion to allow a person to institute the proceedings, cannot be taken
to be part of the ‘prescribed period’. Hence, as per the above decision, the
benefit of exclusion of the period during which a Court or Tribunal is closed
shall be available only when the appeal is filed within the ‘prescribed
period of limitation’ and shall not be available in respect of the period
extendable by the Court in exercise of its discretion.
8. Perusal of the impugned common order dated 10.02.2026 passed
by the NCLAT reflects that the delay of 3 days on the part of the appellant
in filing the recall applications was condoned but the recall applications
came to be dismissed. Though the aforestated decision of this Court was
specifically brought to its notice, the NCLAT opined that the order dated
10.10.2025 was passed by it on merits after hearing the counsel for the
parties and it could not be recalled on the basis of fresh evidence and

material. It was further observed that submission of a precedent, giving a
ruling different from the order sought to be recalled, subsequent to the
passing of the said order, cannot be a reason to recall such order as it
would not fall within the ambit of exercising inherent powers to rectify an
error apparent on the face of the record. According to the NCLAT, the only
scope left for the appellant was to invoke remedies as were available to it
in law. Holding so, the NCLAT rejected the appellant’s recall applications.
9. First and foremost, we may note that the NCLAT was in error in
opining that the decision of this Court in Tata Steel Ltd. (supra) was
subsequent to its order dated 10.10.2025, as Tata Steel Ltd. (supra) was
decided on 07.05.2025, long before the order dated 10.10.2025 was
passed by the NCLAT, condoning the delay in the filing of the appeals by
the respondents. Further, when this Court clarifies the legal position in
relation to a statutory provision, the same would be the law of the land and
there is no question of an order contrary thereto passed earlier by a Court
or Tribunal being saved, unless such order has attained finality.
10. It is no doubt true that the NCLAT does not have the power of review
under the provisions of the IBC but it is vested with the power of recalling
its orders by exercising its inherent jurisdiction under Rule 11 of the
National Company Law Appellate Tribunal Rules, 2016. This was the
import of the 5-Judge Bench decision of the NCLAT in Union Bank of
India (erstwhile Corporation Bank) vs. Dinkar T Venkatasubramanian

                                                        5
and others6, wherein it was held that, though it was not vested with power
to review a judgment, however, in exercise of its inherent jurisdiction, the
NCLAT can entertain an application for recall of a judgment on sufficient
grounds. This 5-Judge Bench decision of the NCLAT was confirmed by
this Court in Union Bank of India vs. Financial Creditors of Amtek Auto
Ltd. and others7. This Court observed therein that it was in agreement
with the view taken by the 5-Judge Bench of the NCLAT and found no
reason to interfere with the said judgment.
11. More recently, in Greater Noida Industrial Development
Authority vs. Prabhjit Singh Soni and another8, upon reviewing the
case law on the subject, a 3-Judge Bench of this Court observed that the
law which emerged from such decisions was that a Tribunal or a Court is
invested with such ancillary and incidental powers as may be necessary
to discharge its functions effectively for the purpose of doing justice
between the parties and, in the absence of a statutory provision, in an
appropriate case, it can recall its orders in exercise of such ancillary or
incidental powers.
12. Therefore, the NCLAT was not justified in refusing to exercise its
inherent jurisdiction even when apprised of the settled legal position with
regard to application of Section 4 of the Act of 1963. More so, as the

respondents did not even establish sufficient cause for condonation of the
the delay on their part in the filing of their appeals. Be it noted that
10.05.2025 was a second Saturday and the NCLAT was of the opinion
that the same constituted sufficient grounds to prolong the extended
limitation period to the 46th day. However, it is an admitted fact that the
appeals were e-filed on the next day, i.e., on 11.05.2025, which was also

well have been done on 10.05.2025 itself. In any event, the application of
Section 4 of the Act of 1963 in the case on hand was not valid, given the
legal position as set out in Tata Steel Ltd. (supra).
13. It has been contended by the learned senior counsel appearing for
the respondents that the common order dated 10.10.2025 passed by the
NCLAT, condoning the delay in the filing of the appeals, was not
separately challenged and as the time to do so has expired, the said order
is not liable to be interfered with. It is pointed out that there can be no
merger of the said order with the order dismissing the recall applications
and, therefore, condonation of delay by the NCLAT is deemed to have
attained finality. However, this specious argument loses sight of the fact
that the condonation of delay in the filing of the appeals did not attain
finality, as the recall applications filed by the appellant in relation thereto
kept the issue alive and when the NCLAT, in its wisdom, refused to
exercise its inherent jurisdiction to recall such order and the same was

                                                        7
subjected to appellate review before this Court, the same can be remedied
by this Court in exercise of jurisdiction under Section 62 of the IBC.
14. On the above analysis, this Court finds that entertainment of the
appeals by the NCLAT was illegal, as the appeals were filed beyond the
time prescribed in Section 61(2) of the IBC. Further, the NCLAT erred in
failing to apply the law laid down by this court in Tata Steel Ltd. (supra).
15. The appeals are accordingly allowed, setting aside the order dated
10.02.2026 passed by the NCLAT, Chennai, in the recall applications in IA
No. 183 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 388 of 2025 and
IA No. 232 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No. 389 of 2025.
The recall applications shall stand allowed and the common order dated
10.10.2025 in IA No. 1139 of 2025 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins) No.
388 of 2025 and IA No. 1141 of 2025 in Company Appeal (AT)(CH)(Ins)
No. 389 of 2025 shall stand set aside. In consequence, Company Appeal
(AT)(CH)(Ins) Nos. 388 and 389 of 2025 on the file of the NCLAT, Chennai,
shall stand dismissed on the ground of limitation.
