6. Heard. Perused the records with the able assistance of the Ld.
Counsels.

6.1 The relevant dates in this case are not in dispute. The impugned order
was pronounced on 17.07.2026 and the appeal was e-filed on 29.08.2026
which is the 44th day from the date of pronouncement of the order. It is not in
dispute that the appeal was not accompanied by a certified copy of the
impugned order.

6.2 I.A. No. 6524 of 2026 and I.A. No. 6525 of 2026 were taken up on
16.09.2026 and thereafter on 17.09.2026 when replies on the IAs were called
for. The Appellant/Applicant filed online application for obtaining the certified
copy of the impugned order only after this, on 29.09.2026, which is the 66th
day from the date of pronouncement of the impugned order.

6.3 The Appellant/Applicant has stated that it had initially tried to obtain
certified copy through offline mode. No evidence regarding this effort has been
furnished and we are thus unable to accept this plea.

8. The appeal has been filed under the provisions of Section 61 of the IBC,

2016. The relevant sub-section (2) of Section 61 reads as under:

            “61. Appeals and Appellate Authority

            (2) Every appeal under sub-section (1) shall be filed within thirty
            days before the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal:

            PROVIDED that the National Company Law Appellate Tribunal

9. A plain reading of the provisions extracted above shows that an appeal

against the order of Ld. NCLT has to be filed within 30 days and this Tribunal

has the authority, if it is satisfied there was sufficient cause for not filing the

appeal in time, to extend the period further by 15 days but no more. The outer

limit for filing an appeal under the IBC, 2016, including the condonable

period, is thus 45 days. In the present case, the appeal was filed on the 44th

day though it was not accompanied with the certified copy of the impugned

order.

10. We now look at the judicial guidelines on the controversy in question.
The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Civil Appeal No. 7882 of 2023 recently vide
judgment dated 14.10.2026 in the case of The Assistant Commissioner of
Central Tax, CGST Division v. Sreenivasa Rao Ravinuthala and another has
observed as under:

“8.  In V. Nagarajan (supra), this Court considered the

scheme of the IBC and, in parallel, examined the applicability of

the provisions of the Act, 1963 to the IBC. Instead of culling out

the excerpts, we will paraphrase the ratio of V. Nagarajan (supra)

as follows:

A.   The IBC is an exhaustive, self-contained code. By virtue

of the non-obstante clause in Section 238 of the IBC, its provisions

prevail over any inconsistencies with other statutes. While Section

238-A of the IBC applies the provisions of the Act, 1963 “as far as

            may be”, this is subject to Section 29(2) of the Act, 1963. Where a

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1720 of 2026
                7

special statute provides a distinct period of limitation, that period

governs to the exclusion of general provisions, and the IBC, being

a special law, would have an overriding effect. Section 61(1) of the

IBC starts with a non-obstante clause. Consequently, the

appellate mechanisms under the Companies Act cannot control or

relax the statutory architecture of the IBC.

B.          Rule 22(2) of the NCLAT Rules, 2016 mandates that

every Appeal be accompanied by a certified copy of the impugned

Order. Section 12(2) of the Act, 1963 excludes the time required to

obtain a copy of the impugned Order. The Explanation to Section

12 of the Act, 1963 makes it clear that any time the Tribunal or

Court takes to prepare the Order before the litigant applies for a

copy cannot be excluded. If a party fails to apply for a certified

copy within the limitation period, no time can be excluded under

Section 12(2) of the Act, 1963. Applying for a certified copy is not

a mere procedural formality; it reflects the aggrieved party’s due

diligence.

C.          Section 61(2) of the IBC prescribes a 30-day period for

filing an Appeal, extendable by a maximum of 15 days upon

demonstration of sufficient cause, and no more. Courts and

Tribunals have no power under Section 5 of the Act, 1963, or

inherent powers to condone delays beyond the outer limit of 15

days prescribed in special statutes. The IBC’s foundational

architecture weaves speed and strict timelines to preserve asset

value, maximise recovery for lenders, and protect economic health.

Procedural rules cannot be interpreted to protract litigation

indefinitely.”

11. The question whether an aggrieved party is under an obligation to apply
for a certified copy of the impugned order, and the consequence of its failure
to do so, is no longer res integra. A three-Judge Bench of the Hon’ble Supreme
Court in V. Nagarajan v. SKS Ispat and Power Ltd. & Ors., (2022) 2 SCC 244,
has held as under:

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1720 of 2026
                                                             8

            “31. The import of Section 12 of the Limitation Act and its
            Explanation is to assign the responsibility of applying for a
            certified copy of the order on a party. A person wishing to file an
            appeal is expected to file an application for a certified copy before
            the expiry of the limitation period, upon which the “time requisite”
            for obtaining a copy is to be excluded. However, the time taken by
            the court to prepare the decree or order before an application for a
            copy is made cannot be excluded. If no application for a certified
            copy has been made, no exclusion can ensue. In fact, the
            Explanation to the provision is a clear indicator of the legal position
            that the time which is taken by the court to prepare the decree or
            order cannot be excluded before the application to obtain a copy
            is made. It cannot be said that the right to receive a free copy
            under Section 420(3) of the Companies Act obviated the obligation
            on the appellant to seek a certified copy through an application.
            The appellant has urged that Rule 14 of the NCLAT Rules
            empowers NCLAT to exempt parties from compliance with the
            requirement of any of the rules in the interests of substantial
            justice, which has been typically exercised in favour of allowing a
            downloaded copy in lieu of a certified copy. While it may well be
            true that waivers on filing an appeal with a certified copy are often
            granted for the purposes of judicial determination, they do not
            confer an automatic right on an applicant to dispense with
            compliance and render Rule 22(2) of the NCLAT Rules nugatory.
            The act of filing an application for a certified copy is not
            just a technical requirement for computation of limitation
            but also an indication of the diligence of the aggrieved
            party in pursuing the litigation in a timely fashion. In a
            similar factual scenario, NCLAT had dismissed an appeal as time-
            barred under Section 61(2) IBC since the appellant therein was
            present in court, and yet chose to file for a certified copy after five
            months of the pronouncement of the order.

            ….

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1720 of 2026
                                                             9

            34. On the second question, Rule 22(2) of the NCLAT Rules
            mandates the certified copy being annexed to an appeal, which
            continues to bind litigants under IBC. While it is true that the
            tribunals, and even this Court, may choose to exempt
            parties from compliance with this procedural requirement
            in the interest of substantial justice, as reiterated in Rule
            14 of the NCLAT Rules, the discretionary waiver does not
            act as an automatic exception where litigants make no
            efforts to pursue a timely resolution of their grievance. The
            appellant having failed to apply for a certified copy, rendered the
            appeal filed before NCLAT as clearly barred by limitation.”

12. A three-Judge Bench of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in A. Rajendra v.

Gonugunta Madhusudhan Rao & Ors., (2025) 6 SCC 618 considered two

appeals which were filed on the 39th day from the date of pronouncement of

the order of the Ld. NCLT, that is, within the outer limit of forty-five days. In

the second appeal, no certified copy was appended and an application for

exemption from filing the certified copy was filed. The appellant had not

applied for the certified copy. The Hon’ble Supreme Court held as under:

            “26. However, where the judgment was pronounced in open Court,
            the period of limitation starts running from that very day. The
            appellant is however entitled to seek relief under Section 12(2) of
            the Limitation Act for excluding the period during which the
            certified copy was under preparation on an application preferred
            by that party.
            27. In light of the above legally settled position, when the facts of
            the present case is seen, the first thing which is apparent is that
            in the absence of any certified copy having been applied by the
            appellant of the impugned orders dated 20th July 2023 passed
            by the NCLT on which it was admittedly pronounced, with Rule

            22 of the NCLAT Rules mandating filing of the appeal along with
            the certified copy. The appeals as preferred by the appellant need
            to be dismissed as they were filed beyond 30 days and no steps
            have been taken by the appellant to seek certified copy of the
            order.

            28. That apart, the second appeal, on this score alone is to
            be dismissed as there is no question of moving an
            application for condonation of delay when no application
            for obtaining a certified copy of the order has been filed.
            Exemption from filing of certified copy, as has been
            referred to above, cannot be claimed as a matter of right in
            terms of the statutory requirements of the Rules. As regards
            the first appeal, which was accompanied with the certified copy
            supplied free by NCLT the same also being beyond the period of
            limitation and the time of ten days as sought to be exempted for
            the preparation and making available the certified copy cannot be

13. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Ashdan Properties Pvt. Ltd. & Ors. v. DSK

Global Education and Research Pvt. Ltd. & Anr., 2025 SCC OnLine SC 1682,

impugned order. The certified copy was applied for only after the expiry of

under:

            “11. … Though the National Company Law Appellate
            Tribunal is clothed with the powers to exempt and to extend
            time under Rules 14 and 15 of the NCLAT Rules
            respectively, such powers cannot be exercised so as to
            render Rule 22(2) thereof nugatory, as pointed out in V
            Nagarajan (supra). Even if an appeal is entertained within
            the initial 30 day-period without a certified copy of the
            order, by granting exemption under Rule 14, it can only be
            for a limited period to enable due compliance with the
            mandate of Rule 22(2) by filing a certified copy at least at
            a later date and within the time stipulated by the National
            Company Law Appellate Tribunal. Such exemption cannot be
            to the extent of completely dispensing with the filing of a certified

            12. Viewed thus, the fact that respondent No. 1 casually e-filed an

            an application for condonation of delay was filed much later, on
            22.09.2023. Presumably, it was at this time that a certified copy
            of the impugned order was filed without even seeking exemption

14. The Hon’ble Supreme Court in Angelwoods Apartment Allottees

Association v. M. Lalitha & Anr., [(2026) ibclaw.in 343 SC], had occasion to

consider an appeal under Section 61 of the IBC, 2016 which was e-filed on

the last day of the outer limit of forty-five days but without a certified copy of

the impugned order and without the appellant having applied for the same.

The Hon’ble Supreme Court held as under:

            “9. Given the strict timelines provided in the Code, which were
            held to be of essence by this Court (See Ebix Singapore Private
            Limited vs. Committee of Creditors of Educomp Solutions Limited
            and another [(2021) ibclaw.in 153 SC]), we may note that
            respondent No. 1 chose to e-file an appeal against the order dated
            14.08.2024 on the very last day available to her, in terms of the
            limitation prescribed under the proviso to Section 61(2) of the
            Code. However, the filing of such appeal, without even
            applying for a certified copy of the order dated 14.08.2024,
            practically meant that there was no filing of an appeal in
            the eyes of law. As pointed out by this Court, a diligent litigant
            is expected to apply for a certified copy of the order sought to be
            appealed against before the period of limitation runs out and, by
            doing so, such litigant would be entitled to seek exclusion of the
            time taken to procure the certified copy for the purpose of
            limitation.
            10. However, as noted earlier, respondent No. 1 did not even
            apply for a certified copy of the NCLT’s order dated 14.08.2024 till
            21.04.2025, long after the refiling of the appeal on 10.03.2025. To
            make matters worse, respondent No.1 did not even choose to file

            an application for exemption from filing such certified copy at any

15. From a conjoint reading of the aforesaid judgments, the following view

emerges:

(i) The period of limitation under Section 61(2) of the IBC, 2016 begins to

run from the date of pronouncement of the order. The time taken for obtaining

a certified copy may be excluded under Section 12(2) of the Limitation Act,

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1720 of 2026
                                                            14
1963 only when the certified copy is applied for within the period of
limitation.

(ii) The act of applying for a certified copy is not merely a technical step for
computing limitation. It is an indicator of the diligence of the aggrieved party.
This obligation is not confined to cases where exclusion of time is sought. It
applies equally where the appeal is filed within the outer limit of forty-five
days. In A. Rajendra (supra), Ashdan Properties (supra) and Angelwoods
(supra), the failure to apply for the certified copy within limitation was
nonetheless held to be fatal.

(iii) An appeal may be presented without a certified copy along with a prayer
for exemption. However, exemption under Rule 14 of the NCLAT Rules, 2016
is discretionary and cannot be claimed as a matter of right.

(iv) An appeal which is presented without a certified copy, where the
certified copy was not applied for within limitation and no exemption has been
validly granted, is not a merely a defective appeal, it is an incompetent appeal.
The defect goes to the very root of the appellate jurisdiction, and such an
appeal is liable to be rejected at the threshold.

(v) The question whether the appeal was instituted in accordance with the
prescribed norms needs to be examined first. Where no application for
obtaining the certified copy has been made within limitation period, there is
no question of considering an application for condonation of delay, as it is an
incompetent appeal.

16. We note that the certified copy in the present case was applied much
after the period of limitation, including the condonable period, and after the
IAs were taken up for hearing and replies were called for from Respondent.
Rule 22(2) of NCLAT Rules, 2016 clearly prescribes that an appeal has to be
accompanied by the certified copy of the impugned order. In the present case,
the certified copy has been applied for after the period of limitation and thus
the appeal is not merely a defective appeal but it is an incompetent appeal in
terms of the requirement prescribed under the IBC, 2016 and the NCLAT
Rules. We are not inclined to extend indulgence to the Appellant/Applicant
regarding exemption from filing of certified copy and condonation of delay in
light of the facts of this case. Since the appeal was not instituted in the
manner prescribed by IBC, 2016 and NCLAT Rules, the appeal is treated as
non-est. I.A. No. 6524 of 2026 and I.A. No. 6525 of 2026 are dismissed. In
consequence, the appeal viz. Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1720 of
2026 is treated as non-est. Pending application(s) are closed. No order as to
costs.
