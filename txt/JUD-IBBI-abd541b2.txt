SHARAD KUMAR SHARMA, MEMBER (J)

         These are two connected company appeals, though they are arising out
of two separate interlocutory applications being I.A. No. 4139/ND/2026 as it
stood engaged consideration in Company Appeal (AT) (Ins.) No. 1928 of 2026

Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1928 & 1929 of 2026

and I.A. No. 4138/ND/2026 as it stood engaged consideration in Company
Appeal (AT) (Ins.) No. 1929 of 2026.
2. A few common facts, which engages consideration are that:

         The proceedings of the Company Petition, being C.P. (IB) No.
723(ND)/2020, were taken up before the Ld. Tribunal, and the Ld. Tribunal
vide its order of 28.08.2025, after recording the absence of the appellant
(who was Respondent No. 5 in the proceedings of the company petition),
directed the matter to be proceeded ex-parte.
3. By virtue of the said order of 28.08.2025, the Ld. Tribunal had
directed the matter to be proceeded ex-parte against the appellant, in terms
of its earlier order of 04.07.2023. The records of the company appeal do not
reflect, as to what proceedings took place on 04.07.2023, which has been
taken into consideration and has been referred by the Ld. Tribunal in its
order of 28.08.2025, while directing the proceedings to be taken ex-parte as
against the present appellants. This order to proceed ex-parte stands good,
having not been set aside, and admittedly, it has never even been sought to
be recalled by the appellant during the pendency of the proceedings of the
company petition.

4. At a much belated stage, a recall was preferred, seeking recall of the
order dated 28.08.2025, which was filed by the appellants herein, before the
Ld. Tribunal on 05.01.2026 i.e. almost after 4 months. What consequences
the said application reached are the facts, which are neither on record nor

pleaded by the appellants. An order was later passed by the Ld. Tribunal on
27.04.2026, after taking into consideration the effect of the earlier order
passed on 28.08.2025, directing to proceed with the matter ex-parte and
observed that the application for recall, being I.A. No.1599/2026 that was
preferred by Respondents No. 5,7 & 8 is in the proceedings for recalling the
order of 28.08.2025. It was observed that since the application itself for
recalling the order to proceed ex-parte was barred by limitation as having not
been preferred within 30 days, as prescribed under Article 122 of 3rd
division of Part-I of the Limitation Act.

5. Hence, the Ld. Tribunal on 27.04.2026 passed an order that, since the
application for recall i.e. I.A. No.1599/2026 was preferred on 05.01.2026
seeking recall of the order dated 28.08.2025 directing to proceed the matter
ex-parte, it had declined to interfere in the application on the ground that
the applicants to I.A. No.1599/2026, seeking recall of the order dated
28.08.2025, the applicants for recall had been negligent in pursuing the
proceedings and imposed a cost of Rs. 25,000/- to be deposited in the Prime
Minister’s Relief Fund, because of the fact that the recall application was
knowingly, it has been preferred on 05.01.2026 after a lapse of a substantial
time period i.e. 174 days and was not supported with the delay condonation
application. Hence, an opportunity was granted to file a delay condonation
application.

6. Accordingly, the appellant filed I.A. No. 4139/ND/2026, seeking
condonation of 174 days of delay, that has chanced in preferring an
application, which was for setting aside the order of 28.08.2025.

7. At this juncture, itself we make it clear, that it is surprising as to the
manner in which the appellant has taken up the proceedings for the reason
being that:

   (i) Order to proceed ex-parte was passed on 28.08.2025
   (ii) Recall was filed on 05.01.2026
   (iii) By an order of 27.04.2026, the appellant was granted time to file delay

         condonation application
   (iv) Meaning thereby, if at all any application was to be filed by the

         appellant for seeking condonation of delay, that ought to have been
         filed at least within 30 days from the date of the order, i.e.,
         27.04.2026, i.e. when the liberty was granted by the Ld. Tribunal to
         file the delay condonation application. But surprisingly the application
         seeking condonation of delay, being I.A. No. 4138/ND/2026, was filed
         by the appellant on 24.08.2026 that means almost approximately after
         four months, of grant of permission to file delay condonation.

8. That itself shows that the appellant was not diligent and vigilant too in
contesting the proceedings, despite when he was made conscious, that the
recall application of 05.01.2026, was mandatorily supposed to be supported
with the delay condonation application, and despite when he was made

Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1928 & 1929 of 2026
                                                            -6-

conscious of the fact by an order of 27.04.2026, he should have filed an
application for the delay condonation application as contemplated under
Article 122 of the Limitation Act i.e. within 30 days at least, and couldn’t
have waited to file the delay condonation application on 24.08.2026 that
itself would be barred by limitation.

9. In order to better elucidation, the provisions of Article 122 of the
Limitation Act are extracted hereunder:

            “122. To restore a suit or appeal or application for review or
            revision dismissed for default of appearance or for want of
            prosecution or for failure to pay costs of service of process or
            to furnish security for costs…… 30 days”
10. We have already observed, that even in the case of the appellant, if the
limitation is considered from the date of his knowledge, i.e., 16.10.2025, as
pleaded in the application for condonation of delay itself as well as in the
recall application. It would be much beyond the period of limitation
prescribed under Article 122 of Limitation Act, as extracted above, which
provides for a determination of 30 days’ delay from the “date of dismissal”,
and not from the date of knowledge. Even in those circumstances, too, the
application for condonation of delay as well as for recall would be falling to
be much beyond the period of limitation as prescribed, and since 174 days
are inordinate, it cannot be condoned.

11. Another glaring feature though earlier observed, but which is required
to be considered is that if the application for delay condonation itself is taken

                                                            -7-

into consideration in paragraph 7, the appellant has raised the following
pleading:

            “7. That the Respondents most respectfully submit that they
            gained knowledge of the order dated 28.08.2025 only on
            16.10.2025 when the Respondents had logged in to observe
            the proceedings. However, the matter did not reach on that
            day. Thereafter, upon perusing the order sheet, the
            Respondents were shocked and surprised to note that vide
            order dated 28.08.2025, they had been proceeded ex-parte
            and it was further observed that there was no representation
            on behalf of the Respondents on several previous dates.”

12. This pleading in itself show, that the appellant got the knowledge of
the order of 28.08.2025, back as on 16.10.2025, when he logged into the
proceedings. If the appellant got the knowledge on 16.10.2025, then at least
the recall of the order of 28.08.2025 should have been filed within 30 days
and the appellant couldn’t have waited till 05.01.2026 and more importantly
what we have already asserted the date when he filed the delay condonation
application i.e. 24.08.2026.
13. If this application, which was preferred by the appellant seeking recall
of the order dated 28.08.2025, which came up for consideration before the
Ld. Tribunal and the same has been rejected by the impugned order, is taken
into consideration in paragraph 7, admitted knowledge is of 16.10.2025.
14. The Ld. Tribunal, while considering the purport and purpose of
seeking condonation of delay, has passed an order rejecting the delay
condonation application, observing thereof, that when an application for
seeking condonation of delay is to be preferred, first of all, the delay has to
be reasonably explained, and looking at the contents of the application in

Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1928 & 1929 of 2026
                                                            -8-

itself, the applicant was expected to explain the same in the application. As
there was no reason given in the application, as to what prevented the
counsel or the parties to appear before the Tribunal on the date when the
matter was proceeded ex parte, i.e., on 28.08.2025. Hence, the delay was
declined to be condoned. And accordingly, as a consequence of the same, an
order passed on I.A. No. 218 of 2023, was declined to be recalled.
15. Another reason, and quite validly too which has been argued and
taken into account in the proceedings before the Ld. Tribunal was, that the
counsel who was pursuing the matter on his behalf had stopped
representing him and his cause in the proceedings without there being any
prior intimation to the appellant. In fact, this ground, which has been carved
out by the appellant in the application for seeking condonation of delay,
doesn’t seem to be justified in the absence of any credible material on record
to show that when the earlier counsel had withdrawn his Vakalatnama from
the records and had stopped pursuing the proceedings on behalf of the
appellant. Besides that, even if is presumed that because of the conduct of
the earlier counsel, he remained unrepresented even prior to 28.08.2025.
There is nothing on record to show that the appellant ever made any
communications with his counsel or had carried any step as against his
earlier counsel for its professional dereliction for not pursuing the matter on
his behalf, particularly when the authority vested by the appellant continued
to operate as on the date when the ex-parte order of 28.08.2025 was passed.
There had been no pleading on record to show that appellant had ever

Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1928 & 1929 of 2026
                                                            -9-

communicated with his counsel, showing his inquisitiveness towards his
case, showing his diligence to contest the proceedings.
16. It’s not the case of the appellant, that the earlier counsel ever refused
to appear. Nor is it the case of the appellant or his counsel too that, upon the
refusal of the earlier counsel, he has ever taken his no objection for engaging
another counsel as contemplated under Order III Rule 4 of CPC, which was
required by the counsel who has filed the delay condonation application, nor
is there any pleading to the said effect in the application for seeking
condonation of delay.
17. Much authenticity cannot be attached to the pleadings raised in that
context about the conduct of the previous counsel as raised in paragraph 5
of the application, since it didn’t contain any credible material or a basis, if
this could be permitted to be accepted by us for the purposes of condonation
of delay in recalling of an order. It would rather be opening a pandora's box;
invariably, any party to the proceedings could be filing a recall application on
that pretext, and more particularly when the other counsel who has been
engaged for the purposes of filing the recall application has not taken his no
objection from the earlier counsel, as required under law.
18. As the delay is inordinate of 174 days and there is no plausible reason,
which has been assigned by the appellant, and more particularly when the
application itself has been preferred much beyond the period of acquiring the
knowledge on 16.10.2025. The reasons in the application cannot be

accepted, as they are being artificially created for the purposes of the present
application itself.
19. Thus, the impugned order rejecting the delay condonation application,
i.e., I.A. No. 4139/ND/2026, doesn’t suffer from any apparent error that
would call for any interference by us. In the exercise of our appellate
jurisdiction, particularly in a proceeding of I&B Code, where the statute itself
intends that the proceedings have to be completed within a specified time
frame and where the time is the essence of the proceedings.
20. The connected company appeal, which arises out of an order of
31.08.2026, is against the rejection of I.A. No. 4138/ND/2026 for recalling
the order of 28.08.2025 because of the fact that we have already rejected the
delay condonation application in Company Appeal (AT) (Ins.) No. 1928 of
2026, i.e., I.A. No. 4139/ND/2026. This appeal too would stand covered by
the said judgment, hence, would stand dismissed, because the consequence
of the rejection of the delay condonation application is obviously an
application to set aside an ex-parte order dated 28.08.2025 would meet its
legal death. Hence, this company appeals too would stand dismissed.
