       The instant appeal has been preferred under Section 61 of the Insolvency
and Bankruptcy Code, 2016(the Code) against the order dated 09.09.2026
(Impugned Order) passed by the National Company Law Tribunal, Guwahati
Bench (the Adjudicating Authority) in C.P. (IB) No. 12/GB/2024, whereby the
application filed by IL&FS Financial Services Limited (Financial Creditor)
under Section 95 of the Code against the Appellant, as Personal Guarantor to
Adhunik Meghalaya Steels Private Limited/Principal borrower/corporate
debtor, has been admitted and the insolvency resolution process against the
Appellant has been initiated.
                                                             2

2. Facts in brief required for disposal of this appeal, as stated in Appeal,
are that Adhunik Meghalaya Steels Private Limited (Corporate Debtor),
approached Respondent No. 1, IL&FS Financial Services Limited (the
Financial Creditor), to avail a term loan facility for its working capital
requirements/general corporate purposes. The Financial Creditor extended a
term loan facility of up to Rs.30,00,00,000/- (Rupees Thirty Crores only) to
the Corporate Debtor vide Loan Agreement dated 27.02.2015 Against the
sanctioned facility of Rs.30,00,00,000/-, an amount of Rs.24,44,44,456/-
was disbursed by the Financial Creditor to the Corporate Debtor on
17.03.2015. The Appellant executed a Deed of Personal Guarantee dated
27.02.2015 in favour of the Financial Creditor in connection with the
aforesaid facility. The aforesaid loan facility was also secured by pledge of
shares of Adhunik Metaliks Limited and at the time of the pledge, these shares
were stated to be valued at Rs.19.85 per share, having an aggregate value of
approximately Rs.50.00 Crores.
3. It is further stated that, according to the Financial Creditor, the
Corporate Debtor was required to repay the facility in eight equal quarterly
instalments commencing from the end of the 27th month from the date of
disbursement and the Corporate Debtor failed to repay the instalments in
accordance with the repayment schedule and committed defaults in respect
of the facility and the account of the Corporate Debtor was classified as a Non-
Performing Asset (NPA) on 01.03.2018.
4. It is further stated that Respondent No. 1 had invoked and realised
portions of the pledged securities during FY 2016–17 and FY 2017–18,
resulting in recoveries aggregating to approximately Rs.8.30 Crores, while a

curing the alleged default. The Financial Creditor issued a Recall Facility
Notice dated 10.08.2018 whereby the facility was recalled and the Corporate
Debtor was called upon to repay the entire facility together with applicable
interest and penalty within 48 hours.
5. It is further stated that Respondent No.1 thereafter instituted

21. It is further submitted that the Appellant’s submission that the

apply to the Appellant since he had not signed it, is totally misconceived. Our
attention is drawn to Clause 5(f) of the Deed of Guarantee which provides that

22. It is further submitted that the issue is no longer res integra and has
been settled by this Appellate Tribunal in State Bank of India v. Bernard
John, Personal Guarantor of GEI Industrial Systems Ltd., 2025 SCC
OnLine NCLAT 1670, wherein it is held that an acknowledgment of liability
in the Corporate Debtor’s balance sheets would extend limitation against a
Personal Guarantor also, particularly where the Deed of Guarantee expressly
provides for such effect, as their liability is concurrent and co-extensive. In
the said case, the personal guarantor had executed a guarantee containing a
clause substantially similar to Clause 5(f) of the guarantee deed executed by
the Appellant and the personal guarantee in that case was invoked in 2016.
Therefore, contention that an acknowledgment of the principal borrower
cannot bind the Personal Guarantor, where the invocation of the guarantee
preceded such acknowledgment, is wholly untenable. The aforesaid legal
position has attained finality in view of the dismissal of an appeal filed against
the above case being (Civil Appeal No. 7898 of 2026) by the Hon’ble Supreme
Court vide its order dated 15.05.2026. Thus, in view of Clause 5(f) of the
guarantee deed, the acknowledgment of debt dated 12.08.2020 by the
                                                            11

Corporate Debtor in its balance sheet is also binding on the Appellant for the
purposes of limitation and will extend the limitation against the Appellant,
rendering the Section 95 Application filed on 08.05.2024 within limitation.

unconditional and continuing guarantee, binding upon the Appellant until
the dues of the Corporate Debtor are discharged in full and the Appellant
undertook to pay the Borrower’s Dues upon first demand and within 24
hours, without recourse to the Borrower. Thus, the Appellant’s liability is
joint, several and co-extensive with that of the Borrower, and Clause 5(c)
further stipulates that the Guarantee shall remain in force until all dues are
finally paid. Clause 13 also provides that the Guarantee is a continuing
guarantee, thereby confirming that it remains operative in respect of the
obligations covered by it until discharged. Ld. Counsel has placed reliance on
Laxmi Pat Surana v. Union Bank of India & Anr., (2021) 8 SCC 481,
wherein the Hon’ble Supreme Court recognised the co-extensive nature of a
guarantor’s liability and the creditor’s right to proceed against the guarantor
upon default by the principal borrower and on State Bank of India v. V.
Ramakrishnan & Anr., (2018) 17 SCC 394. It is also submitted that without
prejudice to the above, it is a settled position of law that partial recovery or
settlement from the CD does not extinguish the liability of the personal
guarantor, for the unpaid debt. Reliance is placed on BRS Ventures
Investments Ltd. v. SREI Infrastructure Finance Ltd. & Anr., (2025) 1
SCC 456 and Amit Bhatnagar v. UCO Bank & Anr., 2026 SCC OnLine

                                                            12

with another entity cannot, by itself, absolve the Appellant of his liability for

24. It is further submitted that the reliance placed by the Appellant on
Syndicate Bank v. Channaveerappa Beleri & Ors., (2006) 11 SCC 506 is totally
misconceived, as paragraph 9 of Syndicate Bank explicitly recognises that a
guarantor’s liability depends on the terms of the guarantee and it does not
hold that a continuing guarantee and a guarantee payable on demand are
mutually exclusive and the fact that the Guarantee is payable on demand
does not negate its continuing character. Further, paragraph 9 of this report
recognises that a claim may be time-barred against the principal debtor but
may remain enforceable against the guarantor, depending on the terms of the
guarantee. In the present case, Clause 5(f) expressly provides that an
acknowledgment by the Borrower binds the Appellant for limitation purposes,
Accordingly, the effect of the acknowledgment dated 12.08.2020 must be
determined in light of this express contractual provision. The Bernard John
judgment in paragraph 73, has taken into account the findings contained in
Syndicate Bank and held that an acknowledgment by the principal debtor
operates as an acknowledgment against the guarantor as well, given that their
liability is concurrent, and renews the limitation period as against both.

28. A perusal of the aforesaid clauses of the Deed of Guarantee reveals that
the guarantor/Appellant, by executing the aforesaid guarantee deed, had
undertaken to make, without delay or protest and on first demand and
without recourse to the borrower, within 24 hours of the demand, payment of
the principal amount not exceeding ₹300 million only, along with interest and
other monies payable by the borrower to the financial creditor. Clause 2 of
the guarantee deed reveals that this guarantee is irrevocable and
unconditional and shall not be revoked or cancelled till the entire claim of the
financial creditor is fully met and paid by the guarantor. Clause 3 of the
guarantee deed specifies that the guarantor's obligation to pay will arise when
notice in writing is given by the financial creditor requiring the guarantor to
make payment. After such notice is given, the financial creditor may proceed
to enforce the guarantor's obligations and liabilities without first proceeding
against and resorting to any of the remedies against the borrower. Thus, it is
evident that the financial creditor was not obliged to first proceed against the
principal borrower. It was stipulated that, without initiating any proceeding
against the CD on the invocation of guarantee, the whole of the money can be
recovered from the guarantor.
29. Perusal of clause 5 (f) of the guarantee deed reveals that it is stipulated
that, for the purpose of limitation, any admission or acknowledgement in
writing given or part payment made by the borrower (in the spirit of the
repayment of the borrower's dues or otherwise in relation to the facility) shall
be binding on the guarantor, notwithstanding that the same may have been
                                                            17

made by the borrower, it shall be treated as given on behalf of the guarantor
also. Therefore, it is reflected that any admission or acknowledgment in
writing made by the principal borrower would be deemed to have been
acknowledged and admitted by the guarantor.
30. A perusal of clause 13 of the guarantee deed suggests that the parties
have stipulated that the guarantee is a continuing guarantee and is without
prejudice to, and in addition to, any other security or security documents
already held or which the financial creditor may hold thereafter on account of
or in relation to the facility to the borrower in terms of said agreement. The
guarantee thus appears to be a continuing guarantee payable on demand.

               only against the Principal Borrower but also against the

               we read with as is delineated by Section 3(11) of the Code, debt
               becomes due both on Principal Borrower and the Guarantor, as
               noted above. The definition of default under Section 3(12) in

               additional expressions i.e. "payable" and "is not paid by the
               debtor or corporate debtor". The expression ‘is not paid by the
               debtor' has to be given some meaning. As laid down by the
               Hon'ble Supreme Court "Syndicate Bank vs. Channaveerappa
               Beleri & Ors." (supra), a quarantor's liability depends on terms of
               his contract. There can be default by the Principal Borrower and
               the Guarantor on the same date or date of default for both may
               be different depending on the terms of contract of guarantee. It is
               well settled that the loan agreement with the Principal Borrower
               and the Bank as well as Deed of Guarantee between the Bank
               and the Guarantor are two different transactions and the

               the guarantor as well.

68. The question as to whether acknowledgment of debt by a
principal borrower extends limitation against the guarantor has
been the subject of consistent judicial interpretation. The
statutory scheme under Section 128 of the Indian Contract Act
provides that “the liability of the surety is co-extensive with that
of the principal debtor, unless it is otherwise provided by the
contract.” This co-extensiveness implies that both the borrower
and the guarantor are liable for the same debt, at the same time,
and to the same extent, unless expressly limited by contract.

73. It is well settled that such contractual stipulations are
enforceable and consistent with Section 128 of the Contract Act.
In Syndicate Bank v. Channaveerappa Beleri, (2006) 11 SCC
506, the Hon'ble Supreme Court observed that acknowledgment
made by the principal debtor extends limitation as against the
guarantor as well, as their liability is concurrent. The Court held
that where acknowledgment is made by the debtor before expiry
of limitation, it renews the limitation period against both the
debtor and the surety.

75. Applying the ratio of the aforesaid Judgments Syndicate
Bank (supra) and State Bank of India (supra) to the present case,
we hold that acknowledgment of liability made by the Corporate
Debtor in its balance sheets for FY 2016-2017 to 2019-2020
constitutes valid acknowledgment not only for the borrower but
also for the guarantor. This is for three reasons:

a) Section 128 of the Contract Act makes the guarantor's liability
co-extensive, meaning that the same acknowledgment of the
same debt that renews limitation for the borrower equally applies
to the guarantor, since their liabilities are inseparable.

b) Clauses 12 and 19 of the Deed of Guarantee expressly deem
acknowledgment by the borrower to be acknowledgment by the
guarantor. The guarantor, having agreed to this term at the time
of executing the guarantee, is estopped from denying its effect.
                                                            30

               c) The ratio in Syndicate Bank v. Channaveerappa Beleri (supra)
               and State Bank of India v. Indexport Registered (supra) highlights
               the concept that acknowledgment in balance sheet made by the
               principal debtor extends limitation to the guarantor as well.”

Bernard John and vide order dated 15.05.2026 passed in Civil Appeal No. Nil
of 2026 arising out of Diary No. 65246 of 2025 the Civil Appeal was dismissed
in timeline.

the facility) shall be binding on the guarantor, notwithstanding that the same
may have been made by the borrower, it shall be treated as given on behalf of
the guarantor also. In view of this clause in the guarantee deed, we do not
have any doubt that the liability of the Appellant guarantor is co-extensive
with the liability of the principal borrower and any acknowledgement or
admission by the principal debtor would be binding on the Appellant personal
guarantor so far as the limitation is concerned and in view of this clause, we
are of the considered view that the other case laws relied on by learned

The instant case appears to be entirely covered by the law laid down by a Co-
