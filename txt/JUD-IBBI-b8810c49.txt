Heard Dr. Ashok Saraf, learned Senior counsel assisted by Mr. A Kaushik,

learned counsel for the petitioner.

2. The petitioner before this court is aggrieved by the initiation of

proceedings by the NCLT at the instance of the operational creditor namely

private respondent No. 1. The private respondent No. 1 had carried out certain

supplies of Stone chips and stone dust for an amount of ₹3,96,96,415.00 to the

petitioner. According to Respondent No. 1, the present petitioner defaulted in

releasing the payments for the supplies made by the respondent No. 1. Being

aggrieved, the private respondent No. 1 initiated proceedings under Section 9

under the IBC before the NCLT, Guwahati.

3. The learned senior counsel for the petitioner submits that in terms of the

prescribed procedure, prior to invocation of Section 9, notice(s) are to be issued

by the operational creditor, namely the private respondent No. 1. The learned

senior counsel for the petitioner submits that from a perusal of the pleadings

enclosed, it is clear that the notices as contemplated under section 8 was issued
                                                                                                            Page No.# 3/6

by the power of attorney holder and not by the operational creditor. He further
submits by referring to the relevant documents enclosed to the petition it is
clear that this power of attorney holder had issued the notice dated 15/7/2025,
which is enclosed as Annexure VI to the writ petition. The further submission of
the learned senior counsel for the petitioner is that this notice was issued by the
power of attorney holder well prior to the power of attorney holder being
conferred with such powers by due execution of a notary affidavit. Referring to
the notary affidavit enclosed to the writ petition, which is available at page 242
of the petition, it is submitted by the learned senior counsel that the notary
affidavit was executed on 14/9/2025. It is therefore submitted that on the date
when the notice on 15/7/2025 was issued purportedly by the power of attorney
holder on behalf of the private respondent No. 1, there was no power conferred
by the respondent No. 1 on the said power of attorney holder. The Notary
affidavit was executed on 14.09.2026. The learned senior counsel therefore
submits that without issuance of the mandatory notice as provided for in the
scheme of the IBC, the proceedings were initiated under Section 9 before the
NCLT.
4. The learned senior counsel for the petitioner submits that upon initiation of
the proceedings before the NCLT and upon receipt of the notice, the petitioner
had approached the authorities by filing an application seeking disclosure of all
documents including the vouchers and bills, which came to be rejected by order
dated 28th of October 2026. This application was filed in the company petition
filed by the Operational Director under Section 9, which has already been
admitted by the Tribunal. It is submitted by the learned senior counsel that a
bare reading of the orders passed by the NCLT, it is clear that the respondent
No. 1 is a student and the proprietorship firm is run by his father Mr. Tayo
                                                                                                            Page No.# 4/6

Taloom and the power of attorney holder Mr. Sonjib Gogoi. The learned senior
counsel therefore submits that the impugned order dated 28.10.2026 as well as
the order dated 28/10/2026, passed in CP (IB)/25/GB/2025, whereby the
company petition filed by the respondent no. 1 stood admitted and the initiation
of Corporate Insolvency Resolution Proceedings were ordered against the
petitioner under Section 9 of the Code, read with Rule 6 of the Insolvency and
Bankruptcy Rule 2016 is with utter disregard to the mandatory provisions of the
statute. It is submitted that where the very initiation of the proceedings is
contrary to the provisions prescribed under IBC, the NCLT Guwahati ought to
have taken into account the objections raised by the petitioner calling for the
complete documents to be placed before the Court and instead the proceedings
were admitted and thereafter the IA filed by the petitioner had also been
rejected.
5. On a pointed query by the Court with regard to the availability of adequate
alternative remedy as specified under the code itself by filing an appropriate
appeal before the NCLAT, the learned senior counsel for the petitioner submits
that where the very initiation of the proceedings are contrary to the enshrined
provisions of the IBC and thereby a question of law arises, the powers of a
constitutional Court are not curtailed in examining matters of this nature where
the Tribunal has failed to take into account the relevant provisions of the statute
particularly the fact that the proper notice as required under the IBC, had not
been issued and also in view of the fact that the relevant documents, the
vouchers on which the claims are made, are not submitted to the petitioner
under the notice nor placed before the tribunal and notwithstanding that the
tribunal proceeded to initiate the CIRP and had appointed the resolution
professional against the respondent No. 3.
                                                                                                            Page No.# 5/6

6. It is also submitted by the learned senior counsel for the petitioner that
the name of the private respondent stood altered subsequently by filing
necessary affidavit whereas the application before the NCLT was filed in the
erstwhile name of the respondent. He has referred to the judgment of Union of
India Vs. Parashtom Dass, reported in (2025) 5 SCC 786 wherein the apex court
has held that availability of alternative remedy is not a complete bar for
entertaining proceedings under the IBC Code under 226, where question of law
is required to be decided.
7. Having heard the counsel for the parties, learned proxy counsel has
entered appearance on behalf of Mr. K. Sharma, learned CGC has submitted
before the court that he is indisposed and is not available.
8. Considering the urgency expressed by the counsel to the petitioner and
upon due consideration of the submissions of the counsel for the petitioner that
the initiation of proceedings as contemplated under the IBC is hit because of
non-fulfillment of the mandatory provisions prescribed under Sections 7, 8, 9
and also in view of the fact that the production of documents as sought for by
the petitioner by filing an IA stood rejected after the company petition stood
admitted, prima facie it appears to the court that the NCLT ought to have duly
considered the IA prior to admitting the company petition and ordering the CIRP
and appointment of the interim resolution professional. In addition to the
defence projected by the petitioner as a party before the NCLT that the
procedure prescribed including issuance of proper notice under the IBC
proceedings had not been adhered to by the applicant before the NCLT.
9. Having heard the learned senior counsel for the petitioner and upon
perusal of the writ petition before this court and also upon carefully noting the
law relied upon, this court is of the view that at this stage the matter requires

examination, keeping the question of maintainability is open to be decided at
the time of admission.
10. Let Notice be issued, returnable in four weeks.
11. The learned counsel appearing for the respondent No. 4 accepts notice,
therefore, notices are waived. However, extra copies be furnished within one
week from today.
12. Steps be taken by Speed post on respondents No. 1 to 3 within one week
from today.
13. Matter be listed for admissions on 11.12.2026. Issue notice on the interim
prayer, returnable on the same date.
14. The proceedings before the NCLT initiated under company petition and the
orders dated 28.10.2026 passed in CP (IB)/25/GB/2025 and
IA(IBC)225/GB/2026 shall remain stayed till the next date of listing.
