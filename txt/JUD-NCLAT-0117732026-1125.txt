[Per: Arun Baroka, Member (Technical)]
         The present appeals (“Appeals”) have been preferred by Mr. Balbir

Singh, the suspended Director of ESPO Developers Private Limited

(“Appellant”), under Section 61(1) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code,

2016 (“Code”) before this Appellate Tribunal assailing the order dated

29.09.2026 (“Impugned Order”) passed by the Ld. National Company Law

Tribunal, New Delhi, Court-II (“Ld. Adjudicating Authority”) in CP (IB) No.

207/ND/2026. The application under Section 7 of the Code was filed by

Omkara Assets Reconstruction Private Limited (“Respondent No. 1”) for

initiation of the corporate insolvency resolution process (“CIRP”) against ESPO

Developers Private Limited (“Corporate Debtor”) (“Insolvency Petition”), which

has been admitted by the impugned order.

2. We observe that CA (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1966 and 2017 of 2026 arise from
the order dated 29.09.2026 passed by the National Company Law Tribunal,
whereby the application filed by Respondent No. 1 under Section 7 of the Code
against ESPO Developers Private Limited, the Corporate Debtor, was
admitted. By the Impugned Order, I.A. No. 3396/ND/2026 filed by the
Corporate Debtor under Section 65 of the Code was rejected, which is under

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  3 of 14
challenge in CA (AT) (Ins) No. 2017 of 2026. The Appellant seeks, amongst
other reliefs, an interim order staying further proceedings arising from the
admission of the Section 7 application. At this stage, we are concerned only
with the prayer for interim protection.

3. We have heard both sides and also perused the material placed on
record by both sides. The observations herein are prima facie and shall not
affect the final decision of the appeals.

4. We observe that the dispute has its origin in the Loan Agreement dated
18.01.2019, under which, according to Respondent No. 1, a sum of ₹ 135.15
Crores was disbursed to the Corporate Debtor.

5. On 01.09.2021, an OTS letter was issued. The Appellant relies upon the
OTS letter as having confined the Corporate Debtor’s outstanding liability to
₹ 67,31,92,314/- together with interest at 11.62% per annum, payable
monthly. Respondent No. 1, on the other hand, contends that the OTS was
breached and that the loan was recalled on 10.11.2022.

6. The material on record refers to a balance confirmation dated
08.11.2022 for ₹ 39,18,14,859/- plus interest. It also refers to a recall notice
dated 10.11.2022. The Appellant asserts that the amount payable under the
OTS was thereafter paid by February/March 2023. Respondent No. 1 disputes
the effect of that payment and submits that the payment of ₹ 39.56 Crores
was made only on 24.03.2023, after the stipulated date, and was only a part-
payment.

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  4 of 14
7. On 29.03.2023, the parties executed a Settlement Agreement. The rival
submissions proceed on the basis that this agreement dealt with two sets of
obligations: (i) financial obligations relating to the loan liability; and (ii)
obligations relating to the agreement-to-sell (ATS) inventory and transactions.
The Appellant describes these as interdependent obligations forming one
composite arrangement. Respondent No. 1 also accepts that the Settlement
Agreement contained both sets of obligations, but submits that a breach of
either set attracted the consequences provided in Clauses 5 and 11, including
revival and enforcement of the original loan agreements.

8. The Settlement Agreement was followed by an Amended and Restated
Agreement to Sell dated 16.05.2023. A Second Addendum dated 22.02.2024
is also relied upon. According to the Appellant, the Second Addendum records
that the purchaser’s rights and obligations under the ATS had been assigned
to the SSL Parties. Respondent No. 1 likewise states that the ATS rights and
obligations concerning the ATS Properties were assigned to the SSL Parties.
The parties, however, differ on the legal effect of these assignments and on
the authority to terminate the Settlement Agreement.

9. On 11.07.2024, a termination notice was issued. Respondent No. 1
contends that the Settlement Agreement was validly terminated for breach
and that the original Loan Agreement consequently stood revived, making the
entire outstanding amount due and payable. The Appellant contends that the
termination notice rested solely on alleged breaches of the ATS obligations,

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  5 of 14
which had already been assigned to the SSL Parties, and that the authority
and validity of the termination are disputed.

10. The Corporate Debtor/Vatika Group thereafter invoked arbitration. The
chronology placed by the Appellant records a Section 9 application dated
18.10.2024, invocation of arbitration by notice dated 05.11.2024,
appointment of the arbitrator by consent on 02.12.2024, and commencement
of arbitral proceedings on 28.12.2024. The Appellant filed its Statement of
Claim on 06.08.2025. Respondent No. 1 and the other concerned parties
participated in the arbitral proceedings. Respondent No. 1 filed its Statement
of Defence and Statement of Counter-Claim. The SSL Parties also filed their
pleadings and sought interim protection in relation to the ATS Properties. A
status quo order concerning the ATS Properties was passed by the Arbitral
Tribunal on 09.07.2026.

11. The Section 7 application is stated to have been filed on 16.04.2026
and the NCLT admitted the application by order dated 29.09.2026.

12. The Appellant submits that the essential requirements for admission
under Section 7 of the Code are the existence of a financial debt and a default
in respect of that debt. According to the Appellant, these requirements were
not clearly established as on the date on which the Section 7 application was
filed.

13. It is submitted that the Settlement Agreement dated 29.03.2023 was
not a simple restructuring of a loan. It was a composite arrangement

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  6 of 14
containing interdependent financial obligations and ATS obligations. The
alleged liability under the original loan could arise only upon a valid
termination of the Settlement Agreement. The termination itself is disputed
and is the subject matter of the pending arbitration.

14. The Appellant further submits that the ATS rights and obligations had
been assigned to the SSL Parties. Respondent No. 1, therefore, had no
subsisting right in respect of those ATS obligations when it invoked Section
7. The Appellant also relies upon the participation of Respondent No. 1 in the
arbitration, including the filing of its defence and counter-claim. Its counter-
claim remains pending before the Arbitral Tribunal.

15. It is argued that the NCLT could not finally determine disputed
questions concerning the construction of the Settlement Agreement,
assignment of the ATS obligations, authority to terminate, and revival of the
original loan. Reliance is placed on Dhanlaxmi Bank Ltd. v. Mohammed
Javed Sultan, 2026 INSC 460; Indus Biotech Pvt. Ltd. v. Kotak India
Venture Fund, 2021 SCC OnLine SC 268; and Bachhaj Nahar v. Nilima
Mandal, (2008) 17 SCC 491.

16. On interim relief, the Appellant submits that he has a prima facie case
because the existence of debt and default is seriously in dispute. The balance
of convenience is stated to favour the Appellant because Respondent No. 1’s
counter-claim is pending in arbitration. It is also submitted that irreparable
harm would be caused if the proposed Interim Resolution Professional takes

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  7 of 14
over the Corporate Debtor while the foundational contractual dispute remains
unresolved.

17. Respondent No. 1 submits that the financial debt and default stand
established. It relies upon the Loan Agreement dated 18.01.2019, the OTS
letter dated 01.09.2021, the recall notice dated 10.11.2022 and the
Settlement Agreement dated 29.03.2023. According to it, the Corporate
Debtor failed to comply with the OTS and thereafter failed to perform its
obligations under the Settlement Agreement.

18. Respondent No. 1 submits that the Settlement Agreement expressly
included the Corporate Debtor as a borrower and included its loan in
Schedule I. It contends that the financial and ATS obligations were
interdependent, and that Clauses 5 and 11 preserved the original loan
agreements and permitted their enforcement upon breach of the Settlement
Agreement. It accordingly maintains that the termination dated 11.07.2024
validly revived the original loan liability.

19. Respondent No. 1 further submits that the assignment of the financial
obligations and ATS obligations did not deprive the concerned parties of
authority to terminate or enforce the settlement. It relies upon the Sub-
Servicer Agreement dated 11.05.2023 and the subsequent chain of
assignments. It also contends that the Section 7 application was within
limitation.

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  8 of 14
20. As regards arbitration, Respondent No. 1 submits that arbitration and
insolvency proceedings operate in different fields. The pendency of arbitration
does not bar a Section 7 application where financial debt and default are
established. It relies upon Indus Biotech Pvt. Ltd. v. Kotak India Venture
Fund, (2021) 6 SCC 436, and Century Aluminium Company Limited v.
Religare Finvest Limited, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins.) No. 1719 of 2024.
It submits that the termination notice has not been stayed by the Arbitral
Tribunal and that the NCLT rightly admitted the Section 7 application.

21. We have considered the rival submissions. At the outset, it is necessary
to keep in view that an application under Section 7 of the Code can be
admitted only when the existence of a financial debt and the occurrence of
default are established. The insolvency jurisdiction is not intended to be used
as a substitute for adjudication of disputed contractual claims or as a means
of recovery.

22. The present matter, at least at this interim stage, cannot be viewed as
a case involving only a straightforward loan transaction. The Settlement
Agreement dated 29.03.2023 appears, from the material placed before us, to
have brought together two sets of obligations. One set related to the financial
liability. The other related to the ATS transactions and obligations concerning
the identified inventory. Both sides rely upon the interdependent nature of
these obligations, though they differ on the consequences of their alleged
breach.

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  9 of 14
23. The case of Respondent No. 1 is that the Settlement Agreement was
validly terminated on 11.07.2024 and that such termination revived the
original loan liability. The Appellant disputes the termination and contends
that the notice was founded on alleged breaches of the ATS obligations. It
further points out that the ATS rights and obligations had been assigned to
the SSL Parties. The effect of the assignment, the authority to issue the
termination notice, and the consequences of termination are all matters that
arise from the construction of the Settlement Agreement and its addenda.

24. The records show that the rights and obligations relating to the ATS
were assigned to the SSL Parties. The precise legal effect of that assignment,
including its bearing on the right to terminate the composite Settlement
Agreement and the right to claim revival of the loan, requires examination. At
this stage, we are not expressing a final view on the validity of the assignment
or the termination notice. We only note that these are substantial contractual
issues and are not merely incidental disputes.

25. It is also relevant that arbitration was invoked in November 2024 and
the arbitral proceedings commenced thereafter. Respondent No. 1 has
voluntarily participated in those proceedings. It has filed its Statement of
Defence and Statement of Counter-Claim. Its counter-claim is pending
adjudication. The arbitration concerns, amongst other matters, the rights and
obligations arising from the Settlement Agreement and the ATS transactions.
The Arbitral Tribunal has also passed an order dated 09.07.2026 concerning
status quo in respect of the ATS Properties.

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  10 of 14
26. The pendency of arbitration, by itself, does not automatically bar
proceedings under Section 7 of the Code. The question, however, is whether
the financial debt and default relied upon for admission are sufficiently clear
and established independently of the unresolved contractual issues. On the
material presently placed before us, the alleged revival of the original loan
liability depends substantially upon the validity and effect of the termination
of the Settlement Agreement. That termination is itself disputed and forms
part of the controversy pending before the Arbitral Tribunal.

27. In these circumstances, we are of the prima facie view that the existence
of the debt and the occurrence of default, as on the date of filing of the Section
7 application, are not free from substantial doubt. The matter requires closer
examination. The NCLT’s findings on the construction of the Settlement
Agreement, the effect of assignment and the validity of termination will have
to be considered at the final hearing of these appeals. We do not propose to
finally decide any of those questions while considering interim protection.

28. The principles relied upon by the parties also need to be noticed. The
Appellant relies upon the following propositions, as set out in his written
submissions:

         o In Dhanlaxmi Bank Ltd. v. Mohammed Javed Sultan, 2026
               INSC 460, the Appellant submits that the Code is a collective
               insolvency resolution mechanism, “not … a forum for the
               adjudication of individual contractual claims”, nor “a tool for
               coercion and debt recovery”. It further relies upon the proposition

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  11 of 14
               that a loan “intrinsically linked” to performance of other
               contractual obligations “cannot be viewed in isolation as a simple
               financial lending arrangement”, and that a “predominantly
               contractual” dispute pending before the appropriate forum cannot
               be converted into “a coercive mechanism for recovery”.

         o In Indus Biotech Pvt. Ltd. v. Kotak India Venture Fund, 2021
               SCC OnLine SC 268, the Appellant relies upon the proposition
               that default is “premature” where the amount payable turns on an
               unresolved contractual dispute.

         o In Bachhaj Nahar v. Nilima Mandal, (2008) 17 SCC 491, the
               Appellant relies upon the principle that a court cannot make out a
               case that was never pleaded.

29. Respondent No. 1, on the other hand, relies upon Torrent Power Ltd.
v. Ashish Arjunkumar Rathi, 2026 SCC OnLine SC 325; Elegna Co-Op.
Housing and Commercial Society Ltd. v. Edelweiss Asset Reconstruction
Company Limited, 2026 SCC OnLine SC 82; Power Trust v. Hiranmaye
Energy Ltd., (2026) 5 SCC 38; E.S. Krishnamurthy v. Bharath Hi-Tecch
Builders (P) Ltd., (2022) 3 SCC 161; Innoventive Industries Ltd. v. ICICI
Bank, (2018) 1 SCC 407; Gaurav Jaiswal v. Indian Renewable Energy
Development Agency Ltd., 2026 SCC OnLine NCLAT 888; Bahadur Ram
Mallah v. ARC (India) Limited & Anr., 2025 SCC OnLine NCLAT 1924;
Indus Biotech Pvt. Ltd. v. Kotak India Venture Fund, (2021) 6 SCC 436;

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  12 of 14
and Century Aluminium Company Limited v. Religare Finvest Limited,
Company Appeal (AT) (Ins.) No. 1719 of 2024.

30. On the question of balance of convenience, Respondent No. 1 is not left
without a remedy. It has already filed its counter-claim in the pending
arbitration. Its claims arising from the Settlement Agreement and the related
transactions can be pursued before the Arbitral Tribunal, subject to the
orders passed by that Tribunal. On the other hand, if the Section 7
proceedings are allowed to continue and the insolvency process proceeds
further, the Corporate Debtor may suffer consequences that may not be
capable of being adequately undone if the Appellant ultimately succeeds in
the appeals.

31. We are, therefore, satisfied that the Appellant has made out a prima
facie case for interim protection. The balance of convenience also favours
maintaining the present position until the appeals are heard. The possibility
of irreparable prejudice to the Corporate Debtor cannot be ruled out if the
insolvency proceedings are permitted to continue in the meantime.

32. In view of the above, and without expressing any final opinion on the
merits of the appeals, we order that further proceedings pursuant to the order
dated 29.09.2026 admitting the application filed by Respondent No. 1 under
Section 7 of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, 2016, including further
steps in the corporate insolvency resolution process of ESPO Developers
Private Limited, shall remain stayed until the next order of this Tribunal. The
interim protection granted by this order shall not prevent the Arbitral Tribunal

Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 1966 & 2017 of 2026  13 of 14
from proceeding with the arbitration, including adjudication of the claims and
counter-claims pending before it, in accordance with law. Respondent No. 1
shall remain at liberty to pursue its counter-claim and all other remedies
available to it before the Arbitral Tribunal, subject to law. In the meantime,
the parties shall complete their pleadings in the appeals.

33. It is clarified that the observations in this order are confined to the
consideration of interim relief. They shall not be treated as findings on the
final rights and liabilities of the parties, nor shall they influence the Arbitral
Tribunal while deciding the disputes before it.
