Ministry of Finance

 Public Sector Banks (PSBs) grow stronger with prudent banking practices and strong underwriting capabilities

PSB asset quality at all-time high with historically low stressed assets

PSBs’ gross advances reach ₹127 lakh crore with 18.8% y-o-y growth

Retail, agriculture and MSME advances grow at 17.0%, 19.5% and 20.4%, respectively

Total deposits grow to ₹165.2 lakh crore at 12.9% y-o-y

Net NPA ratio at 0.33%; Gross NPAs fall to ₹44,100 crore

 Posted On:
 26 NOV 2026 6:47PM by PIB Delhi

 The Public Sector Banks (PSBs) continue to grow rapidly with high asset quality, ably supported by reform enabled prudent credit underwriting and effective risk management policies. The systemic reforms instituted in PSBs are showing fruitful results in the form of high quality rapidly paced credit growth across sectors, especially productive credit growth. Credit delivery to MSMEs and corporates by PSBs has also shown remarkable improvement with both growing at fast pace along with Agriculture and Retail. At the same time, the asset quality continues to improve to all-time best, supported by low slippages and strong recoveries in impaired assets. The institutionalisation of reforms has created a strong system of governance led banking in PSBs, supporting economic upcycle.

PSBs’ advances are growing rapidly at 15.1% in last 5-years and reached ₹127 lakh crore as on 30.9.2026. However, at the same time PSBs are also showing signs of strong prudence in credit underwriting through rapidly declining slippages. As against advances of ₹67.7 lakh crore as on 31.3.2021, the slippages were as high as ₹1.44 lakh crore at a rate of 2.35% in FY2021-22. It has now come down to just ₹38,780 crore against advances of ₹127 lakh crore in H1FY2026-27, recording slippage ratio of just 0.62%, a remarkable sign of growth with prudence. 

The credit growth is not only prudent in terms of asset quality, but also is well diversified and focused across sectors. PSBs are extending credit to productive and consumptive sectors simultaneously with high credit growth in Retail, Agriculture, MSME and corporate sectors evenly balanced. While credit to retail and agriculture continues at strong rate over past more than 5-years, credit to MSME and corporates have also picked up to outpace other sectors marginally.

Looking at bank-wise performance in terms of credit growth, almost all banks are evenly extending credit with Bank of Maharashtra outpacing others, while large banks such as, State Bank of India, Canara Bank, Bank of Baroda and Punjab National Bank are also growing rapidly.

Even with high credit growth, continued improvement in slippages ratio is observed across banks with only Punjab & Sind Bank recording marginal decline in slippage ratio, though already having low slippage ratio of 0.57%. Amongst large banks, State Bank of India, Punjab National Bank and Canara Bank continues to have low slippages. All PSBs now have slippage ratio of less than 1%.

The lower slippages have helped banks in maintaining low NPA levels, though aided by sufficient provisioning and strong recovery also. As a result of combined efforts in prudent underwriting and efficient collections, PSBs are maintaining significantly low net NPAs of 0.33%, amounting Rs. 44,100 crore only, in advances of more than Rs. 127 lakh crore. PSBs continue to have significantly low stress in the form of SMAs also, with aggregate advances of only ₹18,800 crore falling in SMA 1 and 2 amongst advances above ₹5 crore. PSBs continue to improve in SMA levels also with ratio of SMA 1 & 2 advances of above ₹5 crore to total standard advances being only 0.14% as on 30.9.2026, as against 0.24% as on 30.9.2025.

Prudent credit underwriting and efficient risk management practices are paving way for a stronger and robust banking sector having capability to support credit needs of the economy for long term.
