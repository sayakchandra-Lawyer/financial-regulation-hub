The Appellant happens to be the erstwhile Resolution Professional, in

2. The crux of the argument of the Appellant before us, as against the
Impugned Order dated 24.04.2023, that was passed by the Ld. NCLT,
Kolkata in I.A. No. 52/KB/2021 in C.P.(IB) No. 832/KB/2019. It is that, the
effect of the impugned order had been that, the Ld. Tribunal drew a
conclusion that in the light of the provision contained under proviso to
Section 3(31) of the I&B Code. Since, it expressly excludes the “performance
guarantee”, thereunder to be brought within the definition of the “Security
Interest” and it was observed that there was no justified reason, as to why
Respondent No.1 could be directed to hand over the amount received from
“ECGC”. The claim in regards to the sum, however, it was left open for the
appellant to be derived from any other fixed deposits, which do not come
within the purview of the “performance guarantee”, thereunder and would
only be returned to the CIRP account. The provision contained under
Section 3(31) of the Code, reads as under:

              “Section 3 definitions
              (31) “security interest” means right, title or interest or a
              claim to property, created in favour of, or provided for a
              secured creditor by a transaction which secures
              payment or performance of an obligation and includes
              mortgage, charge, hypothecation, assignment and
              encumbrance or any other agreement or arrangement
              securing payment or performance of any obligation of
              any person:

Provided that security interest shall not include a performance guarantee;”

20. If we look into the definition of the Security Interest is extracted above
contained under Section 3(31) of the definition clause. It provides a proviso
which specifically observes that the Security Interest would not include a
Performance Guarantee under normal law of interpretation, the proviso
normally itself explanatory in nature which only qualifies the contents of the
principle provision. But the manner in which the proviso to sub-section 31
of Section 3 has been introduced is in the shape of an exception “Causing
an Exclusion”. By virtue of which the Performance Guarantee had been
brought outside the argument of the Security Interest. The guarantee that
was obtained from the ECGC is not a debt as per Section 3(11) of the Code.
For the reason that the amount obtained after invocation of Performance
Guarantee is not a debt. Rather, it is covered under any of the consortium
documents as it is a third-party guarantee provided a surety for violation
terms contained under the said guarantee and would not be covered under
the loan document, hence it could be safely inferred that the amount

                                                                                                               12 of 14
obtained from the ECGC is not a debt but obtain in lieu of non-performance
of obligation covered under the guarantee. When it is non-performance of an
obligation under the facilities availed under the credit limit, it has been
brought under outside the definition of debt, the performance guarantee has
been excluded from the Security Interest since the guarantee provided under
the said ECGC is the performance-based guarantee and it does not come
under the purview under the Security Interest.
21. On a simpliciter interpretation, the Security Interest under the Code
includes the interest that has been created in favour of the secured creditor
by a transaction, which secures of payment against performance of an
obligation but it will not include in it an obligation and that is why it
excludes the performance-based guarantee from the definition of Security
Interest. The carving out an exception under the proviso to sub-section 31 of
Section 3 of the code. In the matters of Bharat Aluminium Co. Ltd. Vs. M/s
J.P. Engineers Pvt. Ltd., it had laid down as under:

                  “Interalia held that 'the bank guarantee is irrevocable
              and unconditional and payable on demand without
              demur. The assets of the surety are separate from those
              of the corporate debtor, and proceedings against the
              corporate debtor may not be seriously impacted by the
              actions against assets of third party like surety. Bank
              guarantee can be invoked even during the moratorium.
              period issued under Section 14 of the IBC in view of the
              amended provision under Section 14(3)(b) of the Code.'”

                                                                                                               13 of 14
22. Owing to the facts which stand admitted herein the Corporate Debtor
had never deposited the said sum obtained by the Bank from ECGC, hence
it cannot be said to be assets of the Corporate Debtor and most of when it is
not Security Interest that has been created over on any valid claim. Thus,
the rejection of the application so far it pertains to handover the amount
received from the ECGC since being exclusively outside the ambit of Section
3(31) proviso and exclusively since being part of the Collateral Security
under the scheme opted for pre-shipment insurance cover will not fall to be
a Security Interest and it has been rightly denied to be granted to the
Appellant as it was prayed for Interlocutory Application preferred before the
Ld. Tribunal. Thus, for the aforesaid reasons, the Company Appeal lacks
merit and the same is accordingly dismissed.
23. All Interlocutory Applications would stand closed.
