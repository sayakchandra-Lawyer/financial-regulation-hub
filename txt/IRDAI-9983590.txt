A workshop on Aadhaar Authentication, DigiLocker and Dark Patterns for the Chief
Compliance Officers (CCOs) of all insurers was held on 2nd November 2026 at the IRDAI
Head Office, Hyderabad.

The workshop was inaugurated by Mr. Ajay Seth, Chairman, IRDAI, and Mr. Saurabh
Vijay, CEO, Unique Identification Authority of India (UIDAI). In his keynote address, the
Chairman encouraged the insurance industry to adopt Aadhaar authentication services,
integrate DigiLocker into its operational workflows and ensure transparent and fair
digital practices. The CEO, UIDAI, outlined how Aadhaar can strengthen processes
across the insurance lifecycle.

Representatives of UIDAI conducted the session on Aadhaar Authentication, which
covered face, biometric and OTP-based authentication. Insurers were urged to adopt
face authentication, which enables liveness detection and can be accessed anytime,
anywhere through a mobile phone. It was highlighted that the use of Aadhaar during
customer on-boarding enables a seamless, faster and paperless experience, while
improving data accuracy and helping mitigate the risk of fraud. Use cases from customer
onboarding to claims settlement were presented, showing how Aadhaar authentication
can make service delivery to policyholders secure, seamless and efficient. Further, it
was emphasized that Aadhaar authentication should be done exclusively from UIDAI
which is the only source of truth.

Representatives of the National e-Governance Division (NeGD), Ministry of Electronics
and Information Technology (MeitY), conducted the session on DigiLocker, presenting
use cases of DigiLocker and Entity Locker across the insurance value chain and product
lines.

The session on Dark Patterns in the Digital Insurance Journey, conducted by Mr. R. K.
Sharma, Executive Director, IRDAI, covered the key provisions of the Guidelines for
Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023, notified by the Central Consumer
Protection Authority (CCPA). Insurers were told to avoid forcing visitors to their website
to part with personal details such as mobile number, as a condition for getting access
to information or generating a quote. It was stressed that digital insurance journeys
should be free of dark patterns and should empower consumers to understand
insurance products, make informed decisions and control their personal information and
communications. Transparent disclosures, fair choice architecture and purpose-specific
consent were identified as key pillars of a fair, responsible and trustworthy digital
insurance ecosystem that builds long-term consumer trust and loyalty.
