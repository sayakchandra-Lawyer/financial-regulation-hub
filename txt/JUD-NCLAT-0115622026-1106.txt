         CA (AT) (Ins) No. 1959 of 2026 is preferred against the order dated
04.09.2026 (Impugned Order) passed by the Hon'ble NCLT, Mumbai Bench-V
(Adjudicating Authority) in I.A. No. 456 of 2026 in CP(IB) 5l8/MB/C-V/2022,
whereby the Adjudicating Authority has recorded adverse findings against the
Appellant/SRA and directed that the conduct of the erstwhile Resolution
Professional (Respondent No. 1), the Successful Resolution Applicant (Appellant)
and Saraswat Co-operative Bank Ltd. (Respondent No. 2), all members of the
Monitoring Committee, be referred to the IBBI for such action as it may be deemed
fit.
2. The present Appeal CA (AT) (Ins) No. 1978 of 2026 is against the order dated
04.09.2026 (“Impugned Order”) passed by the Hon’ble National Company Law
Tribunal, Mumbai (“Adjudicating Authority”) in I.A. No. 456/2026 in CP(IB) No.
518/MB/C-V/2022, whereby, while taking on record the Third Status Report on
implementation of the approved resolution plan for Sristi Hospitality Private
Limited (“Corporate Debtor"), the Adjudicating Authority erroneously observed
that the conduct of the Appellant was not in accordance with law and is referred
the IBBI.
3. Both the aforesaid appeals are presented against the same impugned order
dated 04.09.2026 and, for the sake of convenience, are being disposed of by
passing this common judgment.
4. Facts in brief necessary for the disposal of these appeals are that the
Corporate Debtor was admitted into Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process
                                                             3

(“CIRP”) vide order dated 27.02.2023 passed by the Adjudicating Authority in
C.P.(IB) No. 518/MB/C-V/2022, in a petition filed under Section 7 of the Code
by the Director of the CD, Mr. Santosh R. Shetty, and the Appellant in CA (AT)
(Ins) No. 1978 of 2026 was appointed as the Interim Resolution Professional of
the Corporate Debtor vide order dated 27.02.2023 and was later on confirmed as
the Resolution Professional. The Committee of Creditors of the Corporate Debtor
was constituted by Respondent No. 1 as the sole member holding 100% voting
share. The resolution plan submitted by Respondent No. 2 was approved by the
COC with a 100% voting share on 05.08.2023. Upon approval of the Resolution
Plan by the CoC, the Appellant/RP preferred I.A. No. 3612 of 2023 under Section
30(6) read with Section 31 of the Code before the Adjudicating Authority for
approval of the plan, and the same was approved by the Adjudicating Authority
vide order dated 12.07.2024. After approval of the said Plan, the suspended
director of the CD preferred Company Appeal (AT) (Ins.) Nos. 1378 of 2024 and
1383 of 2024 before this Appellate Tribunal which were dismissed on 14.07.2026.
5. Appellant/erstwhile Resolution Professional and Chairman of the
Monitoring Committee of Sristi Hospitality Private Limited/ CD preferred an I.A.
(I.B.C.)/456/2026 under section 60(5) of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code,
2016 (“IBC/the Code”), seeking to take on record this Third Status Report and to
relieve the Applicant from filing further status report on implementation of the
Resolution Plan.
6. The Adjudicating Authority however noticed that (i) The Resolution Plan
was approved by it on 12.07.2024 and was required to be fully implemented by
27.08.2024 (ii) The SRA's stated position that it would implement the Plan only
upon receipt of favourable orders from the Hon'ble NCLAT, is in complete violation
                                                             4

of settled law and the approved Resolution Plan (iii) That it is only pursuant to
the order dated 07.07.2025 of the Adjudicating Authority, the Plan was hurriedly
implemented on 30.07.2025. (iv) The Corporate Debtor was handed over to the
SRA on 30.07.2025. (v) The conduct of Chairman of the Monitoring Committee
(erstwhile RP) and other members in accepting the stand of the SRA that Plan
would be implemented only after getting favourable order from NCLAT, the
Adjudicating Authority, is not in accordance with law, while taking the third
status report on record, was of the view that conduct of the erstwhile RP, the
SRA, and the other member of the Monitoring Committee, namely, Saraswat Co-
operative Bank Ltd be referred to the IBBI for their wisdom to take such
appropriate steps as it may deem fit, directed the Registry to forward a copy of
this Order to the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) for such action
as it may deem fit.
7. Being aggrieved by the aforesaid order, the erstwhile RP/Chairman of the
Monitoring Committee as well as the SRA have preferred the aforesaid appeals.
8. Learned counsel for the Appellant/SRA submits that the adjudicating
authority, without any cogent reason and factual matrix, has recorded adverse
remarks against the appellant without providing any opportunity of being heard
to the SRA. Reliance in this regard has been held in Defodill's Pharmaceuticals
Limited versus State of UP (2020 18 SCC 550).
9. It is further submitted that there was no occasion for the adjudicating
authority to have recorded any adverse observations/remarks against the
appellant when the appellant was not even arrayed as a party before the
adjudicating authority.
                                                             5

10. It is further submitted that the adjudicating authority, while recording the
adverse remarks, has not even considered this fact that in the clause5.7(ii) of the
resolution plan (which has been approved by the COC as well as by the
adjudicating authority), there is a clear stipulation that mere pendency of appeals
against the resolution plan would be sufficient for making the plan non-
implementable. Therefore, the very basis of recording adverse remarks against
the appellant is illegal and cannot be sustained.
11. It is further submitted that after the approval of the resolution plan by the
COC and Learned Adjudicating Authority, the terms of the same cannot be
changed or dissented to by the SRA. Reliance in this regard has been laid on
Shashidhar versus Indian Overseas Bank (2019) 12 SCC 150, JP Kensington
Boulevard Apartments Welfare Association versus NBCC (India) Limited,
(2022) 1 SCC 401 and Noida Industrial Development Authority vs Prabjit
Singh Soni, (2024) 6 SCC 767.
12. It is further submitted that the Appellant, being the SRA, was under a bona
fide belief that, having regard to the clause 5.7(ii) of the resolution plan, there is
no mandate for him to implement the plan during the pendency of the appeal
filed by the suspended director of the CD before this Appellate Tribunal. When
the learned adjudicating authority, during the course of hearing of the second
status report, revealed its willingness to get the plan implemented, the SRA,
without any further delay, deposited the money, and the RP filed the third status
report.
13. It is further submitted that when the adjudicating authority itself has
approved a resolution plan having a clause that mere pendency of the appeal filed
against the approval of the plan would be sufficient to make the plan non-
                                                             6

implementable, the same adjudicating authority could not accuse the Appellant-
SRA of non-implementation of the plan. Therefore, the adverse remark recorded
by the learned adjudicative authority in paragraph numbers 19 and 20 of the
impugned order may kindly be set aside and expunged.
14. Learned counsel for the Respondent Saraswat Cooperative Bank Limited
submits that clause 5.7 of the approved Resolution Plan specifically provides that
where litigation or legal proceedings are initiated in relation to the Resolution
Plan or the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process, the SRA shall make
payment of the total Resolution Amount within seven business days from the date
on which the approval of the Resolution Plan attains finality and the approved
Resolution Plan itself contemplated the possibility of litigation and prescribed the
timeline for payment in such circumstances. The parties therefore, were required
to act in accordance with the terms of the Plan and the orders passed by this
Hon'ble Tribunal.
15. It is further submitted that the time taken for implementation cannot, by
itself, be treated as misconduct on the part of the Bank. The circumstances must
be considered as a whole, including the pending Appeals, the relevant clauses of
the Resolution Plan and the order passed by this Hon'ble Tribunal. The conduct
of the Bank ought not to be viewed in isolation from these circumstances,
considering the fact that the bank is custodian of public money.
16. It is further submitted that the respondent has acted in the capacity of
financial creditor and a member of the Monitoring Committee and its conduct
should be examined with reference to its obligations under the Code, the
provisions of approved Resolution Plan and the relevant judicial orders. The
challenge by the suspended Director to the resolution plan was pending before
                                                             7

this appellate Tribunal. Therefore, the delay in implementation, in these
circumstances, cannot automatically lead to an adverse conclusion against the
Bank. Further, the Respondent received payment against its claims upon
implementation of the Resolution Plan. No specific act of misconduct, deliberate
obstruction or violation of the approved Plan could be labelled against the Bank
as the SRA agreed to defer implementation till the Appeals were pending.
Therefore, in absence of any specific material establishing violation of plan by the
Respondent no adverse order should have been passed against the Respondent.
17. Learned counsel for the Appellant/erstwhile RP submits that totally
uncalled-for observation have been recorded by the learned adjudicating
authority. It is highlighted that clause 5.7(ii) of the Resolution Plan, as approved
vide order dated 12.07.2024 stipulated payment of the Resolution Amount within
7 days of attaining finality of the Plan if there is no litigation pending anywhere,

resolution plan, the monitoring committee took a decision to defer the
implementation of the plan pending such appeal and when the learned
adjudicating authority had approved the plan with clause 5.7(ii), it was not open
for the Appellants to implement the plan contrary to the stipulation made therein.
18. It is further submitted that the order dated 07.07.2025 has proceeded on
the view then taken in Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power & Steel Ltd. [(2026)
5 SCC 558, judgment dated 02.05.2025. However, this judgment was recalled on
31.07.2025 in Punjab National Bank v. Kalyani Transco, 2025 SCC OnLine

of justice to have a glance on the impugned order in order to assess what has

relevant parts of the impugned judgment are reproduced as under: -

              “10. It is noted that the Resolution Plan submitted by the

              11. Therefore, Clause 10.3 and Clause 10.15 of the
              Resolution Plan expressly provide that the Plan is binding
              and unconditional save for approval by the Adjudicating
              Authority, and that no further act, deed or approval is
              required for its implementation. Further, the Resolution Plan

              12. After approval of the said Plan, the Suspended Director

              This direction from Hon’ble NCLAT was permissive and did

not stay implementation. Accordingly, it is noted that the
Applicant being the Chairman of the Monitoring Committee
was duty bound either to get the Resolution Plan
implemented within timelines or file for liquidation in the

14. As per Clause 9.3 of the Resolution Plan,
implementation of the Plan was required to be completed on
the 46th day from the date of approval by this Tribunal, i.e.,
on or before 27.08.2024. However, it is noted that

is in violation of Clause 10.3, Clause 10.15 read with

                                        12

“14. However, such a clause cannot be interpreted to
have force to override Clause 10.15 of the Resolution
Plan, which expressly provides that the Plan is
unconditional except for its approval by the Adjudicating
Authority. Also, if at all Resolution Plan is considered

approved by the CoC or by this Authority. Any contrary
clause having effect of postponing implementation
of the plan on account of litigation is therefore
contrary to the express terms of the Resolution Plan
itself and accordingly, non-implementation of the
plan on this account cannot be termed as
justifiable.

18. As per Section 30(2) of the Code, the Resolution
Professional is required to examine the Resolution Plan
submitted by the Successful Resolution Applicant and
confirm its compliance with the provisions of the Code.
Further, under Section 30(4), the CoC may approve the
Plan after considering its feasibility and viability and
ensuring that it fulfills other requirements specified by the
Board. Further, Regulation 38(3) of the CIRP Regulations,
2016 mandates that a Resolution Plan must be feasible,
viable, and implementable, with specific timelines for
implementation. In the present case, the Resolution
Plan stipulated a specific timeline for
implementation, namely the 46th day from the date
of approval by this Tribunal. However, the Plan has
not been implemented within the stipulated period.
Such non-implementation defeats the mandatory
requirement under Regulation 38(3) of the CIRP
Regulations, 2016. The continued delay in
implementation of the plan is contrary to the
provision of the Code and Regulation as well as the
timelines expressly stipulated in the Resolution
Plan, and the Monitoring Committee, has failed to
take the necessary action

19. With regard to the Applicant’s submission in the
Affidavit dated 25.07.2025 that the Resolution Plan is not

mentioned that upon approval of the Resolution Plan, the

Resolution Applicant will acquire the control of the affairs

upon approval of the Plan, the responsibility for the its
implementation vests with the Monitoring Committee.
However, in any event, the commercial wisdom of the
                                            13

   CoC cannot override or contravene the provisions of
   laws.”

17. This Tribunal in the above order dated 06.01.2026, held
that clause 5.7.(ii) of the Resolution Plan, to the extent it
purported to make implementation contingent upon finality
of the NCLAT appeals, could not override the express un
conditionality of the Plan under Clause 10.15 and that the
interim order of the Hon'ble NCLAT dated 23.07.2024
imposed no impediment to implementation and, if anything,
obligated the Monitoring Committee to proceed. Further, the
continued non-implementation of the Plan within the
stipulated timeline of 46 days from the date of approval of
Plan by NCLT (46th day from approval of NCLT being the
Transfer date) was contrary to Section 30(2) and 30(4) of the
Code and Regulation 38(3) of the CIRP Regulations, 2016.
The Chairman of the Monitoring Committee was accordingly
directed to act strictly in accordance with law and take all
necessary and appropriate steps as contemplated under the
Code.

18. It is noted that when the present Application came up
for hearing on 30.01.2026, this Tribunal recorded that the

of Plan to proceed pending the outcome of the said appeals

Chairman of the Monitoring Committee took no steps
towards implementation for nearly a year, and it was only

07.07.2025 that implementation was set in motion.
Moreover, no material has been placed on record to
demonstrate that any extension of time for implementation
of the Resolution Plan was ever sought by the SRA from, or
granted by, the Monitoring Committee. Taking into account,
the absence of any such extension, and the Hon'ble NCLAT's

              order permitting implementation to proceed, the SRA did not

26. We notice that the resolution plan is having a clause namely clause

5.7.(ii) (at Page No.43 of the Resolution Plan) which has been reproduced by

the Adjudicating Authority in the impugned order and is reproduced below:

              “5.7.(ii) The Total Resolution Amount offered in this

or tribunal. Significantly, this clause highlights that mere pendency of the appeal
would be sufficient for making the plan not implementable. In other words, if an
appeal has been preferred and is pending before this appellate tribunal, or if there
are any proceedings with regard to the plan before the Hon'ble Supreme Court
(even if no stay has been granted by this appellate tribunal or by the Supreme
Court), there is no foundation for the resolution applicant to make payment to
the stakeholders as provided under paragraph 9.3 of the resolution plan.

36. The contention of the Ld. Counsels for the appellants is that when it has
been indicated by the Adjudicating Authority that the SRA, despite having clause
5.7.(ii) in the Plan, was bound to implement the plan, in absence of any stay order
by this Appellate Tribunal, the plan was implemented at once and was fully
implemented on 30.07.2025.We find substance in the submissions made by
Learned Council for the Appellant as Clause 5.7.(ii) of the Plan clearly provides
and makes the plan implementable only on the filing of an appeal by any party
against the resolution plan (even if no stay has been granted in the appeal and
the learned adjudicating authority has approved that plan without any
modification or change. Therefore, when mere pendency of an appeal against the
plan was sufficient for the SRA not to make any payment to the creditors or any
stakeholder, the SRA or the RP cannot be held liable for the delay. Per contra, in
our considered opinion, it was the duty of the adjudicating authority to have
noticed this discrepancy, which was conspicuous on the face of it, when the
adjudicating authority was approving the resolution plan and this ambiguity
should not have been left on the discretion of the parties/stakeholders of the
resolution plan and if it was not noticed or was not modified, the stakeholders
including the Appellants cannot be held liable for the delay. The Appellants in
our considered opinion were in under a bona fide belief that they are not obliged
to implement the plan when the appeal is pending before us and as soon as they
were given green signal by the Adjudicating Authority vide order dated
07.07.2025 in the form of a query, they implemented the plan. The observation
of this Appellate tribunal vide order dated 23.07.2024, which permitted the
implementation of Plan to proceed pending the outcome of the said appeals
cannot be construed to be a mandate for the Appellants to implement the plan.
                                                            21

It only suggests that no stay has been granted by this Appellate Tribunal during
the pendency of the Appeal.
37. In our considered view the delay which has occurred in implementation of
the plan was sufficiently explained by the appellants before the Adjudicating
Authority and the same should have been accepted, more so when the plan has
been fully implemented on 30th July 2025.

the Learned Adjudicating Authority and no opportunity has been given to the SRA
