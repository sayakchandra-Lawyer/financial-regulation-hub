This Order disposes, afresh, the Show Cause Notice (SCN) No. RV-13012/3/2024-IBBI/401 dated
06.05.2025 issued to Mr. R Vaidyanathan (RV) under rule 17 read with rule 15 of the Companies
(Registered Valuers and Valuation) Rules, 2017 (Valuation Rules), pursuant to the directions of the
Appellate Authority in its Order No. IBBI/Valuation/Disc.(A)/07/2025 dated 07.01.2026 (Appellate
Order). Mr. R Vaidyanathan is registered with Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India (IBBI) as
a valuer of Securities or Financial Assets (SFA) with the Registration number
IBBI/RV/03/2018/10049.

1. Background, issuance of the SCN and proceedings on remand.

1.1. Rule 17(1) of the Valuation Rules provides that, based on material available on record, if the
        authorised officer is of the prima facie opinion that sufficient cause exists to cancel or
        suspend the registration of a valuer, it shall issue a SCN to the valuer.

1.2. IBBI had taken cognizance of the valuation report dated 12.09.2022 submitted by Mr. R
        Vaidyanathan in the Corporate Insolvency Resolution Process (CIRP) of M/s Uniply
        Industries Limited, Corporate Debtor (CD), and of the clarifications submitted by him vide
        email dated 06.05.2024. Several contraventions of the provisions of the Valuation Rules
        were observed with respect to his assignment in the CIRP of the CD and accordingly the
        SCN dated 06.05.2025 was issued to him. The SCN alleged the following three
        contraventions:

        a) Non-inclusion of brand name/trademarks/licenses/intellectual properties in the valuation
            report;

        b) Issue regarding assigning zero value to the investment of the CD; and
        c) Issue regarding assigning Fair Value and Liquidation value for the assets of the CD as

            ‘0/Not Determinable’.

1.3. Mr. R Vaidyanathan submitted his reply to the SCN on 31.05.2025, availed the opportunity
        of personal hearing through virtual mode on 11.09.2025 and submitted his additional
        response vide email(s) dated 17.09.2025. This Authority, vide Order No.
        IBBI/Valuation/Disc./32/2025 dated 27.11.2025 (Earlier Order), examined the
        contraventions alleged in the SCN vis-à-vis the reply and additional submissions made by
        Mr. R Vaidyanathan and suspended his registration for a period of three months.

                              Page 1 of 15
1.4. Mr. R Vaidyanathan preferred an appeal against the Earlier Order under rule 17(9) of the
        Valuation Rules. The Appellate Authority, after hearing him on 17.12.2025 and considering
        his submissions dated 10.12.2025, 19.12.2025 and 22.12.2025, passed the Order dated
        07.01.2026. The Appellate Authority, inter alia, observed and directed as under:

        a) there was no infirmity in the procedure of issuance of the SCN and no misalignment
            between the findings in the Earlier Order and the contraventions alleged in the SCN; the
            assignment of zero and ‘not determinable’ to several assets gave an impression of
            arbitrary assignment of values without justifiable reason (para 3.1 and 3.2);

        b) under regulation 27 read with regulation 35 of the IBBI (Insolvency Resolution Process
            for Corporate Persons) Regulations, 2016 (CIRP Regulations), registered valuers are
            expected to determine the fair value and liquidation value of all the assets of the CD,
            including intangible assets which were not valued on the ground that intangibles were not
            part of the scope of work communicated by the Resolution Professional (RP) (para 3.3);

        c) Mr. R Vaidyanathan, by assigning zero value, completely decimated the value of financial
            assets having a book value of around ₹960 crore; even cash and cash equivalents of
            around ₹1.06 crore were stated to be ‘Not Determinable’, which should have called for
            greater scrutiny and independent due diligence (para 3.5);

        d) Mr. R Vaidyanathan failed to adhere to the International Valuation Standards (IVS),
            including IVS 200 (Businesses and Business Interests) and IVS 210 (Intangible Assets),
            which he claimed to have adopted, and failed to ensure compliance with regulation 35
            read with regulation 27 of the CIRP Regulations (para 4.3);

        e) the punishment of suspension for three months was grossly insufficient given the gravity
            of the misconduct, and the matter was remanded for fresh consideration of the
            contraventions alleged in the SCN, with liberty to Mr. R Vaidyanathan to submit his
            explanations afresh. The Authority was also requested to consider afresh the other issues
            not raised in the appeal (para 4.4); and

        f) Mr. R Vaidyanathan would not be entitled to take any new assignment of valuation till
            disposal of the SCN (para 4.5).

1.5. Pursuant to the Appellate Order, Mr. R Vaidyanathan made a detailed submission dated
        16.10.2026, forwarded the correspondence exchanged with the RP (along with attachments)
        vide emails dated 13.11.2026, and made a further submission dated 19.11.2026. This
        Authority has considered afresh the SCN, the valuation report dated 12.09.2022, the replies
        and submissions made by Mr. R Vaidyanathan at every stage before this Authority and the

3. Fresh examination of the contraventions alleged in the SCN.

        The contraventions alleged in the SCN, the response of Mr. R Vaidyanathan and the findings
        of the Authority are summarised as follows:

3.1. Non-inclusion of Brand Name/trademarks/licenses/intellectual properties in the
        Valuation Report.

3.1.1.  It was noted in the SCN that vide letter dated 20.11.2021, Mr. R Vaidyanathan was appointed
        as registered valuer to determine the fair value and liquidation value of the financial assets
        of the CD. There was a brand license agreement dated 01.10.2017 between Uniply Industries
        Limited (CD/Licensor) and UV Boards Limited (Licensee), granting the use of trade name
        ‘UNIPLY’ and other trademarks/licenses/intellectual properties by the Licensor to the
        Licensee for a consideration of ₹75.00 crore over 10 years as per point 5.3 (royalty payment)
        under Article 5 of that agreement. Annexure A of the valuation report dated 12.09.2022
        acknowledged the existence of this agreement. Despite having knowledge of the same, Mr.

        Page 3 of 15
        R Vaidyanathan has not assigned any value to the brand ‘UNIPLY’ and other
        trademarks/licenses/intellectual properties.

3.1.2.  In the Earlier Order, this Authority had accepted the explanation of Mr. R Vaidyanathan on
        this allegation, after observing that the scope of valuation, as per the appointment letter dated
        20.11.2021, was limited to the financial assets mentioned in the balance sheet of the CD,
        that the ‘brand’ was not mentioned in the appointment letter and that, the ‘brand’ being
        internally generated, was not recognised as an intangible asset in the balance sheet in terms
        of para 63 of Ind AS 38. The Appellate Authority, however, observed that under regulation
        27 read with regulation 35 of the CIRP Regulations registered valuers are expected to
        determine the fair value and liquidation value of all the assets of the CD, including intangible
        assets, and that a conjoint reading of IVS 200 and IVS 210 indicates that intangible assets
        must be considered where relevant.

        Submissions of Mr. R Vaidyanathan.

3.1.3.  Mr. R Vaidyanathan reiterated his earlier submissions (recorded in paras 2.1.5 to 2.1.10 of
        the Earlier Order) and submitted that the issue about the scope of work in respect of the

        27.11.2025.

        “….The valuation report also reflects the valuer’s ignorance to the provisions of the Code

        IBBI (CIRP) Regulations, 2016, the registered valuers are expected to determine the fair
        value and liquidation value of the assets of the CD which should comprise all assets of the
        CD including intangible assets. The valuer cannot shirk off his duty to conduct a full and
        complete valuation of all assets of the CD merely on the ground that intangibles were

3.1.4.  In this regard, he has submitted that the observations in the Appellate Order relating to

        items which were only a small part of all the items in the Balance Sheet while the other
        valuers were also asked to value other items in the balance sheet. In this scenario, if anyone

3.1.6.  Mr. R Vaidyanathan also submitted that the conduct in question related to the valuation
        exercise, at a time when only specified assets had been referred to him, and that he had

3.1.8.  Mr. R Vaidyanathan submitted that the RP was at all times empowered and, in a position, to

3.1.9.  Mr. R Vaidyanathan has further submitted that the definition of ‘fair value’ in regulation
        2(1)(hb) of the CIRP Regulations, as it stood on the valuation date and the report date, did
        not contain any reference to all assets including tangible and intangible assets. The

3.1.10. Mr. R Vaidyanathan has further referred to the following observation mentioned in para 4.3
        of the Order of the Appellate Authority with respect to applicability of valuation standards:

        “It is pertinent to note that the International Valuation Standards under IVS 210 Intangible
        Assets recognises ‘intangible asset’ as “non-monetary asset that manifests itself by its
        economic properties. It does not have physical substance but grants rights and/or economic
        benefits to its owner”. Further, IVS 200 Businesses and Business Interests stipulate that
        valuation of business includes “the sum of the recorded and unrecorded net tangible and
        identifiable intangible assets of the business””. Therefore, conjoint reading of IVS 200 and
        IVS 210 clarifies that intangible assets may derive value independently or in combination
        with other assets and must be considered where relevant. Consequently, this Appellate
        Authority finds that Mr. R. Vaidyanathan failed to adhere to the International Valuation
        Standards (IVS), despite his explicit assertion in his valuation report that such standards
        were adopted and followed by him. In the present case, the material available on record,
        Mr. R Vaidyanathan miserably failed to carry out his assigned duty…..”

3.1.11. In this regard, he reiterated that the RP had engaged three registered valuers, one each for
        ‘Land and Building’, ‘Plant and Machinery’ and ‘Securities or Financial Assets’, and that he
        had been engaged only for the last category. He submitted that each asset class is governed
        by its own applicable standards: ‘Land and Building’ by IVS 400 (Real Property Interests)
        and, where applicable, IVS 410 (Development Property), and ‘Plant and Machinery’ by IVS

        and value these assets as they did not relate to the assets which had been referred to him for
        valuation. Similarly, he was not required to apply IVS 200 (which applies to valuation of
        business and business interests) and IVS 210 (which applies to intangible assets), as neither
        he had been engaged to value the CD as a going concern nor were intangible assets referred
        to him for valuation.

3.1.12. Mr. R Vaidyanathan further submitted that the statement in the valuation report that IVS had
        been adopted meant that the standards applicable to the assets within the scope of work had
        been followed; it did not mean that every asset covered by these standards was to be valued
        by him merely because he followed IVS. He contended that compliance had to be assessed
        with reference to the standards relevant to this engagement, namely IVS 101, 102, 103, 104,
        105 and 500. He therefore submitted that the Appellate Authority’s observation that, despite

3.1.14. This Authority notes that the scope of valuation of Mr. R Vaidyanathan was confined to
        specified financial assets, identified by line items and book values as appearing in the
        balance sheet of the CD. The ‘brand’ of the CD did not find mention in the appointment
        letter. The ‘brand’ of the CD in the present case is internally generated, has not been acquired
        through any business combination and is, therefore, not reflected in the balance sheet of the
        CD, in terms of para 63 of Ind AS 38, which provides that “Internally generated brands,
        mastheads, publishing titles, customer lists and items similar in substance shall not be
        recognized as intangible assets”.

3.1.15. This Authority again after detailed perusal of the scope of work, notes that the engagement
        letter provided a mechanism for amending the scope of work, which was never invoked by
        the RP to include the brand or any other intangible asset. This Authority notes that
        submission of the RV that the RP, having appointed separate valuers for different asset
        classes, was in the best position to identify any asset of the CD which had not been referred
        for valuation, and the record does not show that the RP ever raised any query with Mr. R
        Vaidyanathan regarding the brand. The reference to the trademark licence agreement in
        Annexure A of the valuation report was a reproduction of the list of agreements furnished
        by the RP, accompanied by a disclosure in para 11.5 of the report that the agreements were
        not necessarily related to the scope of the valuation.

3.1.16. This Authority also finds merit in the submission of Mr. R Vaidyanathan that the statement
        in the valuation report that IVS had been adopted meant that the standards applicable to the
        assets within the scope of work had been followed; it did not mean that every asset covered
        by these standards was to be valued by him merely because he followed IVS.

3.1.17. In view of the above, this Authority finds that the ‘brand’ did not find place in the item-wise
        list of financial assets referred for valuation by the RP to Mr. R Vaidyanathan, and he cannot
        be faulted for not valuing an asset that was not referred to him. Accordingly, this Authority
        accepts the explanation of Mr. R Vaidyanathan on this allegation.

3.2. Issue regarding assigning zero value to the investment of the CD.

3.2.1.  It was alleged in the SCN that the share purchase agreement dated 28.02.2018 for purchase
        of 4,00,000 equity shares of Artmatrix Technology Sdn Bhd at ₹6,500 per share by the CD
        was specifically mentioned in the list of agreements shared by the RP (Annexure A of the
        valuation report), but Mr. R Vaidyanathan did not undertake its valuation, leading to
        undervaluation of the CD.

        Submissions of Mr. R Vaidyanathan.

3.2.2.  Mr. R Vaidyanathan reiterated his earlier submissions (recorded in paras 2.2.4 to 2.2.10 of
        the Earlier Order) that the share purchase agreement stood terminated vide agreement dated
        08.11.2018, under which KKN Holdings Private Limited was to repay the advance money
        of ₹260 crore; that Note 6 of the annual report of the CD for FY 2019-20 lists the investments
        of ₹172.79 crore as comprising only the investments in Uniply Décor Limited and Vector
        Projects India Private Limited; and that the investment in Artmatrix was neither included
        nor contemplated within the assets identified for valuation. He has requested that the findings
        of the Earlier Order on this issue be considered.

        Findings of the Authority.

3.2.3.  This Authority notes from the material available on record that the share purchase agreement
        for purchase of 4,00,000 equity shares of Artmatrix Technology Sdn Bhd at ₹6,500 per share
        was entered into by the CD with KKN Holdings Private Limited on 28.02.2018 and was
        terminated vide agreement dated 08.11.2018. This also finds support from the details of
        investments given in the balance sheet of the CD, wherein the investment in shares of
        Artmatrix has been shown as ₹97,50,00,000 as on 31.03.2019 but as nil as on 31.03.2020.
        Since, as per the latest balance sheet, the shares of Artmatrix were not an asset of the CD,
        this Authority accepts the submission of Mr. R Vaidyanathan regarding not valuing the
        investment of the CD in the shares of Artmatrix.

3.3. Issue regarding assigning Fair Value and Liquidation Value for the assets of the CD as
        ‘0/Not Determinable’.

3.3.1. It was noted in the SCN that the statements in the valuation report acknowledge that Mr. R
        Vaidyanathan had been provided with certain information, data and records in connection
        with the valuation engagement. Despite the existence of total current and non-current

                                                    Page 8 of 15
        (financial) assets of ₹960.45 crore, including trade receivables, he assigned ‘0/Not
        Determinable’ as the fair value and liquidation value. It was alleged that such undervaluation
        of the assets is in contravention of clauses 3 and 6 of the Code of Conduct, which read as
        under:

        “3. A valuer shall endeavor to ensure that he/it provides true and adequate information
             and shall not misrepresent any facts or situations.
            …………

        6. A valuer shall render at all times high standards of service, exercise due diligence,
             ensure proper care and exercise independent professional judgment.”

3.3.2.  In the Earlier Order, this Authority had held that, in light of the unavailability of adequate
        documents/information and considering the effect of the limitation period, the assignment of
        ‘zero’ value to the trade receivables without due justification was not appropriate. The
        Appellate Authority, in addition, observed that Mr. R Vaidyanathan completely decimated
        the value of financial assets having a book value of around ₹960 crore; that even cash and
        cash equivalents of around ₹1.06 crore were stated to be ‘Not Determinable’, which called
        for greater scrutiny and independent due diligence; and that the report did not adhere to the
        reporting requirements of IVS 103.

        Submissions of Mr. R Vaidyanathan.

3.3.3.  Mr. R Vaidyanathan reiterated his earlier submissions (recorded in paras 2.3.5 to 2.3.9 of the
        Earlier Order and paras 2.3 and 2.4 of the Appellate Order) and submitted that the issue of
        assigning ‘Not Determinable’ to the Cash and Cash Equivalents and other assets had been
        examined in para 2.3.12 and 2.3.13 of the Earlier Order dated 27.11.2025.

3.3.4. Mr. R Vaidyanathan has referred to the following observation mentioned in para 3.5 of the
        Order of the Appellate Authority with respect to assigning value as zero or Not
        Determinable:

        “It is observed that Mr. R. Vaidyanathan in his estimation of financial assets of the CD
        completely decimated its value by assigning zero value which otherwise had a book value of
        around Rs.960 crores. Even the value of cash and cash equivalents which had a book value
        of around Rs. 1.06 crores have been stated to be ‘Not Determinable’ citing reasons of lack
        of availability of information. It should have called for greater scrutiny and independent due
        diligence by any valuer. Mr. R. Vaidyanathan cannot shrug off his duty and responsibility of
        due diligence by giving a plain statement that the information related to such assets were
        not available. This conduct of valuer falls short of the reasonable expectation from a
        professional whose expertise and skills are most required in challenging circumstances. It
        may be noted that during the insolvency resolution process, the CD is already in stressed
        condition, the management of which is taken by an independent Resolution Professional,
        and therefore it would require greater professional expertise and higher level of due
        diligence by a valuer to efficiently carry on his duties. This Appellate Authority finds that in

        Findings of the Authority.

3.3.16. This Authority has perused the valuation report, which sets out in detail, asset-wise, the
        valuation process adopted, the information sought, the information received and the reasons
        for the values assigned (as summarised in para 2.3.10 of the Earlier Order). It is noted that
        Mr. R Vaidyanathan sought specific information from the RP through emails dated
        09.01.2022, 10.01.2022, 18.01.2022, 19.01.2022, 31.01.2022 and 24.02.2022, and that the
        RP, in his written representation dated 15.06.2022, recorded that the CD had not produced
        bank statements for any of the bank accounts as on or after the insolvency commencement
        date, that no physical cash had been produced before him during the CIRP, and that no
        further information in terms of ageing, invoices or confirmation of balances was available.

3.3.17. As regards cash and cash equivalents, this Authority finds merit in the submission of Mr. R
        Vaidyanathan. He in his email dated 19.01.2022 raised flags about the handling large cash
        balances which is not a usual business practices, made specific enquiries in this regard, and
        obtained the RP’s representation that no physical cash had been produced. In these
        circumstances, assigning a positive value would have implied the existence of cash that
        could not be verified, while assigning zero would have implied a confirmed nil balance. The
        use of ‘Not Determinable’, with the reasons disclosed in the report, cannot be said to be a
        failure of due diligence or a misrepresentation. The same reasoning applies to the bank
        balances.

3.3.18. This Authority also accepts the submission of Mr. R Vaidyanathan that the book value of an
        asset, by itself, is not conclusive of its realisable value, and that the aggregate book value of
        ₹960.45 crore cannot be the measure of undervaluation in the absence of information
        establishing the existence and recoverability of the underlying assets. In respect of other
        current assets, other non-current assets and other financial assets, the report records the
        information sought, the information not received and the reasons for the conclusion, and the
        explanation of Mr. R Vaidyanathan on these assets is accepted. Further, the valuation report
        discloses the scope, approaches, methods, inputs, assumptions, limitations and principal
        reasons for the conclusions reached and, read with the contemporaneous correspondence,
        this Authority accepts his submission regarding compliance with the reporting requirements.

                                                    Page 13 of 15
3.3.19. However, this Authority notes that the position with respect to trade receivables (other than
        those from KKN) is different. It is noted from the valuation report that the RV did not have
        adequate documents/information for valuation of the trade receivables, as captured in the
        report itself (“Adequacy of Information - NOT ADEQUATE”). In such a scenario of
        inadequate documents/information, according ‘zero’ value to the receivables is not
        appropriate. With respect to cash and cash equivalents, other non-current assets and non-
        current other financial assets, and even the trade receivable from KKN, in the absence of
        necessary documents/information Mr. R Vaidyanathan reported the value as ‘Not
        Determinable’. It is not clear as to why, with respect to the other trade receivables, he
        accorded ‘zero’ value when the same situation of unavailability of adequate
        documents/information persisted. Mr. R Vaidyanathan has himself submitted that a value of
        ‘Zero’ conveys an affirmative conclusion which ought to be supported by adequate evidence;
        no such evidence of irrecoverability has been brought on record in respect of these trade
        receivables.

3.3.20. Further, the valuation report states that the dues are not less than 26/27 months old. Thus, by
        his own admission, the CD still had the right to recover the dues as the limitation period of
        three years had not expired on the valuation date. It is also noted that the last audited financial
        statements of the CD for FY 2019-20, relied upon by Mr. R Vaidyanathan, describe the trade
        receivables as ‘considered good’. While this Authority agrees that legal enforceability is not
        by itself determinative of economic value, the absence of collections during the CIRP and
        the non-availability of records do not, by themselves, establish that the receivables had no
        realisable value. The fact that no recovery was made up to the liquidation commencement
        date is an event subsequent to the valuation date and does not justify the conclusion reached
        as on 04.10.2021.

3.3.21. This Authority notes that in light of unavailability of adequate documents/information and
        considering the effect of the limitation period, assigning of ‘zero’ value to the trade
        receivables (other than those from KKN), without due justification, is not appropriate and is
        in contravention of clause 6 of the Code of Conduct.

4. Order.

4.1. The valuation report is based on the professional opinion of the valuer backed by his
        expertise and skills. Based on the valuation report, various critical decisions are taken during
        the CIRP and liquidation process, such as approving a resolution plan for the CD or fixing
        the base price for auction of the assets of the CD. A valuation report is inherently subjective
        in nature, and its reliability decreases with increase in subjectivity. Therefore, it is necessary
        for a valuer to exercise a high degree of due diligence while preparing the valuation report.

4.2. On fresh consideration of the contraventions alleged in the SCN, this Authority has accepted
        the explanation of Mr. R Vaidyanathan on the allegations relating to non-inclusion of brand
        name/trademarks/licenses/intellectual properties and non-valuation of the investment in
        Artmatrix Technology Sdn Bhd, as well as on the assignment of ‘Not Determinable’ values
        to cash and cash equivalents and the other assets. However, the assignment of ‘zero’ value

        to trade receivables of about ₹161.90 crore, without due justification, has been found to be
        in contravention of clause 6 of the Code of Conduct. This Authority has also taken note of
        the observation of the Appellate Authority regarding the adequacy of the punishment, the
        significance of the trade receivables in the assets of the CD, the fact that Mr. R Vaidyanathan
        has been restrained from accepting new valuation assignments since 07.01.2026 in terms of
        para 4.5 of the Appellate Order, and that there is no allegation of mala fide or personal gain
        against him.
