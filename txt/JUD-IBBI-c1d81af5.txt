The present matter relates to I.A. 7094 of 2026 filed by Applicant-Excel

Arcade Pvt. Ltd. seeking recall of the order dated 22.05.2026 (hereinafter

referred to as the ‘impugned order’) passed by this Tribunal in I.A. No. 1245

of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT)(Ins) No. 330 of 2026.

2. Making submissions on behalf of the Applicant, Shri Gaurav Mitra, Ld.
Sr. Counsel submitted that the Applicant vide Company Appeal No. 330 of
2026 had challenged an order dated 08.12.2025 passed by Adjudicating
Authority in CP (IB) No. 1068/MB/2023. This appeal was accompanied with
an application praying for condonation of delay in filing of the appeal which

Applicant cannot be blamed or held responsible in any manner. The appeal
could thereafter be effectively e-filed only on 28.01.2026.

9. We have heard the arguments advanced by both the parties as well as
perused the records carefully.

10. The first issue before us is the maintainability of the recall application.
To answer this question, we would like to rely on the principles set out by the
judgement of the Hon’ble Supreme Court of India in Budhia Swain v.
Gopinath Deb (1999) 4 SCC 396. This judgement delves into the intricacies
surrounding the powers to review or recall orders and serves as a reference-

                                                                                               Page 5 of 10

I.A No. 7094 of 2026 in I.A. No. 1245 of 2026 in Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) No. 330 of 2026
point in delineating the boundaries of review and recall powers. The relevant

extract from the order is as reproduced below:

   “8. In our opinion a tribunal or a court may recall an order earlier made by
   it if:
   (i) the proceedings culminating into an order suffer from the inherent lack
   of jurisdiction and such lack of jurisdiction is patent,
   (ii) there exists fraud or collusion in obtaining the judgment,
   (iii) there has been a mistake of the court prejudicing a party, or
   (iv) a judgment was rendered in ignorance of the fact that a necessary
   party had not been served at all or had died and the estate was not
   represented.
   The power to recall a judgment will not be exercised when the ground for
   reopening the proceedings or vacating the judgment was available to be
   pleaded in the original action but was not done or where a proper remedy
   in some other proceeding such as by way of appeal or revision was
   available but was not availed. The right to seek vacation of a judgment
   may be lost by waiver, estoppel or acquiescence.
   9. A distinction has to be drawn between lack of jurisdiction and a mere
   error in exercise of jurisdiction. The former strikes at the very root of the
   exercise and want of jurisdiction may vitiate the proceedings rendering
   them and the orders passed therein a nullity. A mere error in exercise of
   jurisdiction does not vitiate the legality and validity of the proceedings and
   the order passed thereon unless set aside in the manner known to law by
   laying a challenge subject to the law of limitation.”
11. It is the case of the Applicant that the recall application is warranted

since the impugned order had failed to appreciate that their attempt to e-file

the appeal within the prescribed limitation period had failed not on account

of any lapse or negligence on their part but because of system failure of the

e-filing portal which had turned unresponsive. To overcome the stalemate

arising out of the technical glitch, the Applicant had been compelled to send

the appeal petition by email but the same got quarantined due to back-end

phishing control checks put in place by the Registry. It was also contended

that while in terms of the extant NCLAT circular, an email cannot substitute

I.A No. 7094 of 2026 in I.A. No. 1245 of 2026 in Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) No. 330 of 2026
the requirement of e-filing, in the present case, this recourse of filing the
appeal through email had to be resorted to because of the exceptional
circumstances on account of non-responsiveness of the e-filing portal of
NCLAT Registry. Furthermore, in view of the fact that in similarly placed
peculiar circumstances, the Mamta Binani judgment had restored the delay
condonation application therein, applying the same ratio, the present recall
application stood well justified.

12. Per contra, it is the case of the Respondent that since none of the
grounds for recall as outlined in the Budhia Swain judgement have been
demonstrated by the Applicant to be applicable in the present case, the
question of entertaining the recall application does not arise. It is also their
contention that it is a misconceived proposition on the part of the Applicant
to rely on the Mamta Binani judgment for in that case the rejection of the
delay condonation application was challenged by filing an appeal before the
Hon’ble Supreme Court and was not a recall application.

13. When we look at the material on record, we find that it has not been
substantiated by the Applicant as to whether and how the impugned order
can be said to have been obtained by fraud or collusion or passed without
their impleadment as necessary party or that the court lacked jurisdiction to
pass such an order or was mistakenly passed so as to justify the filing of a
recall. We also notice that in the present case, the recall application was filed
on 24.09.2026 after lapse of more than four months since the passing of the
impugned order by this Tribunal on 22.05.2026. The Applicant did not
challenge the impugned order and waited until the passing of the Mamta

I.A No. 7094 of 2026 in I.A. No. 1245 of 2026 in Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) No. 330 of 2026
Binani judgment on 10.09.2026 and thereafter within a fortnight therefrom,
filed the recall application on 24.09.2026. The recall application not having
been premised on any of the grounds provided for in the Budhia Swain
judgement, this is clearly a camouflaged attempt to seek review of the
judgment in the guise of recall which cannot be permitted as it upsets the
doctrine of finality.

14. The Budhia Swain judgement as extracted above reinforces the
principle of finality and mandates that challenges to orders must be grounded
in expressly provided legal avenues and processes. The principle of res
judicata and the doctrine of finality is one of the edifices on which the judicial
architecture rests to checkmate arbitrary or unfounded attempts to overturn
concluded decisions.

15. The impugned order which has been sought for recall was a reasoned,
speaking order which was never challenged by Applicant before the Hon’ble
Supreme Court even after having participated in the proceedings therein
before this Tribunal. Upon an order having attained finality, any party
aggrieved by the order must exhaust his remedies within the legal framework.
The principle of res judicata is hinged on the pivotal framework that issues
once decided conclusively in a lis may not be reopened for if it were to be
otherwise, no dispute can be resolved or concluded. It is no longer open to the
litigant to undo the effect of any order unless it is established that the order
was obtained by fraud/collusion or that necessary party was excluded
therefrom or that the court lacked jurisdiction or had mistakenly passed the
order. It matters little as to whether the order passed was erroneous as long

                                                                                               Page 8 of 10

I.A No. 7094 of 2026 in I.A. No. 1245 of 2026 in Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) No. 330 of 2026
as lis is heard and a conclusive finding thereon has been rendered and such
finding has remained unchallenged. In the present case, the delay
condonation application stood decided on the merits and was not challenged
within the prescribed period of limitation before the next higher appellate
body, hence, the order had acquired finality. The recall application, in the
shape that it has been filed, has the characteristics of review of an order
passed on merits. We are of the considered view that allowing the present
recall application will tantamount to disturbing the foundational principles of
res judicata which we do not countenance.

16. This brings us to the second limb of argument canvassed by the
Respondent that in the Mamta Binani judgment, the Hon’ble Supreme Court
took cognisance of the fact that the litigant therein had overshot the 30 + 15
days limitation period by only one day while in this case, the Applicant has
tried to gloss over 5 days delay beyond 45 days after the initial e-filing on
22.01.2026 purportedly failed. To our minds, the plea taken by the Applicant
that there were 3 days of intervening holidays to explain the delay is a
frivolous argument since e-filing facility in the Registry remains functional
and open 24x7x365 and holidays do not come in the way of e-filing of an
appeal. The Applicant has thus grossly failed to explain the mitigating reasons
for the 5 days delay in filing of the appeal beyond the statutorily permissible
period which puts serious question marks on the claim of the Applicant that
they were steadfastly pursuing their quest for filing Company Appeal (AT)(Ins)
No. 330 of 2026 in a bonafide manner.

                                                                                               Page 9 of 10

I.A No. 7094 of 2026 in I.A. No. 1245 of 2026 in Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) No. 330 of 2026
17. At this stage it is also felt necessary to observe that since this is a recall
application, we are neither expected nor do we wish to enter into the merits
of the impugned order. Hence, we are refraining from looking into the
contention raised by the Applicant with regard to the absence of time-stamps
in the report of the NIC regarding the 70 filings purportedly completed on the
e-filing portal on the same day on which the Applicant had admittedly failed
to e-file their appeal. The power of recall is not the power to rehear the case
to find out any apparent error in the judgment. This lies in the scope and
ambit of a review of judgment which power to review has not been vested upon
this Tribunal.

18. In result, the present recall application lacks merit and is accordingly
dismissed. No costs.
