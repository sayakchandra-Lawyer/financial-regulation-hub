As health insurance coverage expands, transparency in claim decisions, timely claim
updates and consistent reporting of claims and grievance outcomes are increasingly
important for policyholders. Standardised metrics and real-time visibility of the claims
journey can facilitate performance assessment, strengthen accountability and improve
policyholder confidence.

To advance these objectives, The IAC Sub-Committee on Health Insurance,
constituted by IRDAI, has formed two study groups to recommend standardised claims
and grievance metrics, a common framework for claim-related reason codes and real-
time visibility of the health insurance claims journey, with a view to enhancing
transparency and accountability.

Study Group 1 — Claims and grievance metrics and standardised reason codes

The first study group will develop outcome-oriented claims and grievance metrics
covering key indicators such as Paid-to-Claimed Ratio, settlement and rejection rates,
partial settlements (disallowances), average deductions, cashless utilisation and
claims turnaround times. It will also suggest a common taxonomy and standardised

definitions to ensure consistency in reporting and facilitate meaningful comparison
across insurers.

The group will also propose a common national code framework for claim rejections,
repudiations, deductions and partial settlements, including mapping of existing insurer
and TPA codes. The framework will seek to ensure that every rejection or deduction is
supported by a specified reason and the corresponding amount affected, thereby
enhancing transparency in claim decisions.

Study Group 2 — Real-time visibility of the health insurance claims journey

The second study group will recommend a mechanism to provide policyholders and
other stakeholders with real-time visibility of the standardised health insurance claims
journey, from pre-authorisation to final settlement or payment.

The group will examine existing communication processes and recommend a
mechanism for capturing key claim events and milestones through time-stamped
records, with minimal intervention. It will also define the information to be made
available at each stage, including claim status, pending requirements, action required
and the party responsible for the next action, along with appropriate modes of
communication to keep policyholders informed.

The group will further recommend an implementation framework, including a pilot
involving selected insurers, TPAs and hospitals, followed by phased adoption across
the industry.

Composition

The study groups comprise representatives from general and health insurance
companies, third-party administrators (TPAs), the Insurance Information Bureau of
India (IIB), the Asian Development Bank and the World Bank, bringing together
regulatory, industry and international expertise to examine the issues and develop
practical recommendations.

Both study groups will be chaired by Dr. Ayyaj Tamboli, Executive Director, IRDAI,
convened by designated officers of the Health Department, IRDAI, and coordinated by
Shri Manoj Kumar, Chief General Manager (Health Insurance), IRDAI.

Both groups will submit their reports to IRDAI by 26th December, 2026, setting out their
findings, recommendations and proposed implementation arrangements, as
applicable.
