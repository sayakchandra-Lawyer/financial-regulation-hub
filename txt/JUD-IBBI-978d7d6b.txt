The present matter relates to I.A. 6907 of 2026 filed by Applicant-

Directorate of Enforcement (‘ED’ in short) seeking recall of the order dated

16.04.2026 passed by this Tribunal in CA(AT)(Ins)No. 460 of 2026 (hereinafter

referred to as the “impugned order”) wherein the order of the Adjudicating

Authority dated 27.02.2026 was set aside and I.A No. 1817 of 2023 filed by

the Resolution Professional (‘RP’ in short) seeking approval of the resolution

plan was revived for consideration by the Adjudicating Authority. Aggrieved

2. Throwing light on the factual background of the matter at hand, Shri
Sanjeev Menon, the Ld. Counsel for the Applicant-ED submitted that the
Adjudicating Authority in its order dated 27.02.2026 had rejected the plan
approval application filed by the RP vide I.A No. 1817 of 2023 and remanded
the plan proposal back to the CoC for its consideration for re-examination in
the wake of subsequent developments including the Provisional Attachment
Order (‘PAO’ in short) dated 09.01.2026 passed by the ED provisionally
attaching some of the assets of the Corporate Debtor. This order of 27.02.2026
was challenged before this Tribunal by the Successful Resolution Applicant
(‘SRA’ in short). This Tribunal on 16.04.2026 passed the impugned order
reviving I.A. No. 1817 of 2023 before the Adjudicating Authority for passing
of fresh order in accordance with law in pursuance of which the Adjudicating
Authority passed an order on 19.05.2026 approving the resolution plan.

3. It was submitted by the Applicant that the impugned order was passed
by this Tribunal on 16.04.2026 without hearing or issuing any notice to the
ED though the order of the Adjudicating Authority which was under challenge
had been passed inter-alia on the basis of submissions of the ED. It was

added that the order of the Adjudicating Authority dealt with properties which
stood attached under PAO orders passed by the ED and the applicability of
Section 32A of the IBC on such properties. It was added that the impugned
order was passed without the Applicant being able to put forth their
arguments on Section 32A of IBC in the light of judgements of this Tribunal

2025 SCC OnLine NCLAТ 1652. Since this Tribunal had passed orders
without affording the ED an opportunity of hearing to field their submissions,
this amounted to breach of the principles of natural justice and therefore the
impugned order deserves to recalled. Reliance was placed on the judgment of
the Hon’ble Supreme Court in Asit Kumar Kar Vs. State of W.B. (2019) 2
SCC 703 wherein it has been held that it is a basic principle of justice that
no adverse orders should be passed against a party without hearing the party
and that an order which is passed without giving an opportunity of hearing
to an affected party can be recalled. It was further argued that it is well settled
that where an order is passed without affording a hearing to a person who is
likely to be affected by the order, such a person may seek recall of the order
by which it is aggrieved and that in this backdrop the ED-Applicant has
approached this Tribunal seeking recall of its order dated 16.04.2026. It was
asserted that the recall application is maintainable under Rule 11 of the
NCLAT Rules, 2016 which Rules do not lay down any specific limitation
period. Once the Applicant became aware of the impugned order dated
16.04.2026, it filed the recall application on 20.07.2026 which was contended
as a reasonable period of time.

7. We have heard Ld. Counsels for the parties and perused the records
carefully.

8. The short question before us is whether there is any cogent ground
made out by the Applicant for filing this recall application.

9. For better appreciation of the matter at hand, we would like to first
notice some of the relevant dates necessary for deciding this recall application.
The Corporate Debtor had been admitted into CIRP on 05.12.2018. The CoC
approved the resolution plan of the SRA on 15.09.2022. Thereafter the RP had
filed plan approval application before the Adjudicating Authority vide I.A No.
1817 of 2023 on 20.01.2023. Much after the plan approval application had
been filed before the Adjudicating Authority, the Applicant-ED had issued
PAO order on 09.01.2026. The Adjudicating Authority did not approve the
plan approval application inter-alia on account of the PAO order dated
09.01.2026 and related developments and instead remanded back the
resolution plan to the CoC on 27.02.2026 for reconsideration and re-
examination. The order of the Adjudicating Authority dated 27.02.2026 was
challenged before this Tribunal. By the impugned order dated 16.04.2026,

I.A. No. 6907 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 460 of 2026
this Tribunal revived I.A No. 1817 of 2023 before the Adjudicating Authority
for passing of a fresh order in accordance with law.

10. It is the case of the Applicant that they were not heard before passing
of the impugned order by this Tribunal. It was asserted that notice should
have been issued to the ED by this Tribunal as it was exercising its appellate
jurisdiction on the order of the Adjudicating Authority of 27.02.2026 which
was passed on the basis of submissions made by the ED. Having not
impleaded the ED it tantamount to denying a necessary party an opportunity
of hearing. A necessary party not being present before this Tribunal makes it
a fit case for recall.

11. When we look at the chronological sequence of events, we find that the
impugned order was passed on 16.04.2026 in which the Applicant was
undoubtedly not a party. It is also an undisputed fact that the Applicant had
become aware of the impugned order on 15.05.2026 as is evident from para
20 of the I.A No. 6907 of 2026 filed by the Applicant as reproduced below:

   “That it is submitted that since the ED was not made a party before this
   Ld. Tribunal, it was not aware of the Order of Ld. Tribunal dated
   16.04.2026 until the matter came up for hearing before the Ld. NCLT in
   the remanded proceedings”
12. The above pleading is an unequivocal admission that the Applicant was
well aware of the impugned order when the proceedings were remanded back
by this Tribunal to the Adjudicating Authority and was being heard.
Furthermore, on a pointed query made by this Bench, it was also admitted by
the Counsel for the Applicant that they had become aware of the remanded
proceedings on 16.05.2026. Thus, when it was pretty much within the

                                                                           Page 6 of 11

I.A. No. 6907 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 460 of 2026
knowledge of the Applicant-ED by 16.05.2026 that the impugned order had
remanded the plan approval application back to the Adjudicating Authority,
they had ample opportunity to challenge the same by exercising the statutory
remedy available to them under Section 62 of the IBC.

13. If the Applicant on gaining awareness of the impugned order harboured
reasons for feeling aggrieved by it, nothing had stopped them from exercising
the statutory remedy of approaching the Hon’ble Apex Court as the limitation
period of 45 plus 15 had not yet expired. The Applicant-ED, instead, waited
until 20.07.2026 to file the present recall application. No credible reason has
been placed on record as to why the Applicant did not proceed to challenge
the impugned order despite having sufficient time to do so. Thus, though the
Applicant had ample opportunity to protect its interest against the impugned
order, for reasons better known to them, they had chosen not to avail of this
opportunity. Where a proper remedy such as by way of appeal was available
but was not availed, in such circumstances, filing of the recall application
tantamount to indirectly filing of an appeal against the impugned order of
16.04.2026 camouflaged as a recall. Allowing the recall in effect would mean
reopening of the hearing of Company Appeal No. 460 of 2026 which is
impermissible as the power of recall is not power of the Tribunal to rehear the
case.

14. Interestingly from the material on record, it is also clear that the
Adjudicating Authority after hearing the remanded proceedings had passed
orders approving the resolution plan on 19.05.2026. However, yet again, the
Applicant-ED did not file any appeal against the 19.05.2026 order passed by

I.A. No. 6907 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 460 of 2026
the Adjudicating Authority approving the plan. A plan once approved by the
Adjudicating Authority and not having been challenged within the period
available for filing of a statutory appeal acquires finality and binds all
stakeholders including government authorities. This lends force and weight to
the contention of the Respondents that the present recall application is crafted
to seek review of the matter under the guise of recall.

15. The other limb of argument canvassed by the Applicant to justify their
recall application is that the impugned order so delivered was adverse to their
interests as the properties attached by the ED could not have formed part of
the resolution plan. To buttress their contention, the findings of this Tribunal
in the context of Section 32A of the IBC as contained in para 11 of the
impugned order has been attacked by the Applicant in their recall application.

16. To return our finding, we may first refer to Para 11 of the impugned
order of 16.04.2026 which is as extracted below:

“11. The next area of concern which led the Adjudicating Authority to
remand the plan back to the CoC relates to the subject property which
had come under PAO of the ED. It is the contention of the RP that the
attachment order had been issued subsequent to the approval of the plan
by the CoC. It was also emphatically asserted that the attachment was
only provisional and not final in nature as borne out from ED’s own
affidavit dated 02.02.2026. It was further contended by the RP that in
terms of Section 32A of the IBC, once a Resolution Plan is approved and
there is a change in management of the Corporate Debtor, the Corporate
Debtor and its properties stand protected from prosecution and
attachment for offences committed prior to the commencement of the CIRP.
Reliance was placed on the judgement of the Hon’ble Supreme Court in
Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Limited & Ors. in Civil
Appeal No. 1808 of 2020 which has clearly recognized the effect of Section
32A of the IBC in protecting the assets of the Corporate Debtor from
enforcement proceedings arising out of prior offences once the Resolution
Plan stands approved and such property is covered under the Resolution

     Plan approved by the Adjudicating Authority under Section 31 of the IBC.
     Further, it was pointed out that this Hon’ble Tribunal in Vantage Point
     Asset Management Pvt. Ltd. v. Gaurav Misra, Resolution Professional of
     Alchemist Infra Realty Ltd in CA(AT)(Ins.) No. 1495 of 2024 reiterated that
     attachments or enforcement actions in relation to prior offences cannot
     impede the implementation of a Resolution Plan once the statutory
     protections under the IBC are triggered. We also notice that the RP had
     kept apprised the CoC of the ED proceedings. It is also the contention of
     the SRA that it had submitted its plan after conducting due diligence and
     after verifying all the information contained in the Information
     Memorandum and it was aware that as SRA it was eligible to claim
     benefit under Section 32A of the IBC which provides them protection in
     respect of properties provisionally attached by the ED. In this backdrop of
     RP having kept apprised the CoC of the ED proceedings and that the SRA,
     before submitting his plan, was also aware of the PAO on having done
     his share of due diligence, mere existence of PAO cannot be seen to render
     the resolution plan incapable of implementation. The Adjudicating
     Authority has failed to appreciate the statutory protection provided under
     Section 32A of the IBC and the binding judicial precedents governing the
     interplay between insolvency proceedings and enforcement actions. We
     therefore fail to appreciate the concern expressed in the impugned order
     of the need to remand the matter back again to the CoC on this ground.”

                                                                                 (Emphasis supplied)

17. It becomes clear from the above excerpts of the impugned order that

what was considered by this Tribunal in CA(AT)(Ins)No. 460 of 2026 was

whether the plan already approved by the CoC should go back again to the

CoC for a fresh exercise of the CoC’s commercial wisdom. The impugned order

had only set aside the decision of the Adjudicating Authority to remand the

plan back to the CoC and in doing so, the impugned order did not either stay

or set aside the PAO of the ED-Applicant.

18. A recall is warranted only where an adverse order is passed against a
party/person without hearing the party. This Appellate Tribunal in the
impugned order has merely directed the Adjudicating Authority to reconsider
the plan proposal in accordance with law and did not adjudicate either on the

                                                                           Page 9 of 11

I.A. No. 6907 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 460 of 2026
sustainability and tenability of the resolution plan or the enforcement actions
qua the PAO orders. Prima-facie, no adverse order had been passed against
the Applicant-ED by this Tribunal in the impugned order, there is no
substance in the argument that the impugned order needs to be recalled on
grounds of non-impleadment of the ED while passing the impugned order.

19. The judgment in Asit Kumar Kar cannot come to the rescue of the
Applicant as the facts in that case are clearly distinguishable for in that case,
the licenses of the Applicants were cancelled without hearing them, while in
the present case, no rights of the ED were adjudicated upon nor any interest
of the Applicant interfered with by the impugned order. As the impugned order
did not contain any directions against the ED, it is a misconceived proposition
on part of the Applicant to claim to have been prejudiced by the impugned
order or that the impugned order could not have been passed without them
having been made a party therein and also heard. Hence, the reliance placed
on Asit Kumar Kar judgment is wholly misplaced.

20. Further, when we see prayer (b) of the recall application which has been
reproduced in the opening paragraph, it clearly seeks declaration that the
properties attached by the ED could not have formed part of the resolution
plan. Effectively this amounts to seeking a substantive declaration on merits
which is wholly outside the scope of a recall application. Thus, we are
persuaded to believe that the Applicant-ED under the guise of recall is seeking
a rehearing and review of the impugned order on merits together with a fresh
declaratory relief which is impermissible in the exercise of power of recall. The

                                                                           Page 10 of 11

I.A. No. 6907 of 2026 in Company Appeal (AT) (Insolvency) No. 460 of 2026
present application of the Applicant is styled as recall but in essence is a
review application and therefore cannot be entertained.

21. In view of the above discussion, we are of the considered view that the
reasons for recall, as narrated in the recall application, do not fall within the
scope and ambit of recall of an order passed by this Tribunal. No cogent
grounds for entertaining the recall having been made out, we reject the recall
application. No costs.
