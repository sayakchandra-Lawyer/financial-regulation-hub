Key concepts

  • Notice (rule 3)
  • Consent Manager registration (rule 4 and First Schedule)
  • Reasonable security safeguards (rule 6)
  • Breach intimation (rule 7)
  • Retention and erasure (rule 8, Third and Seventh Schedules)
  • Verifiable consent for children (rule 10)
  • Significant Data Fiduciary duties (rule 13)
  • Rights requests and grievances (rule 14)
  • Transfer outside India (rule 15)

Key obligations

  • rule 3: a notice must stand on its own, be in clear and plain language, list the personal data item by item, state the specified purpose and the goods, services or uses enabled, and give a link and the means to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board
  • rule 6: reasonable security safeguards include at least encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; access controls; logs, monitoring and review; backups for continuity; one-year retention of logs; security clauses in contracts with Data Processors; and technical and organisational measures
  • rule 7: intimate a personal data breach to each affected Data Principal without delay, and to the Board without delay and again in detail within 72 hours
  • rule 8(3): keep personal data, associated traffic data and processing logs for at least one year from the processing, for the purposes in the Seventh Schedule, then erase them unless another law requires more
  • rule 9: publish on the website or app, and in every reply to a rights request, the business contact information of the Data Protection Officer or person who can answer questions
  • rule 10: before processing a child's data, obtain verifiable consent of a parent who is an identifiable adult, checked against reliable details held or a virtual token issued by an authorised entity such as a DigiLocker service provider
  • rule 13: a Significant Data Fiduciary carries out a Data Protection Impact Assessment and an audit every twelve months, reports significant observations to the Board, checks that its algorithmic software does not risk Data Principals' rights, and keeps specified personal data in India
  • rule 14: publish how Data Principals can make requests and the identifiers needed; respond to grievances within the period published, which may not exceed ninety days
  • rule 15: transfers abroad must meet any requirements the Central Government specifies by general or special order about making personal data available to a foreign State or its agencies
  • rule 23: the Central Government may, for the purposes in the Seventh Schedule, call for information from a Data Fiduciary or intermediary and may bar it from telling the Data Principal

Deadlines and cycles

  • rule 1(2): rules 1, 2 and 17 to 21 in force from 13-11-2025
  • rule 1(3): rule 4 (Consent Managers) in force one year after publication, that is from 13-11-2026
  • rule 1(4): rules 3, 5 to 16, 22 and 23 in force eighteen months after publication, that is from 13-05-2027
  • rule 7(2)(b): detailed breach report to the Board within 72 hours of becoming aware, or such longer period as the Board allows on a written request
  • rule 8(2): tell the Data Principal at least 48 hours before her data is erased under the Third Schedule
  • rule 13(1): Data Protection Impact Assessment and audit once in every twelve months
  • rule 14(3): respond to grievances within a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days
  • rule 19(9): the Board completes an inquiry within six months, extendable by up to three months at a time

If you breach it

  • A breach of the Rules is a breach for the Schedule to the Act: see the penalties under s. 33

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
