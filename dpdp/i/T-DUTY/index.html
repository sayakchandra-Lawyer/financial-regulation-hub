Security, breach and retention

Accountability, security safeguards, breach intimation, accuracy, retention and erasure (s. 8; rules 6 to 9, Third and Seventh Schedules)

Applies to
Every Data Fiduciary, for its own processing and processing done for it by Data Processors; Data Processors through their contracts with Data Fiduciaries

Key concepts

  • Accountability for processors (s. 8(1) and (2))
  • Accuracy where data drives decisions (s. 8(3))
  • Reasonable security safeguards (rule 6)
  • Personal data breach (s. 2(u))
  • 72-hour report to the Board (rule 7(2))
  • Erasure when the purpose ends (s. 8(7))
  • One-year log retention (rules 6 and 8(3))

Key obligations

  • s. 8(1): the Data Fiduciary remains responsible for compliance, including for processing by its Data Processors, whatever the contract says
  • s. 8(2): engage a Data Processor only under a valid contract
  • s. 8(3): keep personal data complete, accurate and consistent where it is used for a decision affecting the Data Principal or shared with another Data Fiduciary
  • s. 8(5) and rule 6(1): take reasonable security safeguards, at least encryption, obfuscation, masking or virtual tokens; access control; logs and monitoring to detect and investigate unauthorised access; backups for continuity; and security clauses in processor contracts
  • rule 6(1)(e): keep the logs and personal data needed to detect and investigate unauthorised access for one year, unless another law requires otherwise
  • s. 8(6) and rule 7(1): on becoming aware of a breach, tell each affected Data Principal without delay, through her user account or registered contact, what happened, the likely consequences, the mitigation, what she can do and whom to contact
  • rule 7(2): tell the Board without delay of the breach, its nature, extent, timing, location and likely impact, then within 72 hours of becoming aware of it send the facts, causes, mitigation, findings about who caused it, remedial measures and a report on the intimations to Data Principals
  • s. 8(7): erase personal data when consent is withdrawn or once it is reasonable to assume the specified purpose is no longer served, whichever is earlier, unless the law requires it to be kept, and make processors erase it; the Act's own illustration is a bank keeping identity records after an account closes because banking law requires it
  • s. 8(8) and rule 8(1): for e-commerce entities and social media intermediaries with at least two crore registered users and online gaming intermediaries with at least fifty lakh, the purpose is deemed over three years after the Data Principal last approached them for the purpose or exercised her rights, or after the Rules commenced, whichever is latest; the thresholds count registered users in India, and keeping data that lets her reach her user account or a stored virtual token usable for money, goods or services is excluded
  • rule 8(3): keep personal data, associated traffic data and processing logs for at least one year from the processing for the Seventh Schedule purposes, then erase them unless another law requires more
  • s. 8(9) and rule 9: publish the business contact information of the Data Protection Officer or a person who can answer questions, and include it in every reply to a rights request
  • s. 8(10): establish an effective mechanism to redress grievances

Deadlines and cycles

  • Breach: intimate affected Data Principals and the Board without delay (rule 7(1) and (2)(a))
  • Breach: detailed report to the Board within 72 hours, or longer if the Board allows on a written request (rule 7(2)(b))
  • Logs and processing records: at least one year (rules 6(1)(e) and 8(3))
  • Notice of erasure under the Third Schedule: at least 48 hours before (rule 8(2))
  • Third Schedule: three years of inactivity for the listed large platforms
  • All of these apply from 13-05-2027

If you breach it

  • Failure to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)): up to Rs 250 crore (Schedule item 1)
  • Failure to notify the Board or affected Data Principals of a breach (s. 8(6)): up to Rs 200 crore (Schedule item 2)
  • Any other breach of s. 8: up to Rs 50 crore (Schedule item 7)

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
