Key concepts

  • Need-to-know access
  • Segregation of customer data
  • Notice of security breach to RBI
  • Contract clauses on data storage and protection

Key obligations

  • para 23 and 24: ensure the confidentiality, security and protection of customer information held by a service provider; give its staff access only on a need-to-know basis
  • para 25: review the service provider's security practices regularly and require it to disclose security breaches
  • para 26 and 27: where a service provider serves several entities, keep each entity's customer information separate and identifiable
  • para 28: notify RBI immediately of a security breach or leakage of confidential customer information; the NBFC is liable to its customers for any damage
  • para 74: IT outsourcing contracts must cover access to data and logs, reporting of data breaches, compliance with the IT Act and other law protecting customer data, storage of data only in India where required, and details of data captured, processed and stored
  • With the DPDP Act: these contracts are also the valid contract a Data Fiduciary needs with a Data Processor (s. 8(2)) and should carry the rule 6 security clauses

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 28: notify RBI immediately of a breach or leak

If you breach it

  • para 28: the NBFC is liable to its customers for damage from a breach or leak

General consequences under the statute. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934: RBI may impose penalties on an NBFC that fails to comply with its directions (s. 58G read with s. 58B), and may cancel its certificate of registration (s. 45-IA(6)). The DPDP Act applies alongside these directions (s. 38).
