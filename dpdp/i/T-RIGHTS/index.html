Rights of Data Principals and grievances

Rights to information, correction and erasure, grievance redressal and nomination, and the duties of Data Principals (ss. 11 to 15; rule 14)

Key concepts

  • Right to a summary of data and processing (s. 11)
  • Correction, completion, updating and erasure (s. 12)
  • Grievance redressal before the Board (s. 13)
  • Nomination for death or incapacity (s. 14)
  • Duties of Data Principals (s. 15)
  • Identifier such as a customer identification file number (rule 14(5))

Key obligations

  • s. 11(1): on request, give a summary of the personal data processed and the processing, and the identities of the other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors it was shared with, with a description of what was shared
  • s. 11(2): the identities need not be disclosed for sharing with a Data Fiduciary authorised by law that asked in writing for the prevention, detection or investigation of offences or cyber incidents
  • s. 12(2): on request, correct inaccurate or misleading data, complete incomplete data and update data
  • s. 12(3): on request, erase the data unless it must be kept for the specified purpose or to comply with law
  • s. 13: give readily available means of grievance redressal; the Data Principal must use them before approaching the Board
  • s. 14: let a Data Principal nominate another individual to exercise her rights on death or incapacity
  • rule 14(1) and (2): publish on the website or app the means of making a request and the username, customer identification file number, application reference number or other identifier needed
  • rule 14(3): publish the grievance response period and put measures in place to meet it; the period may not exceed ninety days
  • s. 15: a Data Principal must not impersonate anyone, suppress material information when applying for an identity document, file false or frivolous complaints, or give information that is not verifiably authentic when seeking correction or erasure (s. 15)

Deadlines and cycles

  • Grievances: respond within the period published, not more than ninety days (rule 14(3))
  • Applies from 13-05-2027

If you breach it

  • Breach of a Data Principal's duties (s. 15): up to Rs 10,000 (Schedule item 5)
  • Failure to honour rights: up to Rs 50 crore (Schedule item 7)
  • s. 28(12): the Board may warn or impose costs on a complainant whose complaint is false or frivolous

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
