Applies to
Processing of digital personal data within India, whether collected in digital form or collected on paper and digitised later (s. 3(a)); Processing outside India if it is connected with offering goods or services to Data Principals in India (s. 3(b)); Banks, NBFCs, HFCs, insurers, insurance intermediaries, brokers and other market entities as Data Fiduciaries for the personal data of customers, employees and others
Does not apply to
Personal data processed by an individual for a personal or domestic purpose (s. 3(c)(i)); Personal data made publicly available by the Data Principal herself, or by a person under a legal duty to publish it (s. 3(c)(ii))

Key concepts

  • Personal data (s. 2(t)) and digital personal data (s. 2(n))
  • Data Fiduciary (s. 2(i)) and Data Processor (s. 2(k))
  • Data Principal, including the parent of a child and the lawful guardian of a person with disability (s. 2(j))
  • Consent (s. 6) and certain legitimate uses (s. 7)
  • Personal data breach (s. 2(u))
  • Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10)
  • Consent Manager (s. 2(g))
  • Data Protection Board of India (s. 18)
  • Overriding effect in a conflict (s. 38(2))

Key obligations

  • s. 4: personal data may be processed only for a lawful purpose, with the Data Principal's consent or for a certain legitimate use
  • s. 5: every request for consent must be accompanied or preceded by a notice of the data, the purpose, how to withdraw consent and exercise rights, and how to complain to the Board
  • s. 6: consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action, and limited to the data needed for the specified purpose; it can be withdrawn as easily as it was given
  • s. 8(1): the Data Fiduciary is responsible for compliance, including for processing done on its behalf by a Data Processor, whatever any agreement says
  • s. 8(5) and (6): take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a personal data breach, and intimate any breach to the Board and to each affected Data Principal
  • s. 8(7): erase personal data when consent is withdrawn or the purpose is no longer served, unless the law requires it to be kept, and make processors erase it too
  • s. 8(9) and (10): publish the contact details of a Data Protection Officer or other person who answers questions, and set up an effective grievance mechanism
  • s. 9: verifiable parental consent for children's data; no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising directed at children
  • s. 10: a notified Significant Data Fiduciary must appoint a Data Protection Officer based in India and an independent data auditor, and carry out periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments and audits
  • ss. 11 to 14: Data Principals' rights to information, correction and erasure, grievance redressal and nomination
  • s. 16: transfers abroad are allowed unless the Central Government restricts transfers to a notified country; a sector law with stricter rules on transfer prevails
  • s. 38: the Act adds to other laws; where a provision conflicts with another law, the Act prevails to the extent of the conflict

Deadlines and cycles

  • 13-11-2025: ss. 1(2), 2, 18 to 26, 35, 38 to 43, 44(1) and 44(3) in force (G.S.R. 843(E))
  • 13-11-2026: s. 6(9) (registration of Consent Managers) and s. 27(1)(d) in force
  • 13-05-2027: ss. 3 to 5, 6(1) to (8) and (10), 7 to 17, 27 (other than 27(1)(d)), 28 to 34, 36, 37 and 44(2) in force
  • s. 29(2): appeal to the Appellate Tribunal within 60 days of receiving the Board's order or direction
  • s. 17(5): the Central Government may exempt Data Fiduciaries from any provision by notification only within five years of commencement
  • s. 43(2): orders removing difficulties can be made only within three years of commencement

If you breach it

  • Schedule item 1: failure to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)), up to Rs 250 crore
  • Schedule item 2: failure to notify the Board or an affected Data Principal of a breach (s. 8(6)), up to Rs 200 crore
  • Schedule item 3: breach of the additional obligations on children (s. 9), up to Rs 200 crore
  • Schedule item 4: breach of the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10), up to Rs 150 crore
  • Schedule item 5: breach of a Data Principal's duties (s. 15), up to Rs 10,000
  • Schedule item 6: breach of a voluntary undertaking, up to the penalty for the breach that led to the proceedings
  • Schedule item 7: any other breach of the Act or the Rules, up to Rs 50 crore

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
