Children and persons with disability

Verifiable consent of parents and lawful guardians, and the limits on processing children's data (s. 9; rules 10 to 12, Fourth Schedule)

Applies to
Data Fiduciaries processing data of anyone under eighteen, such as minor savings accounts, child insurance policies and minor investment accounts; Data Fiduciaries processing data of a person with disability who has a lawful guardian

Key concepts

  • Child: under eighteen (s. 2(f))
  • Verifiable consent of a parent (rule 10)
  • Lawful guardian (rule 11)
  • No tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted advertising at children (s. 9(3))
  • Exempt classes and purposes (rule 12, Fourth Schedule)

Key obligations

  • s. 9(1): obtain verifiable consent of the parent or lawful guardian before processing a child's data or the data of a person with disability who has a lawful guardian (wording as changed by the 2026 Removal of Difficulties Order)
  • rule 10(1): check that the person giving consent as parent is an identifiable adult, by reference to reliable identity and age details already held, or details or a virtual token issued by an entity entrusted by law or Government, including through a DigiLocker service provider
  • rule 11: verify that a person claiming to be the lawful guardian of a person with disability was appointed by a court, a designated authority under the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 or a local level committee under the National Trust Act, 1999
  • s. 9(2): do not process a child's data in a way likely to harm her well-being
  • s. 9(3): do not track or behaviourally monitor children or direct targeted advertising at them
  • rule 12 and Fourth Schedule: the parental consent and tracking limits do not apply to listed classes (healthcare, educational institutions, crèches and their transport) and listed purposes (such as legal functions in a child's interest, email accounts, real-time location for safety, and confirming that a user is not a child); none is specific to financial services

Deadlines and cycles

  • Applies from 13-05-2027

If you breach it

  • Breach of the additional obligations on children (s. 9): up to Rs 200 crore (Schedule item 3)

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
