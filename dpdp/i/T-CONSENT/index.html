Notice, consent and Consent Managers

Notice before consent, the standard for valid consent, withdrawal, and registered Consent Managers (ss. 5, 6; rules 3, 4 and First Schedule)

Key concepts

  • Notice (s. 5 and rule 3)
  • Itemised description of personal data
  • Specified purpose (s. 2(za))
  • Free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous consent
  • Withdrawal of consent (s. 6(4))
  • Burden of proving consent (s. 6(10))
  • Consent Manager (s. 2(g))
  • Consent given before commencement (s. 5(2))

Key obligations

  • s. 5(1) and rule 3: precede or accompany every request for consent with a notice that stands on its own, uses clear and plain language, lists the personal data item by item, states the specified purpose and the goods, services or uses it enables, and links to the means to withdraw consent, exercise rights and complain to the Board
  • s. 5(1) illustration: a bank opening an account through video-based customer identification must give the notice before or with the request for data
  • s. 5(2): for consent obtained before commencement, give the same notice as soon as reasonably practicable; processing may continue until the Data Principal withdraws consent
  • s. 5(3) and 6(3): offer the notice and the consent request in English or any language in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution, with the contact details of the Data Protection Officer or authorised person
  • s. 6(1): consent must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, by a clear affirmative action, and limited to the data necessary for the specified purpose
  • s. 6(2): any part of a consent that infringes the Act or another law is invalid to that extent; the Act's illustration is an insurer whose consent form asks the buyer to waive the right to complain to the Board
  • s. 6(4) to (6): the Data Principal may withdraw consent at any time, as easily as she gave it; the Data Fiduciary must then, within a reasonable time, stop processing and make its processors stop, unless the law requires or allows processing without consent
  • s. 6(10): in any proceeding, the Data Fiduciary must prove that it gave notice and obtained consent in line with the Act and Rules
  • s. 6(7) to (9) and rule 4: a Data Principal may give, manage, review and withdraw consent through a Consent Manager registered with the Board
  • First Schedule Part A: a Consent Manager must be a company incorporated in India with net worth of at least Rs 2 crore, sound management and an interoperable platform independently certified against the Board's standards
  • First Schedule Part B: a Consent Manager must not be able to read the data it routes, must keep records of consents, notices and sharing for at least seven years, may not sub-contract its obligations, acts in a fiduciary capacity, must avoid conflicts with Data Fiduciaries, must disclose shareholders above 2%, must be audited, and may not change control without the Board's approval

Deadlines and cycles

  • 13-11-2026: Consent Manager registration (s. 6(9) and rule 4) in force
  • 13-05-2027: notice and consent duties (ss. 5 and 6, rule 3) apply
  • First Schedule Part B item 4(c): Consent Managers keep consent records for at least seven years

If you breach it

  • Breach of the notice or consent provisions: up to Rs 50 crore (Schedule item 7)
  • Rule 4(5): the Board may suspend or cancel a Consent Manager's registration after a hearing

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
