The Board, inquiries, penalties and appeals

How the Data Protection Board receives complaints and breach reports, inquires, settles, penalises, and how appeals and blocking work (ss. 18 to 37; rules 17 to 23)

Key concepts

  • Board as a digital office (s. 28(1), rule 20)
  • Inquiry and civil court powers (s. 28)
  • Mediation (s. 31)
  • Voluntary undertaking (s. 32)
  • Monetary penalty (s. 33)
  • Appeal to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal (s. 29)
  • Blocking after repeated penalties (s. 37)
  • Calling for information (s. 36, rule 23)

Key obligations

  • s. 27(1): the Board may direct urgent remedial or mitigation measures on a breach intimation, and inquires into breaches on intimation, complaint, Government reference or court direction
  • s. 28(3) to (6): the Board decides whether there are sufficient grounds, may close the case with reasons, and conducts any inquiry following natural justice
  • s. 28(7) and (8): the Board has civil court powers to summon, take evidence and inspect documents and data, but may not stop access to premises or seize equipment in a way that affects day-to-day functioning
  • s. 28(10): the Board may issue interim orders after a hearing
  • s. 31 and 32: the Board may send a dispute to mediation, and may accept a voluntary undertaking at any stage, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless it is breached (s. 32(4) and (5))
  • s. 33: penalties only for a breach the Board finds significant, after a hearing, within the Schedule limits; sums go to the Consolidated Fund of India (s. 34)
  • s. 36 and rule 23: the Central Government may call for information from a Data Fiduciary or intermediary for the Seventh Schedule purposes, and may bar disclosure of the request
  • s. 37: after penalties in two or more instances and on the Board's advice, the Central Government may, after a hearing, order public access to the Data Fiduciary's service to be blocked
  • s. 39: civil courts may not hear matters the Board can deal with
  • rule 22: appeals are filed digitally, with the fee payable for appeals under the TRAI Act, 1997

Deadlines and cycles

  • rule 19(9): the Board completes an inquiry within six months of the intimation, complaint, reference or direction, extendable by up to three months at a time with reasons
  • s. 29(2): appeal within 60 days of receiving the order; late appeals allowed for sufficient cause (s. 29(3))
  • s. 29(6) and (7): the Appellate Tribunal endeavours to dispose of an appeal within six months and records its reasons in writing if it does not
  • s. 29(9): a further appeal from the Appellate Tribunal follows s. 18 of the TRAI Act, 1997

If you breach it

  • Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore, Rs 200 crore, Rs 150 crore, Rs 50 crore or Rs 10,000 depending on the provision breached
  • s. 37: blocking of access to the service after repeated penalties

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
