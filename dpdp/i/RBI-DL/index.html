Key concepts

  • Need-based collection with prior explicit consent
  • No access to contacts, files, call logs
  • Data stored only in India
  • Comprehensive privacy policy
  • Lending service provider (LSP)

Key obligations

  • para 6(2): before engaging a lending service provider, carry out enhanced due diligence including the robustness of its data privacy policies and storage systems
  • para 13(1): collect data through the app only as needed and with the borrower's prior explicit consent, with an audit trail; no access to files, media, contact lists, call logs or telephony; one-time access to camera, microphone or location only for onboarding or KYC
  • para 13(2) to (4): let the borrower give or refuse consent for specific data, restrict disclosure to third parties, limit retention, revoke consent and have the data deleted; disclose the purpose at each stage; take explicit consent before sharing with a third party unless law requires the sharing
  • para 14(1): lending service providers may keep only minimal data such as name, address and contact details; the NBFC stays responsible for data privacy and security
  • para 14(2) and (3): publish a data storage policy covering the data kept, how long, restrictions on use, destruction and breach handling; no biometric data unless the law allows it
  • para 14(4): store all data only on servers in India; data processed abroad must be deleted there and brought back to India within 24 hours
  • para 15: the NBFC and its lending service providers must publish a comprehensive privacy policy compliant with applicable laws and name third parties allowed to collect data through the app
  • With the DPDP Act: the NBFC is the Data Fiduciary and the lending service provider usually its Data Processor; the stricter India-storage rule continues under s. 16(2) of the Act

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 14(4): data processed outside India must be deleted there and brought back within 24 hours

General consequences under the statute. Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934: RBI may impose penalties on an NBFC that fails to comply with its directions (s. 58G read with s. 58B), and may cancel its certificate of registration (s. 45-IA(6)). The DPDP Act applies alongside these directions (s. 38).
