Scope, lawful grounds and exemptions

Where the Act applies, when data may be processed without consent, and the exemptions (ss. 3, 4, 7, 17 and 38; rules 5 and 16)

Key concepts

  • Lawful purpose (s. 4(2))
  • Certain legitimate uses (s. 7)
  • Voluntarily provided data (s. 7(a))
  • Legal obligation to disclose to the State (s. 7(d))
  • Loan default exemption (s. 17(1)(f))
  • Regulatory and supervisory functions (s. 17(1)(b))
  • Research, archiving or statistics (s. 17(2)(b))

Key obligations

  • s. 4: process personal data only for a lawful purpose, that is one not expressly forbidden by law, and only with consent or for a certain legitimate use
  • s. 7(a): data the Data Principal has voluntarily given for a specified purpose may be used for that purpose unless she says she does not consent
  • s. 7(d) and (e): data may be processed to meet a legal duty to disclose information to the State, or to comply with a judgment, decree or order
  • s. 7(i): data may be processed for employment purposes, including preventing corporate espionage and protecting trade secrets, or giving an employee a benefit she seeks
  • s. 17(1): Chapter II (other than s. 8(1) and (5)), Chapter III and s. 16 do not apply where processing is needed to enforce a legal right or claim; by a body with a judicial, quasi-judicial, regulatory or supervisory function; for preventing, detecting, investigating or prosecuting an offence; for non-residents' data under a foreign contract; or for a court-approved merger, demerger or arrangement
  • s. 17(1)(f): the same relief applies to processing to ascertain the financial information, assets and liabilities of a person who has defaulted on a loan or advance from a financial institution, if done in line with the disclosure provisions of other laws (default and financial institution as in the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code)
  • s. 17(1) keeps two duties even in these cases: the Data Fiduciary stays responsible (s. 8(1)) and must keep reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5))
  • s. 17(2)(b) and rule 16: the Act does not apply to processing for research, archiving or statistical purposes that is not used for decisions about a particular Data Principal and follows the Second Schedule standards
  • s. 17(3): the Central Government may exempt notified Data Fiduciaries, including startups, from ss. 5, 8(3), 8(7), 10 and 11
  • s. 38: the Act is in addition to other laws such as KYC, record-keeping and secrecy laws; in a conflict the Act prevails to the extent of the conflict

Deadlines and cycles

  • 13-05-2027: ss. 3, 4, 7 and 17 and rules 5 and 16 apply
  • s. 38 has applied since 13-11-2025

If you breach it

  • Processing without a lawful ground is a breach of s. 4: up to Rs 50 crore (Schedule item 7)

General consequences under the statute. Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023: if the Data Protection Board finds, on concluding an inquiry, that a breach of the Act or the Rules is significant, it may impose a monetary penalty under s. 33 and the Schedule: up to Rs 250 crore for failing to take reasonable security safeguards (s. 8(5)); up to Rs 200 crore for failing to notify a personal data breach (s. 8(6)) or for breaching the obligations on children's data (s. 9); up to Rs 150 crore for breaching the additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary (s. 10); up to Rs 10,000 for a Data Principal who breaches her duties (s. 15); and up to Rs 50 crore for any other breach. The Board weighs the nature, gravity and duration of the breach, the data affected, repetition, any gain made or loss avoided, mitigation, proportionality and the likely impact of the penalty (s. 33(2)). It may accept a voluntary undertaking, which bars proceedings under the Act on its contents unless the undertaking is breached (s. 32(4) and (5)). An appeal lies to the Telecom Disputes Settlement and Appellate Tribunal within 60 days (s. 29). Where penalties have been imposed on a Data Fiduciary in two or more instances, the Central Government may, on the Board's reference, order public access to its service to be blocked (s. 37). These provisions apply from 13-05-2027.
