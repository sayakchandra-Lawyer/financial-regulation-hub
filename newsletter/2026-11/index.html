NBFCs 7 changes

RBIHigh impactCapital & liquidityIssued 23 Nov

Securitisation notes: demat only, ₹1 crore floor at every transfer

What changed

Securitisation notes must now be issued, held and transferred in demat form, with a ₹1 crore minimum ticket at issue and on every later transfer. Public offers follow SEBI's securitised debt rules.

Why it matters

From 1 April 2027 every transfer, including of notes issued earlier, must be in demat and at least ₹1 crore (amortising notes excepted). Smaller, non-amortising holdings may become hard to sell, which affects both originators and investors.

Practical step: List outstanding securitisation notes where the entity is originator or investor, by form (physical or demat) and ticket size.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: NBFCs covered by the NBFC Securitisation Transactions Directions, 2025 (including deposit-taking NBFCs, investment and credit companies, factors, MFIs, IFCs, IDF-NBFCs) and HFCs, across all layers.

  • Issue, holding and later transfer of securitisation notes must be in dematerialised form only.
  • The ₹1 crore minimum ticket applies at issue and at every later transfer.
  • Public offers must comply with Regulation 21 of the SEBI (Issue and Listing of Securitised Debt Instruments and Security Receipts) Regulations, 2008.
  • Notes issued before 1 April 2027 are also covered. There is no grandfathering.
Key date: 1 Apr 2027Source
RBI / NHBMedium impactCustomer conductIssued 12 Nov

Arbitrators must be independent: no unilateral panels

What changed

After a Delhi High Court order, RBI cautioned lenders against appointing the same arbitrators unilaterally across many disputes. NHB passed the caution on to HFCs and asked for an action-taken report.

Why it matters

Awards from a lender-picked panel are open to challenge and may be set aside, which slows retail recovery. Arbitration clauses and panels in standard loan agreements are likely to need review.

Practical step: Review arbitration clauses in loan agreements and how the arbitrator panel is chosen.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Banks and NBFCs, including HFCs. HFCs report the action taken to NHB.

  • Arbitrators are to be appointed only in line with the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996 and court rulings on independence and neutrality.
  • The caution follows a Delhi High Court order of 30 September 2026.
  • HFCs must report the action taken to NHB.
Key date: NowSource
RBIMedium impactGovernanceIssued 23 Nov

Governor meets NBFC and HFC chiefs: the supervisory themes

What changed

No new rules. The Governor stressed responsible credit growth, fair recovery practices, use of CKYCR at onboarding and a risk-based approach to cyber and IT risk.

Why it matters

The themes the Governor raised give a reasonable indication of supervisory priorities, and a useful checklist for internal reviews.

Practical step: Check CKYCR upload and download practice at onboarding, and controls over recovery agents.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Select NBFCs (including Government NBFCs), HFCs and MFIs, with industry bodies. No new directions were issued.

  • Responsible and sustainable credit growth.
  • Fair recovery practices.
  • Use of CKYCR for onboarding.
  • A comprehensive, risk-based approach to cyber and IT risk.
Key date: Next inspectionSource
RBIMedium impactGovernanceIssued 16 Nov

Registrations of 49 NBFCs cancelled

What changed

RBI cancelled the registration of 49 NBFCs under section 45-IA(6) of the RBI Act, with one more cancellation announced on 9 November. A cancelled entity cannot carry on NBFI business.

Why it matters

Co-lending, sourcing and collection tie-ups with an entity on the list carry conduct and continuity risk for its partners.

Practical step: Screen lending partners and counterparties against the list.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: The NBFCs named in RBI's press release, and lenders and partners dealing with them.

  • Cancellations were made by orders passed in October 2026.
  • An entity whose registration is cancelled cannot carry on the business of a non-banking financial institution.
Key date: NowSource
NSE / BSEMedium impactCapital & liquidityIssued Nov

75% margin on highly encumbered shares: list revised

What changed

Shares that meet the encumbrance criteria attract a minimum 75% margin in the equity and equity derivatives segments from 30 November. The list of shares changed.

Why it matters

Higher trading margins may reduce liquidity in these shares. Lenders against shares may want to revisit concentration and haircuts, and brokers must collect the higher margin.

Practical step: Screen pledged collateral and client positions against the revised list.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Trading members and investors in the listed shares, and lenders against those shares.

  • The circular lists shares added to, and moved out of, the framework. BSE issued a matching notice.
  • Inclusion is a surveillance measure, not an adverse finding against the company.
Key date: 30 Nov 2026Source

Housing finance 6 changes

Supreme CourtMedium impactCustomer conductIssued 19 Nov

Homebuyer EMIs under a tripartite agreement: no coercion until disbursement is tested

What changed

In a stalled project, buyers cannot be made to pay instalments, or face coercive action, until the court decides whether the lender paid the builder strictly as the tripartite agreement required (interim order).

Why it matters

Construction-linked and subvention loans in stalled projects become harder to recover through interim orders unless the lender can show each disbursement matched construction progress.

Practical step: Check stage-wise disbursement evidence on builder-tied home loans.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Banks and HFCs with builder-tied home loans under tripartite agreements.

  • Interim protection only: the court will first examine the lender-builder link and whether disbursement followed the agreed stages.
Key date: NowSource

Insurers 6 changes

IRDAIHigh impactCustomer conductIssued 20 Nov

₹2 crore penalty on a health insurer for claim rejections and deductions

What changed

Penalty for repudiating claims on untenable grounds and making deductions not supported by policy terms.

Why it matters

The order shows that unsupported rejections and deductions can lead to monetary penalties, not only grievance redress. The same regulations bind all health insurers.

Practical step: Sample recent repudiations and deductions for a clear policy-term basis.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: The health insurer named in the order. The rules applied are unchanged and bind all health insurers.

  • Penalty under s.102 of the Insurance Act.
  • Breaches of regulations 27(v) and 33(d) of the Health Insurance Regulations, 2016 and regulation 21(3) of the TPA Regulations; warnings and directions also issued.
Key date: NowSource

Study groups on health claim transparency

What changed

Two study groups: one on standard reason codes for rejections and deductions, the other on real-time tracking of the claim journey. Both report by 26 December 2026.

Why it matters

If IRDAI acts on the groups' work, standard rejection codes and customer-visible claim tracking could follow, which would need system changes for insurers and TPAs.

Practical step: Prepare a gap view of claim codes and tracking against what the groups are studying.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Health insurers and general insurers writing health business.

  • Formed under the IAC Sub-Committee on Health Insurance.
  • No rule has changed yet.

Mutual funds & AMCs 5 changes

SEBIHigh impactTech & dataIssued Nov

Client onboarding simplified: KYC data centralised at the KRA

What changed

Supplementary KYC data (income range, FATCA/CRS, PEP status, occupation, CKYC ID, DIGIPIN, document expiry) moves to the KYC Registration Agency. Intermediaries upload within three working days of KYC.

Why it matters

Onboarding forms, RTA and KRA interfaces and upload timelines must change before 24 December. Once live, onboarding should be faster for mutual fund and broking clients.

Practical step: Update account opening forms and KRA upload processes before 24 December 2026.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: All SEBI-registered intermediaries that onboard clients, and KYC Registration Agencies.

  • In force from 24 December 2026.
  • KRAs issue an operating procedure within 30 days.
Key date: 24 Dec 2026Source
SEBIMedium impactCapital & liquidityIssued Nov

Net settlement of funds for mutual fund cash-market trades

What changed

Funds for outright cash-segment trades may settle net at the level of each scheme. Securities still settle gross, and there is no netting across schemes.

Why it matters

Lower intraday funding needs for schemes, with operational changes for AMCs and custodians.

Practical step: Plan the operational changes with the custodian.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Mutual funds, custodians, stock brokers and clearing corporations.

  • In force from 1 April 2027.
Key date: 1 Apr 2027Source

Accredited investors: managers can accredit in-house

What changed

AIF, SIF and PMS managers may determine an investor's accredited status themselves, valid for three years and recognised within the same group.

Why it matters

Easier onboarding of large investors into AIF, SIF and PMS products.

Digital lending & payments 7 changes

DPDPHigh impactTech & dataIssued 13 Nov

DPDP: Consent Manager registration opens

What changed

An Indian company with at least ₹2 crore net worth and an independently certified platform can now apply to register as a Consent Manager. Data Fiduciary duties start on 13 May 2027.

Why it matters

Where processing relies on consent, financial groups that share customer data across entities, or cross-sell through apps, will need consent each entity can rely on. Collecting consent through a registered Consent Manager is one option; collecting and recording it directly is another.

Practical step: Map where processing relies on consent and how that consent will be collected and recorded before May 2027.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Companies that want to register as Consent Managers, and Data Fiduciaries that plan to accept consent through them.

  • Rule 4 and the First Schedule apply from 13 November 2026.
  • Data Fiduciary duties on notice, consent, security and breaches start on 13 May 2027.
Key date: 13 May 2027Source

New SEBI Settlement Regulations, 2026 in force

What changed

Settlement notices before a show cause notice, formula-based amounts and a fast track for disclosure lapses. Apply within 90 days of a show cause notice; older pending cases have 90 days at a 20% higher amount.

Why it matters

Creates new settlement routes, including before a show cause notice. Settlement remains at SEBI's discretion.

Practical step: Note the 90-day window that runs from a show cause notice, and the separate window for older pending cases.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Anyone facing, or likely to face, SEBI proceedings.

  • Replaces the Settlement Proceedings Regulations, 2018.
Key date: Within 90 daysSource

Overseas and IFSC listing widened to 'equity instruments'

What changed

Listing rules under section 23(3) of the Companies Act now cover equity shares, convertible debentures and preference shares. Unlisted public companies in the IFSC are covered and eligibility is relaxed.

Why it matters

More routes to raise capital abroad or in GIFT City.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: Public companies listing on permitted foreign exchanges, including exchanges in the IFSC.

  • A company with negative net worth is eligible if at least 75% of the net offer goes to qualified institutional buyers.
Key date: NowSource

All IFSC entities must act on CERT-In advisories

What changed

IFSC entities must track and act on CERT-In advisories and vulnerability notes, including for systems run by the parent or vendors. A tracking mechanism is due within 30 days.

Why it matters

IFSC units of Indian financial groups, including fund managers and brokers, must cover parent- and vendor-run systems too.

Practical step: Assign an owner to track CERT-In advisories and record the action taken on each.

Who it applies to and key points

Applies to: All IFSCA-regulated entities, including systems run for them by the parent or third parties.

  • Assessment and patching duties apply to advisories issued from 25 November 2026.
  • CERT-In's own directions (six-hour incident reporting, 180-day logs) apply independently.
Key date: 25 Dec 2026Source
