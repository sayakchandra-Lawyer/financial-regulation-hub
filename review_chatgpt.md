### F1
- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “Integrated Filing (Governance and Financial)”
- Problem: The register omits the separate corporate-governance framework for high-value debt-listed entities (HVDLEs). This is a substantial omission for debt-only NBFCs. The January 2026 amendment raised the threshold to ₹5,000 crore and changed governance requirements and the treatment of entities falling outside the revised threshold.
- Suggested fix: Add a dedicated HVDLE entry covering applicability under regulation 62C, Chapter VA obligations, related-party transactions, reporting, and transition/exit provisions. Distinguish these from equity-listed entities’ obligations.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI (LODR) (Amendment) Regulations, 2026, notification SEBI/NRO-GN/2026/295, amendments to regulations 15 and 62C–62Q: [official notification](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jan-2026/1769600297568.pdf).

### F2
- Severity: major
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “reg 57(4) and (5) quarterly details and certificates Rs 1,000 per ISIN”
- Problem: These are historical sub-regulations. Regulation 57 was replaced in June 2023 with the payment-status certificate requirement. Although the cited July 2025 SOP table retains the old references, reproducing them without explaining the inconsistency presents superseded quarterly obligations as current law.
- Suggested fix: Identify the obsolete references expressly; state the operative regulation 57 requirement and reconcile the SOP table before treating its historical rows as enforceable current obligations.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: [LODR consolidated regulations](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2026/1780915347745.pdf), regulation 57 and amendment footnote 532; compare [SEBI Master Circular dated 11 July 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jul-2025/1752233667904.pdf), Annex IX-A, rows (l)–(n).

### F3
- Severity: major
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “No penal action for MPS non-compliance between 01-04-2026 and 30-09-2026”
- Problem: This incorrectly describes a blanket moratorium. Relief applies to entities whose **due date for achieving MPS compliance** falls within that period. An entity already overdue before April 2026 does not qualify merely because its non-compliance continues during the window.
- Suggested fix: State the due-date eligibility condition in both the principal entry and Updates. Limit withdrawal of penal action to qualifying entities and the period specified in the circular.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI circular HO/49/14/14(13)2026-CFD-POD2/I/8772/2026 dated 7 April 2026, paragraph 3: [official circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/apr-2026/1775562977206.pdf).

### F4
- Severity: major
- Category: clarity
- Location: “an entity with listed NCDs must list all non-convertible debt securities it issues afterwards”
- Problem: The summary drops the substituted regulation’s express commencement qualification: the relevant NCDs must be listed on or after publication of the Third Amendment. “Such an entity” in the principal entry has no defined antecedent. The update consequently states a broader, temporally unqualified rule.
- Suggested fix: Reproduce the statutory applicability condition and distinguish the notification’s signing date from Gazette publication/commencement. Explain separately how the previous rule applies to earlier issuances.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI (LODR) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026, notification SEBI/LAD-DOP/2026/329 dated 12 October 2026, clauses 2 and 3 substituting regulation 62A(1): [official publication](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/regulations/oct-2026/securities-and-exchange-board-of-india-listing-obligations-and-disclosure-requirements-third-amendment-regulations-2026_105330.html).

### F5
- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “Disclose a loan default that continues beyond 30 days”
- Problem: The register does not separately explain debt issuers’ event disclosures under regulation 51 and Schedule III Part B, including expected payment defaults and security-creation failures. Readers could incorrectly treat the loan-default waiting period or periodic payment certificate as the complete disclosure regime.
- Suggested fix: Add a distinct debt-event disclosure entry, including the 24-hour outer limit and simultaneous trustee disclosure where regulation 56(1A) applies.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015, regulations 51 and 56(1A), Schedule III Part B: [official consolidated text](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2026/1780915347745.pdf).

### F6
- Severity: major
- Category: clarity
- Location: “File both parts in the prescribed format for every quarter”
- Problem: The summary does not distinguish quarterly submission of the integrated form from the frequency of its components: regulation 23(9) RPT disclosure remains half-yearly. It also risks implying that the regulation 33 equity-reporting package applies universally to debt-only issuers.
- Suggested fix: Specify applicability by listing category and retain each component’s frequency. State that integration does not convert half-yearly RPT disclosure into a quarterly obligation or replace the debt-specific reporting framework.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/CFD-PoD-2/CIR/P/2024/185 dated 31 December 2024, paragraph 3 applicability/frequency table and Annexure 1: [official NSE-hosted copy](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/NEW%20SEBI_0.pdf).

### F7

- Location: “Give advance notice of the record date”
- Problem: The debt-record-date entry specifies the filing subject and fine but omits the principal timing requirements.
- Suggested fix: Add the ordinarily required three clear working days’ exchange notice, excluding intimation and record dates, and the NCS requirement fixing the record date 15 days before the relevant payment.
- Confidence: high

### F8
- Severity: major
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “or amend them within six months of listing”
- Problem: The six-month accommodation is for **first-time issuers**, supported by an undertaking obtained at in-principle approval. It is not a fresh compliance period available to an existing debt-listed issuer for each issue.
- Suggested fix: Restrict the accommodation to first-time issuers and include the undertaking and subsequent compliance-reporting requirements.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI NCS Master Circular SEBI/HO/DDHS/DDHS-PoD/P/CIR/2025/0000000137 dated 15 October 2025, Chapter XXIII, paragraphs 1 and 4: [official circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2025/1760532257519.pdf).

### F9
- Severity: major
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “existing ISINs of these kinds are grandfathered”
- Problem: “These kinds” includes structured and market-linked debt in the preceding list. The grandfathering provision specifically identifies legacy floating-rate bonds, zero-coupon bonds and Tier II instruments; it does not extend that concession to every instrument in the five-ISIN category.
- Suggested fix: Name the three grandfathered categories expressly, retain the pre-circular issuance condition, and explain the restriction on additional ISINs when qualifying legacy maturities already equal or exceed five.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI circular HO/17/11/24(8)2026-DDHS-POD1/I/23125/2026 dated 7 October 2026, paragraph 3, substituted paragraph 1.2(c): [official NSE-hosted circular](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/circular/2026-10/SEBI_CIRCULAR_07102026_05_20261008165054.pdf).

### F10
- Severity: major
- Category: completeness
- Location: “Liquidity Window Facility for bond investors”
- Problem: Operational deadlines are provided without essential eligibility and economic conditions: the facility is optional at issuance; exercise starts only after one year; covered ISINs cannot be reissued; the aggregate facility must cover at least 10% of final issue size; and the window lasts three working days. - Suggested fix: Add these conditions, including the maximum 100-basis-point discount to valuation plus accrued interest, before the operational timetable.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI circular SEBI/HO/DDHS/DDHS-PoD-1/P/CIR/2024/141 dated 16 October 2024, paragraphs 4–6.8, consolidated in Chapter XXV of the [15 October 2025 NCS Master Circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2025/1760532257519.pdf).

### F12
- Severity: minor
- Category: accuracy
- Location: “defence personnel and their spouses”
- Problem: The amendment specifies widows and widowers of defence personnel, not spouses generally. Separately, “lost on transfer” omits transmission: the incentive also does not pass to a successor through transmission.
- Suggested fix: Use “serving and retired defence personnel, and widows and widowers of defence personnel”; state that the incentive is unavailable following transfer **or transmission**.
- Confidence: high
- Evidence: SEBI (NCS) (Amendment) Regulations, 2026, notification SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2026/296 dated 20 January 2026, provisos inserted in regulation 31: [official notification](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jan-2026/1769075268488.pdf).

### Overall
The register is not ready to serve as an operational compliance checklist: several summaries broaden exceptions or omit applicability conditions, and important debt-specific obligations are absent. The most important change is to distinguish equity-listed, debt-only, HVDLE and CP-only obligations before assigning deadlines and penalties. These findings are not confirmation that every remaining circular identifier, fee or operational cut-off has been independently verified.
