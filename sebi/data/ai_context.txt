SHARED STATUTORY PENALTY TEXTS:
P1: SEBI Act, 1992: SEBI may issue directions, including disgorgement and debarment (s. 11, 11B), pass cease and desist orders (s. 11D) and suspend or cancel registration (s. 12(3)). Adjudicating officers impose penalties under ss. 15A to 15HB: for example, failure to file returns or furnish information carries Rs 1 lakh a day up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15A); insider trading and fraudulent or unfair trade practices carry Rs 10 lakh (s. 15G) or Rs 5 lakh (s. 15HA) up to Rs 25 crore or three times the profit, whichever is higher; any breach with no specific penalty carries Rs 1 lakh up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15HB). Contraventions are also offences punishable with imprisonment up to 10 years, a fine up to Rs 25 crore, or both (s. 24). Proceedings may be settled under s. 15JB. Appeals lie to the Securities Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (s. 15T).

GLOSSARY (plain-language meanings, not SEBI text):
SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India): The securities market regulator, set up under the SEBI Act, 1992. It registers intermediaries, makes regulations, inspects and investigates, and can penalise breaches.
LODR (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements): The rulebook every listed entity follows after listing: board and committee rules, disclosures to stock exchanges, results, shareholding pattern and related party transactions.
UPSI (Unpublished price sensitive information): Information about a listed company or its securities that is not generally available and would likely affect the share price if it were, such as results, dividends, mergers or major changes in management.
Insider: A connected person, or anyone in possession of or with access to UPSI. Insiders may not trade while holding UPSI.
Designated person: An employee or connected person whom the company's board names, by role and access to UPSI, as covered by its insider trading code of conduct.
Trading window: The period when designated persons may trade. It closes from the end of each quarter until 48 hours after the results are published.
Trading plan: A pre-set plan an insider files and discloses in advance so that trades can go through even while the insider holds UPSI. A cool-off period of 120 calendar days applies.
Structured digital database (SDD): The record a listed company or intermediary keeps of everyone with whom UPSI has been shared, with time stamps.
Material event: An event or information a listed entity must disclose to the stock exchanges. Some are always material (Schedule III Para A); others are tested against the materiality criteria in regulation 30.
Related party transaction (RPT): A transfer of resources, services or obligations between a listed entity and a related party. Material ones need prior shareholder approval.
High value debt listed entity (HVDLE): A listed entity with listed non-convertible debt of Rs 5,000 crore or more outstanding. It follows the LODR corporate governance rules on a comply-or-explain basis.
Non-convertible securities (NCS): Debt securities and non-convertible redeemable preference shares that do not convert into equity, such as NCDs.
Recovery Expense Fund: A fund a debt issuer sets up with the stock exchange so the debenture trustee can meet the costs of enforcing security on default.
Debenture trustee: A SEBI-registered entity that protects debenture holders: it monitors security and asset cover and acts on default.
Electronic book provider platform (EBP): The exchange platform through which private placements of debt above the set size are bid and allotted.
Open offer: An offer an acquirer must make to the public shareholders of a listed company on crossing the takeover thresholds, normally for at least 26% of the shares.
Creeping acquisition: Buying up to 5% more of a listed company's voting rights in a financial year without an open offer, allowed for a holder of 25% or more.
Minimum public shareholding (MPS): Every listed company other than a public sector company must keep at least 25% of its shares with the public.
Promoters' contribution: The share of post-issue capital, at least 20%, that promoters must hold after an IPO. It is locked in for 18 months, or three years in some cases.
Asset management company (AMC): The company that runs a mutual fund's schemes under the supervision of the trustees.
Trustees: The trustee company that holds a mutual fund's property in trust for unitholders and supervises the AMC.
Total expense ratio (TER): All the expenses charged to a mutual fund scheme's investors as a share of its assets. Only the categories allowed by the MF Regulations may be charged.
Exit load: A charge on redemption of mutual fund units. It goes back to the scheme and cannot exceed 3% of NAV for an open-ended scheme.
NAV (Net Asset Value): The value of one unit of a mutual fund scheme, computed every day from the scheme's net assets.
Specialized Investment Fund (SIF): A category under the MF Regulations 2026 that lets a mutual fund run investment strategies with more flexibility than ordinary schemes, for investors who meet the set thresholds.
MF Lite (Mutual Fund Lite): A lighter mutual fund route under Chapter X of the MF Regulations 2026 for passive schemes such as index funds and ETFs.
Alternative Investment Fund (AIF): A privately pooled fund registered with SEBI that invests for its investors. Category I and II funds are close ended; Category III may be open or close ended.
Accredited investor: An investor whose income, net worth or securities market exposure meets the AIF Regulations' thresholds and who is accredited by an agency or, since October 2026, by a specified manager. Some minimum investment rules do not apply to them.
Private placement memorandum (PPM): The offer document of an AIF scheme. Compliance with its terms is audited every year.
Continuing interest: The minimum stake an AIF's manager or sponsor must keep in the fund: the lower of 2.5% of corpus or Rs 5 crore (5% or Rs 10 crore for Category III).
Stock broker: A member of a recognised stock exchange registered with SEBI to trade for clients or on its own account.
Clearing member: A member of a clearing corporation that clears and settles trades for itself (self-clearing) or for other brokers.
Qualified stock broker (QSB): A stock broker that SEBI designates because of its size, client base and market impact, with extra obligations on governance and systems.
Running account settlement: The periodic return to clients of the funds a stock broker holds for them, as the Stock Brokers Master Circular specifies.
MITC (Most Important Terms and Conditions): The short statement of key terms a stock broker gives clients when opening an account.
KRA (KYC Registration Agency): An agency that holds clients' KYC records so that one KYC works across securities market intermediaries.
Fit and proper person: The integrity, reputation and competence test that every SEBI intermediary and its key people must meet at all times.
SCORES (SEBI Complaints Redress System): SEBI's online system for investor complaints against listed companies and intermediaries. Complaints must be resolved within 21 calendar days.
ODR (Online dispute resolution): The Smart ODR portal for disputes between investors and listed companies or intermediaries: online conciliation first, then online arbitration.
CSCRF (Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework): SEBI's cyber security framework for regulated entities, graded by size. Cyber incidents must be reported within 6 hours.
Settlement: Closing SEBI enforcement proceedings by paying a settlement amount and meeting other terms, without admitting or denying the findings. Governed by the 2026 Settlement Regulations from 7 November 2026.
Adjudicating officer: The SEBI officer who holds an inquiry and imposes penalties under the SEBI Act.
SAT (Securities Appellate Tribunal): The tribunal that hears appeals against SEBI orders. An appeal must be filed within 45 days.
Disgorgement: An order to give up profits made, or losses avoided, through a breach of securities law. SEBI can direct it under s. 11B of the SEBI Act.

SEBI CHANGES (2026-09-01 to 2026-11-25):
- 2026-11-25 Circular: Simplification of client on-boarding and rationalisation of risk management framework at KYC Registration Agencies (). Applies: All SEBI-registered intermediaries that on-board clients, and KYC Registration Agencies.. Changes: Common supplementary client information (such as income range, FATCA/CRS details, PEP status and occupation) is centralised at the KRA, along with CKYC ID, DIGIPIN and the expiry date of the officially valid document. Intermediaries upload the specified KYC information to the KRA within three working days of completing KYC. In force from 24-12-2026; KRAs issue an operating procedure within 30 days. Actions: Update account opening forms and KRA upload processes before 24-12-2026.
- 2026-11-11 Circular: FATCA and CRS reporting revised under the Income-Tax Act, 2025 and Income-Tax Rules, 2026 (). Applies: Registered intermediaries that are Reporting Financial Institutions.. Changes: Reporting and due diligence now follow s. 508 of the Income-Tax Act, 2025 and rules 238 to 240 and Form 166 of the Income-Tax Rules, 2026. Self-certification, controlling person and account classification systems must capture the new fields; FATCA/CRS certifications are uploaded to the KRA. Actions: Review on-boarding and reporting systems for the new mandatory fields.
- 2026-11-07 Regulations: SEBI (Settlement of Administrative and Civil Proceedings) Regulations, 2026 in force (SEBI/LAD-DOP/2026/318). Applies: Anyone facing or likely to face SEBI proceedings.. Changes: Replace the Settlement Proceedings Regulations, 2018. Settlement notices before a show cause notice, a formula-based settlement amount, and fast track settlement for delayed or wrong-format disclosures. Applications within 90 days of a show cause notice; pending cases not settled under the 2018 rules may apply within 90 days at a 20% higher amount. Actions: Check any pending show cause notices against the 90-day window.
- 2026-11-06 Consultation paper: Consultation paper: AML/CFT certification for Principal Officers of intermediaries (). Applies: All intermediaries registered under s. 12 of the SEBI Act.. Changes: Proposes a mandatory AML/CFT certification for the Principal Officer responsible for PMLA compliance. No rule has changed yet. Actions: 
- 2026-11-05 Circular: Review of the framework for Accredited Investors (). Applies: AIF managers, SIF AMCs and portfolio managers, and their investors.. Changes: Managers may determine and record an investor's accredited status themselves, valid for three years and recognised across the same group. Securities market exposure qualifies: at least Rs 5 crore for individuals, HUFs, family trusts and sole proprietorships, and Rs 20 crore for bodies corporate and other trusts. Actions: Set up a documented accreditation process if using the manager route.
