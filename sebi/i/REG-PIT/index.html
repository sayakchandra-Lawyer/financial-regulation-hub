Key concepts

  • Unpublished price sensitive information (UPSI)
  • Communication of UPSI only for legitimate purposes (regulation 3)
  • Trading while in possession of UPSI (regulation 4)
  • Trading plans (regulation 5)
  • Disclosures by promoters, KMP, directors and designated persons (regulation 7)
  • Codes of conduct and fair disclosure (regulations 8, 9)
  • Structured digital database
  • Trading window

Key obligations

  • regulation 3: UPSI may be shared only for legitimate purposes, performance of duties or discharge of legal obligations
  • regulation 3(5): the board must ensure a structured digital database of persons with whom UPSI is shared
  • regulation 4(1): no insider may trade while in possession of UPSI, subject to the defences listed
  • regulation 5: a trading plan must be approved by the compliance officer and disclosed to the exchanges; the trade price may be capped at up to 20% above or below the closing price
  • regulation 9: listed companies and intermediaries must adopt a code of conduct for designated persons, including trading window and pre-clearance rules
  • Schedule B: the trading window closes from the end of every quarter until 48 hours after results are published

Deadlines and cycles

  • regulation 7(1)(b): a person appointed as KMP or director, or becoming a promoter or member of the promoter group, discloses holdings to the company within 7 days
  • regulation 7(2)(a): promoters, promoter group members, designated persons and directors disclose trades worth over Rs 10 lakh in a calendar quarter to the company within two trading days
  • regulation 7(2)(b): the company informs the stock exchanges within two trading days of receiving that disclosure
  • regulation 5(5): the compliance officer approves or rejects a trading plan within two trading days of receipt, and notifies an approved plan to the exchanges on the day of approval
  • regulation 5(2)(i): trading under the plan may start no earlier than 120 calendar days after its public disclosure

If you breach it

  • SEBI Act s. 15G: Rs 10 lakh up to Rs 25 crore or three times the profit, whichever is higher
  • Schedule B: breach of the code of conduct is reported to SEBI and may lead to wage freeze, suspension or recovery

General consequences under the statute. SEBI Act, 1992: SEBI may issue directions, including disgorgement and debarment (s. 11, 11B), pass cease and desist orders (s. 11D) and suspend or cancel registration (s. 12(3)). Adjudicating officers impose penalties under ss. 15A to 15HB: for example, failure to file returns or furnish information carries at least Rs 1 lakh, and up to Rs 1 lakh a day while it continues, capped at Rs 1 crore (s. 15A); insider trading and fraudulent or unfair trade practices carry Rs 10 lakh (s. 15G) or Rs 5 lakh (s. 15HA) up to Rs 25 crore or three times the profit, whichever is higher; any breach with no specific penalty carries Rs 1 lakh up to Rs 1 crore (s. 15HB). Contraventions are also offences punishable with imprisonment up to 10 years, a fine up to Rs 25 crore, or both (s. 24). Proceedings may be settled under s. 15JB. Appeals lie to the Securities Appellate Tribunal within 45 days (s. 15T).
