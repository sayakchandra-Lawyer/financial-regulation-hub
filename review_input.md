# Stock exchanges (NSE/BSE) register summary for review

Public material: NSE, BSE and SEBI circulars. Please check each point for errors of law or fact (amounts, days, thresholds, effective dates), important omissions for listed NBFCs/debt issuers/lenders, and misleading wording. Number your findings.

## SECC Regulations, 2018 (SEBI notification of 03-10-2018, last amended by the Fourth Amendment Regulations, 2025; issued 2018-10-03)
- Recognition, ownership and governance of stock exchanges and clearing corporations
- Ownership limits: at least 51% public holding in a recognised stock exchange (reg 17(1))
- Fit and proper test for every shareholder of an exchange or clearing corporation (reg 19)
- An exchange may not list securities of its own associates (reg 45(2))
- reg 17(2): no person resident in India may hold more than 5% of a recognised stock exchange's paid-up equity, alone or with persons acting in concert; a stock exchange, depository, banking company, insurance company or public financial institution may hold up to 15%
- reg 17(3) and (4): a person resident outside India may hold up to 5% (up to 15% for a foreign exchange, depository, bank, insurer, commodity derivatives exchange or approved multilateral institution), and combined foreign holding is capped at 49%
- reg 18: at least 51% of a recognised clearing corporation must be held by recognised stock exchanges; other Indian residents may hold up to 5% (15% for a depository, bank, insurer or public financial institution)
- reg 19(1): no person may acquire or hold equity shares or voting rights in an exchange or clearing corporation unless fit and proper; the exchange or clearing corporation must ensure this
- reg 17(5): a clearing corporation may not hold any stake in a recognised stock exchange

## SEBI Master Circular for exchanges and clearing corporations (SEBI Master Circular of 30-12-2024; issued 2024-12-30)
- Consolidates SEBI's circulars on how exchanges and clearing corporations run trading, clearing, settlement, risk management and technology
- Replaced the master circular of 16-10-2023
- On 22-06-2026 SEBI announced a comprehensive review (press release 35/2026): a single master circular for all exchanges, a separate one for clearing corporations and about 50% fewer pages; no replacement had been issued as at 26-11-2026
- Exchanges and clearing corporations must give effect to the master circular through their bye-laws, rules, regulations and circulars

## NSE Rules and Bye-laws (NSE Rules and Bye-laws; issued 2026-06-19)
- The Bye-laws cover trading segments, dealings in securities, trading members, participants, transactions and settlements, rights and liabilities of members and constituents, arbitration, dispute resolution, default and the investor protection fund
- 'Exchange securities' are those admitted to the official list of NSE securities; an 'issuer' includes any body whose security is admitted to dealings on NSE
- Rules and regulations are subject to the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956, its rules and the SEBI Act
- Members and issuers must comply with the Rules, Bye-laws, Regulations and the circulars issued under them

## NSE debt segment (WDM) trading regulations (NSE WDM Trading Regulations; issued )
- Chapters on dealings on the exchange, dealings in securities, conduct of business by trading members, uniform practice code on instruments, listing regulations, arbitration, accounts and records, and inspection
- Trading members must conduct debt-segment business in line with the regulations and keep the specified accounts and records

## BSE Rules, Bye-laws and Regulations (BSE Rules, Bye-laws and Regulations; issued )
- BSE's equivalent of the NSE Rules and Bye-laws, covering membership, dealings, clearing and settlement, defaults and arbitration
- Members and issuers must comply with BSE's Rules, Bye-laws, Regulations and notices

## Uniform Listing Agreement (Exchange format; issued )
- A short agreement that binds the issuer to LODR, SEBI circulars and the exchange's bye-laws, regulations and circulars
- An issuer already listed on the exchange that lists a new kind of security (NCDs, CPs, perpetual debt) does not sign afresh; it ticks the new security in 'Information about the Company and Securities' and files it with a covering letter
- cl. 1 and 2: comply with all statutes on issue, listing and continued listing, LODR and other SEBI regulations and circulars, and the exchange's bye-laws, regulations, circulars and directions
- cl. 3: pay listing and other fees and fines levied by the exchange within the prescribed period
- cl. 4: keep the exchange informed of any change in the issuer's information
- cl. 5: admission and continued admission to dealings is at the exchange's discretion and subject to its power to prohibit, suspend or withdraw listing
- cl. 6: a board (or authorised committee) resolution for initial listing must be in place

## NSE checklists for privately placed NCDs (NSE debt private placement checklists; issued 2026-08-07)
- Two-stage process on NSE's NEAPS portal: in-principle approval before the issue, then a final listing application after allotment
- In-principle documents: draft General Information Document (GID); latest rating press release (not older than one year) with rating letters; debenture trustee consent and due diligence certificate (needed even for unsecured issues); issuer certificate (Annexure I); PAN, Aadhaar, driving licence, bank account and passport numbers of promoters and PAN of directors; SCORES ID
- First-time issuers also file incorporation papers, MoA and AoA, three years' annual reports and, for an NBFC, RBI's certificate showing whether it is deposit-taking or not
- The GID must carry audited or limited-review financials not older than six months, audited by a peer-reviewed auditor
- Final listing documents: depository credit confirmation; board or committee resolutions authorising the borrowing and the allotment with the list of allottees; debenture trust deed; GID and KID; rating; DT due diligence certificate; LPCC confirmation of the settlement guarantee fund contribution; material contracts; REF (where NSE is the designated exchange); listing fees; SEBI fee of Rs 5,000 per KID
- Obtain NSE's in-principle approval before giving the placement memorandum and term sheet (GID and KID) to the EBP, or before the issue opens where EBP does not apply
- Annexure I certificate: confirm eligibility under the NCS Regulations; that the issuer, promoters, promoter group and directors are not debarred by SEBI; whether any of them is a wilful defaulter (with GID disclosure); that no promoter or director is a fugitive economic offender; whether any SEBI or exchange fine is pending; RBI or other approvals; any past delay in listing and the penal interest paid; REF deposit; and that the issuer will activate the ISIN in both depositories
- Confirm that the Articles allow the debenture trustee to appoint a nominee director, or amend them within six months of listing
- File applications complete in all respects on NEAPS: in-principle by 3 p.m. and final listing by 1.30 p.m. for same-day approval
- Pay fees into the issuer's PAN-based virtual account at IDBI Bank
- In-principle approval: before the GID/KID goes to the EBP (EBP issues) or before issue opening (non-EBP issues)
- Listing application and listing: by T+3 working days, T being the issue closing date (Chapter VII of the SEBI NCS Master Circular)
- Delay in listing beyond T+3: penal interest of 1% a year over the coupon to investors from allotment to listing (Chapter VII, para 6 of the SEBI NCS Master Circular); past delays must be disclosed in later in-principle applications
- Incomplete applications are not processed the same day

## BSE Master Checklist 2026 (BSE notice 20260430-51; issued 2026-04-30)
- BSE's single index of its listing checklists for every kind of issue; it replaced the 2025 master checklist and the 2021 checklist for privately placed debt
- The individual checklists are attached to the notice as embedded documents; the BSE notice is the official copy
- Use the checklist in force on the date of application and file through the BSE Listing Centre

## NSE checklists for public issues of NCDs (NSE debt public issue checklists; issued 2026-06-25)
- Public issues of debt securities and NCRPS list on T+3 working days: optional for issues opening on or after 01-11-2024 and mandatory after one year (SEBI NCS Master Circular)
- Obtain in-principle approval on the draft offer document and apply for final listing with the documents in the checklist
- Listing on T+3 working days from issue closure

## NSE listing fees for privately placed debt (NSE fee page (updated 31-10-2023); issued 2023-10-31)
- Initial listing fee Rs 7,500
- Annual listing fee per ISIN by issue size: up to Rs 100 crore Rs 55,000; Rs 100-200 crore Rs 70,000; Rs 200-300 crore Rs 1 lakh; Rs 300-400 crore Rs 1.5 lakh; Rs 400-500 crore Rs 2.5 lakh
- Above Rs 500 crore: Rs 2.5 lakh plus Rs 1,000 for every Rs 1 crore or part above that
- Annual listing fee payable by an issuer is capped at Rs 10 lakh a year; all amounts exclude taxes
- Pay initial and annual listing fees (cl. 3 of the listing agreement)

## Recovery Expense Fund (REF) (NSE REF guidance; SEBI/HO/MIRSD/CRADT/CIR/P/2020/207; issued 2020-10-22)
- A fund held by the designated stock exchange that the debenture trustee can draw on to enforce rights after a default
- Amount: 0.01% of the issue size (number of bonds x face value), capped at Rs 25 lakh per issuer across all issues
- Paid in cash (into the issuer's PAN-based virtual account) or by bank guarantee in NSE's format
- Deposit REF with the designated stock exchange named in the offer document before final listing
- A transferee company under a scheme of arrangement must deposit REF before or at final listing
- Bank guarantee: at least 12 months with a 3-month claim period (or 15 months if no claim period), kept valid until 6 months after the maturity of the listed security; NSE does not accept a guarantee issued by the issuer itself
- REF and interest are released only after the last listed security has been repaid and the debenture trustee gives a no-objection certificate

## REF: when trustees can use it (NSE/CML/2025/12 (BSE 20251127-66), forwarding SEBI circular HO/17/11/12(3)2025-DDHS-POD1/I/145/2025 of 25-11-2025; issued 2025-12-02)
- REF is for prompt enforcement or legal proceedings by the debenture trustee after a default
- Lead debenture trustee: one chosen by the other trustees, or one representing more than 50% of the outstanding value
- para 2.1: after a default the trustee may be reimbursed from REF for consents from debenture holders, voting, meetings, court applications, legal fees, asset-recovery services and legal consultants
- para 2.2: no prior approval of holders is needed for those purposes; the trustee informs holders by email and on its website; for any other purpose it needs holders' prior consent and informs the designated exchange
- para 2.3: the trustee submits an independent auditor's certificate of expenses, which the exchange verifies before release
- para 2.6 and 2.7: the trustee keeps accounts of REF spending and updates holders annually
- para 2.4: the designated exchange releases REF to the trustee within five working days of the intimation

## ISIN limits for private placement debt (2026) (NSE/CML/D/2026/05 (BSE 20261008-21), forwarding SEBI circular HO/17/11/24(8)2026-DDHS-POD1/I/23125/2026 of 07-10-2026; issued 2026-10-08)
- Replaces paras 1.1 to 1.3 of Chapter VIII of the SEBI NCS Master Circular with effect from 07-10-2026
- Up to 17 ISINs may mature in a financial year, plus six more for s. 54EC capital gains bonds by authorised issuers
- Within 17: up to 12 for plain vanilla debt (secured and unsecured together); once the amount outstanding across those 12 maturing in a year reaches Rs 15,000 crore, one more ISIN for each further Rs 3,000 crore
- Up to 5 for structured debt, market-linked debentures, floating rate bonds, zero coupon bonds and Tier II bonds; existing ISINs of these kinds are grandfathered, but no new ISIN in the category for a year that already has five maturing
- An issuer that issues only these instruments may have 12 maturing in a year
- Excluded from the count: Government-serviced (EBR) bonds and ESG debt securities
- Plan new issues and re-issues within the ISIN maturity caps for each financial year

## Rs 10,000 NCDs without a merchant banker (NSE/CML/D/2026/06 (BSE 20261008-22), forwarding SEBI circular HO/17/11/24(7)2026-DDHS-POD1/I/23122/2026 of 07-10-2026; issued 2026-10-08)
- Para 1.3 of Chapter V of the SEBI NCS Master Circular: a Rs 10,000 face value private placement normally needs at least one merchant banker with public-issue responsibilities
- No merchant banker is needed if all of these are met: (i) the issuer is regulated by SEBI, RBI, IRDAI or PFRDA (so an RBI-regulated NBFC or HFC qualifies on this test); (ii) it has been listed on an exchange for at least a year with no pending SEBI or exchange fine for LODR non-compliance, which the exchange confirms at in-principle approval; (iii) no default on debt securities, NCRPS, SDIs, CP, deposits or loans in the last three financial years and the current year, certified by the statutory auditor to the exchange; (iv) the security is senior and secured by a first or pari passu charge on identifiable assets (CPSEs, PSUs and statutory bodies may issue unsecured); (v) rated at least AA- (lowest rating counts)

## Rs 10,000 face value: zero coupon allowed (NSE/CML/2025/14 (BSE 20251222-26), forwarding SEBI circular HO/17/11/24(1)2025-DDHS-POD1/I/491/2025 of 18-12-2025; issued 2025-12-19)
- A Rs 10,000 face value security may now be either interest-bearing with regular coupons or a zero coupon security, in each case with a fixed maturity and no structured obligations
- Applies to private placements proposed to be listed from 18-12-2025

## Credit Risk-o-Meter (NSE circular of 08-10-2026 (BSE 20261008-23), forwarding SEBI circular HO/17/11/22(1)2026-DDHS-POD1/I/23092/2026 of 07-10-2026; issued 2026-10-08)
- A colour-coded meter mapping the credit rating (AAA to D; A1+ to D) to six risk levels, from 'lowest credit risk' (AAA, A1+) to 'high to very high risk of default'
- Shows only credit risk; where there are several ratings, the meter uses the lowest
- Show the meter in the offer document, abridged prospectus, private placement memorandum, all advertisements of the issuer or OBPP, and OBPP web and mobile platforms
- Below the meter, state the rating agency, the actual rating and, for unsecured instruments, the word 'unsecured' in bold red
- In force 45 days after 07-10-2026

## What issuers send to debenture trustees, and when (NSE circular of 27-11-2025 (BSE 20251127-64), forwarding SEBI circular HO/17/11/12(3)2025-DDHS-POD1/I/144/2025 of 25-11-2025; issued 2025-11-27)
- Quarterly: security cover certificate (Annex-VA format) and statements of the value of pledged securities, the debt service reserve account or other security
- Half-yearly: net worth certificate of a personal guarantor
- Annually: financials of a corporate guarantor
- Every three years: valuation report and title search report for the secured assets
- Quarterly items within 60 days of quarter end (75 days for the last quarter); half-yearly within 60 days; annual within 60 days of year end; three-yearly within 60 days of the financial year end
- Applies from the quarter ended 31-12-2025

## Framework for listing commercial paper (NSE/CML/2019/33 (BSE 20191226-32); issued 2019-12-26)
- Upload the disclosure document on the exchange's EBP platform for qualified institutional buyers, stating it is an 'Issue of Commercial Papers intended to be listed'
- Submit listing documents by 3 p.m. on the value or settlement date
- T-1: disclosure document filed online; T: deal confirmation; T+1 or T+2: funds received
- List within one day of receiving funds from investors

## CP listing checklist and FAQs (NSE and BSE CP checklists and FAQs (BSE checklist updated 21-12-2021); issued 2021-12-21)
- Listing CP is mandatory only for issuers seeking investment from mutual funds (FAQ 1)
- No in-principle approval is needed for CP (FAQ 3)
- Market lot and allotment value Rs 5 lakh and multiples (FAQ 4)
- LODR does not apply to an entity whose only listed security is CP; it follows Chapter XVII of the SEBI NCS Master Circular instead (FAQ 17)
- Activate the CP ISIN in both NSDL and CDSL (FAQ 5)
- Confirm that securities have been credited to investors' demat accounts and upload the list of allottees on NEAPS (FAQ 6 and 7)
- Disclose three years' audited financials not older than six months from the listing application, with stub-period financials (FAQ 8), and declare that the rating is valid on the issue and listing date (FAQ 9)
- First-time issuers sign the uniform listing agreement (FAQ 10); pay the SEBI fee of Rs 5,000 plus GST to the designated exchange at listing (FAQ 11 and 12)
- Record date: one working day before maturity, intimated to the exchange 3 working days before the record date (FAQ 16)

## CP payment status within one working day (NSE/CML/2024/38, forwarding SEBI circular SEBI/HO/DDHS/DDHS-PoD-1/P/CIR/2024/117 of 06-09-2024; issued 2024-09-10)
- Para 8.4 of Chapter XVII of the SEBI NCS Master Circular: file a certificate confirming fulfilment of payment obligations within one working day of payment becoming due (previously two days), aligned with reg 57 of LODR for NCDs
- Non-submission of the payment certificate for listed CP: Rs 1,000 a day per ISIN (SEBI master circular on listing obligations for debt, Annex IX-A Part B)

## NSE EBP operating guidelines (NSE/DS/71301; issued 2025-11-14)
- EBP is mandatory for private placements of debt securities and NCRPS of Rs 20 crore or more (single issue including green shoe, shelf tranches in a year, or cumulative issues in a year), and for any issue by an issuer in existence for less than three years
- PDIs, PNCPS and other non-equity regulatory capital instruments of banks and NBFCs must use EBP whatever the size
- Optional for smaller issues, SDIs, security receipts, CPs, CDs and units of REITs, SM REITs and InvITs; bilateral deals must be routed through EBP
- Arrangers: SEBI-registered merchant bankers, brokers or RBI-registered primary dealers, enabled by the issuer
- Anchor portion capped by rating: up to 30% of base issue for AAA to AA-, 40% for A+ to A-, 50% for others
- Minimum bid lot equals face value; for NBFC and HFC issuers the minimum bid lot is Rs 1 crore; Rs 1 crore for Chapter V (regulatory capital) securities
- Allotment on price priority (coupon fixed by issuer) or yield priority (coupon discovered); pro-rata at the cut-off
- Get in-principle approval from the listing exchange before uploading the GID and KID on EBP
- Issuer: give the issue notification (GID, KID, term sheet, bidding mode, allotment manner, coupon type, settlement route and cycle, anchor details) at least two working days before issue opening (three for a first-time EBP issuer); bidding announcement at least one working day before bidding, in market hours, or the issue is deemed cancelled
- Green shoe may not exceed five times the base issue; anchors confirm on EBP by 5 p.m. on T-1
- QIBs and non-QIBs bidding through arrangers: not more than Rs 100 crore or 5% of base issue, whichever is lower, per investor
- For an NBFC or HFC issuer, eligible participants may bid subject to any RBI requirements that apply to them
- Participants must declare they do not use algorithms, bots or automation that give unfair access
- Issuer accepts or withdraws within one hour of bidding close; estimated cut-off yield, if disclosed, must be given at least one hour before bidding; the issue may be withdrawn up to one hour before opening
- Pay-in only from bank accounts registered on EBP; third-party money is refunded and no allotment is made
- Bidding 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. on working days, for at least 60 minutes; the window can be extended once, before its last 10 minutes; no pre-closure
- In the last 10 minutes no cancellation; revisions only to improve rate or size
- Settlement on T+1 or T+2: funds pay-in to the clearing corporation by 10.30 a.m.; issuer confirms by 12 noon; RTA instructs the depositories by 12 noon
- An issuer that neither accepts nor withdraws in time: issue withdrawn and issuer debarred as SEBI specifies
- Investor or arranger that fails to pay in, wholly or partly: barred from all EBPs for 30 days
- Arranger whose clients default three times across EBPs: barred from bidding on any EBP for 7 days
- Debarments take effect from the next working day across all EBPs; a debarred issuer cannot run bidding and pending issues in the period are cancelled

## BSEBOND (BSE EBP) operating guidelines (BSE notice 20251114-47; issued 2025-11-14)
- Same SEBI framework and Rs 20 crore threshold as NSE-EBP
- An issuer may withdraw after bidding without debarment only if bids fall short of the base issue size, a bidder defaults so the base size cannot be met, or the cut-off yield exceeds the estimated cut-off yield disclosed beforehand (with the base issue fully subscribed); BSEBOND then discloses the estimated cut-off
- Map eligible participants to the issue not later than one hour before bidding starts
- Anchors confirm participation by 5 p.m. on T-1
- Bidding between 9 a.m. and 5 p.m. on the platform's working days
- Funds pay-in by 10.30 a.m. on the settlement day
- Withdrawal for any other reason: issuer barred from all EBPs for seven days from the withdrawal
- Bidder pay-in default: 30-day bar across all EBPs; arranger with three client defaults: 7-day bar

## Integrated Filing (Governance and Financial) (NSE/CML/2025/02 (BSE 20250102-4), forwarding SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/CFD-PoD-2/CIR/P/2024/185; issued 2025-01-02)
- Integrated Filing (Governance) combines the investor grievance statement (reg 13(3)) and the corporate governance report (reg 27(2)(a))
- Integrated Filing (Financial) combines related party transactions (reg 23(9)), the quarterly disclosure of outstanding defaults on loans and debt securities (Section V-B of the LODR Master Circular), the statement of deviation (reg 32(1)) and financial results (reg 33(3))
- Some Schedule III events are now reported quarterly in the Governance filing: acquisitions in unlisted companies of 5% (or further 2%), fines below the Para A(20) thresholds, and updates on tax litigation
- File both parts in the prescribed format for every quarter from the quarter ended 31-12-2024, through the exchange portals; mask PAN
- Keep filing the existing XBRL returns for each component until the exchanges say otherwise
- Governance: within 30 days of quarter end
- Financial: within 45 days of quarter end, and 60 days for the last quarter and the year
- Late corporate governance report: Rs 2,000 a day; late RPT disclosure: Rs 5,000 a day; late financial results: Rs 5,000 a day (SOP fines)

## FAQs on Integrated Filing (Governance) (NSE/CML/2026/16 (BSE 20260612-13); issued 2026-06-12)
- Guidance on filling the XBRL Governance report (board and committee composition, meetings, Schedule III quarterly items); guidance only, not law
- Submit within 30 days of each quarter end (reg 27(2))
- A newly listed entity files from the first quarter ending after listing

## Single filing across NSE and BSE (NSE/CML/2026/17 (BSE 20260619-11); issued 2026-06-19)
- A filing made on one exchange is shared with the other through an API, so commonly listed entities file once; extended on 20-06-2026 to more Regulation 30 events
- For the covered Regulation 30 events, the PDF filing counts as compliance, but the XBRL filing must follow within 24 hours

## Reg 50 board meeting intimations in XBRL (NSE/CML/2025/09 (BSE 20251121-72); issued 2025-11-21)
- From 01-12-2025, file reg 50 prior intimations (board meetings and meetings of holders) only in XBRL through the common utility; a PDF or other mode is treated as non-submission
- Delay in intimation of a board meeting or holders' meeting under reg 50: Rs 5,000 per instance per item (SOP fines for debt)

## Record dates for debt: where and how to file (NSE/CML/2025/02 (debt, 29-04-2025) and NSE/CML/2025/04 (07-02-2025); issued 2025-04-29)
- Give advance notice of the record date for interest payments, call or put exercise and early, voluntary or partial redemption under the subject 'Record Date Updates' on NEAPS; filings under other subjects may not be taken on record
- Keep PDF and XBRL disclosures consistent
- Equity: keep at least three clear working days between the board or shareholder approval and the record date; book closure is no longer announced (reg 42(5) omitted)
- Delay in notice of record date for debt: Rs 10,000 per ISIN (SOP fines for debt)

## Credit ratings disclosed straight from the agencies (NSE/CML/2025/08; issued 2025-08-01)
- From 02-08-2025 rating agencies upload new ratings and revisions daily, and the exchange publishes them automatically on the company's page
- Rating agencies and ESG rating providers report ratings to the exchange through the prescribed upload

## Joint approval of further issues by NSE and BSE (NSE/CML/2026/22; issued 2026-10-21)
- File the application with all exchanges as before; the exchanges assign a lead exchange in turn; the other exchange sends its queries through the lead exchange, and the company answers only the lead exchange
- Applies to further issue applications filed on or after 01-11-2026

## NSE Master Circular for listed companies 2026 (NSE Master Circular of 30-04-2026; issued 2026-04-30)
- An index of NSE's operative circulars by subject, with links: IPO and direct listing, schemes of arrangement and resolution plans (including the joint SOP under regs 37 and 59A of 31-07-2025), further issues, continuous disclosures, and compliance
- Follow the circulars listed as current; superseded circulars are dropped from the index

## BSE Master Circular for listing compliance 2026 (BSE notice 20260430-29; issued 2026-04-30)
- BSE's consolidation of its listing-compliance notices on filings through the BSE Listing Centre, XBRL, disclosures and SOP actions
- Follow the consolidated BSE notices on listing compliance

## LODR Third Amendment 2026: list all later NCDs (SEBI/LAD-DOP/2026/329 (BSE notice 20261023-12); issued 2026-10-12)
- Reg 62A(1) as substituted: such an entity must list all non-convertible debt securities it issues after that listing; reg 62A(2) and (3) are omitted

## NCS Amendment 2026: incentives for retail and others (SEBI/LAD-NRO/GN/2026/296 (BSE notice 20260203-13); issued 2026-01-20)
- New reg 2(1)(gga): a 'retail individual investor' applies or bids for debt securities of not more than Rs 2 lakh
- Reg 31 proviso: an issuer may offer additional interest or a discount to the issue price to senior citizens, women, serving and retired defence personnel and their widows or widowers, retail individual investors or other categories SEBI specifies; the incentive is only for the initial allottee and is lost on transfer

## Disclosure of defaults on bank loans (SEBI/HO/CFD/CMD1/CIR/P/2019/140 (NSE/CML/2019/07), now Section V-B of the LODR Master Circular updated 30-01-2026 (BSE 20260203-14); issued 2019-11-21)
- 'Default': non-payment of interest or principal in full on the pre-agreed date; for revolving facilities, the balance staying above the sanctioned limit or drawing power, whichever is lower, for more than 30 days
- Each disclosure names the lender, date and amount of default (principal and interest), the facility terms, total bank and FI borrowings and total financial indebtedness
- Disclose a loan default that continues beyond 30 days promptly, and not later than 24 hours from the 30th day
- Disclose a default on unlisted NCDs or NCRPS within 24 hours of the default
- Report outstanding defaults on loans and debt securities every quarter as part of Integrated Filing (Financial)
- 24 hours after day 30 of a loan default; 24 hours for unlisted debt securities; quarterly with Integrated Filing (Financial)

## Reasons for promoter pledges (NSE/CML/2019/19, forwarding SEBI circular SEBI/HO/CFD/DCR1/CIR/P/2019/90 of 07-08-2019; issued 2019-08-08)
- Encumbrance includes a pledge, lien or other interest given to a lender over promoter shares
- Where combined encumbrance by the promoter and PACs reaches 50% of their holding or 20% of the company's share capital, disclose detailed reasons in Annexure II to every exchange and the company, and again whenever encumbrance rises further
- This is in addition to the reg 31(1) encumbrance disclosure
- The company puts the disclosure on its website within two working days; the exchanges publish a separate list of such companies
- Within two working days of creating the encumbrance

## Surveillance of companies with high promoter pledge (BSE notice 20260902-39 (continuing notices 20191024-30 and 20200417-31); issued 2026-09-02)
- The exchanges review the list periodically; inclusion is a market surveillance step, not an adverse finding against the company
- Securities on the list attract a minimum 35% margin in the equity and equity derivatives segments on all open and new positions
- A company may make a representation against inclusion by the deadline in the notice

## Lock-in of pledged shares (NSE/CML/2026/07, forwarding SEBI circular HO/49/(17)2026-CFD-POD2/I/8965/2026 of 08-04-2026; issued 2026-04-10)
- Where lock-in cannot be created on pledged shares, the depositories record them as 'non-transferable' for the lock-in period
- Issuers follow the depositories' framework: suitable provisions in the Articles, intimation to the lenders or pledgees, and disclosure in the offer document

## SOP fines for equity-listed entities (SEBI/HO/CFD/CMD/CIR/P/2020/12 of 22-01-2020 (NSE circular of 22-01-2020), now Chapter VII-A of the LODR Master Circular; issued 2020-01-22)
- Fines are the first resort; they accrue until the default is cured or trading is suspended and go to the exchange's investor protection fund
- Examples: no qualified company secretary as compliance officer Rs 1,000 a day; board composition Rs 5,000 a day; audit committee Rs 2,000 a day; RPT disclosure Rs 5,000 a day; corporate governance report Rs 2,000 a day; no in-principle approval before issuing securities Rs 50,000 per instance; late board meeting intimation Rs 10,000 per instance per item; shareholding pattern Rs 2,000 a day; financial results Rs 5,000 a day; annual report Rs 2,000 a day; record date lapses Rs 10,000 per instance per item; AGM not held within five months Rs 25,000 per instance
- Where the entity is listed on more than one exchange, the exchanges act uniformly in consultation
- The exchange issues a notice within 30 days of the due date; the entity must comply and pay within 15 days; failing that, notice to the promoters to comply and pay within 10 days
- Then the depositories freeze the entire shareholding of the promoters in the entity and all other securities in their demat accounts until compliance and payment
- The entity must place the non-compliance and the exchange's actions before its board at the next meeting and send the board's comments to the exchange
- Suspension if the same default runs for two consecutive quarters (board composition, audit committee, governance report, shareholding pattern, financial results, share capital audit) or two consecutive years (annual report): move to the Z category (trade-for-trade) and then suspend trading
- Compulsory delisting is started if the entity does not comply and pay within 6 months of suspension
- Fines, promoter demat freeze, Z category, suspension and compulsory delisting under the SCRA, SCRR and Delisting Regulations

## SOP fines for debt and CP issuers (SEBI master circular SEBI/HO/DDHS/DDHS-PoD-1/P/CIR/2025/0000000103 of 11-07-2025; BSE guidance note on SEBI circular of 13-11-2020; issued 2025-07-11)
- Fines (Annex IX-A Part A): no compliance officer, no RTA, investor complaints Rs 1,000 a day each; reg 50 intimations Rs 5,000 per instance per item; financial results under reg 52 Rs 5,000 a day; reg 52(4)/(6) line items Rs 1,000 a day; use of proceeds Rs 1,000 a day; annual report Rs 2,000 a day; security cover disclosure (reg 54) Rs 1,000 a day
- Payment obligations: reg 57(1) status Rs 2,000 a day per ISIN; reg 57(4) and (5) quarterly details and certificates Rs 1,000 per ISIN; no prior exchange approval for a structural change in the securities (reg 59) Rs 50,000 per instance; late record date notice (reg 60(2)) Rs 10,000 per ISIN
- Listed CP (Part B): financial results Rs 5,000 a day; line items Rs 1,000 a day; payment certificate Rs 1,000 a day per ISIN
- No double fine where the same common obligation has been fined under the equity SOP
- Fines start from the working day after the due date; the exchange reviews within 30 days and the issuer must comply and pay within 15 days of the notice, then within 10 days of a reminder
- Place the non-compliance before the board at its next meeting and send the board's comments to the exchange
- If the issuer still does not comply and pay, the exchange tells the other exchanges and all EBPs, which then refuse any new issue by it on the EBP platform and any further listing of its NCS or CP until it complies and pays
- An issuer with unpaid SOP fines must disclose them when applying for in-principle approval, and loses the merchant-banker exemption for Rs 10,000 NCDs

## Waiver of SOP fines (NSE/CML/2025/35; BSE policies for exemption of SOP fines (equity and debt); issued 2025-08-26)
- For an entity listed on both exchanges only one exchange decides the waiver and its decision binds the other: NSE for names starting A to K or digits 0 to 4, BSE for L to Z or digits 5 to 9; a non-refundable processing fee applies
- Grounds considered: natural calamity; seizure of books or computers by authorities; impossibility (pending government or regulator approval of a director or KMP, a nominee appointed by an authority, casual vacancy); court or tribunal orders (such as NCLT orders or appointment of a liquidator); and events beyond the company's control such as strikes and lockouts
- Disclose the event promptly with supporting documents and an impact assessment certified by the company secretary or compliance officer (by the statutory auditor where filing of financials is affected)
- Waiver is considered only if the company has otherwise complied with all its compliances
- Applies to waiver applications filed on or after 01-09-2025

## NSE Clearing: debt segment, RFQ and reported deals (NCL/DS/74002; issued 2026-04-30)
- Normal market deals on the debt segment settle on DVP III (netted) on T+1 with settlement guarantee
- Trades reported on NSE's CBRICS platform, NSE RFQ and the CCIL platform settle participant-wise on DVP I (gross), with no settlement guarantee
- Entities required by SEBI to clear corporate bond trades through a clearing corporation (mutual funds, FPIs, venture funds, portfolio managers and RBI-regulated entities as RBI specifies) register with NSE Clearing
- Stamp duty of 0.0001% on the buyer for transfers of corporate bonds, securitised debt and CP; 0.00001% on CDs; for repo, on the borrower's interest on the reverse leg
- Also covers settlement of Corporate Debt Market Development Fund transactions and the liquidity window
- Sign the Corporate Bond Settlement Agreement and register bank and demat accounts before settling
- Pay in securities and funds (including stamp duty) by the cut-off; RFQ trades may be paid by net banking, UPI or NEFT
- Settlement cycles T+0, T+1 and T+2, Monday to Friday; pay-in cut-off 5.45 p.m.
- If either side fails to pay in fully by the cut-off, the trade is cancelled and what was paid is returned; unpaid stamp duty is treated as a funds shortfall

## Liquidity Window Facility for bond investors (NSE/DS/72742 (11-02-2026) and NSE/CML/2026/1 (04-05-2026), under SEBI circular SEBI/HO/DDHS/DDHS-PoD-1/P/CIR/2024/141 of 16-10-2024; issued 2026-02-11)
