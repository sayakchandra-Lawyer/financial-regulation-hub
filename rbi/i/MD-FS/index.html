Key obligations

  • para 9: financial year ends 31 March; change needs RBI approval
  • para 10: follow Ind AS or Companies (Accounting Standards) Rules
  • para 12(1)(iii): Board approves the ECL methodology; parameters and assumptions documented; no changes to ECL parameters for profit smoothing
  • para 12(1)(v): rebuttal of the 30-days-past-due SICR presumption only with documented justification placed before the Audit Committee; no deferral of SICR recognition for any exposure overdue beyond 60 days
  • para 12(2): compare Ind AS impairment with IRACP; shortfall to Impairment Reserve
  • para 13-15: show provisions separately; charge to P&L
  • para 21(4)(a): list of all active co-lending partners displayed prominently on the website
  • para 23: an NBFC-UL must be listed within three years of being identified as UL, with a Board-approved roadmap to meet SEBI LODR disclosure requirements (not for UL NBFCs fully owned and controlled by Government)
  • para 21(1), 21(13): Notes to Accounts include loans against gold and silver collateral; liquidity disclosures every quarter on the website and in the Notes to Accounts for non-deposit NBFCs of ₹100 crore+, CICs and all deposit-taking NBFCs

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 9: finalise balance sheet within 3 months
  • para 21(2): project-finance disclosure every quarter from 01-10-2025
  • para 21(3): non-fund-based credit disclosures from 01-04-2026 (or earlier by internal policy)
  • para 23: UL listing within three years of identification

If you breach it

  • not stated in the Direction

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
