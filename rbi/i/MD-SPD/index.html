Key concepts

  • Standalone Primary Dealer
  • CRAR 15%
  • Subordinated debt as Tier 2
  • Counterparty credit risk
  • Securities financing transactions

Key obligations

  • para 16: maintain CRAR ≥15% at all times
  • para 13: NOF ₹10 crore for NBFI business plus SPD authorisation conditions
  • para 9(5): sub-debt counts in Tier 2 up to 50% of Tier 1
  • para 11: Board-approved policies incl. investment, outsourcing, fit and proper
  • para 14: non-G-Sec and FATF jurisdiction investments per SBR Directions
  • para 115: minimum NOF ₹150 crore for SPDs doing only core activities; ₹250 crore if they also do non-core activities
  • para 116: at least 50% of total financial investments (long and short term) in G-Secs at all times (incl. own stock, stock with RBI under liquidity support / IDL / LAF, repo and CCIL-pledged stock)
  • para 173: publish audited annual results in financial dailies and on the website (Annex III format), with call borrowing, valuation basis, leverage, quarterly CRAR and non-G-Sec issuer mix in the notes

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 12(1): Board reviews non-G-Sec investments at least quarterly
  • para 135: secondary-market OTC trades in corporate bonds and securitised debt reported on a stock exchange within 15 minutes of the trade

If you breach it

  • para 144(7): settlement failures in exchange-traded G-Sec trades are treated as SGL bouncing and attract the SGL penalties; para 174: RBI publishes a press release on any penalty imposed on an SPD

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
