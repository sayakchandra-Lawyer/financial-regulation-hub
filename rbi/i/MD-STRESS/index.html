Key concepts

  • Special Mention Accounts (SMA-0/1/2)
  • Review period and Inter-Creditor Agreement
  • Resolution plan / restructuring
  • Compromise settlement and technical write-off
  • CRILC reporting
  • Accelerated / additional provisioning

Key obligations

  • para 13: Board-approved stressed-asset policy with timelines
  • para 13A: Board policy must cover calamity-relief resolution under Chapter VI-A (objective relief terms, verifiable parameters, delegation matrix)
  • para 16: Board policies on compromise settlements and technical write-offs (approver one level above sanctioning authority)
  • para 16(5): settlements with fraud / wilful defaulters need Board approval
  • para 16A: policy on acquiring and disposing of specified non-financial assets (SNFA): limit as share of total assets, eligibility, delegation, recovery steps first, disposal within at most 7 years
  • para 18-19: classify SMA immediately on default: SMA-0 up to 30 days, SMA-1 more than 30 up to 60 days, SMA-2 more than 60 up to 90 days overdue; applies to all loans incl. retail
  • para 20: monthly CRILC reporting for exposures ≥ ₹5 crore
  • para 21: weekly CRILC default report by Friday

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 10(13): 30-day review period
  • para 16A: SNFA disposal period set by policy, not exceeding 7 years
  • para 45-47: for borrowers with aggregate exposure of ₹1,500 crore or more to specified lenders, the resolution plan must be implemented within 180 days from the end of the 30-day Review Period (reference dates 07-06-2019 for ₹2,000 crore+, 01-01-2020 for ₹1,500-2,000 crore); para 43-44 say when a plan counts as implemented
  • para 48-49: if a viable plan is not implemented, additional specific provision of 20% of outstanding after 180 days from the end of the Review Period, and a further 15% (total 35%) after 365 days from the start of the Review Period; total provisions capped at 100%
  • para 122I, 122Q: calamity relief: invoke resolution within 45 days of the calamity declaration and implement within 135 days; upload relief data on CIMS half-yearly within 30 days of 30 September / 31 March (NIL statement if none)

If you breach it

  • para 22: accelerated provisioning and supervisory action for non-reporting or evergreening
  • para 26: concealing an account's status or evergreening attracts stringent supervisory / enforcement action, incl. higher provisioning and monetary penalties

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
