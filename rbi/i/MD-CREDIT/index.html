Key concepts

  • Digital lending / LSP / DLA
  • Default Loss Guarantee (DLG)
  • Gold and silver collateral (LTV)
  • Microfinance loan
  • Project finance
  • Partial credit enhancement
  • Commercial real estate
  • Gold and silver collateral lending: Chapter IV (paras 32-50) holds the prudential rules (policy, LTV, valuation, ceilings); conduct, assaying, auction and release of collateral sit in the Responsible Business Conduct Directions (paras 43-75)

Key obligations

  • para 6(2): enhanced due diligence before engaging an LSP
  • para 8(2): no automatic increase in credit limit without borrower's explicit request
  • para 9(4): website listing products, LSPs, DLAs and grievance officer
  • para 10(1): disburse only into borrower's bank account (limited exceptions)
  • para 13(1): DLA data collection need-based with explicit consent and audit trail
  • para 14(4): store data only on servers in India
  • para 18(1): report all DLAs on RBI CIMS portal
  • para 33: full credit appraisal for gold/silver loans above ₹2.5 lakh
  • para 40: value gold/silver at reference price for actual purity
  • para 78: update project-finance parameters within 15 days of change
  • para 43-44: LTV caps for consumption loans against gold / silver: 85% up to ₹2.5 lakh, 80% above ₹2.5 lakh up to ₹5 lakh, 75% above ₹5 lakh (bullet loans measured on the amount repayable at maturity), maintained throughout the tenor
  • para 35, 37, 39: no loans to buy gold or against primary gold / silver or gold-backed ETFs / MF units; no re-pledging; per-borrower caps of 1 kg gold and 10 kg silver ornaments, 50 g gold and 500 g silver coins
  • para 106(1) (Chapter VIII, loans against security of shares): NBFCs with asset size of ₹100 crore and above lending against listed shares must maintain an LTV of 50% for loans against the collateral of shares; any shortfall from share-price movements must be made good within 7 working days

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 11(1)-(2): digital loans: cooling-off period set in the NBFC's credit policy, not less than one day; the borrower may exit by paying principal and proportionate APR without penalty; a reasonable one-time processing fee may be kept if disclosed in the KFS
  • para 12(4): LSP/DLA complaint reply within 30 days
  • para 14(4): data processed abroad to be brought back within 24 hours
  • para 27(1): DLG invoked within 120 days overdue
  • para 28(2): monthly DLG disclosure within 7 working days
  • para 38: bullet gold consumption loans max 12 months
  • para 67(3): project loan tenor ≤85% of economic life

If you breach it

  • not stated in the Direction

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
