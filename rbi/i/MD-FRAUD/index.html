Key concepts

  • Early Warning Signals (EWS)
  • Fraud Monitoring Return (FMR)
  • Special Committee of the Board for Monitoring Frauds
  • Natural justice (show-cause notice, reasoned order)
  • Staff accountability
  • Five-year debarment

Key obligations

  • para 5: Board-approved fraud risk management policy
  • para 6: show-cause notice and reasoned order before classifying a fraud (SBI v. Rajesh Agarwal)
  • para 8: Board committee on frauds (≥3 members incl. CEO and 2 IDs)
  • para 12: disclose frauds in Notes to Accounts
  • para 13-15: UL/ML maintain an EWS framework
  • para 40: report frauds to police / law enforcement immediately
  • para 43: file FMR for every fraud regardless of amount
  • para 53: legal audit of title documents for loans ≥ ₹1 crore
  • para 54: complete fraud examination before selling accounts to lenders/ARCs

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 6(2): at least 21 days to reply to show-cause notice
  • para 7: Board reviews policy at least every 3 years
  • para 40: immediate reporting to LEA
  • para 43: FMR within 14 days of classification
  • para 35: 5-year debarment from date of full repayment / settlement
  • para 57: theft, burglary, dacoity and robbery (incl. attempts) reported to RBI's Fraud Monitoring Group immediately and not later than seven days from occurrence, in the RBR format by email
  • para 58: quarterly RBR return on the online portal within 15 days of the end of the quarter

If you breach it

  • para 35: fraud-classified borrowers and associates barred for 5 years from RBI-regulated credit
  • para 46: staff accountability for delays in detecting or reporting fraud
  • para 33: Government NBFCs refer frauds ≥ ₹3 crore to ABBFF

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
