Key concepts

  • ALCO
  • Liquidity risk management framework
  • Structural liquidity statement and mismatch limits
  • Stress testing
  • Contingency funding plan
  • Liquidity Coverage Ratio / HQLA

Key obligations

  • para 8-9: Board has overall responsibility for ALM; sets up an ALCO headed by the CEO / MD or ED; frames a liquidity risk framework with a cushion of unencumbered HQLA
  • para 15: quarterly disclosure of the Annex I liquidity information on the website, and in the Notes to Accounts
  • para 28: regular stress tests across short-term and protracted, NBFC-specific and market-wide scenarios
  • para 29: contingency funding plan with sources, amounts, escalation procedures and lead times
  • para 39: net cumulative negative mismatches in the Structural Liquidity Statement capped at 10% (1-7 days), 10% (8-14 days) and 20% (15-30 days) of cumulative outflows
  • para 40: Board-approved internal limits on cumulative mismatches in all other buckets up to 1 year; same limits for consolidated operations
  • para 57: file DNBS04A (Short Term Dynamic Liquidity) and DNBS04B (Structural Liquidity and Interest Rate Sensitivity)
  • para 60, 62: LCR of at least 100% on an ongoing basis (HQLA covering 30-day net cash outflows under stress); HQLA may be used in stress, but the NBFC must report to RBI DoS immediately with reasons and corrective steps

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 15: liquidity disclosure on the website every quarter
  • para 57: DNBS04A quarterly; DNBS04B monthly
  • para 78: LCR disclosed every quarter (and as the Financial Statements Directions require)

If you breach it

  • not stated in the Direction

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
