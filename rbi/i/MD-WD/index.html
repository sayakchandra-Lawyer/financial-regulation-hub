Key concepts

  • Wilful default and wilful defaulter (≥ ₹25 lakh)
  • Large defaulter (≥ ₹1 crore)
  • Identification and Review Committees
  • Diversion and siphoning of funds
  • Natural justice: show-cause notice and hearing
  • Penal measures on wilful defaulters

Key obligations

  • para 4: Board policy on implementation
  • para 5(4): show-cause notice with 21 days to respond
  • para 5(7)-(11): written representation to the Review Committee within 15 days; opportunity of personal hearing (no right to a lawyer, as it is an in-house proceeding); reasoned order communicated to the wilful defaulter
  • para 5(14): non-executive/independent directors not classified unless complicity proven
  • para 6: examine all NPAs ≥ ₹25 lakh; classify within 6 months of NPA
  • para 7(3): no fresh credit to wilful defaulters; 1-year bar after delisting; 5-year bar on new ventures
  • para 11-12: monthly reporting of wilful and large defaulters to all CICs
  • para 14(1): examine wilful default before transferring loans ≥ ₹25 lakh

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 5(4): 21 days
  • para 5(7): 15 days
  • para 6(2): 6 months from NPA
  • para 11-12: monthly CIC reporting
  • para 12(2): delisting reported within 30 days

If you breach it

  • para 7(3): penal measures (credit and new-venture bars) on the borrower
  • para 20-21: lender considers a complaint to NFRA / ICAI against a negligent statutory auditor of the borrower, forwarding it to RBI (DoS, CO) and IBA after a hearing; negligent third parties reported to IBA for its caution list; forensic audit above a Board-set threshold

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
