Key concepts

  • Customer Due Diligence
  • Beneficial owner (10% / 15%)
  • Officially Valid Documents
  • Video-based CIP (V-CIP)
  • Periodic updation (risk-based)
  • CKYCR
  • Designated Director / Principal Officer

Key obligations

  • para 6: Board-approved KYC policy (acceptance, risk management, CIP, monitoring)
  • para 8: group-wide AML/CFT programme
  • para 10: documented ML/TF risk assessment, reviewed annually, placed before Board
  • para 11: risk-based approach to CDD
  • para 5(2)(iv), 21: CDD when an account-based relationship starts, for occasional or walk-in transactions of ₹50,000 or more (single or connected) and for international money transfers
  • para 5(2)(xiv), 26-27: V-CIP that meets the prescribed standards is treated on par with face-to-face identification
  • para 42(1)-(2), (7): periodic KYC updation at least every 2 / 8 / 10 years for high / medium / low-risk customers; low-risk individuals may keep transacting and update within one year of falling due or by 30-06-2026, whichever is later; three advance intimations and three reminders, one of each by letter
  • para 63(1): upload the customer's KYC records to CKYCR within 10 days of starting an account-based relationship (PML Rules r.9(1A)); communicate the KYC Identifier to the customer

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 10(3): annual risk assessment
  • para 41(1): risk categorisation of accounts reviewed at least once every six months
  • para 47(1)-(2): transaction records kept at least 5 years from the transaction; identification and address records at least 5 years after the relationship ends
  • para 54(2), 56: UNSC sanctions lists, the 2007 Order's Schedules and the UNSCR 1718 (DPRK) list checked daily; matches reported to FIU-IND and MHA (para 54(3)); WMD Act s.12A checks at onboarding and periodically (para 55(3))
  • para 42(3)(ii), 42(6): changed address verified within two months of the customer's self-declaration; customers to give updated documents within 30 days of a change
  • para 55(9): WMD Act unfreezing applications forwarded to the Central Nodal Officer within two working days

If you breach it

  • para 17(2), 43, 51: no account is opened where CDD cannot be applied (consider an STR); account operations temporarily stopped if PAN / Form 60 is not given, and the account closed if the customer refuses; each day of delay in reporting to FIU-IND, or in correcting a misreported transaction, is a separate violation. FIU-IND penalties arise under PMLA s.13 (not stated in the Direction)

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
