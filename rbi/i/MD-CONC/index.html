Key concepts

  • Single and group borrower limits
  • Large Exposure Framework (Upper Layer)
  • Group of connected counterparties
  • Sensitive sector exposure (capital market, CRE)
  • Unsecured consumer credit limits
  • IPO financing ceiling

Key obligations

  • para 6: BL: Board-approved single/group limits and consumer sub-segment limits
  • para 9: Board limits for unsecured consumer credit sub-segments, monitored by RMC
  • para 10: top-up loans on depreciating movables treated as unsecured
  • para 11: IPO financing capped at ₹1 crore per borrower
  • para 13-14: ML: exposure to a single party ≤25% and to a group ≤40% of Tier 1 capital (plus 5% / 10% if the extra is infrastructure lending or investment); NBFC-IFC 30% / 50%; Tier 1 taken from the latest audited or limited-review financials
  • para 24: ML: Board limits for capital-market and CRE exposures
  • para 33-34: UL: single counterparty ≤20% of eligible capital (Board may allow +5%)
  • para 35: UL: group of connected counterparties ≤25%
  • para 36: UL: Board policy to identify connected counterparties
  • para 49: UL: Board-set internal exposure limits on other important sectors and an internal limit on exposure to the NBFC sector

Deadlines and cycles

  • para 33, 35: limits to be met at all times
  • para 48: UL: report Large Exposures to RBI (DoS, CO) in the Annex I template: all large exposures, other and exempted exposures ≥10% of eligible capital, and the 10 largest exposures (frequency not stated in the Direction)

If you breach it

  • para 45-47: UL: a Large Exposure breach is allowed only in exceptional conditions beyond the NBFC's control; report to RBI (DoS, CO) immediately and rectify rapidly; no further exposure until back within the limit; failure may lead to penalties by the supervisor

General consequences under the statute. Statutory consequences under the RBI Act 1934: RBI may impose a monetary penalty under s.58G: up to ₹25,000, or, for NBFI business without a CoR (s.58B(4A)) or for accepting deposits or not complying with a Chapter IIIB direction or order (s.58B(5)(a), (aa)), up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved, whichever is more, plus up to ₹1 lakh a day while the default continues; a show-cause notice and hearing come first, and penalty and prosecution are alternatives (s.58G(2), (5)-(6)). As offences, s.58B(5) carries up to 3 years and fine, and s.58B(6) a fine up to ₹1 lakh plus ₹10,000 a day. RBI may also cancel the CoR (s.45-IA(6)), prohibit deposits or bar asset transfers (s.45MB), remove a director (s.45-ID), supersede the Board (s.45-IE) and petition for winding up (s.45MC). For HFCs the parallel provisions are NHB Act ss.29A(6), 49 and 52A (NHB or RBI may impose penalties, e.g. up to ₹10 lakh or twice the amount involved plus ₹1 lakh a day for not complying with a direction or order under Chapter V, s.52A(1C)).
