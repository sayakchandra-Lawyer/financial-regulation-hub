Arbitration and Conciliation Act: loan arbitration

Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996: arbitration agreements, interim relief, appointment and independence of arbitrators, time limits, setting aside and enforcement of awards

Key concepts

  • An arbitration agreement must be in writing; it may be a clause in a contract or a separate agreement, and an exchange of communications by electronic means that records the agreement counts (s. 7)
  • Interim measures from the court before or during the arbitration, or after the award but before enforcement, including securing the amount in dispute and the preservation, custody or sale of goods (s. 9(1)); interim measures from the arbitral tribunal, enforceable as if they were court orders (s. 17)
  • Independence of arbitrators: a prospective arbitrator must disclose circumstances giving rise to justifiable doubts about independence or impartiality (s. 12(1)); a person whose relationship with the parties, counsel or the dispute falls in the Seventh Schedule is ineligible, notwithstanding any prior agreement, unless the parties waive this by an express written agreement after the dispute has arisen (s. 12(5))
  • Once the arbitral tribunal is constituted, the court will not entertain a s. 9 application unless the remedy under s. 17 may not be efficacious (s. 9(3))
  • The Limitation Act, 1963 applies to arbitrations as it applies to court proceedings (s. 43)

Key obligations

  • s. 9(2): where the court grants an interim measure before the arbitration starts, commence the arbitral proceedings within 90 days of the order, or within any further time the court allows
  • s. 29A(1): in domestic arbitrations the award must be made within twelve months from completion of pleadings; the parties may extend by consent for up to six months (s. 29A(3)), after which only the court can extend the mandate (s. 29A(4))
  • s. 34(3): an application to set aside an award must be made within three months of receiving it, with a further 30 days only on sufficient cause
  • s. 36: once the time to challenge has expired the award is enforced like a court decree under the CPC; filing a s. 34 challenge does not by itself stay the award, and a stay of a money award is considered with regard to the CPC rules on stay of money decrees; where the court is satisfied that there is a prima facie case that the arbitration agreement, the underlying contract or the making of the award was induced by fraud or corruption, it stays the award unconditionally pending the s. 34 challenge (s. 36(3), second proviso)

If you breach it

  • s. 29A(4): if the award is not made in time and the court does not extend the period, the arbitrator's mandate terminates; the court may cut the arbitrator's fees by up to 5% for each month of delay attributable to the tribunal
  • s. 12(5): an arbitrator who is ineligible under the Seventh Schedule cannot act unless the parties have expressly waived it in writing after the dispute arose
