Factoring Regulation Act: assignment of receivables

Factoring Regulation Act, 2011: registration of factors with RBI, assignment of receivables, notice to debtors and filing with the Central Registry

Applies to
Factors: RBI-registered NBFCs, banks, statutory corporations and companies engaged in the factoring business (s. 2(i)); Assignors (sellers of goods and services) and debtors whose receivables are assigned

Key concepts

  • Factoring business: acquiring receivables by assignment for consideration, for collection or for financing against the assignment; credit facilities provided by a bank or an NBFC in its ordinary course of business against security of receivables are excluded (s. 2(j)(i))
  • No factor may commence or carry on factoring business without a certificate of registration from RBI; the NBFC registration requirements of the RBI Act apply (s. 3). Banks, corporations set up under a Central or State Act and Government companies do not need this registration (s. 5)
  • An assignment of receivables must be by agreement in writing, and the assignor must disclose the debtor's defences and rights of set-off to the assignee (s. 7)
  • The assignee cannot demand payment from the debtor until notice of the assignment is given to the debtor (s. 8); once notice is given the debtor must pay the assignee, and payment to the assignee in discharge of the receivables specified in the notice fully discharges the debtor to that extent (s. 9)
  • Recovery of an assigned receivable through a court or tribunal must be within the period of limitation under the Limitation Act (s. 28)

Key obligations

  • s. 19(1): register the particulars of every assignment of receivables in the factor's favour with the Central Registry set up under SARFAESI, within the time and in the manner prescribed; where receivables are financed through a Trade Receivables Discounting System, the TReDS files on the factor's behalf (s. 19(1A))

If you breach it

  • s. 21: default in filing under s. 19 makes the company and every officer in default liable to a penalty of up to Rs 5 lakh, and up to Rs 10,000 a day while it continues, imposed by RBI
  • s. 22: failure to comply with an RBI direction, penalty of up to Rs 5 lakh, and up to Rs 10,000 a day while it continues
  • s. 23: contraventions with no specific penalty, imprisonment up to one year, fine, or both; courts take cognizance only on a complaint by an authorised RBI officer (s. 24)
