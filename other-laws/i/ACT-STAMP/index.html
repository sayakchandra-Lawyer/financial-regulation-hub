Indian Stamp Act: duty on loan and security documents

Indian Stamp Act, 1899: stamp duty on loan agreements, mortgages, pledges, hypothecations and guarantees, and the effect of under-stamping

Applies to
Every lender executing loan, security or guarantee documents in India; Borrowers and guarantors who execute them

Key concepts

  • Duty rates are in Schedule I; most States substitute their own rates for instruments such as mortgages and agreements relating to deposit of title-deeds, pawn or pledge, so the State where the document is executed or the property lies usually decides the duty
  • Schedule I articles relevant to lending: Article 6 (agreement relating to deposit of title-deeds, pawn or pledge), Article 40 (mortgage deed) and Article 57 (security bond or mortgage deed executed by a surety)

Key obligations

  • s. 17: instruments chargeable with duty and executed in India must be stamped before or at the time of execution
  • Check the State Act or State Schedule for the rate; pay the higher-State differential where a document executed in one State is brought into another

If you breach it

  • s. 35: an instrument not duly stamped is not admissible in evidence and cannot be acted upon, registered or authenticated, unless the duty and a penalty (Rs 5 or ten times the deficient duty, whichever is more) are paid
  • s. 33: a public officer or court before whom an unstamped instrument is produced must impound it
