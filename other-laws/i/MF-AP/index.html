Andhra Pradesh microfinance law (2011)

Andhra Pradesh Micro Finance Institutions (Regulation of Money Lending) Act, 2011

Applies to
Micro finance institutions lending to women self-help groups and their members in Andhra Pradesh, which by definition include companies and NBFCs whose principal or incidental activity is lending to the below poverty line population (s. 2(d))

Key concepts

  • MFIs must register with the district Registering Authority, stating the area of operation, interest rates, due-diligence and recovery systems (s. 3)
  • Interest recovered may not exceed the principal; a loan stands discharged once twice the principal has been realised (s. 9)
  • No further loan to an SHG with an outstanding bank loan without the Registering Authority's prior written approval, and no loan to an SHG member during two subsisting loans from any source (s. 10)
  • Challenges by microfinance NBFCs to the State's power to regulate them were disposed of by the High Court in 2013 without deciding legislative competence. The Supreme Court later held (for the Kerala and Gujarat money lenders Acts) that state money-lending laws do not apply to RBI-regulated NBFCs (Nedumpilli Finance Co. v. State of Kerala, 2022 INSC 545)

Key obligations

  • s. 8: display interest rates; charge nothing beyond the prescribed application charge
  • s. 11: give the borrower a loan statement within seven days with the effective rate of interest, signed receipts and copies of documents; repayments are made at the Gram Panchayat office or a designated public place; no recovery agents and no coercive recovery
  • s. 12: file a monthly statement of borrowers, loans and interest with the Registering Authority before the 10th of each month

If you breach it

  • s. 16: persons responsible for an MFI who use coercive measures against SHGs, members or their families, imprisonment up to three years, fine up to Rs 1 lakh, or both
  • s. 17: carrying on business without registration, or lending in breach of s. 10, is separately punishable
