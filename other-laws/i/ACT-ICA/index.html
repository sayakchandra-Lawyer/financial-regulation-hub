Indian Contract Act: guarantee, indemnity, pledge, lien

Indian Contract Act, 1872: contracts of indemnity and guarantee (ss. 124-147), bailment, bankers' lien and pledge (ss. 148-181), and related general provisions

Applies to
Every lender taking a guarantee, an indemnity or a pledge of goods, shares or other movables; Guarantors (sureties), pawnors and borrowers; Banks, as holders of the general lien in s. 171

Key concepts

  • Contract of indemnity: a promise to save the other party from loss caused by the promisor or any other person (s. 124); rights of the indemnity-holder when sued (s. 125)
  • Contract of guarantee: a promise to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person on default; the parties are the surety, the principal debtor and the creditor; it may be oral or written (s. 126)
  • Co-extensive liability: the surety is liable to the same extent as the principal debtor unless the contract says otherwise (s. 128)
  • Continuing guarantee: one that extends to a series of transactions (s. 129); it may be revoked as to future transactions by notice (s. 130) and is revoked as to future transactions by the surety's death, unless the contract says otherwise (s. 131)
  • Discharge of the surety: by a variance in the terms made without the surety's consent (s. 133); by a release or discharge of the principal debtor (s. 134); by a composition, a promise to give time or a promise not to sue made with the principal debtor without the surety's assent (s. 135); by an act or omission of the creditor that impairs the surety's eventual remedy (s. 139); and to the extent of the value of any security the creditor loses or gives up without the surety's consent (s. 141)
  • Rights of a surety who pays: subrogation to all the creditor's rights against the principal debtor (s. 140) and the benefit of every security the creditor held when the guarantee was given (s. 141)
  • Bankers' general lien: bankers may retain goods bailed to them as security for a general balance of account, unless the contract says otherwise (s. 171)
  • Pledge: the bailment of goods as security for a debt or the performance of a promise; the bailor is the pawnor and the bailee the pawnee (s. 172)
  • Penalty clauses: where a contract names a sum or a penalty for breach, only reasonable compensation up to that sum is recoverable; a stipulation for increased interest from the date of default may be a penalty (s. 74 and Explanation)
  • Agreements that limit the time to enforce rights, or extinguish rights on the expiry of a specified period, are void to that extent (s. 28). Exception 3 saves a written guarantee by a bank or a financial institution that ends liability after a period of at least one year from a specified event; 'bank' and 'financial institution' (a public financial institution) are defined narrowly in the Explanation, so most NBFCs and HFCs are not covered

Key obligations

  • s. 176: on the pawnor's default the pawnee may sue on the debt and keep the goods as collateral, or sell the goods after giving the pawnor reasonable notice of the sale; any shortfall remains payable and any surplus must be paid to the pawnor
  • s. 177: a defaulting pawnor may redeem the goods at any time before their actual sale, paying the expenses caused by the default
  • s. 173-174: the pawnee may retain the goods for the debt, interest and necessary expenses, but not for any other debt unless the contract says so; such a contract is presumed for subsequent advances by the pawnee
  • s. 133, 135 and 141: obtain the surety's consent before varying the loan terms, giving the borrower time or a composition, or releasing or losing any security, or the surety may be discharged
  • s. 130: a continuing guarantee may be revoked by the surety for future transactions by notice to the creditor; past liabilities survive
  • s. 137: mere forbearance to sue the borrower does not discharge the surety, unless the guarantee says otherwise; a binding agreement to give time does (s. 135)

If you breach it

  • s. 133: a variance without the surety's consent discharges the surety as to transactions after the variance; ss. 134, 135 and 139: release of the debtor, a binding promise to give time or not to sue, or an act impairing the surety's remedy discharges the surety; s. 141: losing or parting with security discharges the surety to the value of that security
  • s. 74: a lender can recover only reasonable compensation, not the full stipulated penalty, for breach; higher default interest may be treated as a penalty
  • s. 28: a clause cutting down the time to sue is void to that extent, except within Exception 3
