Sale of Goods Act: title to goods financed

Sale of Goods Act, 1930: passing of property, transfer of title and the unpaid seller's rights

Applies to
Lenders financing or taking security over goods: vehicles, equipment, inventory and receivables from goods sold; Buyers and sellers of goods, including borrowers who buy goods with borrowed money

Key concepts

  • Property passes when the parties intend it to pass, judged from the contract, the parties' conduct and the circumstances (s. 19); for specific goods in a deliverable state it passes when the contract is made, even if payment or delivery is postponed (s. 20)
  • For unascertained or future goods, property passes when goods of the contract description are unconditionally appropriated to the contract (s. 23)
  • Nemo dat: a buyer from a person who is not the owner gets no better title than the seller had, except where the owner's conduct precludes him, or a mercantile agent in possession with the owner's consent sells in the ordinary course of business to a buyer in good faith (s. 27)
  • A buyer in good faith from a seller holding under a voidable contract that has not been rescinded gets good title (s. 29)
  • A seller or buyer left in possession of goods or documents of title can pass good title, including by pledge, to a person taking in good faith without notice (s. 30)
  • Unpaid seller: one who has not been paid or tendered the whole price, or whose negotiable instrument taken as conditional payment was dishonoured (s. 45)

Key obligations

  • s. 46-47: the unpaid seller keeps a lien on goods in his possession for the price, a right of stoppage in transit on the buyer's insolvency and a limited right of resale, even after property has passed; a financier of the buyer generally takes subject to these rights (s. 53(1)), except that a good-faith pledgee for value of a document of title takes free of the lien and stoppage to the extent of its rights (s. 53(1) proviso)
  • s. 27 and 30: before lending against goods, confirm the borrower's title and possession; a pledge from a person who is not the owner binds the owner only in the cases the law allows, such as s. 30 here and Contract Act ss. 178 (mercantile agent) and 178A (possession under a voidable contract)

If you breach it

  • s. 27: a lender taking goods from a non-owner, outside the exceptions, takes no better title than the borrower had
