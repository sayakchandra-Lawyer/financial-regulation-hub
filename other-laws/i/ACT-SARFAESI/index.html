SARFAESI Act: enforcing security without court

Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act, 2002

Applies to
Secured creditors as defined in the Act: banks, financial institutions, including NBFCs and HFCs only where they fall within a Central Government notification and meet its conditions, asset reconstruction companies and debenture trustees; Borrowers and guarantors whose secured assets are enforced; Every creditor filing security interests with CERSAI (Central Registry)

Key concepts

  • Enforcement without court: a security interest may be enforced by the secured creditor without the intervention of a court or tribunal, notwithstanding ss. 69-69A of the Transfer of Property Act (s. 13(1))
  • Central Registry (CERSAI) records creation, modification and satisfaction of security interests (Chapter IV)
  • Priority: once registered, the secured creditor's debts are paid in priority over all other debts and Government dues, subject to the IBC where insolvency proceedings are pending (s. 26E)
  • Exclusions: the Act does not apply to liens under the Contract or Sale of Goods Acts, pledges of movables, aircraft and vessels, unpaid sellers' rights, property not liable to attachment or sale under the first proviso to CPC s. 60(1) (but not property specifically charged with the debt), financial assets of Rs 1 lakh or less, agricultural land, or where the amount due is less than 20% of principal and interest (s. 31)

Key obligations

  • s. 13(2): after default and NPA classification, serve a written demand on the borrower to discharge the liability in full within 60 days; NPA classification is not needed for issuers of debt securities, where the debenture trustee enforces
  • s. 13(3): the notice must state the amount payable and the secured assets to be enforced
  • s. 13(3A): consider any representation or objection and, if rejecting it, communicate reasons within 15 days of receipt
  • s. 13(4): on non-payment, take possession of secured assets, take over management of the business, appoint a manager, or require third-party debtors to pay
  • s. 13(8): if the full dues and costs are tendered before publication of the auction or sale notice, the assets cannot be sold and the creditor must take no further step
  • s. 14: to take physical possession, apply to the Chief Metropolitan Magistrate or District Magistrate with the affidavit the proviso requires
  • s. 26D: no secured creditor may exercise Chapter III enforcement rights unless its security interest is registered with the Central Registry
  • s. 13(9): where more than one secured creditor finances the asset, s. 13(4) measures need the agreement of secured creditors holding at least 60% in value of the outstanding amount on a record date, subject to the IBC
  • s. 36: no s. 13(4) measure may be taken unless the claim is within the limitation period under the Limitation Act

Deadlines and cycles

  • 60 days: s. 13(2) demand notice period
  • 15 days: reply to the borrower's objection (s. 13(3A))
  • 45 days: borrower's or aggrieved person's application to the DRT from the date of the measure (s. 17(1))
  • 60 days: DRT to dispose of the s. 17 application, as far as possible (s. 17(5))
  • 30 days: appeal by any aggrieved person to the DRAT (s. 18(1)); a borrower appealing must deposit 50% of the debt, reducible to 25%

If you breach it

  • s. 18: no appeal to the DRAT is entertained unless the borrower deposits 50% of the debt claimed or determined, whichever is less; the DRAT may reduce it to not less than 25%
  • s. 26D: an unregistered security interest cannot be enforced under the Act
