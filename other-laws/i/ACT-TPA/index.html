Transfer of Property Act: mortgages, charges, assignment of debts

Transfer of Property Act, 1882: mortgages and charges of immovable property (ss. 58-104) and transfer of actionable claims (ss. 130-137)

Applies to
Lenders taking a mortgage or charge over land or buildings, including home loans and loans against property; Mortgagors, sureties and later purchasers or lenders over the same property; Lenders buying or selling loan receivables (assignment of actionable claims)

Key concepts

  • Mortgage: the transfer of an interest in specific immovable property to secure money advanced or to be advanced, an existing or future debt, or an engagement that may give rise to a pecuniary liability (s. 58(a))
  • Kinds of mortgage: simple (s. 58(b)), by conditional sale (s. 58(c)), usufructuary (s. 58(d)), English (s. 58(e)), by deposit of title-deeds in the notified towns (s. 58(f)) and anomalous (s. 58(g))
  • Form: where the principal money is Rs 100 or more, a mortgage other than one by deposit of title-deeds can be made only by a registered instrument signed by the mortgagor and attested by at least two witnesses (s. 59)
  • Right to redeem: once the principal is due, the mortgagor may pay and require return of the title documents, re-delivery of possession and a re-transfer or acknowledgement (s. 60)
  • Charge: where immovable property is made security by act of parties or operation of law without amounting to a mortgage; the rules for simple mortgages apply so far as may be (s. 100)
  • Priority: later rights over the same property are subject to earlier rights, absent a special contract (s. 48)
  • Assignment of unsecured debts: an actionable claim (a debt not secured by a mortgage of immovable property or by hypothecation or pledge of movables, s. 3) is transferred by a signed written instrument; the transfer is complete on execution and the transferee may sue in its own name, but the debtor's dealings with the transferor stay valid until the debtor has express notice (ss. 130-131); the transferee takes subject to the transferor's liabilities and equities, such as set-off (s. 132)

Key obligations

  • s. 59: register and attest every mortgage deed where the principal is Rs 100 or more, unless it is a mortgage by deposit of title-deeds
  • s. 67 and 67A: a mortgagee enforces through a suit for sale or foreclosure once the money is due, and must sue on all mortgages from the same mortgagor on which money has become due, absent a contrary contract
  • s. 69(1): a mortgagee may sell without the court only in the listed cases: certain English mortgages; where the deed expressly confers the power and the mortgagee is the Government; or where the deed expressly confers it and the property is in Kolkata, Chennai, Mumbai or a town the State notifies. Otherwise lenders use SARFAESI or a suit
  • s. 69(2): even where the power exists, it can be used only after a written notice demanding the principal has gone unpaid for three months, or interest of at least Rs 500 has been in arrear for three months
  • s. 69A: a mortgagee with a s. 69 power of sale may appoint a receiver of the income of the property; commission is capped at 5% of gross receipts unless the court allows otherwise
  • s. 65A: a mortgagor in lawful possession may grant leases binding on the mortgagee only on the terms in s. 65A(2) (ordinary management, best rent, no premium, buildings up to three years), unless the deed provides otherwise
  • s. 130-132: for unsecured receivables, take a signed written assignment and give each debtor express written notice, so that later payments to the assignor do not bind you; secured loans move with the security under the TPA and the Registration and Stamp Acts

If you breach it

  • s. 59 with Registration Act s. 49: an unregistered mortgage deed that needed registration does not affect the property
  • s. 91-92: a surety or later encumbrancer who redeems is subrogated to the mortgagee's rights
