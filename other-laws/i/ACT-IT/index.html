Key concepts

  • Where a law requires a signature, an electronic signature in the prescribed manner satisfies it (s. 5); electronic signature techniques include Aadhaar or other e-KYC based e-authentication (s. 3A and Second Schedule)
  • A contract is not unenforceable merely because the proposal, acceptance or revocation was expressed in electronic form (s. 10A)
  • The Act does not apply to the documents in the First Schedule (s. 1(4)). Since 26-09-2022 the exclusion for negotiable instruments does not cover a cheque, demand promissory note or bill of exchange issued in favour of or endorsed by an entity regulated by RBI, NHB, SEBI, IRDAI or PFRDA, and the exclusion for powers of attorney does not cover one that empowers such a regulated entity to act for the executant; trusts and wills remain excluded. The same 2022 notification (S.O. 4720(E)) omitted the entry for contracts for the sale or conveyance of immovable property; stamp duty and registration law still apply to such contracts

Key obligations

  • s. 43A: a body corporate handling sensitive personal data must implement and maintain reasonable security practices; s. 43A is omitted from 13-05-2027 by the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (see the DPDP section)
  • s. 72A: do not disclose personal information obtained under a lawful contract without the person's consent, with intent to cause or knowing it likely to cause wrongful loss or gain

If you breach it

  • s. 43A: negligence in maintaining reasonable security practices that causes wrongful loss or gain makes the body corporate liable to pay damages by way of compensation
  • s. 66C: fraudulent or dishonest use of another person's electronic signature, password or other unique identification feature, imprisonment up to three years and fine up to Rs 1 lakh
