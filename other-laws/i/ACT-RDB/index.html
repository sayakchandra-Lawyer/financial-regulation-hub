Recovery of Debts and Bankruptcy Act, 1993 (formerly the Recovery of Debts Due to Banks and Financial Institutions Act)

Applies to
Banks, notified financial institutions, asset reconstruction companies and debenture trustees for secured debt securities; Borrowers and guarantors sued for debts of Rs 10 lakh or more (or other notified amount, not below Rs 1 lakh)

Key concepts

  • Threshold: the Act does not apply where the debt due is less than Rs 10 lakh or such other amount, not less than Rs 1 lakh, as the Central Government notifies (s. 1(4))
  • Financial institution: a public financial institution, a registered asset reconstruction company, a registered debenture trustee for secured debt securities, or any institution the Central Government notifies (s. 2(h))
  • Recovery certificate: the DRT issues a certificate and the Recovery Officer recovers by attachment and sale, taking possession of secured or other property, arrest and detention, or appointing a receiver (s. 25)
  • Priority: secured creditors' debts have priority over other debts and Government dues (s. 31B)

Key obligations

  • s. 19: file the original application before the DRT with jurisdiction; the defendant must file a written statement within 30 days of service of summons
  • s. 19: the DRT is to dispose of the application within 180 days of filing, as far as possible
  • s. 21: an appeal by the debtor is not entertained unless 50% of the debt determined is deposited, reducible to 25%

Deadlines and cycles

  • 30 days: appeal to the DRAT from receipt of the DRT order (s. 20(3)), extendable for sufficient cause
  • 6 months: DRAT to endeavour to dispose of the appeal (s. 20(6))

If you breach it

  • s. 26: the defendant cannot dispute the correctness of the amount in the recovery certificate before the Recovery Officer
