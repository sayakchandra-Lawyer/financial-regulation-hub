[ACT-ICA] Indian Contract Act: guarantee, indemnity, pledge, lien | Indian Contract Act, 1872: contracts of indemnity and guarantee (ss. 124-147), bailment, bankers' lien and pledge (ss. 148-181), and related general provisions | Act 9 of 1872 | Act | In force (as amended) | issued 1872-04-25 | subject: Contracts, guarantees and pledges
applies: Every lender taking a guarantee, an indemnity or a pledge of goods, shares or other movables; Guarantors (sureties), pawnors and borrowers; Banks, as holders of the general lien in s. 171
concepts: Contract of indemnity: a promise to save the other party from loss caused by the promisor or any other person (s. 124); rights of the indemnity-holder when sued (s. 125)
concepts: Contract of guarantee: a promise to perform the promise or discharge the liability of a third person on default; the parties are the surety, the principal debtor and the creditor; it may be oral or written (s. 126)
concepts: Co-extensive liability: the surety is liable to the same extent as the principal debtor unless the contract says otherwise (s. 128)
concepts: Continuing guarantee: one that extends to a series of transactions (s. 129); it may be revoked as to future transactions by notice (s. 130) and is revoked as to future transactions by the surety's death, unless the contract says otherwise (s. 131)
concepts: Discharge of the surety: by a variance in the terms made without the surety's consent (s. 133); by a release or discharge of the principal debtor (s. 134); by a composition, a promise to give time or a promise not to sue made with the principal debtor without the surety's assent (s. 135); by an act or omission of the creditor that impairs the surety's eventual remedy (s. 139); and to the extent of the value of any security the creditor loses or gives up without the surety's consent (s. 141)
concepts: Rights of a surety who pays: subrogation to all the creditor's rights against the principal debtor (s. 140) and the benefit of every security the creditor held when the guarantee was given (s. 141)
concepts: Bankers' general lien: bankers may retain goods bailed to them as security for a general balance of account, unless the contract says otherwise (s. 171)
concepts: Pledge: the bailment of goods as security for a debt or the performance of a promise; the bailor is the pawnor and the bailee the pawnee (s. 172)
concepts: Penalty clauses: where a contract names a sum or a penalty for breach, only reasonable compensation up to that sum is recoverable; a stipulation for increased interest from the date of default may be a penalty (s. 74 and Explanation)
concepts: Agreements that limit the time to enforce rights, or extinguish rights on the expiry of a specified period, are void to that extent (s. 28). Exception 3 saves a written guarantee by a bank or a financial institution that ends liability after a period of at least one year from a specified event; 'bank' and 'financial institution' (a public financial institution) are defined narrowly in the Explanation, so most NBFCs and HFCs are not covered
duties: s. 176: on the pawnor's default the pawnee may sue on the debt and keep the goods as collateral, or sell the goods after giving the pawnor reasonable notice of the sale; any shortfall remains payable and any surplus must be paid to the pawnor
duties: s. 177: a defaulting pawnor may redeem the goods at any time before their actual sale, paying the expenses caused by the default
duties: s. 173-174: the pawnee may retain the goods for the debt, interest and necessary expenses, but not for any other debt unless the contract says so; such a contract is presumed for subsequent advances by the pawnee
duties: s. 133, 135 and 141: obtain the surety's consent before varying the loan terms, giving the borrower time or a composition, or releasing or losing any security, or the surety may be discharged
duties: s. 130: a continuing guarantee may be revoked by the surety for future transactions by notice to the creditor; past liabilities survive
duties: s. 137: mere forbearance to sue the borrower does not discharge the surety, unless the guarantee says otherwise; a binding agreement to give time does (s. 135)
breach: s. 133: a variance without the surety's consent discharges the surety as to transactions after the variance; ss. 134, 135 and 139: release of the debtor, a binding promise to give time or not to sue, or an act impairing the surety's remedy discharges the surety; s. 141: losing or parting with security discharges the surety to the value of that security
breach: s. 74: a lender can recover only reasonable compensation, not the full stipulated penalty, for breach; higher default interest may be treated as a penalty
breach: s. 28: a clause cutting down the time to sue is void to that extent, except within Exception 3

GLOSSARY (plain-language meanings, not statutory text):
Guarantee (Contract of guarantee): A promise to pay or perform for a borrower if the borrower defaults. The guarantor is the surety, the borrower the principal debtor and the lender the creditor (Contract Act s. 126).
Continuing guarantee: A guarantee that covers a series of transactions, such as a running cash-credit account. The surety can revoke it for future transactions by notice; past liability survives (ss. 129-130).
Discharge of surety: The guarantor is released, fully or partly, if the lender changes the loan terms, gives the borrower time, releases the borrower or loses security without the guarantor's consent (ss. 133-141).
Indemnity (Contract of indemnity): A promise to make good a loss suffered by the other party, whether caused by the promisor or anyone else (s. 124).
Pledge (Pawn): Security over goods or other movables by handing them over to the lender. The lender may sell them after reasonable notice if the borrower defaults (ss. 172, 176).
Hypothecation: A charge over movable property, present or future, without handing it over to the lender; it includes a floating charge (SARFAESI s. 2(1)(n)). Common for vehicle, equipment and stock loans.
Banker's lien (General lien): A bank's right to keep goods or securities of a customer in its hands as security for the customer's general balance, unless agreed otherwise (s. 171).
Mortgage: Security over land or buildings by transferring an interest in the property to the lender (TPA s. 58). There are six kinds, including simple, English and by deposit of title-deeds.
Equitable mortgage (Mortgage by deposit of title-deeds): A mortgage made by handing over the title documents of a property, with intent to create security, in a notified town. It needs no registered deed (TPA s. 58(f)).
Charge (Charge on immovable property): Security over immovable property that does not amount to a mortgage, created by agreement or by law. The rules for simple mortgages apply to it (TPA s. 100).
Right to redeem (Redemption): The borrower's right to pay off a mortgage and get back the title deeds and the property (TPA s. 60).
Foreclosure: A court decree that ends the borrower's right to redeem a mortgage. In everyday banking the word also means early repayment of a loan, which is a different thing.
Assignment of debt (Transfer of actionable claim): The transfer of an unsecured debt or receivable by a signed written instrument. Until the debtor gets express notice, its payments to the original creditor remain valid (TPA ss. 130-131). Secured loans are transferred with their security under other rules.
Secured creditor: Under SARFAESI, a bank, notified financial institution, asset reconstruction company or debenture trustee holding a security interest. Only secured creditors can use SARFAESI.
Security interest: Any right, title or interest in property created for a secured creditor, including a mortgage, charge, hypothecation or assignment (SARFAESI s. 2(1)(zf)).
Demand notice (Section 13(2) notice): The SARFAESI notice asking a defaulting borrower whose account is an NPA to pay in full within 60 days, before the lender can take possession.
Possession notice (Section 13(4)): The step after the 60-day demand notice: the lender takes possession of the secured asset, takes over the business or asks the borrower's debtors to pay it directly.
CERSAI (Central Registry): The Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India, where lenders record security interests. A secured creditor cannot enforce under SARFAESI unless its security is registered there (s. 26D).
DRT (Debts Recovery Tribunal): The tribunal that hears recovery cases of banks and notified financial institutions above the RDB Act threshold (Rs 10 lakh in the Act, or another amount the Central Government notifies) and borrowers' challenges to SARFAESI action.
DRAT (Debts Recovery Appellate Tribunal): The appeal tribunal above the DRT. A borrower appealing must usually deposit 50% of the debt, which can be reduced to 25%.
Recovery certificate: The certificate the DRT issues for the amount due. The Recovery Officer then recovers it by attachment and sale, arrest, or a receiver (RDB s. 25).
Acknowledgement of debt: A written, signed admission of liability by the borrower or guarantor before limitation expires. It starts a fresh limitation period (Limitation Act s. 18).
Limitation period: The time within which a lender must sue: generally three years for a loan, twelve years to enforce a mortgage (Limitation Act Schedule).
Stamp duty: A tax on documents such as loan agreements, mortgages and guarantees. Under-stamped documents cannot be used as evidence until duty and penalty are paid (Stamp Act s. 35).
Impounding: When an unstamped or under-stamped document is produced before a court or officer, it is kept back until the proper duty and penalty are paid (Stamp Act s. 33).
Compulsory registration: Documents that create or transfer rights of Rs 100 or more in immovable property must be registered with the Sub-Registrar within four months, or they do not affect the property (Registration Act ss. 17, 23, 49).
Cheque bounce (Section 138 offence): The offence when a cheque given for a debt is returned unpaid for lack of funds and the drawer does not pay within 15 days of a demand notice.
E-mandate bounce (Section 25 PSS Act): The offence when an ECS, NACH or other electronic debit for a debt fails for lack of funds and the payer does not pay within 15 days of a demand notice.
Section 63 certificate: The certificate required to put an electronic record, such as an e-signed agreement or system statement, in evidence. It is signed by the person in charge of the system and an expert (BSA s. 63(4)).
Certified copy of bank records: A copy of an entry in a bank's books, certified by an authorised officer, which courts accept as evidence of the entry (Bankers' Books Evidence Act, 2026 s. 5).
Coercive action: Recovery methods that State laws ban, such as threats, violence, following the borrower, visiting the home or workplace to pressure, or seizing documents (for example Tamil Nadu Act 40 of 2025 s. 20).
Credit information company (Credit bureau): A company registered with RBI that collects credit data from lenders and supplies credit reports. Every lender must be a member of at least one (CICRA s. 15).
Unpaid seller: A seller who has not been paid in full. He keeps a lien on the goods in his possession and can stop them in transit, even after ownership has passed (Sale of Goods Act ss. 45-47).
Substituted performance: Getting a broken contract performed by someone else and recovering the cost from the party in breach, after 30 days' notice (Specific Relief Act s. 20).
Deficiency in service: A shortfall in the quality or manner of a service, including banking and lending, that a consumer can complain about to a consumer commission (CPA s. 2(11)).
